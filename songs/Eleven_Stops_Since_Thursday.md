### What the song is
A glossy sad-pop music video sung completely straight, with a raccoon in the window seat and a dumpster as the ex. For twenty seconds the lead is his interior voice, off-picture, breathy and double-tracked, and the song never says what "you" is. It says he still smells like you, and it says he would go through your trash. Then the bus has stopped, the streetlight is on, the lid lifts on the key change, and for the first time his mouth opens. He sings one held "Oh" a whole step too low, because the band changed key and he did not, and then he catches up and says what you say to a dumpster that never left: "You're still here."

The song never names the dumpster, the raccoon, the bus or the rain. The noun is in the picture and in "your trash." The video's working title is **Window Seat**. The song is titled *Eleven Stops Since Thursday* because the title of a deadpan song should not say what the picture is about to do.

### The structural flaw (held, never fixed)
The **band takes the key change on time and the voice takes it one bar late.** At 20.0 the band lands in G minor with the lid. The voice enters on that same downbeat on one held "Oh" that is still in the old key, a whole step low: sung on A-flat, the third of F minor, against a G minor chord, where it grinds half a step against the root. It holds that one note for all of bar 9 with no bend, swell, vibrato or slide. On the downbeat of bar 10 (22.5) it steps up a whole step to B-flat, the third of G minor, and sings "You're still here" in the right key. It is not repeated, re-sung, apologised for or fixed: no second Oh, no extra bar, no vocal earlier than 20.0, no button chord. The track ends on a clean cut, not on a resolution to cover it.

This is the lateness itself, and only the lateness. The key change is not the flaw; the voice missing it is. It is a different kind from *Box, Box, Hat* (a miscount) and *Just a Sunday* (a dead tine, a missing note): here every note is there and every count is right, and the voice is simply in the wrong key for exactly one bar. Nearest catalog neighbors: *Temp Love* (the bridge modulates into an unrelated key and the last chorus just starts back in the old one: there the whole band changes and nothing is late) and *Closed Since Ninety-Eight* (a harmony a bar late for the entire runtime: there it is a second voice, all song). Here the band is right, the lead is late once, and it recovers.

### Sonic rules checked (`notes/song-suggestions.md`)
Nobody is acting: the voice is plain, breathy and unbelted, and the emotion arrives through the arrangement (the pad, the bells, the top end opening) rather than a louder performance. One structural flaw, held and never fixed: the late voice on the key change. The top end is kept dark and rounded all the way to 20.0; it opens once, on the light, and that is the one deliberate exception, not a new habit. The rain and the street are picture sounds laid in the edit, under the bed. Original lyric and melody; no real names, brands or artists; no parentheses in the lyric.
