# Master Control, 4 AM

*Long-form dialog scene — EyeWash Station. Anchor piece for the network framing device.*

**Slugline:** INT. EYEWASH STATION — MASTER CONTROL — 4:00 AM
**Runtime target:** 3–5 min (≈4 script pages)
**Music cue:** **Haunted or Unscheduled** (Master Control bed) — rack hum + 60Hz mains as the bed. Sparse Rhodes figure, four notes, enters at Beat C and never develops. **Rinse Cycle** (sign-off theme) plays twice — identically — at 3:44 and again at 3:44. Under the last beat, audio from an unlogged program bleeds in: a laser hum, a gavel, a poodle.
**Status:** draft
**Dialog versions (alts):** [`versions/master-control-4am-versions.md`](versions/master-control-4am-versions.md)


**Cast:** OZ (overnight master control, on camera), CONTINUITY VOICE (V.O. only — never seen, never explained), MARGUERITE FLOOD (doorway, late), ONE IMPOSSIBLE MONITOR

**Spine:** Oz logs the impossible without escalating. The Continuity Voice sells apocalypse in the cadence of a tire ad. Nobody in this room is frightened, and nobody in this room is lying.

**Hard rule:** **Never explain** whether the feed is haunted or unscheduled. Oz says one thing. Marguerite says the other. The scene does not adjudicate. Any draft that resolves this is the wrong draft.

**Emotional notes:** This is a scene about a man doing his job correctly. Play it warm, not creepy. Oz talks to equipment because equipment answers in the only way he trusts — by working or not working. Marguerite is not a threat; she is a weather system with a license. The dread is entirely structural: the room is calm and the clock is not.

### A — Levels

```
INT. EYEWASH STATION — MASTER CONTROL — NIGHT

Windowless. A wall of CRT monitors, four across and five down, each
one showing something different and none of them showing the same
station.

Row one: lava. A woman in evening wear choosing basalt.
Row two: two glowing serpents in a suburban hedge.
Row three: a laundromat, empty, dryers turning.
Row four: a corridor of mirrors, unoccupied, and then not.
Row five: bars and tone. Bars and tone. Bars and tone. A room.

OZ (50s, cardigan, reading glasses pushed up into gray hair) sits at
the board. A thermos. A laminated log sheet with a grease pencil tied
to it by a shoelace.

The clock above the monitor wall reads 3:41.
```

```
OZ
(to the board, turning a pot a quarter inch)
There you are.
(beat)
You've been doing that since Tuesday. I'm not going to
mention it if you don't.

(He writes on the log. He speaks as he writes, the way people
do when the writing is the important part.)

OZ (CONT'D)
Oh-three-four-one. Levels nominal. Channel steady.
(looking up at row five)
Steady-ish.
```

```
Row five, position two: a shot of THIS ROOM. The monitor wall, the
board, the back of Oz's cardigan.

Oz does not look at it. He looks at the audio meters, which is where
he is supposed to be looking.
```

### B — Bump One

```
INT. MASTER CONTROL — CONTINUOUS

Oz slides a fader. On the program monitor, a card: EYEWASH STATION —
CHANNEL 12½.
```

```
CONTINUITY VOICE (V.O.)
(warm, over-rehearsed, institutional)
You're watching EyeWash Station. Channel twelve and a half —
between the ones you meant to watch.
(beat)
Coming up next: something you'll want to sit down for.

OZ

He does that.

Oh-three-four-two. Bump one, clean.
```

### C — The One He Doesn't Look At

```
INT. MASTER CONTROL — CONTINUOUS

On row five, position two — the monitor showing this room — OZ STANDS
UP.

In the room, Oz is sitting down.

He is writing. He has not stopped writing.

Hold on both. Let the audience do the work.
```

```
OZ
(without looking up, to the monitor)
Not yet.
```

```
He finishes the line on the log. Caps the grease pencil. Then he
stands — to top off the thermos cup, an errand of absolutely no
consequence.

On the monitor, the room is empty.

He sits back down. On the monitor, some seconds later, he sits back
down.
```

```
OZ (CONT'D)
(settling, to the board)
Don't get smug.
(beat)
You were four seconds off in March. I logged it. It's in the
book. Anybody can look.
```

### D — Marguerite in the Doorway

```
INT. MASTER CONTROL — DOORWAY — CONTINUOUS

MARGUERITE FLOOD fills the doorway without entering it. Business
formal from a decade that keeps sliding. Reading glasses on a chain.
One earring that is unmistakably a transmitter.

She has a folder. She does not open it. That's not what folders are
for.
```

```
MARGUERITE
We have a complaint.

OZ
(not turning)
Mm.

MARGUERITE
A viewer in the northeast quadrant reports seeing a program
between one and two a.m. that she describes as — 
(she does not consult the folder)
— "the inside of my own house, but earlier."

OZ
Was it on the log?

MARGUERITE
That's the question I'm here to not ask.

OZ
It was on the log.
(he turns the log around on the console so she could
read it, if she came in, which she won't)
Oh-one-fifteen. Unlisted acquisition. Ran forty-two minutes.
Went out clean.

MARGUERITE
Clean.

OZ
No dropouts. No level issues. Audio was hot on the front but
it settled.
(beat)
It was a good-looking program, Marguerite.

MARGUERITE
Don't editorialize on a log sheet.

OZ
I didn't. I'm editorializing now, out loud, where it doesn't
count.
```

```
She steps in. One step. That's the whole entrance.
```

```
MARGUERITE
Here is the position of this station, which you will not be
required to repeat, because you will not be asked.
(and she is not performing this; she has said it so
many times it has worn smooth)
We are not liable for what the affected saw. We are liable for
the aspect ratio. If a viewer reports a distortion, we check
the distortion. If a viewer reports content, we check the
carriage agreement. If a viewer reports that a program showed
them their own kitchen at a quarter past one in the morning
from the height of the smoke detector, we ask whether the
image was pillarboxed.
(beat)
Was it pillarboxed?

OZ
Four by three. Full frame.

MARGUERITE
Then we are compliant and she is describing weather.

OZ
It was indoors.

MARGUERITE
(already writing)
Indoor weather.
```

```
OZ
For the record — 

MARGUERITE
There is no record. There's a log and there's a license.

OZ
For the log, then. It's not haunted.

MARGUERITE
(pausing, glasses coming down the chain)
No?

OZ
It's unscheduled.

MARGUERITE
(a long beat; she has an answer and she has decided
the answer costs more than the silence)
The tower predates the town, Oz. That's a zoning matter, not
a mystery.
(she taps the doorframe twice with two knuckles,
the way you'd knock on wood you owned)
Everyone gets fifteen minutes. That's not fame. That's the
rinse duration.
```

```
She goes. The folder goes with her, still closed.
```

```
OZ
```

### F — Sign-Off at 3:44

```
INT. MASTER CONTROL — CONTINUOUS

The clock rolls to 3:44.

On the program monitor: stock footage of a cul-de-sac at dawn.
Sprinklers. A flag that is very slightly the wrong proportion.
**Rinse Cycle** plays under it, warm and institutional and one bar
too long in every phrase.

The card comes up: WE NOW CONCLUDE OUR BROADCAST DAY.
```

```
CONTINUITY VOICE (V.O.)
We now conclude our broadcast day. On behalf of everyone at
EyeWash Station, thank you for your eyes tonight.
(beat)
Rinse for fifteen minutes. Do not blink.

OZ
(writing, calmly)
Oh-three-four-four. Sign-off.
```

```
He caps the pencil. He reaches for the master fader.

The card does not go away.

It sits there. Twelve seconds. Fifteen. The card is patient in a way
that cards are not.

Oz's hand stays on the fader. He does not pull it. He looks — for
the first time in the scene, deliberately — at the clock.

The clock reads 3:44.
```

```
CONTINUITY VOICE (V.O.)
(identical. not a re-read. the same take.)
We now conclude our broadcast day. On behalf of everyone at
EyeWash Station, thank you for your eyes tonight.
(beat)
Rinse for fifteen minutes. Do not blink.

OZ
(a beat; then he uncaps the pencil)
Oh-three-four-four.
(he writes it under the line that already says it)
Sign-off.
```

### G — He Goes For Coffee

```
INT. MASTER CONTROL — CONTINUOUS

Oz stands. Takes the thermos. Checks the monitor wall the way you'd
check a sleeping house — not worried, just counting.

Row five, position two: the room. Empty already.
```

```
OZ
(to the room, on his way out)
I'm not programming it.
(beat)
I just make sure it goes out clean.
```

```
He goes. The door swings, does not latch, swings.

The monitors keep airing.

On row three, the laundromat — empty all night — now has two people
in it, standing very close, not dancing yet.

On row five, position two, the room is empty, and then the door
swings, and Oz comes back in and sits down.

The real room stays empty for another eleven seconds.

Then the door swings, and Oz comes back in, and sits down, and
uncaps the pencil.
```

```
CONTINUITY VOICE (V.O.)
(under it, faint, already in progress)
...and now, a program you have already seen.
```

## Production Notes

- D: full dropout under Marguerite's liability speech; returns on "Indoor weather"
- F: **Rinse Cycle** plays twice, identical file, no re-record
- G: unlogged program audio bleeds under — laser, gavel, poodle

1. Monitor wall wide, operator in silhouette
2. The monitor showing this room, offset by seconds
3. Marguerite in the doorway, folder closed
4. Sign-off card + wall clock at 3:44
5. Empty room, all screens still airing

**Continuity Voice — Appears:** V.O. only. Never seen. **Do not cast a face for this.**
**Marguerite — Appears:** Beat D. One step into the room. Never fully enters.
**Time motif:** 3:44 appears twice, in identical inserts of the same clock.
**Key Props:** laminated log sheet · grease pencil on a shoelace · dented thermos (**tea** — Beat G's title "He Goes For Coffee" is the expectation, not the contents) · closed folder · master fader that never gets pulled
**Running gags used:** "It's not haunted, it's unscheduled" · "That was a rerun. So is this." · "Indoor weather" · aspect-ratio liability · the rinse duration · the 3:44 loop
**Unresolved on purpose:** haunted vs. unscheduled. Never adjudicate. Never confirm the Continuity Voice is the Ep2 Radio Voice.
