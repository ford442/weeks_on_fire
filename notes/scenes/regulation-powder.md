# Regulation Powder

*Long-form **action** scene — building cast. Rubella and Lillith fight a snowball fight in the woods at night under formal match rules nobody wrote down. Mostly choreography. The dialog is the paperwork.*

**Slugline:** EXT. WOODS BEHIND THE BUILDING — THE FURNITURE CLEARING — NIGHT
**Runtime target:** 3–4 min (≈2 script pages of dialog, the rest is blocking)
**Music cue:** **None.** The **piano is the score.** Every ball that lands on the abandoned upright plays the note it lands on, and that is the entire arrangement. No bed, no pad, no drums, no Minimax track. If a later draft scores the woods, the later draft is wrong — same rule as [`no-other-human-sounds.md`](no-other-human-sounds.md).
**Status:** draft — Episode 2 candidate insert (night-furniture motif), parked on the Timeline at order 99
**Dialog versions (alts):** [`versions/regulation-powder-versions.md`](versions/regulation-powder-versions.md)

**Cast:** RUBELLA VALE (lead) · LILLITH VALE (lead) — **two-hander. Nobody else. No intercom, no memo, no Continuity Voice, no Radio Voice.**

**Spine:** Lillith declares a match. Rubella accepts the match in order to litigate the match. Seven beats of real, physical, escalating snowball combat around two pieces of furniture that should not be in a forest. One ball is illegal. Nobody apologizes and nobody stops. The score is disputed and then abandoned, which is how both of them wanted it to end.

**Juxtaposition (wrong channel):** A **sanctioned sporting event** — terms, a formal count, a service order, a conceded point, a scoring dispute — committed to absolutely, in a pitch-dark wood, by two women in black lace who never once raise their voices or laugh. The comedy is **procedure applied to snow**. Same family as the ping-pong rally in [`house-of-mirrors-rally.md`](house-of-mirrors-rally.md), except the ball is a consumable and the court has trees in it.

**Action rule (read before blocking a single throw):** *Weeks on Fire* does not do slapstick. **Every throw is competent.** Nobody windmills, nobody shrieks, nobody does a comedy pratfall. They throw the way people throw who have done this before and intend to hit. The funniness lives in the **contrast between the violence and the volume** — hard throws, flat voices.

**Emotional notes:** This is the warmest scene in the building-cast set and nothing warm is said in it. Affection is expressed as accurate aim. The closest either comes to tenderness is Lillith brushing snow off a piano key with two fingers so it can go back to keeping score.

**Hard rules for this scene:**
- **The mascara does not smudge.** Snow melts on jaw, throat, collarbone. It does not melt on the eye makeup. Nobody remarks on this until Beat G, and then only once.
- **No laughing.** Not a giggle, not a breath of one. Conversational volume throughout, even mid-throw, even buried.
- **No snow angels. No kiss. No hug. No warming of hands.** If a draft adds a gesture of comfort, cut the gesture and keep the throw.
- **Falls are real and unremarked.** Rubella goes down twice. Neither fall is scored, neither fall is helped up, neither fall is mentioned again.
- **Lillith is in heels and never slips.** Not once, not on the deadfall, not on the ice. Do not "pay this off." It is simply true.
- **The piano is never played by a hand.** Only by snow. The one exception is Beat G — two fingers clearing a key, no note.
- **One ball has ice in it.** It lands. There is a thin red line. Nobody apologizes, nobody says "are you okay," and the match does not stop — it gets *slower*.
- **Nobody wins.** The count is contested and then dropped. Do not let a later draft award the point.
- **No animals.** No owl, no deer, no crunching wildlife. The wood is empty and the only sounds are boots, cloth, impact, and the piano.
- **Do not explain the furniture.** The piano and the bathing cabinet are in the woods. That is the whole finding. See `episodes/episode-02/synopsis.md` ("random pieces of furniture found during nighttime adventures").

## The Clearing (standing set — memorize before blocking)

Roughly forty feet across, in second-growth mixed conifer. Eight inches of fresh dry snow over two inches of old crust. No wind. No moon worth the name — the light is the orange underlight the city puts on low cloud, coming down through the canopy in a flat, shadowless grey that makes distance unreadable.

Three things in it that should not be:

1. **THE UPRIGHT PIANO.** North edge of the clearing, facing in. Case swollen, veneer lifting, lid off entirely and leaning against the trunk behind it. Strings and hammers open to the sky. A three-inch cap of untouched snow across the whole exposed action. It is in tune with nothing, including itself. **It is the scoreboard.**
2. **THE BATHING CABINET.** South-west, twenty feet from the piano. A hip-high enameled hip-bath in a hinged wooden surround, door swinging. Full of snow. Excellent cover for exactly one person who does not mind being at hip height.
3. **THE DEADFALL.** East, a chest-high tangle of three downed pines with the root plate still standing. Flanking route. Loud to cross. Impossible to cross quietly, which is the point of Beat D.

**Ammo continuity (track it — this is the actual plot):**

| Source | Ammo type | Available from | Exhausted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open clearing floor | Dry powder, poor binding, ~40% flight failure | Beat A | never |
| Piano lid cap (the leaning lid) | Dense, wet, holds shape, hits like a thrown apple | Beat B | Beat F |
| Bathing cabinet interior | **Pre-built stockpile — nine balls, stacked, rolled tight** | Beat C | Beat E |
| Old crust under the powder | Slab ice. Not ammunition. | — | **Beat E, once** |

### A — The Terms

```
EXT. WOODS BEHIND THE BUILDING — THE FURNITURE CLEARING — NIGHT

Black lace against eight inches of white. Two trails of footprints
come in from the same direction and then separate, which means they
separated recently and on purpose.

RUBELLA stands at the clearing's centre in a coat she has not
buttoned, in the building's lost-and-found galoshes, one size too
large, over bare feet.

LILLITH is already at the piano. Heels. No coat. She is packing a
snowball with the flat, bored competence of someone icing a cake.

She packs it. She turns it. She checks it against the light that
isn't there. She sets it on the piano's exposed bass strings, where
it depresses nothing and plays nothing.

She starts a second one.
```

```
LILLITH
Match.

RUBELLA
No.

LILLITH
Match to eleven. Win by two.

RUBELLA
I said no and you started building.

LILLITH
I started building at the treeline. The no is late.
```

```
Rubella looks at the piano. At the ball sitting on the strings. At
the second one taking shape in Lillith's hands. Then she bends,
scoops, and packs — badly, fast, the powder crumbling out the
bottom of her fists — and this is the acceptance.
```

```
RUBELLA
Terms.

LILLITH
Body and below.

RUBELLA
And the head.

LILLITH
Not the head.

RUBELLA
Then not the face either. Say it properly.

LILLITH
Not the head, not the face, not the hair.

RUBELLA
And nothing out of the cabinet.

(Beat. Lillith does not answer this. She sets the second
ball beside the first.)

RUBELLA (CONT'D)
Lillith.

LILLITH
I heard the clause.

RUBELLA
You didn't take it.

LILLITH
I heard it.
```

```
Lillith picks up ball one. Holds it out at arm's length, shoulder
height — the formal offer of service, the way you show an opponent
the ball before a serve.

Rubella, forty feet away, nods once.

Lillith drops her arm and throws in the same motion, no wind-up,
no warning, flat and hard and already too late to duck.
```

### B — The Opening Exchange (the piano starts keeping score)

```
EXT. CLEARING — CONTINUOUS

Ball one takes Rubella high on the left shoulder and comes apart in
a flat white star across her coat.

She does not flinch, stagger, or react above the waist. She brushes
the shoulder once, downward, with two fingers.
```

```
RUBELLA
One.

LILLITH
One.
```

```
Rubella throws. It is a bad ball — powder, no binding — and it
disintegrates in the air at twenty feet and arrives as weather.

Lillith does not move. She does not need to.
```

```
LILLITH
Nothing.

RUBELLA
Nothing.
```

```
Now the rally. Six exchanges, fast, and the blocking is the joke:

1. Rubella packs from the crust line this time — slower, tighter —
   and throws. Lillith steps ONE PACE left. Miss. It hits the piano
   case with a wooden thud. No note.

2. Lillith throws. Rubella takes it in the ribs and says nothing.
   The count does not need saying twice.

3. Rubella throws high and wide, deliberately, to the north — and
   it lands in the open action of the piano. A cluster of low
   strings sounds. Wrong, wet, and loud in a wood with nothing
   else in it.

   Both of them stop and look at the piano.

4. Lillith throws while Rubella is still looking at the piano.
   It takes her in the hip.

5. Rubella throws immediately, at the piano again, on purpose.
   Another cluster. Higher. It rings for four seconds.

6. Lillith, already moving, breaks for the bathing cabinet.
```

```
RUBELLA
Three.

LILLITH
(mid-run, not looking back)
Two. The piano isn't a person.

RUBELLA
The piano is the scoreboard.

LILLITH
The piano is furniture.

RUBELLA
Then stop hiding behind furniture.
```

### C — The Stockpile (premeditation, discovered)

```
EXT. CLEARING — THE BATHING CABINET — CONTINUOUS

Lillith slides in behind the cabinet, drops to a crouch, and
reaches into the hip-bath without looking.

She comes up with a finished snowball. Then another. Then she tips
the cabinet door open six inches so we see it:

NINE SNOWBALLS. Stacked in a pyramid on the enamel. Rolled, packed,
and rounded. Dense. Half-glazed on the outside where they were
built early enough to have refrozen.

They have been there for at least an hour.
```

```
Rubella sees it from forty feet. She stands fully upright in the
open — completely exposed, entirely uninterested in cover — and
looks at the pyramid for a long moment.
```

```
RUBELLA
You built those before you asked me.

LILLITH
I built those before I asked you.

RUBELLA
That's the cabinet clause.

LILLITH
The cabinet clause was proposed and not accepted.

RUBELLA
It was proposed and not *rejected.*

LILLITH
Those are different sentences and only one of them is mine.
```

```
Rubella nods slowly, the way she nods at an HOA agenda.

Then she turns her back on Lillith entirely — a forty-foot open
shot, offered — walks to the piano, and begins harvesting the
LEANING LID: the three-inch cap of dense wet snow along its top
edge, which packs like clay and weighs like fruit.

She builds four. She takes her time. She sets them in a row along
the piano's top rail.

Lillith does not throw at her back. Not once. This is never
mentioned.
```

```
LILLITH
Are we resuming.

RUBELLA
I'm sourcing.

LILLITH
It's a snowball fight.

RUBELLA
It's a match to eleven, win by two, body and below, and you have a
warehouse.
```

### D — The Flank (the quiet minute)

```
EXT. CLEARING — THE DEADFALL — CONTINUOUS

Rubella takes two of the piano-lid balls and goes east into the
deadfall, which is chest-high tangled pine and is impossible to
cross quietly.

She does not try to cross it quietly. She crosses it at conversational
pace, snapping branches the whole way, in oversized galoshes, holding
two snowballs like eggs.

Lillith tracks the noise from behind the cabinet without raising her
head. She loads her right hand and waits.

The noise stops.

Nine seconds. Neither of them is visible in the same frame.

Then Lillith, still not looking, throws a ball over the top of the
cabinet, blind, at the exact place the noise stopped.

Thud. Wool. A hit.
```

```
RUBELLA (O.S.)
(flatly, from the dark)
Four.

LILLITH
Four.

RUBELLA (O.S.)
That was blind.

LILLITH
That was arithmetic.
```

```
Rubella comes out of the deadfall at a different angle than she went
in — thirty degrees north, having used the nine seconds — and throws
BOTH piano-lid balls in a two-count while walking forward.

The first one Lillith avoids by moving her head four inches.

The second one hits the cabinet door dead centre, and the door
SLAMS SHUT on its hinge, sealing the pyramid inside.

Lillith looks at the closed door. Her hand is empty.
```

```
RUBELLA
Five.

LILLITH
The door isn't a person either.

RUBELLA
No. But it's your warehouse.
```

### E — The Ice (the illegal ball)

```
EXT. CLEARING — CONTINUOUS

Lillith stands up from behind the cabinet. Fully. In the open.

She does not go for the door. She reaches DOWN, past the powder, and
lifts a plate of the old crust — a slab of grey refrozen ice the size
of a dinner plate — and breaks it across her knee into a piece that
fits her hand.

She packs powder around it. Four seconds. Working it smooth until it
looks exactly like every other ball in the scene.

Rubella watches her do all of this from twenty feet away and does not
move, does not speak, and does not take cover.
```

```
RUBELLA
Don't.

LILLITH
Body and below.
```

```
She throws it.

It takes Rubella just under the collarbone — body, and below — and
it does not come apart. It lands, and falls, intact, into the snow
at her feet, and stays there, grey and obvious.

Rubella takes one step back to absorb it. One.

She puts two fingers to her collarbone. When they come away there is
a thin red line on them, and a thin red line on her, four inches
long, already beading.

Nobody says are you okay. Nobody says I'm sorry. Nobody says
anything for six seconds.
```

```
RUBELLA
Six.

LILLITH
(quietly)
Six.
```

```
Rubella bends, picks up the grey ball, and carries it — walking, not
throwing — the twenty feet to the piano, where she sets it down on
the open bass strings.

It is heavy enough to sound them. One low, flat, wrong note that
holds for a long time in a wood with nothing in it.

Then she walks back to her mark.
```

```
RUBELLA
Match continues.

LILLITH
Match continues.

RUBELLA
Body and below.

LILLITH
Body and below.
```

```
And the fight resumes — and this is the direction note: it does not
speed up. It gets SLOWER, and harder, and more accurate. Four
exchanges, each one taken standing, neither of them using cover
again for the rest of the scene.

Seven. Eight. Eight. Nine.
```

### F — The Bough (nine-all, and the wood ends it)

```
EXT. CLEARING — CONTINUOUS

Nine all. Both of them out of built ammunition. Both of them
standing in the open at thirty feet, packing from the floor, which
does not bind, which means the last six throws have all been
weather.

Rubella takes the last dense scrape off the piano lid — a bad,
lopsided, final ball — and throws it hard and high and badly.

It misses Lillith by nine feet.

It hits the loaded pine bough above and behind her.

There is a pause of about a second and a half.

Then roughly a hundred pounds of accumulated snow comes off that
bough in one piece and lands directly on Lillith, burying her from
above to mid-thigh, in total silence except for the impact.

Snow settles. The clearing is very quiet.

A hand comes up out of the pile. Then a shoulder. Then Lillith,
standing, in heels, entirely white, having not moved her feet.
```

```
LILLITH
Ten.

RUBELLA
That was the tree.

LILLITH
The tree is furniture.

RUBELLA
The tree is a *tree.*

LILLITH
Then stop hiding behind trees.

RUBELLA
I'm standing in the middle of a field.

LILLITH
Ten.
```

```
Lillith brushes exactly one forearm clean. She leaves the rest.
```

### G — The Count (disputed, then dropped)

```
EXT. CLEARING — CONTINUOUS

They stand at thirty feet in the flat grey light. Nobody is holding
a snowball. Both of them are breathing hard, and neither of them is
going to be the first to stop.
```

```
RUBELLA
Nine.

LILLITH
Ten.

RUBELLA
The tree doesn't count.

LILLITH
It landed.

RUBELLA
It landed because of a *tree.*

LILLITH
It landed because you threw it.

RUBELLA
I threw it nine feet wide.

LILLITH
Wide of me. Not wide of the tree.

RUBELLA
And the ice.

LILLITH
The ice landed.

RUBELLA

LILLITH
```

```
Silence. Four seconds. This is as close as the scene gets to an
apology and it is not one.

Lillith walks to the piano. She does not pick up the grey ball,
which is still sitting on the bass strings. She reaches past it,
and with two fingers brushes the snow off one white key —
carefully, the way you would clear a plaque — and does not press
it.

Then she turns and looks at Rubella properly for the first time
since the treeline.
```

```
LILLITH
Your face is perfect.

RUBELLA
I know.

LILLITH
You were buried at the collarbone and hit twice in the ribs.

RUBELLA
I know.

LILLITH
That's not a product. That's a policy.
```

```
Rubella looks at the piano. At the grey ball on the strings. At
nine white balls sealed in a bathing cabinet in a forest at night.
```

```
RUBELLA
Call it nine-all.

LILLITH
It's ten-nine.

RUBELLA
Call it nine-all and I don't file about the cabinet.

(Lillith considers this the way she considers everything —
for exactly as long as it takes to lose nothing.)

LILLITH
Nine-all.

RUBELLA
Nine-all.

LILLITH
Win by two.

RUBELLA
Obviously.
```

```
set of trails going back instead of two.

Neither of them looks back at the piano.

HOLD on the empty clearing: forty feet of churned snow, a piano
with its action open to the sky, one grey slab-ice ball resting on
the bass strings, and a bathing cabinet with its door shut on nine
finished snowballs that nobody is coming back for.

HOLD long enough to be uncomfortable.

Then, off the piano — not touched, not thrown at, with nobody in
frame — the grey ball, melting, settles a quarter inch and sounds
ONE low note.
```

```

```

## Production Notes

**Runtime target:** 3–4 min. The dialog is roughly two pages; the rest is blocking. **If a version of this scene runs long, the throws are too slow, not the lines too many.**

**Placement:** Episode 2 candidate insert — the "random furniture found during nighttime adventures" motif (bathing cabinet, piano) is Episode 2's, and this is what that motif looks like when the furniture gets used. Parked on the Timeline at order 99 so it does not lead the Ep2 cut. It is **not** an Episode 1 scene: no chandelier, no paddles, no ping-pong metronome, no champagne, and these are **Rubella and Lillith**, not Vivienne and Liliane.

**Beat / action map:**

| Beat | Action | Score | Ammo state |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Terms negotiated; cabinet clause proposed, not answered; formal service offer | 0–0 | Lillith: 2 built + hidden 9 · Rubella: none |
| B | Opening rally, six exchanges; the piano starts scoring | 3–2 | Both working the floor; lid discovered |
| C | Stockpile revealed; Rubella turns her back and harvests the lid | 3–2 | Rubella: 4 dense · Lillith: 9 sealed-ish |
| D | The loud flank; blind hit; cabinet door slammed shut | 5–4 | Lillith locked out of her own pyramid |
| E | **The ice.** Lands, doesn't break, draws a line. Match slows. | 9–8 | Everyone on floor powder from here |
| F | Nine-all; bad throw hits the bough; hundred-pound burial | 10–9 disputed | Nothing built anywhere |
| G | Count disputed, traded for the cabinet complaint, dropped at nine-all | **nine-all** | Nine balls abandoned in a box in a forest |

**Written silences (non-negotiable):** 11s of Rubella's exposed back in Beat C · 9s with neither of them in frame in Beat D · 6s after the ice lands in Beat E · 1.5s before the bough lets go in Beat F · the long empty-clearing hold in Beat G.

**Sound design (this scene lives or dies here):**
- Wool impact: dull, close, no whip.
- Piano case: wood, flat.
- Open strings: the only music. Wet, detuned, too loud for the location.
- Cabinet door slam: the single loudest event before the bough.
- Bough drop: one dry WHUMP, no reverb, no whoosh, no comedy sting.
- Breath: present the whole scene, never sweetened.
- **No wind, no animals, no distant traffic.** The wood is as empty as the one in *No Other Human Sounds*, and for the same reason.

**Cold / continuity discipline:** Breath is visible from the first frame and never stops. Lace gets wet and stays wet. Hair gets wet and stays wet. **The eye makeup does not.** Snow on shoulders accumulates across the scene and is never fully brushed off after Beat B — by Beat G, Rubella is half-white and Lillith is entirely white except one forearm.

**Safety / practical notes:** All thrown snow is practical soft powder; the "ice" ball is a sugar-glass or gel prop and is never thrown at a performer (throw, impact and reaction are three setups). The bough drop is a rigged overhead release of loose snow, shot in one, with the performer's mark surveyed beforehand. The makeup line on the collarbone is applied between setups, not gagged live.

**Grok Imagine priority shots:**
1. Wide establish — two women in black lace, forty feet apart, piano and bath cabinet in a snowy night wood *(hero)*
2. The formal service offer — ball held out at arm's length, shoulder height
3. Snow exploding off the open piano action
4. The cabinet door open six inches on a pyramid of nine finished snowballs
5. The ice ball lying intact and grey in the snow, and the thin red line *(hero)*
6. The bough letting go — hundred-pound slab mid-fall onto a woman in heels
7. **Empty clearing, churned snow, one grey ball on the bass strings, door shut** *(hero — final frame)*

**Running gags used:** "the piano is the scoreboard" / "the piano is furniture" · "then stop hiding behind furniture" (twice, escalating to a tree) · clauses proposed and not accepted · the count said flatly by both, in turn · mascara as policy, not product · Lillith in heels on terrain.

**Open questions for the room:**
- Does the cabinet stockpile stay at nine, or does the reveal land harder at a number too large to be a joke (twenty-plus, filling the bath)? Recommendation: nine. Nine is premeditated. Twenty is a different character.
- Is the ice ball Lillith's only illegal act, or does Rubella have one we don't see? Recommendation: only Lillith's. Rubella's counter is the cabinet door, and the door is legal.
- Does the final low note stay diegetic, or is it the seed of a Minimax cue for a later cutaway? Recommendation: diegetic here; if a track wants it, the track can quote it.
- Does this scene ever get spoken of again? Recommendation: never, except that the cabinet stays shut in every subsequent night-woods frame.
