STYLE:

Sincere 1930s Paris chanson réaliste, a torch ballad at 66 BPM in A minor, about two and a half minutes, sung entirely in French with clear Parisian diction. A musette accordion carries the melody with slow, sobbing bellows and tremolo on held notes, a cabaret upright piano plays heavy downbeats, an upright bass is bowed on long notes, and a small string section enters only on the second chorus. No drums. One female voice: dry, slightly raspy, low, rolling her r's, delivering a tragic love song with complete seriousness, building from almost spoken in the verses to a full, aching belt on each chorus, then dropping back to nothing. Spoken bridge, close-mic, no music under it. The last chorus slows down. It ends on an unresolved accordion chord held until the bellows run out. Smoky small-club room sound, mono-leaning, vinyl warmth. No parody, no comedy sound effects. Use the Rubella voice, with one spoken line from Lillith.

NOTES:

**English gloss** (for the room, not for singing):
- *V1:* At the back of my cupboard, behind the broom, I have one bottle left, the last one
  that still smelled of Sunday laundry and sun on the sheets, everything the machine will
  never give back to me.
- *Chorus:* O my detergent, I look at you and I don't dare. One single dose, the last one,
  and after that there's only me.
- *V2:* The washer opens its door the way you open your arms. The dryer spins empty,
  murmuring. My socks are queued in procession on the tiles. They've been waiting since
  March. They've aged.
- *V3:* The neighbours have come down, a basket under every arm. The elevator has stopped in
  the basement and won't leave. The whole building holds its breath, a hundred baskets of
  dirty laundry, and I hold the bottle like the Grail.
- *Bridge:* "What's the occasion?" "I'll know."
- *Final:* I put you back behind the broom. One dose, the last one. For the end of the
  world. Or never.
- *Outro:* "Go back upstairs."

**Callback:** Rubella's contraband pre-dystopian detergent. The bank's lock says it stands
in the basket like a trophy and is **never used**. This whole song is that lock, sung as a
tragedy. She never pours it.

**The elevator** stops in the basement and waits. It doesn't speak or sing (it says floor
numbers only, and here it says nothing).

**Do-not:** no brand name or readable label on the bottle. No laundromat (*The Laundromat
Saints* owns it); this is the building's own laundry room. No Glam bottle.
