### What the song is
A dragon at a volcano says please, and then thank you, in the biggest voice in the world. He is at dinner and the lava is the dinner. He asks the lava for some more, thanks it, and the third thing a child is taught to say after that is the one thing he does not say: his third rear-back produces a soap bubble the size of a car, the band stops dead, and six seconds of weather go by while it floats away and pops somewhere out of sight. Please. Thank you. (Burp.) The song never says "excuse me", and neither does he.

The lyric gives the picture its burp. On its own, the third rear-back could be read as blowing a bubble; after "some more lava, please" and "thank you for the lava" it is a belch at the table, and the silence is the table's silence. The song never says burp, bubble, dragon, smoke, ring, storm or silence. Working title of the video: **Bubble Dragon**. The song is retitled *Thank You for the Lava* because the title of a deadpan song should not say what the picture is about to do, and because it sounds like a perfectly serious metal title.

### Minimax generation (`docs/song-method.md`, item 3)
- **Genre:** melodic death-metal gallop with a power-metal-style twin-lead hook. The dragon is a headliner on a cliff, so the band should sound like it is introducing someone, and the gallop is the hoofbeat for a slow, huge animal that walks out of the dark. The growl is native to the genre, so the joke is not a costume: the voice is exactly what a dragon's voice would be. A doom stomp was considered and cut (too few bars in 24 seconds to fit two roars, a long third inhale and a return). Thrash was cut (no room for the heroic hook that makes the final riff the funniest music in the piece).
- **Locked:** 160 BPM, C minor, 4/4, 16 bars, 0:24. Instruments: seven-string rhythm guitars in drop C, twin-lead hook in thirds, picked bass, double-kick gallop, snare, toms, one 8-inch splash. One growl. Original melody, original lyric, official section tags ([Intro], [Verse], [Build Up], [Break], [Outro]), no parentheses, no inflection marks.
- **The structural flaw (held, never fixed): the cymbal is the wrong size.** The kit has no crash, ride or china. Its only cymbal is an 8-inch splash, mixed as if it were a full crash, so every big hit in the track (0.0, 4.5, 7.5, 18.0, 22.5) is crowned by a small, bright, instantly dying tsh. It is never swapped for a real crash, never made bigger, never made to ring. The thunder supplies the only real crash in the piece, at 0.0 and 22.5, on the same frame as the splash. The last chord ends on a splash. It is a mix/kit flaw, not a timing flaw, a pitch flaw or a count flaw: it differs in kind from the miscount in *Box, Box, Hat* and the dead tine in *Just a Sunday*, and from the drawer's late stamp (see below). It is also not the stop: the stop at 12.0 is clean and the splash is not on it.
- **Why not the other flaws I tried:** a late chug (the owner's example) is a near-duplicate of *Stamp the Riff* (a stamp one sixteenth late, `songs/_drawer/joke-metal/`). A whole-step key drop on the return (the band "deflated") is funnier to musicians but is a pitch flaw like the dead tine. A riff one beat short is a count flaw like the miscount.
- **Expect to fix in the edit.** Minimax will not reliably leave six seconds of true silence, will not keep the two lines to the two bars, will not hold a splash instead of a crash, and will put vocals into the empty sections. Make several takes. Keep the one where the growl's words are readable and the first three bars are cleanest. Build the stop by hard-cutting the audio at 12.0 (a 5ms fade at most) and delete everything until 18.0; paste bars 1-2 (0.0-3.0) again at 18.0 plus bar 1 again at 21.0 for the last riff, so it is the same take, and put the final chord from the take at 22.5, choked at 23.0. Mute any vocal that is not one of the two lines, and any growl over a shut mouth. Replace any crash, ride or china with a splash sample on the five big hits; the splash is about 6 dB over the hat level and dies in under 300ms. If the growl is unreadable, reroll the vocal; do not add on-screen text. Trim anything past 24.0. The edit decides the seconds; the prompt only asks for them.
