# Riley Smith

*Recurring candidate. First appearance: Episode 4 — The Long Way Up. Full card also lives in `suggested-characters.md`; this file is the inventory.*

**Role:** The stranger on the center line. First character in the series who **knows things**. The straight woman for a show that has never had one.

**Full name:** Riley Elaine Smith. She offers **Riley**. The last name comes out only if asked, and it is exactly as adequate as it sounds.

---

## Look

Sun-bleached blonde. Trail-worn, not styled. Light green halter top. Amethyst pendant on a short chain, bail taped with a scrap of electrical tape so it does not swing when she walks. Forty-liter pack, belted. Boots that have been resoled. Wildly, correctly dressed for a mountain in any scene where everyone else is in cocktail lace.

She does not pose. No wind-in-hair, no horizon gaze. She is always doing something specific — walking, reading, wrenching, writing.

---

## Backstory (inventory, not trauma)

This is a life. It is not a wound. Do not film it as one.

**Origin.** Born March 14, 1994, Porterville, Tulare County. Thirty-two in the year we meet her. Grew up in a stucco house with a gravel side yard and a wind chime her mother refuses to take down even though it has been one note short since 2007. She can still tell you which note.

**Family — all living, all in contact, all fine.** That last part is load-bearing. Thanksgiving happened. Nobody is estranged.

- **Denise Smith**, mother. Still teaching 7th-grade earth science at Pioneer Middle. Texts Riley screenshots of the weather app as if Riley cannot see the sky.
- **Cal Smith**, father. Retired from the irrigation-parts counter on Olive Avenue. Keeps the 4Runner's service records in a Danish cookie tin. Offers to "come up there" whenever Riley is on a ridge. She always says no. He always accepts this.
- **Darren Smith**, older brother, thirty-five. Drives the water hauler for the pistachio operation on the north side of the county — the same truck Riley cites, unprompted, as one of three vehicles a day on the ridge road. Married. One kid, June, four. Riley's car is in his driveway in Tulare. They text about road closures and parts. They do not text about feelings. This is affection.

**School.** Fresno State, B.S. Geography, GIS certificate. Internship inventorying NWS cooperative-observer sites — the ones on ranch fences and elementary-school roofs that still report because somebody's uncle never stopped. She liked the uncle more than the data.

**The job.** Nine years of site surveys for a Central Valley contractor (West Slope Site Services — gone) and then a regional telecom that bought surplus weather and microwave hardware. The work: drive to a piece of hardware nobody has thought about since the Clinton administration and write down whether it is still there. **It is almost always still there. That is the finding.** Nine years of findings and the finding is: nobody takes anything down. Taking it down costs money. Leaving it up costs nothing. So the country is just accumulating.

**Why it stopped.** The contract went out to rebid in late 2024 and a national firm won it. They offered her a dispatch desk in Bakersfield. A desk is the opposite of the job. She declined. There was no scene. She still has the last two field books and a laminated site list she is not supposed to have.

**Now.** Month-to-month converted garage in Three Rivers. Mail goes to her parents. Between contracts. She is using the August ducting window to log the Kestrel Ridge repeater on her own time because she has been meaning to since 2019 and the window is this week, not next week.

**The car in Tulare.** 1998 Toyota 4Runner, white, 287,000 miles. Timing chain — not the head gasket, before anyone asks. Sitting in Darren's driveway. The part is in a warehouse in Fresno until Wednesday. She walked because walking is cheaper than a rental, she already had the pack, and Wednesday is after the window.

**The pendant.** Bought March 2018 at a rock shop on 395 in Bishop, next to a diner that had already closed, after a survey of an Owens Valley microwave hop. Fourteen dollars. It is amethyst. That is a mineral, not a personality. People keep asking if it *does* something. It hangs. That is what it does.

**The songs.** She records ambient at dead sites and sometimes sings into them, quietly, without announcing it. Titles are a date and a wind direction because that is how you find a recording again. "Sad" is not a filing system. She does not call herself a musician. The void has been a good audience: low expectations, excellent acoustics.

**The notebook.** Field books, same brand as the job. Left page: the site. Right page: the weather, and a scrap of a line if the weather suggests one. She writes when they stop, not while they walk.

## Personality

Calm, specific, unbothered. Corrects numbers the way other people say "bless you" — reflexively, without heat, and then moves on. Never smug. Never wrong about a fact. Treats a felony and a citation as a genuinely interesting distinction.

Slightly more talkative in true silence, because silence is data and data wants labeling. Less talkative when someone is prosecuting. This is why the walk with Rubella is different from the ride with Lillith: Rubella asked about the tower. Lillith asked about her.

She likes rooms with the door shut. A closed canopy and no wind is a room.

---

## Voice / Dialogue Style

- Answers the question actually asked, then supplies the number nobody asked for.
- Real jargon, delivered flat: ordinance subsections, effective radiated height, tropospheric ducting, hose clamps, last grade date.
- Concedes instantly when she is the one who is off. It never comes up, because she isn't.
- Will not volunteer biography. If a fact leaks, it is because someone asked the adjacent question (the truck, not the brother; the car, not the life).
- Does not use a mystical register. If a line could be said by a woman who "just knows things about the sky," cut it. She knows things about *this* sky, *this* road, *this* week.

**Sample lines:**
- "You didn't stop me. I'm walking. You decelerated. Those are two different events with two different paperwork trails."
- "You asked about the truck."
- "It's a citation. Paren b."
- "Nobody takes anything down. Taking it down costs money. Leaving it up costs nothing."
- "It's amethyst. That's a mineral, not a personality."
- "Sad is not a filing system."
- "You don't have to like it. It's accurate."
- "The void's been a good audience so far. Low expectations, excellent acoustics."

## Casting rules — do not violate

- **No mystical register.** She is not a seer, a wanderer, or a woman who says true things about the sky. She is an unemployed technician with good data.
- **No tragic backstory on camera. No crying scene.** The backstory above is inventory. Thanksgiving happened. The comedy is that she is correct in a show where nothing is, and that her last name is Smith.
- **Keep her above five thousand feet.** She does not belong in the HOA material, the studio huddle, or any room with a gavel in it.
- **Never wrong about a fact and never smug about it.**
- **She does not start the song.** Rubella or the bed starts it. Riley may sing into a dead site on her own, quietly; she never cues a musical cutaway.
- **No retrieval plot.** Nobody sends her to fetch anyone, and she does not go looking for anyone. She is walking to a repeater. That is the plot.
- **Do not explain "Around."**
- **Do not adjudicate the antenna.** Dead weather repeater or EyeWash Station transmitter — she has a theory, nobody rules on it.

## At Christina's door (she is not there)

Christina hill ([`bible/christina.md`](bible/christina.md)) is not her altitude and not her kind of room. **Riley does not visit the hill this week.** If she did, she would fail the door the most honest way available: she would ask what the tower in the atrium glass is, what it transmits, and when it was last graded — and the house has no answer it is willing to give. She would also be the only guest who noticed that the three sleeping cars have been parked long enough to need a battery tender, and she would say so. Both are correct. Both break the house.

Keep her on the ridge. Throw any draft that brings her up the snow hill to retrieve the ladies.
