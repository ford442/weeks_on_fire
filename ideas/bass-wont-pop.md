## Tone

**Silly and simple, and kids will get it.** Big thing can't beat small thing, then small
thing beats everything. The visuals are serious materials physics: soap film, nesting,
minimal surfaces, frost on a bubble. Treat it as a gorgeous science film that happens to be
a joke.

## Physics it uses (real, and good on camera)

- **Bubbles bounce on moving air** without popping: a speaker cone's pulse makes a
  bubble hop like a trampoline.
- **Nested bubbles:** a bubble inside a bubble inside a bubble.
- **Cube bubble:** a soap film on a wire cube makes a square bubble with a tiny cube floating
  in the centre (minimal surfaces).
- **Freezing bubbles:** in cold air, frost ferns crystallise across the film until the bubble
  is a fragile glass ornament.
- **Chain pop:** one pop's shockwave pops touching neighbours.

## Beat sheet

1. **One bubble (0:00–0:10).** A single soap bubble drifts into a shaft of light in front of a
   wall of 12 black speaker cabinets. The kick hits. The woofer cones pump, and the bubble
   bounces on the air in time, fat and iridescent, and doesn't pop.
2. **Multiply and nest (0:10–0:20).** Dozens of bubbles pour into the light. The bass pushes them
   into a slow orbit. Three nest inside each other. Every kick visibly pushes the whole cloud
   back an inch, and none pop.
3. **The square one (0:20–0:30).** A wire cube dips out of frame and lifts out a **square
   bubble** with a tiny cube floating in its centre. It floats into the cloud and bobs on the
   beat like everything else, square.
4. **Freeze (0:30–0:40).** Breakdown. Cold breath rolls across the floor, and frost ferns spread
   over every bubble on the beat until the room is full of floating glass ornaments. They
   clink against each other on the hi-hats.
5. **The giant (0:40–0:46).** One huge bubble drifts down and swallows the whole speaker stack.
   The speakers keep pumping inside it, and its skin wobbles on every kick, but it holds.
6. **The drop is silence (0:46–0:52).** The music cuts dead. In the quiet, one tiny bubble, the
   smallest in the room, drifts down and touches the concrete floor. *Pip.* The shockwave
   runs outward: every soap bubble pops in a cascade from smallest to largest, the frozen ones
   drop and shatter into glitter, and the giant goes last, popping off the speakers. The beat
   slams back in on an empty room full of falling sparkle.

## Do-not

No hands, wands or bubble machines on screen. Bubbles just arrive. No logos on the speakers
and no brand-shaped grilles. No soap bottle (Glam lane). No slow-motion on the chain pop:
real speed, it's fast. Don't let the frozen bubbles turn into Christmas ornaments with caps.

## Why it's funny

All that power, bass you can see, and it can't beat a soap bubble. Then the smallest,
quietest thing in the room ends the party.

**Difficulty: 4 / 5.** Thin-film iridescence, a square bubble, frost growth and a chain-pop
cascade are four hard material events. Each is one per clip, per the soft-gyre rule, which
helps.

**Riskiest shot: #6, the chain pop.** Models tend to explode everything at once or pop
nothing. Hardener: *pops travel outward in a ring from the floor point; small bubbles first,
largest last; frozen ones fall before shattering.* Fallback: build it in post. Generate a 6s
of the tiny pop plus the ring, and a separate 6s of glitter falling in the empty hall, then
cross-cut with three single-frame flashes.
