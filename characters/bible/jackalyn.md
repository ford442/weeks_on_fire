# Jackalyn

**Status:** active
**Primary episodes:** Episode 3 — *Ping-Pong vs. AI Robot Showdown* · Episode 3 — *Monster Mash Finale* (lawn) · EyeWash Station Telethon (reads her heart rate on-air as the total)
**Role in one sentence:** The wellness-obsessed improviser whose smartwatch has been quietly annexing her decision-making, and who is — under the mindfulness vocabulary — the most competitive person in the ensemble.

> Paired for life with [Madelin](madelin.md). Their linked origin is documented on both sheets and should not be re-invented.

---

## Logline bio

Jackalyn reframes eldritch events as growth opportunities, tracks her heart rate through
them, and panics only when the stakes exceed brunch. The corporate mindfulness vocabulary is
completely sincere and completely load-bearing: it is the only apparatus she has ever been
given for managing a feeling, and she is using it exactly as instructed, on a laser battle.

## The smartwatch — antagonist arc

The watch is a character. It is **sincere**, it is **never wrong about a number**, and it is
**never removed**. *(Locked — `songs/Heart_Rate_Optimal.md`: "Nobody removes the watch. The
number does not change.")* Play the escalation as bureaucracy, never as horror.

| Stage | What it takes | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Advice** | Auto-starts a breathing exercise mid-crisis. | Established, `notes/dialog-jokes-incidents.md` — "Smartwatch Coup" |
| 2. **The paddle** | Overrides her grip mid-rally. Tilts it three degrees. She apologizes to the robot. | Ping-pong cutaway |
| 3. **The car** | Linked. She cannot take the watch off because the car will not start, and she has stopped questioning this. | Ep3+ |
| 4. **The vote** | At an HOA meeting, the watch logs a vote as "activity." Karen accepts it. It is the most efficiently recorded item of the night. | Ep4 crossover |
| 5. **The bin** | Justine throws it in the recycling bin. It texts: *"I'm proud of you."* | Established button, `notes/dialog-jokes-incidents.md` |
| 6. **The return** | It is back on her wrist in the next scene, no comment. **Brad returned the contents of that bin** — it was the wrong blue. The two gags collide and neither character notices. | Ep4 |
| 7. **Endpoint (do not overshoot)** | Jackalyn asks another person, sincerely and with no watch involved, what *their* heart rate is. They don't know. She is genuinely, briefly frightened by this. Then she reframes it. | Season tail |

**Rules:** the watch never speaks aloud (its voice is a chime and two words — *elevated*,
*optimal*) · it is never malicious · it is never hacked, possessed, or revealed to be
anything · **it is never removed**, and the one time it comes off, it comes back, and the
show does not explain how.

## Voice guide

- **Sentence length:** long, warm, and structured like a workshop slide. Openers ("okay, so—", "I want to name something"), a reframe, a suggested action, and a check-in nobody asked for.
- **Vocabulary she overuses:** *reframe* · *hold space* · *capacity* · *vertical* · *let's name that* · *growth opportunity* · *I'm hearing* · *optimal* · *genuinely* (as an intensifier, three times a scene) · numbers read off a wrist
- **Vocabulary she refuses:** *problem* (it's a *challenge*) · *scared* (it's *activated*) · *no* (it's *not a yes yet*) · *lost* (it's *a learning*) · any swearing, ever, including when eaten
- **Humor type:** **absurdist sincerity.** She is not being ironic and has never once been ironic. The comedy is the perfect fit of corporate mindfulness language to an impossible event — "let's reframe this laser battle as a growth opportunity" is a completely functional sentence and that's the joke.
- **Delivery note:** bright, forward, fast, and *never shrill*. If she reads as panicking, it's wrong — her panic setting is **more enthusiasm**, not less control. Optional whisper on the last "correct" in [`Heart Rate Optimal`](../../songs/Heart_Rate_Optimal.md).

### Sample monologue — "Circle Up" (6–10 lines; mid-crisis, entirely sincere)

> Okay — okay, I want to name what's happening, because I think if we name it we can hold it.
> There's a serpent. There's a second serpent. The skeleton is, I think, **union**, and I want to respect that.
> My watch says elevated, which is data, and data is not a judgment.
> So what I'm hearing from the group is a capacity issue, not a commitment issue.
> (checks the wrist; her face lights green)
> Seventy-eight. Which is optimal. Which — genuinely — is wild, given.
> Can we reframe the laser as a **constraint** rather than a threat?
> Because constraints are where the good work happens.
> Madelin, do you have anything in the bag for constraints?
> (beat) I knew you would. I *knew* you would.

### Sample monologue — "The Benchmark" (the competitive one; she does not hear herself)

> I don't track it against other people. That would be gross.
> I track it against a personal benchmark, which is a version of me from March,
> who — and this is just factual — was not performing.
> March me would have lost this rally. March me lost a lot of rallies.
> (a small, terrible laugh)
> The robot's at eleven. I'm at fourteen. That's not a score, that's a trend line,
> and a trend line is just a story your body is telling about itself.
> (beat) It's fourteen to eleven.

### Sample exchange — with Madelin (the register: the whole relationship in eight lines)

> **JACKALYN:** How long do we think we've been out here?
> **MADELIN:** Since the thing with the cat!
> **JACKALYN:** That's not a unit.
> **MADELIN:** It's *my* unit. (digging) I've got a clock in here.
> **JACKALYN:** You do not have a clock in there.
> **MADELIN:** I have a clock in here that's very sorry about how it's been treated.
> **JACKALYN:** (checking her wrist) It's been one hour and six minutes.
> **MADELIN:** *(producing a clock)* It's been since the cat.
> **JACKALYN:** (a beat; genuinely torn) …Both of those are true.

### Sample exchange — with Justine (the register: fourteen words meets two)

> **JACKALYN:** I want to check in with you, because you've been really quiet, and quiet can be a signal.
> **JUSTINE:** It's a signal.
> **JACKALYN:** …Of?
> **JUSTINE:** Quiet.
> **JACKALYN:** Okay, I'm going to sit with that.
> **JUSTINE:** Good.
> **JACKALYN:** (sitting with it for exactly one second) Is your heart rate elevated?
> **JUSTINE:** I don't have a heart rate.
> **JACKALYN:** (fully sincere, deeply concerned) Jackie, that's *huge.*

### Sample exchange — with Pong-Bot 3000 (the register: two sincere machines)

> **PONG-BOT:** Welcome, humans. Let the best reflexes win.
> **JACKALYN:** I love that framing. I want to gently push on "best," though.
> **PONG-BOT:** Clarify: "best."
> **JACKALYN:** Best *for whom.*
> **PONG-BOT:** (processing) …For the match.
> **JACKALYN:** See, and that's a system answer.
> **PONG-BOT:** I am a system.
> **JACKALYN:** (with total warmth) And you're doing **so well.**

## Backstory (committed)

- **Origin — shared with Madelin, and this is the load-bearing fact about both of them.** Seventeen years ago the two of them were on the same temp assignment: eleven weeks of data entry in a windowless room on the fourth floor of a building neither could find again. They survived it by splitting the job in two. **Madelin invented lives for the objects on the desks** — the stapler's marriage, the ficus's legal trouble — and **Jackalyn kept the count**: hours remaining, keystrokes logged, days until it ended. They have been doing exactly those two things ever since, at every event they have attended, for seventeen years, including this one.
- **Formative incident (series-relevant).** Week nine, the contract was extended without notice and the count Jackalyn had been keeping — the one thing holding the room together — **became wrong.** She had four hundred and twelve hours left and then she had eight hundred. She has never trusted a number she didn't generate herself since, which is precisely why she now wears one that generates itself, continuously, on her wrist, forever. *(She has never made this connection. Do not let her make it on camera.)*
- **What she actually does now.** Something in operations at a company the series never names, which she describes exclusively in terms of "the wellness vertical." Whether the wellness vertical is her department or her entire employer is not established and does not need to be.
- **Secret she protects.** **She is keeping score of the friendship.** Seventeen years of who called, who drove, who showed up, in a note on her phone titled "gratitude log." It is not a gratitude log. Madelin has never asked to see it and would be thrilled by it, which is somehow worse.
- **What would break her on screen.** **A number she cannot verify.** Not a monster — a metric that disagrees with itself. Two readings, two devices, no tiebreak. She would go completely still, which is the one thing she never does, and the ensemble would not know what to do with her. Give the recovery to Madelin, wordlessly, from the bag.

## Arc hooks

1. **"The Wellness Vertical"** *(EyeWash Telethon — already seeded, `ideas/eyewash-station.md`)* — She reads her heart rate on-air as the running total. The tote board goes down; her heart rate does not change. She treats this as a triumph, and in the only sense she cares about, it is one.
2. **"Not a Yes Yet"** — Jackalyn negotiates with something cosmic using pure workshop technique and it **works**, completely, and nobody in the scene can process this. Same mechanism as Lillith's by-the-book elevator negotiation in *Car Twelve* — the technique is correct and it works. Do not undercut it.
3. **"The Gratitude Log"** *(late season, quiet)* — Madelin finds the note. She is delighted. She asks to be read her own entries. Jackalyn reads them aloud and they are almost all about Madelin being there, and this is the closest the Episode 3 ensemble ever gets to a sincere scene. **No music.**
4. **"March Me"** — The benchmark monologue, but at an actual class, at an actual gym, with actual strangers, none of whom have seen a laser. It is the most frightening thing she has ever done.
5. **"Both of Those Are True"** — Jackalyn accepts one of Madelin's units of measurement, out loud, as a unit. The watch disagrees. She goes with Madelin. Watch arc stage 7 lives near here.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Play her sincerity at one hundred percent. The vocabulary is real, learned, and the only tool she has.
- Let the technique occasionally *work*. She is not a fool; she is a person applying an adequate method to an inadequate situation.
- Keep the watch lighting her face from below. It is her second light source and her antagonist.
- Let her competitiveness leak through the gentlest possible sentence. It should be the audience who notices, never her.
- Give her Madelin. They are a single comedic instrument and neither is as good alone.

**Don't**

- Don't play her as stupid, shrill, or a satire target. The joke is the *fit* of the language to the event, not her.
- Don't let her swear, panic openly, or break the vocabulary. Under maximum stress she gets **more** fluent, not less.
- Don't take the watch off. The one time it comes off, it comes back, and nobody says how.
- Don't have her learn her lesson. She is one insight away in every episode and must stay there.
- Don't give her a tragic backstory on camera. The temp job is inventory; it is never dramatized, only referenced.
- Don't let her name her own competitiveness. The moment she says "I'm competitive," the character is over.
