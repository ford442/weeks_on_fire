STYLE:

Sincere honky-tonk road-trip duet, 104 BPM, G major, about two minutes twenty. Shuffle drums with brushes, walking upright bass, crying pedal steel answering every Rubella line, twangy clean electric guitar fills, a honky-tonk upright piano and a fiddle on the turnaround. A windshield-wiper rhythm on a soft shaker locked to the beat for the whole song. Female lead one: bright, conspiratorial, a little twang, sings each call line. Female lead two: dry, slightly raspy, unhurried, answers each call line **with the same melody and rhythm**, so the swapped words sit exactly where the original words were. Both sing the tag together in close harmony. One short spoken line in the bridge, dry, no music under it. Dancehall-warm, earnest, no yodel parody, no comedy sound effects. Use Lillith and Rubella voices.

NOTES:

**The device is the tactic, not the subject.** The song is a road trip. Nobody mentions
swapping sounds. Each spoonerism is answered as if it were the next fact about the day,
and it is: the light rain is real, and so is the right lane; the sad ballad is on the
jukebox, and the bad salad is in Rubella's lap.

**Swaps used (all original pairs, both halves real words):** light rain / right lane ·
long road / wrong load · nosey cook / cozy nook · sad ballad / bad salad · mad bunny / bad
money. None of the famous historical spoonerisms are used.

**The production rule:** Rubella's answer must reuse Lillith's melody and rhythm, so the
swap lands in the same notes. If the answers come back as a new melody, regenerate.

**Callback:** "Extra napkins are in the bag." The Pizza Guy's exit line, said flatly by
someone else after a disaster (the salad), where it lands wrong. That's a portable form
from the callback bank. The Pizza Guy is not in the song.

**Picture, if it ever gets one:** the rubber ducks. A truck shedding one duck per mile
down a wet highway, and a car behind it in no hurry.

**Do-not:** no real diner, gas station or truck brands. No real song titles on the
jukebox.
