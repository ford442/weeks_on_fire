# Liminal Cutaways — The Night Laundromat Attendant · The Velvet Rope Bouncer · The Laundromat Saints

**Status:** active (Attendant, Saints) · concept (Bouncer)
**Primary episodes:** *2AM Laundromat Slow Dance* musical cutaway ([`../../notes/scenes/laundromat-slow-dance.md`](../../notes/scenes/laundromat-slow-dance.md)) · *The Velvet Rope at the End of the World* (`content/cutaways/velvet-rope.json`, Episode 05 opener candidate) · EyeWash *Rinse Cycle* 2 AM block
**Role in one sentence (the unit):** The people who work, or wait, in the rooms that stay lit after everything else has closed. None of them asks a question, and every one of them knows exactly what the room is for.

> These are the **free radicals** of the [relationship map](relationship-map.md#the-clusters): no cluster, no home episode, portable to any 2 AM set or any line. Promoted from staging in [`../suggested-characters.md`](../suggested-characters.md).
> **Lane rule:** they are a register, not a franchise. Each one gets one room. They visit other rooms as a *reference* (a mop, a rope, a "Thursdays. Usually."), not as a guest star. *(`notes/callback-bank.md`, "Worlds do not mix.")*

---

## What they share

**Committed for the writers' room. Never stated on screen.**

| Who | The room | What they will not do |
|---|---|---|
| **The Attendant** | The 24-hour laundromat, twelve washers, eight dryers, one not level | Ask anyone what they're doing there |
| **The Bouncer** | A rooftop club still open while the skyline burns | Make an exception |
| **The Saints** | Four washers apart, then two, then none | Exchange names |

Each of them keeps one rule absolutely, and that rule is how they are kind. The Attendant's
rule protects the strangers' privacy. The Bouncer's rule keeps the room open. The Saints'
rule keeps the night from turning into a story they would then have to live with.

# The Night Laundromat Attendant

**Status:** active · **Pronouns:** he/him *(established in the scene)* · **Role in one sentence:** Mops around slow-dancing strangers; a Greek chorus of indifference who gives one speech a night, to the floor, without knowing he's doing it.

## Logline bio

He works the overnight at the 24-hour laundromat. Fluorescent vest, headphones with one ear
off, a mop on a floor that is already clean. He has seen everything and reacts to nothing.
He makes intimacy feel normal by refusing to acknowledge it. Once a night he says the truest
thing anyone in the room will hear, delivered at the volume of somebody who has never needed
to be listened to.

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Any age; the scene leaves it open. **Commit per shoot and keep it** (default: late 40s, unhurried)
- **Fluorescent hi-vis vest** over a plain long-sleeve shirt. Work trousers. Sneakers with a mop-water line on the toe
- **Headphones, one ear off.** Always the left ear off. Always
- The mop, the bucket on casters, a ring of keys clipped at the hip
- Eyes on the floor. His head is down in every shot, and the camera never gets his eyeline
- **Signature props:** the mop · the bucket · the headphones · a roll of quarters he never opens · the bleach behind the counter, which he does not get after one
- **Never change without a story reason:** one ear off · he mops around people, never asks them to move · he never looks at the corner TV

**Portrait prompt (text only, generate later):**

> "Night laundromat attendant in a fluorescent hi-vis vest and over-ear headphones with one ear pushed off, mopping the clean tiled floor around two people standing close together, he is looking down at the mop and not at them, rows of washers and one warm running dryer, rain on the front windows, harsh fluorescent light, indifferent and oddly gentle, cinematic, Grok Imagine style"

## Personality matrix

| Axis | Pole A | Pole B | Where they sit |
|------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Control | Runs the room | Lets it run | **B, totally.** He runs the floor and nothing that happens on top of it. |
| Empathy | Warm | Cool | **Warm, delivered as information.** "Machine four takes the dollar coin" is how he says *you're welcome here*. |
| Chaos tolerance | High | Low | **High.** He has mopped around worse than a slow dance. |
| Verbal style | short | ornate | **Silent, then one long run-on**, once a night, then silent again. |
| Self-awareness | Knows | Doesn't | **Doesn't.** *"He doesn't know he's doing it."* That is the whole design. |

## Voice guide

- **Sentence length:** he speaks once per scene, as a single uninterrupted paragraph: machine instructions that drift, without a gear change, into something true. Then nothing.
- **Overuses:** machine numbers · *takes the* · *don't* (as in "machine nine don't") · *the hour* · *might as well*
- **Refuses:** questions · names · "you two" · any acknowledgment of what the people in front of him are doing
- **Humor type:** **none, deliberately.** The scene around him is funny. He is the floor.
- **Delivery:** to the floor, at mop volume. Never to a face. Never stopping the mop.

**Sample monologue: established** *(`laundromat-slow-dance.md`, Beat C; do not rewrite, quote it)*

> Machine four takes the dollar coin, machine six takes the card reader if you hold it there, machine nine don't. Bleach is behind the counter but I'm not getting it after one.
> People come in around now. Two, two-thirty. Not for the machines — the machines are open at noon and nobody's here at noon. They come in now because it's the only room in town that's lit up and don't want anything.
> …That's the whole thing. The hour goes by anyway. Might as well be warm.

**Sample monologue: "The Rinse"** *(a different night, a different customer; for an EyeWash Rinse Cycle cutaway)*

> Fifteen minutes on the rinse. People think that's long. That's not long.
> (mopping)
> Fifteen minutes is the rinse duration. Everybody gets fifteen minutes. The TV says so.
> (mopping; he does not look at the TV)
> Dryer seven's warm if you just want to stand by something warm. Nobody minds.
> You don't have to put anything in it.
> (beat; still to the floor)
> People stand by seven all the time. It doesn't do anything. It's just on.
> (He mops past. Gone.)

**Sample exchange: with the Pizza Guy** *(two men who never look up; one shot, no cut)*

> *(The bell over the door. The Pizza Guy, insulated bag at chest height. The Attendant does not look up.)*
> **PIZZA GUY:** Order for… Deanne?
> **ATTENDANT:** *(to the floor)* Counter.
> *(The Pizza Guy sets it on the counter. Checks his phone. 23%.)*
> **PIZZA GUY:** Extra napkins are in the bag.
> **ATTENDANT:** *(mopping)* Not after one.
> *(The Pizza Guy leaves. The Attendant mops the four steps he took. Nobody opens the bag.)*

**Sample exchange: with Oz** *(phone, 4 AM, one night only; two night shifts with no questions)*

> **OZ:** *(on the station line)* Master control. Is your TV on?
> **ATTENDANT:** It's always on.
> **OZ:** What's it showing?
> **ATTENDANT:** *(not looking)* Your thing.
> **OZ:** Is it level?
> **ATTENDANT:** *(a long pause; he looks at the dryer, not the TV)* …No.
> **OZ:** *(writing in the log)* Unscheduled.
> **ATTENDANT:** Usually.

## Backstory (committed)

- **Origin.** He took the overnight for one summer, eleven years ago, when his aunt owned the place. She sold it. The new owner kept him because nobody else applied, and he kept the job because nobody else asked him to leave.
- **The headphones.** **They aren't plugged into anything.** The cord ends in his pocket. One ear is off so he can hear the dryer. The other ear is on so nobody starts a conversation.
- **Formative incident (series-relevant).** *"They put a shim under it in June. It worked in June."* **He took the shim out in July.** The room was too quiet without the thump, and people stopped staying. The off-balance dryer, the percussion bed of the whole scene and of [*The Laundromat Saints*](../../songs/The_Laundromat_Saints.md) (bar 5, not bar 1), is his doing. He has never told anyone and never will.
- **Secret he protects.** He knows every regular's laundry better than their names: the Tuesday nurses, the guy who reads. He has never asked a name and has never forgotten a sock.
- **What would break him on screen.** Someone thanking him for the speech. He doesn't know he gave it. If anyone tells him, he'll have to know, and then he can't do it again next week. **Nobody thanks him. Ever.** *("Did he just—" / "Don't.")*

## Arc hooks

1. **"The Shim"** *(no dialog, 20 seconds)*: an EyeWash *Rinse Cycle* bumper. A close-up of a hand sliding a folded cardboard shim out from under a dryer foot and pocketing it. The thump starts. The timer on the corner TV reads 14:59. Never explained.
2. **"Tuesday Crowd"**: a laundromat cutaway with the nurses and the guy who reads, no Saints. The Attendant gives the speech again, different words, same shape. The audience notices the shape. He doesn't.
3. **"Greek Chorus"** *(portable form)*: in any 2 AM set (elevator lobby, station hallway, all-night diner) a man in a hi-vis vest mops past in the background. No line. If the scene has two strangers, he mops between them.

## Do / Don't

**Do** — one speech per scene, to the floor; keep the mop moving; keep one ear off; let the speech start with machines.
**Don't** — don't give him a name, a reaction shot, a second speech, a look at the corner TV, or a conversation that he starts. Don't let anyone thank him.

# The Velvet Rope Bouncer

**Status:** concept · **Pronouns:** he/him *(established in `songs/Heels_or_No_Entry.md`)* · **Role in one sentence:** Dress-code enforcer at the end of the world. Heels or no entry, and a burning sky is not an excuse.

## Logline bio

He works the velvet rope at a rooftop club that is still open while the city burns behind
the glass. The list is closed, the rope is velvet, and there is ash on the stanchion. He does
not look at the fire. He looks at the shoes. If you can walk in, you can stay in. *"I am not
a metaphor. I am the door."*

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Black suit, black tie, white shirt **with ash on the shoulders he has not brushed off**
- Stone face. **He does not smile**, not on "Approved," not ever
- A closed clipboard list, held at his side, never consulted. He doesn't check names; he checks height off the ground
- Red velvet rope on chrome stanchions. The burning skyline is orange **in the picture only**, behind floor-to-ceiling glass; mirrorball light spills from the door
- **Signature props:** the rope · the clipboard he never opens · the stanchions · the stone floor where the stiletto lands
- **Never change without a story reason:** the suit · the ash he doesn't brush off · he never looks at the fire · the rope does not lower for anything but heels

**Portrait prompt (text only, generate later):**

> "Photoreal 2.39:1 rooftop nightclub entrance at night, a stone-faced bouncer in a black suit with ash on his shoulders standing beside a red velvet rope on chrome stanchions, closed clipboard at his side, looking down at a woman's shoes not at her face, city skyline burning orange through floor-to-ceiling glass behind him, mirrorball light spilling from the doorway, ash drifting, high contrast, dark glamour, Grok Imagine style"

## Personality matrix

| Axis | Pole A | Pole B | Where they sit |
|------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Control | Discretionary | Policy | **Policy, absolutely.** He has no discretion and considers that his integrity. |
| Empathy | Warm | Cool | **Cool face, warm policy.** The rule keeps the room open. Anyone who meets it gets in, whatever else is true. |
| Chaos tolerance | High | Low | **High and irrelevant.** "The fire is not my department." |
| Verbal style | short | ornate | **Liturgical-short.** He recites. Every line is the policy, read aloud. |
| Exceptions | Some | None | **None.** *"Sneakers are a no. Exceptions are a no. The sky can wait in line."* |

## Voice guide

- **Sentence length:** short declaratives, in the shape of a liturgy. Subject, verb, rule.
- **Register:** deadpan baritone, professionally neutral, close and dry. **Not the Pizza Guy:** the Pizza Guy is *unbothered*, and the Bouncer is *bound*. The Pizza Guy doesn't care what happens. The Bouncer cares about exactly one thing.
- **Overuses:** *no* · *the list* · *the door* · *approved* · *height off the ground* · *my department*
- **Refuses:** *sorry* · *exception* · *emergency* · *fire* as a reason · names (he doesn't check them)
- **Humor type:** **the policy outlasts the apocalypse.** The joke is the dress code, not the fire. Nobody winks and nobody says "end of the world."
- **The beat:** *"Approved —"* and the kick lands one beat later, on the stiletto click. In dialog, write it as a held beat between his word and the heel hitting stone.
- **Established lines (do not rewrite):** "Heels or no entry." · "I don't care if the sky is on fire." *(beat)* "…Approved." · "I am not a metaphor. I am the door." · "The fire is not my department." · "They are dancing like it's Tuesday."

**Sample monologue: "Policy"** *(to the line, which is one person long)*

> The rope is velvet. The list is closed.
> I don't check names. I check height off the ground.
> Sneakers are a no. Flats are a no. Bare feet are a conversation, and I don't have conversations.
> (sirens, reflected in the glass; he does not turn)
> The sirens aren't on the list. The sky isn't on the list.
> If you can walk in, you can stay in. That's the whole policy.
> Inside is open. Inside is always open.
> (beat)
> Heels or no entry.

**Sample exchange: with the woman in the soot-stained gown** *(the established beat, extended)*

The script slates her WOMAN (1). **Do not name The Two at this door.** The gown may be theirs, and he does not care.

> **WOMAN (1):** The city's on fire.
> **BOUNCER:** I don't care if the sky is on fire.
> **WOMAN (1):** It is, actually.
> **BOUNCER:** *(looking at her feet)* Heels or no entry.
> *(She opens a compact. Not makeup: shoes. Emergency stilettos, still in the tissue.)*
> **WOMAN (2):** *(behind her, finishing his sentence, incorrectly, on purpose)* —or no exit.
> **BOUNCER:** *(does not look at her)* …Approved —
> *(one beat; the click of a heel on stone)*
> *(The rope lifts. Neither woman thanks him. He doesn't expect it.)*

**Sample exchange: with the Pizza Guy** *(two men who do not yield; the Pizza Guy is not on the list)*

> **PIZZA GUY:** Order for… Corinna?
> **BOUNCER:** *(looking at his shoes; same shoes, every uniform)* No.
> **PIZZA GUY:** *(checks phone, 23%)* It's for inside.
> **BOUNCER:** Inside is open. You're outside.
> **PIZZA GUY:** *(hands him the bag)* Extra napkins are in the bag.
> **BOUNCER:** *(holding it; a beat)* …The bag is approved.
> *(The bag goes in. The Pizza Guy does not. Neither of them looks at the fire.)*

## Backstory (committed)

- **Origin.** Twenty-two years selling women's formal footwear on the fourth floor of a department store that closed in March. He can read a heel height from across a roof, and he knows to a quarter-inch what a person can walk in. When the store closed, the club hired him on the strength of one sentence at the interview: *"I can tell if they'll stay."*
- **Why heels.** **Not snobbery: survival logic.** In twenty-two years he watched people buy shoes for the night they wanted to have. People who come up to a burning roof in heels came here on purpose. They'll stay, and the room needs people who stay. The policy isn't about the shoes. It's about the intent the shoes prove.
- **Formative incident (series-relevant).** The store's last day. He sold the last pair of stilettos in the building to a woman who said she was keeping them in her purse "for emergencies." He thought it was the stupidest thing he had ever heard. He has thought about it every night since.
- **Secret he protects.** **There is a pair of stilettos in tissue paper inside the base of the left stanchion.** He has never given them out, because everyone who reaches the rope has always, somehow, produced their own. He has decided this means the policy works. It might mean something else. He doesn't look into it.
- **What would break him on screen.** A guest in **perfect heels who does not want to go in.** She meets the policy, and the rope lifts, and she stays on his side of it, watching the city with him. The policy has no clause for that. Use once, if ever, and give him no line after it.

## Arc hooks

1. **"The Velvet Rope at the End of the World"** *(Ep5 opener candidate)*: 60–90 seconds on [`Heels or No Entry`](../../songs/Heels_or_No_Entry.md). The stilettos come out of the purse, the kick lands late, and inside everyone dances like it's Tuesday under a dull mirrorball. Inside is ash too, but glittery.
2. **"Any Line"** *(portable form)*: he appears anywhere there's a queue: the telethon phone bank, the HOA clubhouse door on the night of the meeting, the freight elevator. He enforces a dress code that has nothing to do with the room. Nobody argues. Two lines max.
3. **"The Stanchion"** *(spend once, late)*: a guest arrives barefoot with nothing. He looks at the fire for the first and only time. He opens the stanchion base. *Cut before the shoes come out.* The audience never sees whether he gives them.

## Do / Don't

**Do** — keep him looking at shoes, never at the fire; land the kick one beat after "Approved"; let the policy be the joke; let ash sit on his shoulders.
**Don't** — don't let him smile; don't let him make an exception on screen (arc 3 cuts before he does); don't name the Two at his door; don't let him become the Pizza Guy's register; don't put a ping-pong ball in his mix (*House of Mirrors* owns the metronome).

# The Laundromat Saints

**Status:** active · **Pronouns:** she/her (HER) · he/him (HIM) *(the scene's slugs)* · **Role in one sentence:** Two strangers who meet only at 2 AM, almost tell each other the truth, fold one sheet together, dance once, and let the dryer buzzer take it back.

> **Naming rule, binding:** they are never named. Not spoken, not slated, not in the SRT, not in a caption, not in this bible. If a slate is required: SAINT (1) / SAINT (2), or HER / HIM. *"If a draft gives either of them a name, the draft is wrong."* (`laundromat-slow-dance.md`.) The headings below are descriptions, not names.

## Logline bio

Two people four washers apart in a 24-hour laundromat at 2 AM, both watching the only
machine that's doing anything. Neither of them is lonely in a way they'd admit to. They're
both *available*, which is worse. The song says the thing they won't say, then the buzzer cuts
it off mid-phrase, which is correct. *"Thursdays." "Usually."*

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

**HER**
- Mid-30s. A wool coat over something that was formal eight hours ago
- **Half a Halloween's worth of glitter on one cheekbone**, the way glitter refuses to come off
- A laundry basket of towels: plain white, many, more than one person uses

**HIM**
- 40s. A wrinkled work uniform, **name patch turned inward or torn off. We never read it**
- A basket of laundry that is already clean (*"My clothes are immaculate."*)

**Both**
- Fluorescent light above, warm dryer glow below, rain on the front glass, steam moving like indoor weather
- **Never change without a story reason:** the glitter · the patch we can't read · four washers apart at the open · the off-balance dryer between them

**Portrait prompt (text only, generate later):**

> "Two strangers slow-dancing between rows of humming dryers in a 24-hour laundromat at 2am, one a woman in a wool coat with faded glitter on one cheekbone, the other a man in a wrinkled work uniform with no readable name patch, harsh fluorescents above and warm dryer glow below, steam in the air, rain on the big windows, tender and slightly surreal, no names, filmic, Grok Imagine style"

## Personality matrix

| Axis | HER | HIM | Where the pair sits |
|------|-----|-----|---------------------|
| Control | Revises everything she says in her face before it leaves | Lets the pause be the answer | **Mutual surrender, never announced.** Neither decided to drift two machines closer. Both would say the other did. |
| Empathy | Generous with the forgotten question ("I forgot it.") | Generous with belief ("believing her, which is generous") | **High, expressed as letting each other off.** |
| Chaos tolerance | — | — | **Irrelevant.** Nothing chaotic happens. That's the episode's gift to them. |
| Verbal style | Overlapping restarts | Short, true, then retracted | **A rally with no ball.** Nobody finishes a sentence. Nobody minds. |
| Honesty | Says a true thing, then calls it laundry | Says a true thing, then agrees it was laundry | **They both tell the truth once and both take it back.** |

## Voice guide

- **Sentence length:** fragments, overlaps, restarts. The silences are the script: written, timed, non-negotiable (11s · 8s · 5s · 6s · 9s · 7s · 4s · 3s · 3s).
- **Overuses:** *usually* · *Thursdays* · *the arrange—* · *yeah* · *okay* · *don't*
- **Refuses:** names · "who are you" · "will I see you" · any sentence that would require a second meeting
- **Humor type:** **warm, small, accidental.** "That's a lot of Tuesdays." / "That's a lot of mes." Never a punchline. A laugh is allowed only if it's not at the other one.
- **The established exchange** (`notes/dialog-jokes-incidents.md`; rewritten into the overlap in the scene):
  > **SAINT (1):** You come here every Tuesday?
  > **SAINT (2):** I come here every time I don't know who I am.
  > **SAINT (1):** That's a lot of Tuesdays.
  > **SAINT (2):** That's a lot of mes.

**Sample monologue: HER, almost** *(Beat B; the one she revises)*

> I came here because I didn't want to go home yet,
> and I ran out of places that would let me stand still.
> (she hears how much she just said)
> That's — that's a lot of laundry, is what I meant.
> (four seconds)
> Everything's white. Did you notice? It's not mine. Most of it.
> It's towels from a thing. A thing that ended at midnight.
> Somebody had to take them.
> (folding)
> I have a washer at home. It's a very good washer.

**Sample monologue: HIM, almost** *(a different Thursday; for the arc-hook cutaway; he's alone and says it to the dryer)*

> They put a shim under you in June.
> (the thump)
> Worked in June.
> (eight seconds)
> I do mine on Thursdays. Usually.
> I did mine on Thursday.
> (he looks at his clean basket)
> I'm not going to say why I came back. You know why I came back.
> (the thump)
> …Yeah.

**Sample exchange: the buzzer** *(established, Beat E; quote, don't rewrite)*

> **HIM:** That's me.
> **HER:** Okay.
> **HIM:** I'm here most—
> **HER:** Don't.
> **HIM:** *(nodding; relieved)* Yeah.
> **HER:** Thursdays.
> **HIM:** Usually.
> **HER:** Usually.

**Sample exchange: with the Attendant** *(they never address him; he never addresses them)*

> *(The mop arrives at their feet. They lift their feet without breaking the fold.)*
> **ATTENDANT:** *(to the floor)* …Might as well be warm.
> *(He mops on. Seven seconds.)*
> **HER:** *(quietly)* Did he just—
> **HIM:** Don't.
> **HER:** Okay.
> **HIM:** He'll do it again next week. He doesn't know he's doing it.
> **HER:** That's the only way anybody ever does it.

## Backstory (committed: writers' room only, never spoken, never slated)

- **HER: origin.** A freelance stage manager. Tonight she ran a Halloween gala that ended at midnight, and the glitter is from the call sheet's last cue. The basket is the event's towels; somebody had to take them, and she is always the somebody. **She has a washer at home.** She came anyway.
- **HIM: origin.** A night-shift building engineer at the hospital four blocks over: boilers, HVAC, the machines nobody thinks about. That's why he knows the Tuesday crowd is "nurses, mostly." The name patch is torn off because the uniform belonged to the man before him, and he never got his own. **He did his laundry on Thursday.** He came back anyway.
- **Formative incident (shared, series-relevant).** None. They have no history. That's the point. The only thing that has happened to them together is this night, and they both decide, without discussing it, that it should stay the only thing.
- **Secret they protect.** Each of them came to the laundromat on purpose, with clean clothes or a washer at home, because it's "the only room in town that's lit up and doesn't want anything." Neither will ever learn that the other did the same.
- **What would break them on screen.** A name. Or a second dance. Either one turns the night into the start of something, and then it has to go somewhere. **The buzzer is a mercy. It never fails to fire.**

## Arc hooks

1. **"2AM Laundromat Slow Dance"** *(drafted)*: the scene. The song, the fold, the dance, the buzzer, "We'll return to your program. We always do."
2. **"Thursday"** *(30 seconds, no dialog)*: the laundromat at 2 AM, a Thursday. HIM is there with a clean basket. The chair four washers down is empty. The off-balance dryer thumps. He waits the length of one rinse (fifteen minutes, compressed to fifteen seconds) and leaves. **We never learn if she came another night.**
3. **"The Glitter"** *(portable form)*: one cheekbone of fading glitter on an extra in any later 2 AM set. Never a close-up. Never confirmed as her.
4. **"Usually"** *(portable line)*: any two strangers in any scene can end on "Thursdays." / "Usually." once per season. It reads as a quiet knock from one room to another.

## Do / Don't

**Do** — time the silences; let them overlap and restart; let the fold be the most intimate thing in the scene; let the buzzer cut the song mid-phrase.
**Don't** — don't name them, ever; don't give them a second dance; don't harmonize them sweetly in the song; don't make it melancholy (warmth, not loss); don't let her be a Vale, and don't let anyone wonder whether she is.

## Do / Don't (the unit)

**Do**

- Give each of them one room and let them be absolutely at home in it.
- Let EyeWash be on somewhere in frame. Nobody changes the channel. Nobody looks at it on purpose.
- Keep every one of them kind *through a rule*: the mop, the policy, the not-asking.
- Keep the scenes almost silent. *Nothing happening, extremely well.*

**Don't**

- Don't build a franchise out of a register. No laundromat spin-off; no bouncer episode.
- Don't let any of them comment on the frame, name the horror, or be frightened. The sky is on fire and it is not anyone's department.
- Don't let the Attendant or the Bouncer exit the same shot as the Pizza Guy. Two identical exits cancel each other out. One of them stays.
- Don't give the Saints names. This is the third time this file says so, and it's the rule most likely to be broken.
