# Funny Rules — comedy brief for silly drawers

*Brief, not a farm. Text only. 2026-10-19. `promote?: no`.*

Every drawer built from this sheet follows it. It exists because the house style in
[`short-packet-farm.md`](short-packet-farm.md) and its children
([`joke-shorts-10-20.md`](joke-shorts-10-20.md), the possible-* drawers) got dry: a
napkin that does not end, a form that duplicates, a key that rotates. Those are good
loops. They are not jokes a kid laughs at. This sheet is for the ones that are.

---

## The seven rules

1. **Deadpan characters, LOUD world.** Lillith and Rubella stay calm. The chaos happens
   *around* them. Rubella endures it like a dryer cycle. Lillith's energy goes into the
   thing she built, not her face — she watches the disaster like it is going to plan.
   Nobody screams, nobody runs, nobody names the horror. The Pizza Guy arriving exactly at
   chaos peak is the model: three ordinary knocks while the ceiling comes down.
2. **Name the laugh in one sentence a 9-year-old would get.** Every card has a `laugh:`
   line. *"The hair is so tall a plane lands on it."* If the sentence needs the word
   "policy," "technically," or "nobody mentions," rewrite the card.
3. **The drop changes the picture.** When the music drops, something on screen
   **appears, falls, multiplies, transforms, or reverses.** Name which one on the card
   (`drop:`). No drop lands on stillness, a held stare, or a slow push-in.
4. **Prefer these engines:**
   - **Escalation** — rule of three with a wrong third (chair, chair, *giraffe*).
   - **Wrong scale** — tiny thing doing a huge job, huge thing doing a tiny job.
   - **Animals doing human jobs** — a goose lifeguard, a raccoon parking cars.
   - **Literal phrases** — "raining cats and dogs," "break a leg," "hold the elevator."
   - **Things that keep happening** — and keep happening, and keep happening.
   - **The very-end reaction** — the deadpan holds the whole short, then one character
     finally reacts in the last second (a blink, a sigh, one word). Spend it once per card.
5. **Banned as the main joke:** paperwork, forms, stamps, inventory, procedure, audits,
   "a flaw nobody mentions," and any joke that only works if you read text on screen. These
   may sit in the background. They may not be the punch.
6. **Locks still hold.** Every character and voice lock in `characters/building-cast.md`
   and `characters/bible/` stands: Rubella dry and raspy, Lillith bright and conspiratorial
   in voice, the Pizza Guy unnamed and unhurried, no real actor names, no copyrighted
   lyrics. **Worlds do not mix** — one world per card, callbacks cross as a reference
   (a sting, a line, a prop) and never as a set.
7. **What this sheet lifts.** The farm's **one-room rule** and its **bureaucracy materials
   list** (honey-oak, brass, porcelain, marble insert, honeycomb guard, pale pink
   lamp-eyes) do **not** apply here. A short may travel, and the world may be made of
   anything loud. Text only, no stills, no `content/` file, `promote?: no` until the owner
   says otherwise.

## Card shape

`id` · `world` · `length` · `laugh:` (one kid sentence) · `drop:` (appears / falls /
multiplies / transforms / reverses — and what) · `end reaction:` (who, what, or "none") ·
`do-not` · `promote?: no`.

## Six pairs — dry → punched up

| # | Dry (house style) | Punched up (this sheet) |
|---|-------------------|-------------------------|
| 1 | A folding chair unfolds into a second folding chair. The Two say *"We'll stand."* | Rubella unfolds one chair. On the drop, forty chairs **multiply** out of it and stack to the ceiling. She sits on the top one. **Laugh:** *the chair has babies until the room is full.* |
| 2 | A napkin pulled from the dispenser is one sheet and does not end. | Lillith pulls a napkin. On the drop it keeps coming — out the window, down the street, until a passing goose **pulls the other end** and they tug-of-war. Rubella hands her another. **Laugh:** *a goose wants the napkin too.* |
| 3 | The elevator opens on a supply closet with one more folding chair in it. | "Hold the elevator." Lillith holds it — literally lifts the whole car over her head. On the drop the doors **open** and a marching band walks out, still playing. **Laugh:** *she holds the elevator like a sandwich.* |
| 4 | Madelin's tote produces a clock, then a stool, then twine. Plausible, in order. | Same tote: a clock, a stool, a *second tote*, and on the drop a whole **piano falls** out. "Not in this one." **Laugh:** *her bag has a piano in it.* (Bag rules: still never on the ground.) |
| 5 | A weather card on the lobby TV reads RAINING CATS AND DOGS. | It's raining cats and dogs — literal, tiny, in umbrellas. On the drop they all **reverse** and fall back up into the cloud. Rubella, dry and unbothered, finally opens her own umbrella — **end reaction**. **Laugh:** *the rain is puppies and they go back home.* |
| 6 | The lint screen comes out as one clean grey sheet, then another, stacked and counted. | A hamster runs the laundry room — tiny apron, tiny register, one giant dryer. Rubella's sock goes in; on the drop the dryer **transforms** it into a sock the size of a car. The hamster charges her extra. Pizza Guy walks past: *"Extra napkins are in the bag."* **Laugh:** *a hamster shrinks your money and grows your sock.* |

Each punched-up version: calm faces, loud world, a kid sentence, a visible drop, and no
text to read. Pair 1 keeps the forty-chairs callback, pair 4 keeps Madelin's bag, pair 6
keeps the Pizza Guy's exit line — callbacks spent as references, not sets.

## Quick test before a card goes in a drawer

- Can a 9-year-old say the laugh back to you? 
- Mute the music: is the drop still visible?
- Are the main characters the calmest thing in frame?
- Cover every word on screen: is it still funny?
- One world?

Five yeses or it goes back in the bag.
