STYLE:

Sincere 1930s Paris bal-musette waltz, 3/4 at 160 BPM, G major, about two minutes twenty, sung entirely in French with soft Parisian diction. A bright musette accordion with the classic wet, slightly detuned reed sound carries the melody and fills between lines, a manouche-style rhythm guitar plays the oom-pah-pah, upright bass plucked on beat one, and a light snare brushes beats two and three. A glockenspiel plays one note each time the word "réverbère" lands. One female voice: dry, slightly raspy, warm underneath, unhurried, telling a strange night walk as if it happens every week. The final chorus slows into a gentle rubato and ends on a soft major chord with the accordion alone. The bridge is two short spoken lines, close-mic, no music under them. Cobblestone-at-midnight atmosphere, warm and intimate, earnest, no parody, no sound effects. Use the Rubella voice, with one spoken line from Lillith.

LYRICS:

[Verse 1]
RUBELLA: Il est minuit passé, je rentre par la rue
Mes talons sur les pavés, la ville s'est tue
Derrière moi, un petit bruit, un pas de fer
Je me retourne: c'est un réverbère

[Chorus]
Tourne, tourne, la valse du réverbère
Il me suit sur le pavé, il me fait de la lumière
Je m'arrête, il s'arrête, je repars, il repart
C'est un réverbère perdu qui rentre un peu tard

[Verse 2]
Je lui dis: rentre chez toi. Il baisse un peu la tête
Sa lumière clignote, comme une chose qui regrette
Je fais trois pas, il en fait trois, il tient la distance
Et quand je tourne au coin, toute la rue s'élance

[Chorus]
Tourne, tourne, la valse du réverbère
Il me suit sur le pavé, il me fait de la lumière
Je m'arrête, il s'arrête, je repars, il repart
C'est un réverbère perdu qui rentre un peu tard

[Verse 3]
Ils sont vingt derrière moi, en file sur le trottoir
Ils ont laissé la rue toute noire, ce soir
Ils montent l'escalier, ils éclairent chaque palier
Jusqu'au bout du couloir, sous l'enseigne E X I

[Bridge]
LILLITH: Il peut rester?
RUBELLA: Un. Un seul.

[Final Chorus]
Tourne, tourne, la valse du réverbère
Il y en a un dans ma chambre, il me fait de la lumière
Les dix-neuf autres sous ma fenêtre, en rang, bien sages
Et la rue, toute la nuit, sans éclairage

NOTES:

**English gloss:**
- *V1:* It's past midnight and I'm walking home. My heels on the cobbles; the city has gone
  quiet. Behind me, a small sound, an iron step. I turn round: it's a streetlamp.
- *Chorus:* Turn, turn, the streetlamp's waltz. It follows me on the cobbles and gives me
  light. I stop, it stops; I go, it goes. It's a lost streetlamp coming home a bit late.
- *V2:* I tell it, "go home." It lowers its head a little. Its light flickers like something
  sorry. I take three steps, it takes three, keeping its distance. And when I turn the corner,
  the whole street sets off.
- *V3:* There are twenty behind me, in a line on the pavement. They've left the street
  completely dark tonight. They climb the stairs and light every landing, all the way to the
  end of the corridor, under the sign that says E X I.
- *Bridge:* "Can it stay?" "One. Only one."
- *Final:* One is in my room, giving me light. The other nineteen are under my window, in a
  row, well-behaved. And the street is unlit all night.

**Callback:** **E X I**, the neon EXIT sign at the end of the sixth-floor corridor with its
T dark for three weeks. It's sung as three letters. Nobody fixes it in this song.

**Lillith's line:** she wants to keep it. It's the only thing in the song she asks for.
Rubella rations: one.

**Do-not:** the lamps never get faces, just a lowered head (a bent lamp arm) and a flicker.
Not the Pizza Guy's porch light (Ep3). Lamps don't break anything on the stairs.
