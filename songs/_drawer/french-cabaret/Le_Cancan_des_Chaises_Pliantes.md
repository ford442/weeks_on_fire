STYLE:

Sincere 1890s Paris music-hall galop, the can-can, 2/4 at 168 BPM, F major, about two minutes, sung entirely in French. A full theatre pit band: bright brass with cornets and trombones, piccolo runs, a bass drum and crash cymbal on every accent, a snare playing the galop rhythm, a pit piano, and a cancan accordion doubling the tune. Shouted "Hop!" from a mixed chorus on the first beat of each refrain line, with a crash cymbal. Lead one: bright, conspiratorial, breathless with delight. Lead two: dry, slightly raspy, totally flat, singing as if reading a municipal notice over a can-can. The bridge is a counted kick line that speeds up. The song stops dead before the outro: silence, then a few dry wooden creaks instead of applause, then one spoken line. Big brassy hall sound, earnest, no parody, no comedy sound effects beyond the creaks. Use Lillith and Rubella voices.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
RUBELLA: Quarante chaises. Un objet par foyer.

[Verse 1]
LILLITH: Sur le trottoir ce matin, quarante chaises en rang
Une étiquette au dos qui dit: encombrants
Elles attendaient le camion, pliées, sans un bruit
Mais l'orchestre a commencé, et une chaise a dit oui

[Chorus]
TOUS: Hop! Le cancan des chaises pliantes!
Hop! Ça se plie, ça se déplie!
Hop! Quarante chaises, cent soixante jambes!
RUBELLA: Et le camion attend ici.

[Verse 2]
RUBELLA: Elles lèvent la jambe en ligne, elles claquent comme des éventails
Le facteur s'arrête, le chien s'assoit, le boucher lâche son travail
Le camion des encombrants klaxonne au coin de la rue
Les chaises font le grand écart, et le camion n'en veut plus

[Chorus]
TOUS: Hop! Le cancan des chaises pliantes!
Hop! Ça se plie, ça se déplie!
Hop! Quarante chaises, cent soixante jambes!
RUBELLA: Et le camion est reparti.

[Bridge]
LILLITH: Une! Deux! Trois! Quatre! Cinq! Six! Sept! Huit!
RUBELLA: Quarante.

[Verse 3]
BOTH: Elles descendent le boulevard, en ligne, jusqu'au music-hall
On leur donne une loge, des plumes et un rôle
Ce soir, au premier rang, il ne reste qu'une chaise
RUBELLA: Je m'assois dessus. Elle se tient tranquille. Je suis à l'aise.

[Final Chorus]
TOUS: Hop! Le cancan des chaises pliantes!
Hop! Ça se plie, ça se déplie!
Hop! Quarante chaises, cent soixante jambes!
BOTH: Au music-hall, toute la nuit!

[Outro]
RUBELLA: Elles n'applaudissent pas. Elles grincent.

NOTES:

**English gloss:**
- *Intro:* "Forty chairs. One item per household."
- *V1:* On the pavement this morning, forty chairs in a row, a tag on the back that says "bulky
  waste." They were waiting for the truck, folded, without a sound. But the orchestra started,
  and one chair said yes.
- *Refrain:* Hop! The folding chairs' can-can! It folds, it unfolds! Forty chairs, a hundred and
  sixty legs! / "And the truck is waiting here." (Then: "And the truck has driven
  off.")
- *V2:* They kick their legs in a line and snap like fans. The postman stops, the dog sits, the
  butcher drops his work. The bulky-waste truck honks at the corner. The chairs do the splits,
  and the truck doesn't want them any more.
- *Bridge:* One, two, three... eight! / "Forty."
- *V3:* They go down the boulevard in a line to the music hall. They're given a dressing room,
  feathers and a part. Tonight, in the front row, there's only one chair left. I sit on it. It
  keeps still. I'm comfortable.
- *Final:* At the music hall, all night!
- *Outro:* "They don't applaud. They creak."

**Callbacks:**
- The **forty folding chairs tagged at the curb for bulk pickup** ("One item per household.").
- **Forty folding chairs creak; they do not clap.** That's Rubella's laugh track, and the
  outro line is the lock, sung.
- The kicks are the chairs folding and unfolding: four legs each, so 160.

**Do-not:** no human dancers in the kick line; the chairs are the troupe. No feathers on the
chairs until verse 3. Not the HOA clubhouse chairs (wrong world). Nobody gets kicked.
