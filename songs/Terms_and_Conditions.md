STYLE:

Vegas big-band showstopper, 124 BPM at the top, performing an end-user license
agreement in full. Full horn section, walking upright bass, ride cymbal, a
string sweep on every subheading. Deadpan baritone crooner in a tuxedo who
believes this is the best material he has ever been given — warm, generous,
selling it to the back row. Never winking; the sincerity is the entire act.
**Structural flaw:** the tempo increases 2 BPM at every section break because
the free trial is expiring, and nobody in the band acknowledges it. By the
arbitration clause the band is at 138; by the final section it is at 152 and
the words are no longer fully intelligible. The song does not end — it stops
mid-word on a sampled interface chime. Horn stabs on all-caps clauses only.
Optional tiny audience applause bed that never grows. Runtime ~1:50–2:15. Use
a deadpan baritone or Continuity Voice with a bigger room; do not use Rubella.

NOTES:

## What the song is
A tuxedo crooner performs a licensing agreement end to end as if it were the
great American songbook standard nobody has had the courage to attempt. He is
not mocking the document. He thinks it is beautiful. The audience bed never
grows, and he never notices that either.

The gag is legal-adjacent set dressing only — invented clauses for a fake
low-power affiliate, not advice, not a real agreement, not a parody of any
specific company's terms.

## Craft (keep this off-mic)
The comedy is the tempo. Every section break is +2 BPM and the band follows
without comment, so the last section is genuinely too fast for the words. Cut
it live if possible; do not time-stretch a slow take. The crooner must stay
warm and unhurried in *tone* even while the tempo makes that impossible — he
sounds like a man being carried off by a train he booked himself.

Never let him wink. One raised eyebrow and the song is dead.

## Production notes
- **Episode placement:** EyeWash Station — end of any programming block; works
  as a full number or as a 20-second bumper (Overture + Chorus 1 + chime).
  Sibling to *Ultra Screech* (joke commercial) but the opposite temperature:
  glossy, not loud.
- **Voice:** deadpan baritone in a bigger room than the rest of the catalog.
  Continuity Voice acceptable if given reverb he is not normally allowed.
- **Structural flaw:** +2 BPM per section (124 → 152), unacknowledged; song
  stops mid-word on an interface chime. No ritard, no button, no final chord.
- **Runtime target:** 1:50–2:15. Bumper cut: 0:20.
