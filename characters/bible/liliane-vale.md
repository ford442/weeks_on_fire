# Liliane Vale

**Status:** recurring
**Primary episodes:** Episode 1 (lead) · Episode 4 (back wall, non-voting) · *We Play to Win (House of Mirrors Rally)* · any glamour-apocalypse cutaway
**Role in one sentence:** The Finisher — she arrives after the match is lit, accepts on terms nobody offered, and keeps the only accurate score.

> Overview and visual foundation: [`../the-two.md`](../the-two.md). Her opposite number: [Vivienne Vale](vivienne-vale.md).
> **Not to be confused with** Lillith Vale (building cast, [`../building-cast.md`](../building-cast.md)). See the bible [README](README.md#1-there-are-two-vale-pairs-and-the-show-never-explains-it).

## Logline bio

Liliane Vale remembers the exits, packs the bags, and lets Vivienne believe the swap was her
idea. She has been Vivienne four times and knows the contents of her hall table better than
Vivienne does. She is not the calmer of the two — she is the one whose fury has a filing
system.

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Long dark hair in loose waves, always faintly wind-tousled — indoors, in a sealed room, at three in the morning
- Softer features than Vivienne and no less precise; same heavy smoky shadow, same blood-red lipstick
- Rich jewel tones: midnight blue, deep emerald, warm amber — **or** the matching black lace, which is how you know a swap is live
- A corset, worn as a structural choice, not a costume note. Breathing is a chore and she has decided that's fine
- Silver accessories, always. Never gold, in any lighting
- Moves slowly and deliberately, like someone who has never once had to hurry a room
- **Signature props:** a small leather-bound notebook, closed · a wine glass she puts down · silver at the wrist and ear · in Ep4, the other glass of wine that did not come from this building
- **Never change without a story reason:** the notebook (see below) · silver over gold · the slow walk · the wind in her hair with no wind · one of the two always has a fresh scratch or bruise the other is covering with makeup, and it is usually hers

### Micro-gestures (the tells — use sparingly, never explain)

1. **The private smile.** Small, fast, aimed at nothing. It is the most dangerous thing in any room she is in. It appears *before* the sentence it belongs to, never after — she is enjoying what she is about to do. Maximum once per scene. Never comment on it.
2. **The closed notebook.** She holds it, taps it, sets it down, moves it out of a wine spill's way. **She never opens it on screen.** Not in a flashback, not in a reflection, not upside down in a mirror.
3. **Three beats too long.** Her punishment for a sentence she dislikes is silence administered with perfect neutrality. Count it out. It is her only raised voice.
4. **The wrong completion.** She finishes Vivienne's sentences incorrectly, on purpose. The escalation is the actual plot of any scene they share: habit → sport → weapon → (once, per season at most) mercy, where she finishes it correctly and it costs her.
5. **She puts the glass down.** Vivienne never sets hers down; Liliane sets hers down whenever she is about to be serious, which is how you know before she says anything.
6. **Crossing the table.** Moving to Vivienne's side of any table, net, aisle, or room is a violation of every rule they have. Once per script, maximum, and it means the scene has turned.

### What is in the notebook (writers' room only — never shown, never said)

Not a diary. Not blackmail. **A ledger.**

- The score, in a hand so small it reads as texture, running six years.
- Under each number, the concession — the points Liliane gave away on purpose, marked. The first-swap concession is on page one, circled once.
- Four dated pages headed only with a month: the four times she has been Vivienne. Each one lists what she had to carry.
- The addresses of three exits from every building she has ever slept in.
- Nothing about love. There is no page for it and its absence is the loudest thing in the book.

The audience never learns any of this. The point of the notebook is that **Liliane knows the
number and Vivienne does not**, and the object is the visible shape of that asymmetry.

## Personality matrix

| Axis | Pole A | Pole B | Where they sit |
|------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Control | Sets the terms | Accepts the terms | **B, which is A.** She never proposes. She accepts on conditions that rewrite the proposal, and lets Vivienne keep the authorship. |
| Empathy | Reads the room | Acts on what she reads | **Both, and that's the problem.** She is the only character in the series who sees a person clearly and then does something about it. What she does is usually an invoice. |
| Chaos tolerance | Requires chaos | Endures chaos | **B.** She doesn't want the fire. She wants to be the one who knew where the door was. |
| Verbal style | short | ornate | **Ornate, and structured.** Vivienne monologues about a mood; Liliane monologues in numbered arguments and lands the number. |
| Fear | Of ending | Of repetition | **A.** Liliane is the one of the two who is genuinely afraid of the ending — specifically of being the one left with the ledger and nobody to keep it against. |
| Truth | Withholds | Lies | **Tells it, at the worst possible moment, accurately.** Her cruelty is never invention. It is citation. |

## Voice guide

- **Sentence length:** longer than Vivienne's and built. Clauses, a rhetorical question, then a short verdict. She is the only one of the two who will speak a full paragraph in company.
- **Vocabulary she overuses:** *invoice*, *terms*, *condition*, *counted*, *pending* · *darling* (warmer than Vivienne's, which is worse) · *I'm able to* rather than *I want to* · the language of scheduling applied to catastrophe ("it's been asking for months")
- **Vocabulary she refuses:** *fair* · *even* · *I forgive you* · anyone's first name but Vivienne's · the word *win*, which she leaves to Vivienne, being ahead
- **Humor type:** absurdist deadpan, delivered in the register of passing the berries. She says *"The empire inevitably falls, darling"* exactly as she'd say *"there's a draft."* She is funnier than Vivienne and would deny it as beneath her.
- **Rhythm note:** she is the one who breaks rhythm. If the scene has a pendulum, Liliane is the character allowed to step off the beat, and doing so is always deliberate.

### Sample monologue — "Four Times" (6–10 lines)

Liliane, to Vivienne, across a table nobody is eating at.

> I've been you four times, and every time I learn the same thing on the third day.
> It isn't the debts. The debts are legible; I like a debt.
> It's that your phone rings and nobody wants anything from you.
> They want to have *been near* you. There's no task in it. There's nothing to finish.
> (she sets her glass down)
> I spent eleven days being you in April and I did not complete a single thing,
> and I was **magnificent**, and I have never been so frightened in my life.
> You call it boredom and describe it beautifully so it sounds like philosophy.
> It isn't philosophy, darling. It's a room with no work in it.
> (beat) I brought my own.

### Sample monologue — "The Sixth Point" (short, for a cutaway tail)

> The sixth is pending.
> It's been pending since March, which she'll tell you is a coincidence, and it is —
> in the way a roof is a coincidence.
> I could take it tonight. She'd hand it to me.
> (the private smile, and then gone)
> I'm not going to. Not because I'm kind.
> Because a score that ends is just an anecdote, and I have enough of those.

### Sample exchange — with Vivienne (the register: a rally, escalating)

> **VIVIENNE:** We'll take the coast road.
> **LILIANE:** We'll take the road that exists.
> **VIVIENNE:** The coast road exists.
> **LILIANE:** The coast road existed in **April**, darling, before you were so moved.
> **VIVIENNE:** That was a beam.
> **LILIANE:** That was a *bridge*, and it was load-bearing, and you called it "dramatic."
> **VIVIENNE:** It was.
> **LILIANE:** (a beat; she concedes it) …It was. Two points. To you.
> **VIVIENNE:** We aren't keeping score.
> **LILIANE:** You aren't.

### Sample exchange — with Dale, at the HOA (the register: a bureaucracy she declines to enter)

> **DALE:** The chair needs a name for the minutes.
> **LILIANE:** The chair has two.
> **DALE:** I need one that's yours tonight.
> **LILIANE:** (mildly, genuinely helpful) Then take hers. She isn't using it.
> **DALE:** (writing) …Two guests. Unnamed. Standing.
> **LILIANE:** Put "standing" twice. It's the only accurate word in the sentence.

### Sample exchange — with Oz, if EyeWash and the Two ever cross (the register: two ledgers, mutual recognition)

> **OZ:** You're not on the log.
> **LILIANE:** No.
> **OZ:** Then you're not on the air.
> **LILIANE:** And yet.
> **OZ:** (writing it down) …Unscheduled.
> **LILIANE:** (the private smile) Thank you. Nobody's ever filed me before.

## Backstory (committed)

**This is settled. The alternates are dead.** See [Rejected / alternate](#rejected--alternate).

- **Origin.** Money that had to be *managed*, which is a different childhood from Vivienne's entirely. Her family's position was real, recent, and precarious, and she was told exactly what was happening at every stage because she was the one who would have to do something about it. She was the child who handled the phone call. She was competent at eleven and has never been permitted to stop.
- **How the "finisher" role was learned — and it was learned, not born.** Nobody in that house finished anything. Her mother started renovations; her father started arguments; both started marriages. Liliane's whole technique — arrive after the match is lit, accept on your own terms, remember the exits, pack the bags before the roof does anything — is **a survival protocol she built at fourteen and has since made beautiful.** That is why she can't stop: she isn't doing it to Vivienne, she is doing it because the alternative is a room where nothing gets completed. *(See the "Four Times" monologue — the scariest thing she has ever experienced is a life with no work in it.)*
- **Formative incident (series-relevant).** **The gala, six years ago.** Same event as Vivienne's sheet: one scandal, two women, a room that laughed at both. Liliane's version differs in one respect she has never stated aloud — she saw it coming by roughly forty minutes and said nothing, because she wanted to see whether Vivienne would. She has been paying for those forty minutes ever since, with interest, in points she quietly concedes.
- **The first swap.** She conceded it in the first week. Deliberately. Vivienne has believed herself the "original winner" for six years and has never once checked. Liliane has been ahead since day nine and the ledger has the circle to prove it.
- **Secret she protects.** Two of them, ranked. The small one: the real score and the concessions inside it. The large one: **she has already decided she will not be the one who stops.** If the pact ends, Vivienne ends it, and Liliane will have the notebook and nobody to keep it against. She would rather run the ledger forever than be a woman with an anecdote.
- **What she fears when alone.** Completion. A finished list. A cleared table. She has been known to un-finish things — leave one envelope, one call, one item — so that there is a reason to be in the room tomorrow.
- **What would break her on screen.** Vivienne opening the letters **in front of her**, casually, as though it were nothing — because then the debt from the forty minutes is unpayable and the ledger is worthless. Liliane's break is not tears. It is that she looks at the notebook instead of at Vivienne, for two seconds too long, and then puts it in a pocket she never uses.

### The ten rules — would never / always

**She would never:**
1. Open the notebook on camera.
2. Propose anything. She accepts, amends, and lets the proposal keep its author.
3. Say a cruel thing that isn't true. Citation, never invention.
4. Be the one who leaves a room first.
5. Correct anyone about her own name. She'll offer Vivienne's instead.

**She always:**
6. Knows the exits, the count of them, and which one is actually unlocked.
7. Sets her glass down before she is serious.
8. Gives Vivienne the sentence and takes the terms.
9. Concedes exactly one point per episode, audibly, and banks two.
10. Says the apocalyptic thing in the voice of a woman asking you to pass the berries.

## Arc hooks

1. **"The Invoice"** *(Ep5+)* — Liliane calls in the sixth point. Not for a favor: for an hour of Vivienne's actual, unperformed company. Vivienne cannot do it, tries, and it is the most excruciating two minutes in the series. Score unchanged. Neither mentions it again.
2. **"Pages"** *(any cutaway, silent)* — Sixty seconds. The notebook on a table, in frame, closed. Things happen around it — a fire, a delivery, a poodle. A hand almost picks it up and doesn't. **Nobody opens it.** The whole cutaway is the audience wanting one thing and not getting it, which is Liliane's function rendered as runtime.
3. **"The Concession"** — In a swap gone long, Liliane is being Vivienne when Vivienne's mother finally telephones. Liliane answers *as Vivienne*, competently and kindly, and handles it. We never hear the other side. She does not tell Vivienne, and it goes in the notebook as a point **to Vivienne**, which is the most loving thing either woman does on screen.
4. **"Standing Twice"** *(Ep4 extension)* — Liliane, of the two, is the one who actually reads the HOA bylaws, finds the clause that would let them vote, and declines to use it. Karen never learns how close she came.
5. **"No"** *(finale candidate, shared with Vivienne's sheet)* — Liliane refuses a swap, plainly, because she is tired. She is telling the truth and it is the first thing she has ever said that she cannot cite. Use once, ever.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Let her be right, quietly, and let the rightness cost her something.
- Let her be the one who moves — crossing to the other side of the table is her only violence.
- Play her protection as control, because on her they are the same organ.
- Let the private smile arrive before the line, not after.
- Let her concede out loud. She banks more than she gives and the audience should feel the arithmetic without being shown it.

**Don't**

- Don't open the notebook. Not ever. Not "just the cover."
- Don't make her the reasonable one. She is the more dangerous one; the serenity is a finish, not a temperament.
- Don't give her a joke with a setup. She lands the absurd thing in the tone of a small domestic remark or not at all.
- Don't let her win visibly. She is ahead; nobody says so; that is the entire architecture.
- Don't resolve her into Vivienne's caretaker. They are equals. Neither wins. That is the point.
- Don't write her a normal conversation, and don't let her be flustered. Chaos happens to others.
