STYLE:

Sincere late-1950s supper-club cha-cha written for a fashion runway, 120 BPM, A minor verses opening into a bright C major chorus, about two and a half minutes. Timbales, güiro scraping on every beat, congas, a cowbell, a piano playing a montuno figure, upright bass, a three-trumpet section with bright stabs, and a flute doubling the chorus melody. A male backing trio sings "Cha-cha-cha!" in tight harmony after each chorus line and nothing else. Two female leads. Lead one is the runway host, bright and conspiratorial, with sudden energy spikes, half sung and half announced over the vamp. Lead two is the model, dry and slightly raspy, unhurried, answering in flat short lines that still land on the beat. Breakdown before verse three is timbales and claps only. One loud brass hit before verse four, then the band comes back fuller. Final chorus goes up a whole step. The band stops dead on the last "Cha-cha-cha!", followed by three plain door knocks and spoken lines with no music. Glossy, warm, mono-leaning 1958 hi-fi mix. Earnest, no parody tone, no laugh track, no animal sound effects. Use Lillith and Rubella voices.

NOTES:

**Why the animals are fine.** They are alive, warm, comfortable and in charge. Nobody
skinned anything; it is closer to a hug than to a coat. The joke is the literal logic: rare
things are precious, so you keep them close, so you *wear* them. Then the animals turn the
same logic around: there is only one Rubella. No pelts, no taxidermy, no hunting, no
conservation lecture, no real charity or fashion-house names.

**Escalation.** Verse 1: small and sleepy (panda, parrot, axolotls). Verse 2: the animals
start doing jobs (pangolins as roller skates, a turtle handbag that is slow, forty penguins
as bridesmaids). Verse 3: the wrong-scale third. The coat is an elephant, and when it sits
down the corridor tilts. Verse 4 reverses everything.

**Lines kept on purpose.** "It's not fur, it's a friend" is the kid line. "At my own parade"
and "So did the hall" are Rubella being dry. "Spring collection!" is Lillith calling the
disaster part of the plan.

### Drop clip plan (Grok Imagine), 4 clips, ~34s of picture

Default still style (from `ideas/silly-packet-farm.md`): *photoreal cinematic still,
2.39:1, Grok Imagine building-cast, no real actor names, locked camera, 35mm, fine grain,
Grok Imagine style.* **Rubella** = a glamorous, exhausted woman with long dark hair and
blood-red lipstick in a modern black lace evening dress. **Lillith** = a glamorous woman in
black lace with flawless, never-smudged mascara and a bright, conspiratorial half-smile.

1. **10s → trim 8 (1:42–1:50, verse 3).** *Start still:* a long shabby sixth-floor
   apartment corridor set up as a fashion runway, strings of mismatched salvaged marquee
   bulbs along both walls, worn patterned carpet, numbered doors. At the far end, above the
   stairwell door, a neon EXIT sign with the T dark so it reads E X I. Under it, an aluminum
   stepladder with a full red fire extinguisher standing at its foot. Lillith beside it,
   holding a hand microphone, a coil of speaker wire over her shoulder. Mid-corridor, Rubella
   walks toward camera, but her "coat" is a full-size grey Asian elephant: her head and shoulders
   come out of the top of its back like a coat collar. A red panda sleeps around her
   neck, a fat green flightless parrot sits on her head, and two tiny fishbowl earrings each
   hold a pink axolotl. Forty small penguins in single file carry the hem of her train. Her
   face is calm. *Motion:* the elephant sits down on the beat; the whole corridor tilts a few
   degrees and the marquee bulbs swing. Rubella doesn't change expression.
2. **6s (1:50–1:56, the drop — REVERSES).** Start = last frame of 1. On the brass hit,
   every animal climbs, rolls and waddles off Rubella at once. Last frame: Rubella standing
   alone in a plain white terry bathrobe, calm; around her the animals now wear her things:
   the red panda in her black lace evening dress, the parrot in her sunglasses, the elephant
   holding a "World's Okayest Tenant" mug in its trunk, forty penguins each in a tiny black
   lace gown.
3. **15s → trim 14 (1:56–2:10, verse 4 / final chorus — MULTIPLIES).** Start = last frame
   of 2. The animals walk the runway toward the E X I sign in a proud line, forty penguins
   in forty little lace gowns, pangolins rolling, the sea turtle last. Rubella sits in a
   folding chair at the side of the runway in the bathrobe. At the far end Lillith claps,
   delighted, as if this was the plan all along. The extinguisher is still full at the
   ladder foot.
4. **6s (2:24–2:32, outro).** Start: the elevator doors open mid-corridor. A delivery man
   in a dry-cleaning courier uniform (pressed grey work shirt, illegible name tag, dead
   calm eyes, phone at chest height) steps out holding a plastic garment bag level at chest
   height. He knocks three ordinary knocks on door 6-C, right beside Rubella's chair. The
   penguins pass behind him; he does not turn. He hands Rubella the bag. **End reaction,
   final second:** she looks down at her bathrobe, then one slow blink. ("Rare." lands on
   the blink.)

**Do-not:**
- No animal harmed, skinned, caged or frightened. Nobody screams or runs. No pelts or fake
  fur on anyone at any time. The animals are real-looking but plainly happy.
- No Biscuit, no Black Cat. Those are named animals with their own rules; this collection
  is all unnamed animals.
- Not the 12-A roof, not the laundry room, not the Two's ballroom runway. No chandelier,
  champagne or paddles.
- The extinguisher is never used. The E X I sign is not fixed. Madelyn is not seen or
  heard.
- Pizza Guy: never named, name tag illegible, unhurried, never looks at the animals, says
  only the order line and the napkin line. The napkins really are in the garment bag.
  Nobody corrects "Robella."
- No text on screen is needed for the joke. The mug is set dressing.
