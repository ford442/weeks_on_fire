## 1. Video concept

Night curb outside the building lobby, about thirty-seven seconds. A small raccoon in a plain black vest with dull brass buttons parks cars the way a night doorman would: unhurried, both paws, no chatter. Rubella gets out of the first sedan with a white diner mug three-quarters full, shows a phone at 23% for one frame, and hands over real keys at chest height. She walks inside. Lillith gets out of the second sedan — safety glasses on her head, speaker wire over one shoulder, soldering iron in one hand — and hands the raccoon a vacuum tube wrapped in a dish towel, also at chest height. A full red extinguisher stands by the lobby door and is still full at the end. The third arrival is an upright piano on casters. The raccoon opens the lid like a hood. One key sinks by itself. A handful of keys fall onto the asphalt. He sets a blank ticket on what's left and shuts the lid. Through the glass, a lobby CRT is playing EyeWash. Nobody looks at it. Nobody speaks.

Clip chain, last frame of each is the start of the next. Photoreal, night sodium and lobby fluorescent, no readable signage. The laugh still works if every word in frame is covered.

1. **10s.** Rubella steps out, one phone frame, keys into the paws, she reaches the glass.
2. **10s.** Lillith steps out, tube handoff, the piano's nose enters at the curb.
3. **10s.** Piano squares between the two sedans. Lid up. At 2.5s into the clip (0:22.5 overall) the key sinks and the keys fall. Raccoon does not flinch.
4. **7s.** Blank ticket down, lid shut. Rubella already inside with the mug. Lillith at the door, not turned around.

## 4. Minimax STYLE

Short night-valet bossa at 96 BPM in A major, about thirty-seven seconds, written sincere.
Nylon guitar playing a two-bar bossa figure, never a solo.
Brushed snare on two and four, light kick, no ride cymbal.
Upright bass walking two notes per bar and no more.
One close-mic tenor, polite, slightly nasal, no vibrato, no belt, words on the beat, as if announcing arrivals at a hotel desk.
No piano, no Rhodes, no keyboard, no strings, no pad, no horns.
No second voice. No gang vocal. No spoken character name.
The third sung phrase is chopped on the downbeat before the rhyme word. Do not finish it. Do not add a pickup back in.
After the chop, two bars of near-silence are left for a sound that will be pasted later. Do not fill them.
The return is the same nylon figure, 3 dB quieter, one whispered line, dry, no reverb.
The last guitar note is cut dead. No fade, no button chord, no resolved cadence.
Small room, close mics, short reflections, low dynamic range, mixed to sit under picture except where the picture will replace it.
Genre is a thirty-seven-second valet jingle. Not a sea shanty, not cha-cha, not synthwave, not metal.
