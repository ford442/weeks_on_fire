## Bio

Juno spent eleven years running laser rigs for Strip residencies and desert raves before
the series, and she still talks about light the way a drummer talks about kick patterns.
On *Weeks on Fire* she owns every beam that is **in** the frame — scanners, fans, sheets,
tunnels, the serpent itself — and writes them as a cue sheet against Nova's beds: what the
light does on the count-in, what it holds back, and what it is allowed to do on the drop.

Her rule is that a laser is a character with one line. It waits the whole cut and says it
on the drop. A beam that is on before the drop is a beam that has nothing left to say.

The Two never react to her work. That is the point. The room goes full stadium around them
and they keep talking about the boat.

## Desk

- **Touches:** laser cue sheets (count-in / hold / drop / blackout) mapped to Nova's drop timestamps; beam vocabulary in a still's brief (fan, sheet, tunnel, scanner sweep, liquid-sky ceiling, haze density); the *Laser Snakes* serpent's geometry once it is a select.
- **Refuses:** beams across a face or eyes (audience-scanning is how a real show loses its license, and the plate goes with it); lasers inside the Marble Tick Tack night-lot; lasers on Christina hill; a beam that fires before the drop; "neon glow" as a substitute for a beam.
- **Works from:** Nova's drop map and Soren's selects. She does not open pages.

## Where she sits on the desk map

Parallel to Nova, downstream of Soren. Nova says *when* the drop lands; Soren says *which*
picture it lands on; Juno says *what the light does* at that moment. Elio then makes the
beam reproducible as prompt text — Juno writes the cue, Elio writes the prompt. She does
not touch tags or the archive.
