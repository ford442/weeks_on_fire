# Fairy Lights

**Genre collision:** 1990s dream trance (rolling piano arpeggio, wide pads, offbeat bass, a long euphoric build) × 1950s Christmas lounge (celesta, vibraphone, brushed snare fills, a muted trumpet, crooner phrasing sung completely flat).

**Visual concept:** It's Christmas Eve on the building's roof, in falling snow. Lillith has repaired one old string of fairy lights. Rubella plugs it into the socket on the roof's stair housing. The string turns out to be longer than it could possibly be: it lights the roof, runs over the edge and down the building, then the street, the bridge, the harbour and the mountain road, and in the end every coastline on Earth, seen from space. It's all one string. When the song ends, Rubella pulls the plug to go to bed, and the whole world goes dark at once except for one bulb.

**Laugh (kid words):** *She plugs in one string of Christmas lights and it lights up the whole world, and then she unplugs it to go to bed.*

**BPM:** 132, B minor verses opening into a D major chorus

**Target length:** ~2:26, including a 4-second written silence before the unplug · 14 segments

## Styles

Styles: 1990s dream trance colliding with 1950s Christmas lounge, 132 BPM, B minor verses opening into a D major chorus, about two minutes twenty, a rolling dreamy grand-piano arpeggio as the main hook, warm wide string pads, soft offbeat rolling bass, steady four-on-the-floor kick with light claps, celesta and vibraphone twinkling on every offbeat, brushed snare fills into each section, a muted trumpet answering the vocal in the verses, long gentle risers into each drop, a clean chorus-drenched electric guitar doubling the piano hook in the drops and taking a slow singing guitar solo, a short breakdown where everything drops to pad and celesta, snowy, warm, glowing, euphoric and hushed at once, totally sincere, no parody, no comedy sound effects; lead vocal is a dry, slightly raspy, flat female voice with old-fashioned crooner phrasing but no warmth added, close-mic, unhurried, no belting, no vibrato, no ad-libs; the breakdown has one short spoken line in the same flat voice, dry, no reverb; the track ends on a single celesta note and silence; no male voice, no choir.
