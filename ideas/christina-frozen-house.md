## What it is

A house on a hill that looks like snow. You come to the wrong door — carved wood, gold in
the throat of it — and the aria is already on. Inside is two centuries: a gilt foyer where
the harpsichord lives, and one cut later a white marble atrium with a wall of ocean glass.
Christina stands in the white in an ivory shag gown. She is still. She has a hostess face.
The gown is the score.

Rubella and Lillith get thirty seconds at the door. Swedish hospitality. They leave. She
stays. The song goes on.

That is the cutaway. It is short because the house is a threshold, not a place to be.

## Beats

Short. Pick from these; do not stack them all into one cut.

**1. Harpsichord before the door.**
Snow. Blue. A drift that isn't. We hear the harpsichord before anything opens — thin, dry,
D minor, somebody already playing. The aria is on somewhere under it. We are late. The
door is carved and dark, and the throat of it is gold, and it was not supposed to be there.

**2. Thirty seconds at the threshold.**
Rubella and Lillith in the gilt. Christina in the white, one cut behind them, as if the
house folded. *Välkommen.* Lillith looks at the gold. Rubella says they are not staying.
Christina already agrees. *Tack för att ni kom.* They go. Swedish bed the whole time. She
does not turn to watch them leave. The aria doesn't either.

**3. The Lambo sleeps.**
Outside. Three low cars in the snow beside the mound, dusted over, lights off, bedded down.
One slow pass. Nobody gets in. Nothing starts. It is a barn at night.

**4. The tower in the glass.**
Atrium. The ocean-glass wall is sea-green and cold. There is a tower in it — only in it,
a reflection with no source in frame. We never turn around. It is the house's one leak
from outside, and it stays one.

**5. The century line.** *(other night — see company page)*
A tuner kneels where the gilt floor meets the marble. Taps a key. Taps it again one step
to the right. Different room, different century, different pitch. He writes a number and
does not look up.
