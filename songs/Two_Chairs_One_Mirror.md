STYLE:

Torch-song waltz / dressing-room chamber pop in 3/4 at 72 BPM, A minor with a Picardy third that only arrives on the final word. Upright piano slightly out of tune with itself, a single cello, brushed snare with the wire loosened, a string quartet that enters only on the second chorus and is gone by the bridge. Two female voices in dialogue — Lillith dry and clipped, Rubella warm and unbothered — who never sing the downbeat together: one is always a beat early, the other a beat late, so the waltz limps like a heel that's about to give. Chorus in close harmony a third apart, mixed so you cannot tell which sister is on top. Intro is vanity-bulb hum and a lipstick cap clicking in 3. Bridge is one voice, then the other, then a mirror-image of the melody (retrograde) sung by both. Mood: elegant, bored, terrified of boredom, trading lives at a mirror while the town glows orange through the window. No drums louder than a brush. Ends on a single held note, both voices, and one bulb going out.


LYRICS:

[Intro — Vanity Bulbs]
(Bulb hum, lipstick cap click on 1-2-3, piano enters)

[Verse 1 — Lillith]
You take the mouth. I'll take the eyes.
We split the face like we split the prize.
Nobody in the town will know
Which one of us is the one to go.

[Verse 1 — Rubella]
You take the story. I'll take the name.
Every mirror tells it the same.
Slide the pearls across the glass —
We'll trade again before the hour can pass.

[Chorus — Both]
Two chairs, one mirror

Two chairs, one mirror
Which one's there?

Let it burn in tune
Two chairs, one mirror
One of us leaves soon

[Verse 2 — Rubella]
You never smudge. I never blink.
We learned the trick before we learned to think.
The window's orange. The powder's white.
There's a house of mirrors hiring tonight.

[Verse 2 — Lillith]
I'll be the witness. You be the cause.
We'll swap on the chorus, no applause.
Hand me the compact. Check the line.
If they ask which sister — say "she's fine."

[Chorus — Both]
Two chairs, one mirror
One face we share
Two chairs, one mirror
Which one's there?
If the town is burning
Let it burn in tune
Two chairs, one mirror
One of us leaves soon

[Bridge — Mirror Image]
(Lillith, alone)
I was you on Tuesday.
(Rubella, alone)
I was you at the fire.
(Both, retrograde of the chorus melody)
Soon leaves us of one —
Mirror one, chairs two.

[Final Chorus — Both, strings gone]
Two chairs, one mirror
One face we share
Two chairs, one mirror
Nobody's there
If the town is burning
Let it burn in tune
Two chairs, one mirror
One of us left at noon

[Outro — Bulb]
(Held note, both voices, one vanity bulb pops, piano holds A major)


NOTES:

## Production notes
- **Episode placement:** The Two cutaway — Episode 1 house-of-mirrors register, but usable anywhere the sisters swap lives (Velvet Rope aftermath, Episode 3 costume change, any dressing room). The town fire stays outside the window.
- **Emotional arc:** Business-like division of a face → the chorus admits they can't tell either → the bridge is the only time either sister is alone, and she immediately becomes the other → final chorus: the chair is empty and the waltz keeps going.
- **Characters:** Lillith Vale and Rubella Vale, alternating verses, never on the downbeat together. No third voice. Grok Imagine: both sisters at one vanity, the reflection shows only one of them, never the same one twice.
- **Structural flaw:** the early/late downbeat is the whole point; do not quantize it. If Minimax locks them together, use the take where it drifts most.
- **Runtime target:** ~2:45. 15s bumper = intro + first chorus.
- **Reference energy:** Kurt Weill in a Sephora; Julee Cruise if she had a sister; Tom Waits' waltzes without the gravel.

## Music video treatment
Single vanity, two chairs, one mirror, bulbs around the glass. Both sisters in frame; the reflection shows only one, and which one changes on every cut. Town-fire orange fills the window behind them but never gets closer. Last shot: one chair empty, the reflection still shows two.
