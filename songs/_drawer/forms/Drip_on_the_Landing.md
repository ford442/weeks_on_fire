STYLE:

Onomatopoeic minimal, 66 BPM, about one minute twenty. The only lyric is the word plink, sung softly and short by one close female voice, dry, with a little landing reverb. Plink is the drip and it is the pulse: one plink per beat. Under it there's one felt-piano note, B-flat, struck on every downbeat and left to ring, perfectly on the grid. No other notes, no chords, no melody, no bass, no drums, no knock, no water sample, no rain. Start with the piano note alone for one bar. Structural flaw: every plink lands one sixteenth note late against the piano, every time, for the whole track. The piano stays on the grid and the drip stays late. Neither moves, and nothing is quantized. After the second section, hold eight seconds with the piano decaying and no plink, then plink resumes, still late. End on a plink with the piano already gone. No English, no chorus, no bass line, no accordion, no reed organ, no intercom, no door, no Tattarrattat. Mood: a leak nobody has reported yet.

NOTES:

Structural flaw: the drip is late by a sixteenth, and the piano is on the grid. The two never meet, and the eight-second hold doesn't reset the drip.

Callback: **Nothing happening, extremely well.** The eight-second hold is written and timed. It is never filled, and it's labeled in the lyric with its length.

Why it is not the neighbors:
- **Not a knock / not Tattarrattat.** It's water, not knuckles. There's no door, no spacing pattern and no palindrome.
- **Not The Landing Stays Dark.** That one has English and a chorus bass. This one has a single word and no bass at all.
- **Not Quiet on the Landing.** That's reed-organ pop with an intercom that runs one line late. Here the lateness is a sixteenth, and there's no grille.
