**Why it matters:** the power-of-perspective beat. Everything that was loud shrinks to a pattern

**Geography (all three framings):** **after the click** — 117 Elmwood's caged porch bulb is now
the brightest warm point on the street, and the Pizza Guy stands under it, perfectly vertical,
not moving. The battle is still going in the backyard (two colored beams, a white speck dancing).
String lights on every third house; jack-o'-lanterns in rows along the sidewalks; trick-or-treaters
on the street, unaware. One house further up the street has its own small situation that nobody
explains (a faint green flicker in an upstairs window and a lawn sprinkler running backwards,
read only as light and spray). **The strange sky:** a faint violet glow under a low cloud bank
over one distant cul-de-sac, with no source anyone could point to. Never explained, never
brighter than the moon. The moon is full, perfect, round, and saying nothing.

**Callbacks spent (as image, not dialog):** the porch-light contract with the neighborhood ·
he arrives exactly when chaos peaks (and stays) · the violet glow (unadjudicated; Ep4's purple
light can claim it or not) · the Black Cat as the yard's other still point.
