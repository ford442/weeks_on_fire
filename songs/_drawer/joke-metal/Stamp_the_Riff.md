STYLE:

1970s–80s heavy metal played completely straight, 112 BPM, E minor, about two minutes five. Committed and doctrinal, no wink. One riff on one overdriven guitar through a warm vintage tube amp, doubled by bass, repeated for the whole song. Dry 1970s kit with no double-kick; the snare is replaced by a rubber office stamp hitting an ink pad, a dull wooden thunk with a short pad slap, on two and four. Vocals: Rubella dry and flat in the verses, reading a rule like a clerk, close and unhurried, no belting. Each chorus opens with Rubella speaking one form number, dry, unpitched, no reverb, then a gang of stacked Lillith and Rubella double-tracks chants the title in unison, hard but not shouted. The gang chants the title and nothing else. Verses are four short lines. Middle: one melodic guitar solo of about sixteen bars over the same riff, warm and singing, no shred. Bass and kit dry and close, small room, mixed like a 1978 record. No djent, no dropped tuning, no scream, no growl, no blast beat, no occult lyric, no male voice, no Kenji, no product name, no laugh track, no chair creak. Structural flaw: in every chorus the stamp lands one sixteenth note late, and the band plays straight through without waiting or correcting it. In the verses the stamp is on time. Nobody fixes it in any chorus, including the last.

NOTES:

The flaw: the stamp is a sixteenth late in every chorus and nothing waits for it. The verses are on time, so the lateness is audible as a chorus property, not a sloppy take. Same family as the series' shared flaw rule (one cue, one wrongness, structural, never performed).
