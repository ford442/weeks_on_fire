## Tone

Silly, warm and simple. The carol is sung completely straight by a village brass band and
choir, every word as written in 1853. The picture takes each line literally: snow "deep and
crisp and even" is checked with a spirit level, the "mountain" is the snowplow pile at the end
of the street, and the pine logs are a whole decorated Christmas tree. Nobody hurries, shouts
or shivers on camera.

## Beat sheet and shot list

Clip lengths are Grok Imagine **6 / 10 / 15s**. The last frame of each clip is the start of
the next unless marked **new still**. **Lip-sync: none needed.** Everyone outside has a
scarf pulled up to the nose, so the singing never needs a mouth. The one window shot is
behind frosted glass.

**Anchors (paste into stills):** **Lillith** = a glamorous woman in black lace under a long

## Callbacks

- **Lillith's repairs work for exactly one glorious night**, with a vacuum tube from the
  weather-predicting ping-pong table taped to each heel. The boots burn out on the walk home
  (shot 17).
- **Rubella's full extinguisher** rides on her belt through the whole blizzard, never used,
  even next to the heated boots.
- **The World's Okayest Tenant mug** carries the wine (shots 7 and 10).
- **The Pizza Guy arrives exactly at the peak**, and has already delivered by the time the
  feast gets there. He's unhurried, never looks at anything, and says only his exit line,
  off camera: *"Extra napkins are in the bag."*

## Do-not

- No Santa, no sleigh bells, no reindeer. The Pizza Guy is in his red uniform, never a
  costume (Halloween rule applies to Christmas too).
- The neighbour never gets a face or a line. He's not Qing Rao, not the Pizza Guy, and not
  anyone else from the cast.
- Don't rewrite the carol's words. The lyric is sung as written; the joke is the picture.
- No real brand on the boots, crown or pizza bag. No readable signage.
- Not the night woods from *Regulation Powder*. This is the street, with no piano and no
  snowball fight.
- Nobody is cold in a way that's sad: the snow is a set, not a hardship.

## Five-question check

1. Can a 9-year-old say the laugh back? *Her boots are so hot flowers grow in her footprints.* Yes.
2. Mute the music: is the drop still visible? The flower path. Yes.
3. Are the main characters the calmest thing in frame? The blizzard, the tree and the
   flowers are loud; the two of them are flat. Yes.
4. Cover every word on screen: still funny? Nothing needs reading. Yes.
5. One world? The building's street and the end of its block. Yes.

## Why it's funny

The oldest carol in the room, played by an oom-pah band and taken word for word: the snow
gets measured, the "pine logs" are a whole tree, and the miracle in the footprints is a
vacuum tube with a wire.

**Difficulty: 3 / 5.** No lip-sync and a single street, but a lot of props on Rubella in shot
7 and a growth effect at the drop.

**Riskiest shot: #14, the drop.** Models tend to carpet the whole street in flowers or put
flowers on the snow instead of in the prints. Hardener: *flowers grow only inside the
boot-shaped holes, one print at a time, starting nearest camera; the snow between prints stays
white.* Fallback: generate a 6s close of one print blooming and a 6s wide of the finished
flower path, and cut between them on "heat."
