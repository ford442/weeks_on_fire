# Ambient Lighting with Ambition

*Long-form dialog scene — building cast. Lillith repairs a neon EXIT sign with parts from a ping-pong table. Rubella holds the ladder and the extinguisher.*

**Slugline:** INT. SIXTH-FLOOR CORRIDOR — NIGHT
**Runtime target:** 4–5 min (≈4 script pages)
**Music cue:** **"Neon Exit Sign"** — original Minimax track. **Intro and Verse 1 only.** The tube dies before the chorus and the chorus never arrives in this scene. See **Music rule** below; the full song belongs to Episode 1 and the burning-town walk, and this corridor does not get to keep it.
**Status:** draft
**Dialog versions (alts):** [`versions/ambient-lighting-with-ambition-versions.md`](versions/ambient-lighting-with-ambition-versions.md)

**Cast:** LILLITH (lead; ladder) · RUBELLA (lead; ladder foot, extinguisher) · PIZZA GUY (cameo; one beat; order name almost right) · MADELYN (memo only — a piece of paper, no voice, no body)

**Spine:** The corridor EXIT sign has read **EXI** for three weeks. Lillith fixes it with components that do not belong together. It works for one glorious minute and then it does not, and the corridor goes back to fluorescent, and neither of them calls that a failure.

**Juxtaposition (wrong channel):** The show changes into a **daytime home-improvement makeover program** — the segment structure, the before-and-after, the confident sourcing patter, the reveal held for a music sting. Lillith hosts it without ever acknowledging she is hosting it. There is no crew, no camera, no b-roll of a truck arriving. The register is complete and unexplained. Same family as the Monster Mash lawn party and Rubella's ground-current lecture: **full commitment to a channel that does not belong in this hallway.**

**Emotional notes:** This is a love scene between two people who would both rather be electrocuted than say so. The affection is entirely in logistics — who brought what, who stands where, who does not say the obvious thing about the ladder. Warmth through procedure. **No confession. No hug.** Rubella arrived with a fire extinguisher without being asked, three weeks running; that is the whole relationship and it is never discussed.

**Hard rules for this scene:**
- **The extinguisher is never used.** It is carried, set down, picked up, and carried away full. If a draft discharges it, the draft is wrong.
- **The chorus never arrives.** The tube fails on the last line of Verse 1. *Neon Exit Sign* owns mascara and the burning-town walk; this corridor is allowed the hum and one verse, and nothing else.
- **Madelyn does not appear and does not speak.** She is a memo, taped to the ladder at a height that required intent.
- **The Pizza Guy's order name is almost right and is never corrected.** Series rule. He does not comment on the sign.
- **No explanation of why it works.** Lillith has a theory. The theory is not adjudicated, same as haunted vs. unscheduled.

### A — EXI

```
INT. SIXTH-FLOOR CORRIDOR — NIGHT

Long. Carpet that was once a pattern. Doors on both sides, numbered in
brass, two of them numbered in marker.

At the far end, above the stairwell door, a neon EXIT sign. The T has
been dark for three weeks. It reads:

                              E X I

Under it, a fluorescent ceiling fixture doing the thing fluorescent
fixtures do at the end of their lives — a light that is technically
present.

An aluminum stepladder is already open under the sign. Taped to the
second-from-top step, at a height that required somebody to climb up
there to do it, is a laminated memo.

LILLITH comes down the corridor carrying: a shoebox, a soldering iron,
a spool of solder, and a vacuum tube wrapped in a dish towel like an
egg.

She reads the memo without removing it.
```

```
                         LILLITH
                    (reading, aloud, evenly)
          "Residents are reminded that corridor egress
          signage is a life-safety fixture and may not be
          serviced, modified, improved, or encouraged by
          residents."
          (beat)
          "Encouraged."
          (beat)
          She laminated it. Up a ladder.
```

```
Behind her, RUBELLA arrives from the stairwell. Work coat. Mug. And a
five-pound fire extinguisher, held down at her side, casually, the
way you carry a bag of groceries you have carried a lot.

She does not announce herself. She sets the extinguisher on the carpet
at the foot of the ladder, squares it with her toe, and puts her hand
on the ladder's side rail.

Neither of them mentions any of this. Neither of them has ever
mentioned it.

Seven seconds.
```

```
                         RUBELLA
          Third Thursday.

                         LILLITH
          It's Tuesday.

                         RUBELLA
          It's the third Thursday of the sign.
```

### B — Sourcing

```
INT. CORRIDOR — CONTINUOUS

Lillith goes up three steps and opens the shoebox on the top cap.

What follows is the sourcing patter. Play it exactly like a makeover
host walking the audience through the haul: bright, confident,
generous with the word "so," slight upward inflection at the end of
every third sentence. She is not doing a bit. This is how she talks
when she is happy.
```

```
                         LILLITH
          Okay. So. The T is not dead, the T is starved —
          the transformer's putting out about nine hundred
          volts less than the tube wants, and everybody
          looks at the tube, because the tube is the part
          that's pretty.
          (unwrapping the dish towel)
          This is a 6BQ5. Nineteen sixty-two, Dutch, and
          before you ask, yes, it's from the ping-pong
          table.

                         RUBELLA
          I wasn't going to ask.

                         LILLITH
          You were going to make the face.

                         RUBELLA
          The face is free.
          (beat)
          Does the table still do the weather.

                         LILLITH
          The table does Thursday's weather. On Tuesday.
          Which everybody keeps calling broken.
          (fitting the tube)
          It's not broken. It's early.
```

```
(Three seconds. Rubella looks down the corridor. Nothing is coming.
She checks anyway.)
```

```
                         RUBELLA
          Is it going to hold nine hundred volts.

                         LILLITH
          It's going to hold about eleven hundred for
          somewhere between forty seconds and never.

                         RUBELLA
                    (both hands on the rail now)
          Good. That's — 
          (she decides not to)
          That's a range.
```

### C — The Insult

```
INT. CORRIDOR — CONTINUOUS

She wires it in. The soldering iron ticks as it cools on the ladder
shelf. She reaches up and puts two fingers on the dark T, which is the
most tender gesture either of these women will make on camera this
season.

Then she insults it.
```

```
                         LILLITH
          You are the cheapest sign in this building and
          the stairwell is better looking than you are.

(Nothing.)

                         RUBELLA
          You have to do the whole thing.

                         LILLITH
          I know how to do the whole thing.
          (to the sign)
          And you have never once been where I needed you
          to be.

(The T flickers. Comes up dull. Holds.)

(Four seconds.)

                         RUBELLA
          That's not a repair.

                         LILLITH
          The neon sign only works if you insult it first.
          I have the data.

                         RUBELLA
          You have a pattern. Data has a control.

                         LILLITH
                    (already coming down the ladder)
          I said nice things to it in August for eleven
          days.

                         RUBELLA
                    (a long pause; genuinely caught)
          ...That's a control.
```

### D — The Reveal (Forty Seconds)

```
INT. CORRIDOR — CONTINUOUS

The T comes up to full.

And then the sign overdrives — eleven hundred volts into a fixture
rated for a quieter life — and the corridor goes red.

Not lit. Red. The carpet finds a pattern again. The brass numbers on
the doors are jewelry. The dying fluorescent overhead gives up
entirely, which improves it, and for forty seconds this hallway is a
different and considerably more expensive show.

The gated snare enters. The bass synth locks to it.

NEITHER OF THEM MOVES. This is important. They do not dance, they do
not pose, they do not walk toward camera in slow motion. They stand
where they were standing — one at the foot of a ladder with a fire
extinguisher by her boot, one three steps up with a soldering iron
cooling in her hand — and they let it be beautiful at them.

Play Verse 1 clean. No dialogue.
```

### E — Forty Seconds Was the Number

```
INT. CORRIDOR — CONTINUOUS

The pre-chorus begins.

On its second line, the 6BQ5 gives out — not a bang, not a shower of
sparks. A small, dignified, glassy TICK, and the red drains out of the
corridor from the far end toward camera, like water leaving a bath.

The music stops with it. Mid-phrase. Nothing rides out.

The dying fluorescent, having missed its moment, comes back on.

The sign reads:

                              E X I

Six seconds. Neither of them says anything. The carpet has no
pattern again.
```

```
                         RUBELLA
          Forty seconds.

                         LILLITH
          Forty-one.

                         RUBELLA
                    (picking up the extinguisher)
          I wasn't counting.

                         LILLITH
          Neither was I.
```

```
(Lillith comes down the ladder. Folds it. Leans it against the wall
under the sign, where it will stay, and where it has stayed before.

She picks up the pizza box.)
```

```
                         LILLITH (CONT'D)
          I can get a transformer. There's a guy in the
          annex who has four of them and doesn't know what
          they are.

                         RUBELLA
          It's a life-safety fixture. It says so. Up a
          ladder, laminated.

                         LILLITH
          It's not a fire hazard. It's ambient lighting
          with ambition.

                         RUBELLA
                    (starting down the corridor)
          Put that in the portal. Category: other.
```

```
(They walk the corridor together, away from camera, under a light that
is technically present. The pizza box between them. The extinguisher
in Rubella's other hand, still full.

At 6-C, Lillith stops.)

                         LILLITH
          Third Thursday.

                         RUBELLA
                    (not turning around, continuing to
                     her own door)
          Third Thursday.

(Doors. Two of them, not together.)

(Hold on the empty corridor and the sign that reads EXI.)

(Five seconds. Out.)
```
