STYLE:

Sincere roots dub reggae, one-drop rhythm, 72 BPM, A minor, about two and a half minutes. Deep round bass guitar carrying the song, rim-shot snare on the three, hi-hat skank, organ bubble, offbeat guitar chop drenched in spring reverb, and a melodica playing the melody between verses. Big tape-echo and spring-reverb sends used as an instrument, dub-style: drums and bass drop in and out, and the guitar chop throws long echoes into a concrete room. One female lead: dry, slightly raspy, calm, singing questions into an empty garage. Every line marked ECHO is the **same voice, quieter, through tape delay and spring reverb, singing only the one short word** in the lyric, on the same pitch as the end of her line, as if bouncing off concrete. The echo never sings more than that one word. Night-time, cavernous, warm low end, earnest, no parody, no toasting, no airhorns, no sirens. Use the Rubella voice.

NOTES:

**The device is the tactic, not the subject.** Rubella never sings about echoes or words.
She asks a garage where her car is. The echo is the dub delay, a sound the genre already
makes, and each echoed tail turns into a reply: *later* (don't take the escalator), *one*
(yes, you're alone), *meant* (it was meant to be), *trance* (you're in one), *low* (go down),
*mine* (it's yours), *on* (you left the headlights on).

**The joke:** she starts on level four, walks all the way down, and comes back up to level
four, where the car was the whole time. The only way she finds it is that the headlights
were left on. The last echo answers twice, fading.

**Callback:** *Pay machine clock says three forty-four*, sung in verse 1 and again,
identically, in verse 4: the 3:44 insert, twice, identical, from the callback bank. Nobody
comments on it. The garage clock didn't move while she walked four floors.

**The production rule:** each echo is one word only, same pitch as her line's last syllable,
through the delay. If MiniMax sings the whole line back, add "the echo repeats only the final
syllable" to the STYLE and regenerate.

**Do-not:** no car brands, no parking-company signage, no Pizza Guy (not his garage). Not the
wet open lot from *Empty Parking Lot Waltz*. This is indoors, concrete, and four floors.
