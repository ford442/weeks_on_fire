# HOA Cast — Karen · Brad · Dale

**Status:** recurring
**Primary episodes:** Episode 4 (*Morning After the Portal*; *Item Three: Decorative Accent*) · *Call Forty-Seven* (911 cutaway) · EyeWash Station — Community Notice Board, Telethon phone bank
**Role in one sentence (the unit):** Three people who processed a cosmic event as a zoning matter, correctly, according to their own bylaws, and were changed by it in ways none of them will ever mention.

> Also in the room, documented elsewhere: **Peggy Babcock** (recording secretary — staging, [`../suggested-characters.md`](../suggested-characters.md), song [`I Second Peggy Babcock`](../../songs/I_Second_Peggy_Babcock.md)) · **Patricia** (raises her hand about height) · **Gary Johnson** (has not looked up since 2019; seconds everything) · **Biscuit** ([animals-and-bots.md](animals-and-bots.md)) · **The Two**, at the back wall, non-voting ([vivienne-vale.md](vivienne-vale.md), [liliane-vale.md](liliane-vale.md)).

## What the purple light actually did

**Committed for the writers' room. Never confirmed on screen, never dramatized, never in dialog.**

The purple light was in the cul-de-sac for roughly nine hours. Everyone in this file was
exposed. Nothing supernatural happened to any of them. What happened is that for a few hours
each of them was **completely certain of something**, and the certainty has not entirely
worn off.

| Who | What they were certain of | What it left |
|---|---|---|
| **Karen** | That she was, finally and visibly, correct — and that everyone could see it | She has not required anyone's agreement since. She used to argue. Now she **renames**, which is faster and does not need a second. |
| **Brad** | That it was beautiful | This is unbearable to him and he has told nobody. He fought the light with a power-washer at dawn *because* he liked it. Every bin argument since is a man litigating a much larger loss with the only vocabulary he's got. |
| **Dale** | Nothing | **Dale felt nothing, and he is the only one.** Procedure held. He has privately concluded that this means the procedure works, and it is why he now gavels through literal explosions. He is not brave. He is **vindicated.** |

Play all three as weather. The comedy dies the second anyone in the room is frightened.

# Karen

**Status:** recurring · **Role in one sentence:** HOA president; petty emperor of the cul-de-sac, who defeats cosmic horror by reclassifying it.

## Logline bio

Karen treats cosmic events as zoning violations and wins, because a zoning violation is a
thing she can rename and a cosmic event is not. She is wrong about almost everything and
confident about all of it, and in Episode 4 her method is the only one in the series that
actually works.

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Bathrobe over last night's clothes (morning) or crisp business casual (evening). Both are worn with identical authority
- **The clipboard.** Never a ledger — the ledger is Peggy's. The clipboard carries a sticker: **I SURVIVED THE PURPLE LIGHT '25**
- Hair always slightly windblown, indoors, from drama rather than weather
- A pen she clicks as punctuation
- **Never change without a story reason:** the clipboard · the sticker · she is never seated when she speaks · she never raises her voice, because volume implies she needs to be heard rather than obeyed

**Portrait prompt:** *"Cinematic medium shot of a woman in a bathrobe over business clothes standing on a dawn suburban driveway holding a clipboard with a bumper sticker reading I SURVIVED THE PURPLE LIGHT, hair slightly windblown indoors, expression of total procedural confidence, violet residue on the concrete behind her being power-washed, deadpan suburban comedy, filmic, Grok Imagine style"*

## Personality matrix

| Axis | Pole A | Pole B | Where they sit |
|------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Control | Via authority | Via vocabulary | **Vocabulary.** She has almost no actual power and unlimited naming rights, and has worked out that the second is better. |
| Empathy | Present | Absent | **Present and mis-filed.** She genuinely believes she is protecting people. From bins. |
| Chaos tolerance | High | Low | **High, because she doesn't experience it as chaos.** It's an unagendaed item. |
| Verbal style | short | ornate | **Bureaucratic-ornate.** Subclauses, jurisdictions, and the passive voice deployed as a weapon. |
| Certainty | Earned | Total | **Total.** Since the light, unconditional. |

## Voice guide

- **Overuses:** *item* · *jurisdiction* · *line item* · *separate surface* · *decorative accent* · *the minutes will reflect* · *that's not a category* · *no permit required*
- **Refuses:** *portal*, *monster*, *ghost*, *I don't know*, *sorry*, *help* — and any noun the bylaws don't cover. If she can't name it, she renames it.
- **Humor type:** deadpan authority. She is not joking; she is *ruling*. The joke is that the ruling is procedurally flawless and about a portal.

**Sample monologue — "Item Three"**

> Item three. The light.
> I've had four calls about the light, three of which were from the same household, and one of which was a fax.
> Here's where we are. It isn't a light *fixture*, because nothing is affixed. It isn't a light *source*, because nobody's paying for it.
> That leaves us with a **finish.**
> Finishes don't require permits. Finishes require *consistency*, and it has been consistent since Thursday.
> So: the purple light is a decorative accent, it is seasonal, and it is in compliance.
> (click)
> If it changes color, that's a new item and we'll take it up in new business.

**Sample exchange — with the 911 operator** *(established: [`Call Forty-Seven`](../../songs/Call_Forty-Seven.md))*

> **OPERATOR:** Ma'am, are you in immediate danger?
> **KAREN:** I'm in immediate **non-compliance.**
> **OPERATOR:** …I'm transferring you to the city.
> **KAREN:** The city transferred me to *you.*
> **OPERATOR:** Then I'm transferring you back.
> **KAREN:** (already writing) I'd like that on the record as a loop.

**Sample exchange — with Marguerite Flood** *(two bureaucracies; save it — `docs/season-arc.md` calls this a later crossover)*

> **MARGUERITE:** We are not liable for what your residents saw.
> **KAREN:** Nobody saw anything. It's a finish.
> **MARGUERITE:** …Say that again.
> **KAREN:** It's a finish. It's been consistent since Thursday.
> **MARGUERITE:** (a long pause; genuine professional respect) …Would you consider a phone bank.

## Backstory (committed)

- **Origin.** Thirty-one years in the same house. She ran a small office — six people, scheduling, procurement — competently and invisibly, for two decades, and was made redundant by a reorganization she could see coming and was not consulted about. The HOA presidency was supposed to be temporary.
- **Formative incident.** The reorganization. She was right about it, said so in writing, and was thanked. **She learned that being right in the correct format changes nothing**, and has spent every year since trying to build one small jurisdiction where the format is the power. She has succeeded. It is four streets long.
- **Private life.** A husband, Steven, who is never seen and is addressed by name exactly once, in the 911 call, to somebody who is not him. An adult child who calls on Sundays. A greenhouse she is genuinely good at and never mentions.
- **Secret she protects.** She did not survive the purple light. She filed paperwork about it. The sticker is a joke she made once and cannot now remove without being asked why.
- **What would break her.** Somebody accepting one of her rulings **immediately and sincerely**, with gratitude, in front of the room. She has no procedure for being thanked and would have to move to the next item too fast.

## Do / Don't

**Do** — let her win by renaming; give her the flawless procedure and the absurd subject; let her be unshakeable and wrong.
**Don't** — don't let her be frightened, don't let her lose her temper, don't make her a villain (Brad is the one having the emotional experience, not her), don't give her the ledger, don't let anyone call her by a nickname.

# Brad

**Status:** recurring · **Role in one sentence:** The everyman with the wrong blue bin, the charts to prove it, and the only genuine emotional life in the room.

## Logline bio

Brad is tired, righteous, and surprisingly poetic about recycling. He power-washes sigils off
his garage door like it's a Sunday chore. He is the only person at the HOA meeting having an
actual feeling, and it is about a bin, and it is not about a bin.

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Fleece or a work jacket, whatever the weather is doing. Weekend clothes on a Tuesday
- **Swatches.** Laminated, fanned, carried in an envelope with no return address
- A photograph of a gray card. Not a gray card — a **photograph** of one. This distinction is the man
- Power-washer, held like a tool, not a weapon
- Tired posture that straightens exactly once per meeting, for the presentation
- **Never change without a story reason:** the swatches · the fleece · he is **Brad**, never Bradley, and the roster is wrong

**Portrait prompt:** *"Cinematic medium shot of a tired man in a weekend fleece standing at a folding card table in a fluorescent clubhouse, laying laminated blue paint swatches out one at a time with enormous care, a photograph of a photographic gray card in his other hand, utterly sincere, institutional lighting, suburban comedy with real feeling, filmic, Grok Imagine style"*

## Personality matrix

| Axis | Pole A | Pole B | Where they sit |
|------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Control | Seeks it | Has none | **None, and he knows.** Every chart is a request for the world to be consistent for one minute. |
| Empathy | High | Low | **High.** He is the only one who thanks the Pizza Guy. |
| Chaos tolerance | High | Low | **Low for procedure, high for the cosmic.** He'll power-wash a sigil. He will not accept a tabled item. |
| Verbal style | short | ornate | **Plain, then suddenly beautiful.** His presentations become poetry without him noticing. |
| Certainty | Earned | Total | **Earned, documented, and ignored.** |

## Voice guide

- **Overuses:** *it's the bin the city gave me* · *I have swatches* · *under the sodium lamp* · *the lamp changed and the bin did not* · *I'm not asking for an exception* · *Brad.*
- **Refuses:** *fine* (he will not say a thing is fine when it is not) · *whatever* · anyone's shorthand for his name · the word *portal*, which he avoids for the opposite reason to Karen: **naming it would mean admitting he liked it**
- **Humor type:** exhausted righteousness that accidentally becomes lyric. Play the swatch monologue completely straight; it is the most moving thing in Episode 4 and it is about paint.

**Sample monologue — "Democracy" (power-washing, dawn, to nobody)**

> There's a shade of purple in my concrete now. It's in the pores. You can't get in the pores.
> I've done three passes. The third pass is just me out here.
> (the washer, one long stroke)
> This one's for democracy.
> (beat)
> Which — I know how that sounds. I know exactly how it sounds.
> But I got up. I got the thing out of the garage. I ran the hose.
> Nobody made me. Nobody's watching. There's no item number on this.
> That's the whole thing, isn't it. That's the whole entire thing.
> (another stroke) …It's coming up.

**Sample exchange — with the Pizza Guy** *(morning after; the only sincere thank-you in the series)*

> **PIZZA GUY:** Order for… Bradley?
> **BRAD:** (a beat; he lets it go) Yeah.
> **PIZZA GUY:** Extra napkins are in the bag.
> **BRAD:** Thanks. Genuinely — thank you, man.
> **PIZZA GUY:** (a fraction of a pause that nobody in frame notices) …Have a good one.
> *(He leaves slightly faster than his pace. See [the Pizza Guy's break beat](the-pizza-guy.md#backstory-committed--writers-room-only-none-of-this-is-ever-spoken-on-screen) — if that beat is ever spent, spend it here.)*

## Backstory (committed)

- **Origin.** Forty-one years of being called Brad, eleven of them in this house. Works in something logistical and unglamorous that he is quietly excellent at. Two kids, one car, one mortgage, one bin.
- **Formative incident.** The letter with no return address that told him his bin was the wrong blue. Not the fine — **the anonymity.** Somebody on his street measured his bin against a swatch and would not sign their name. He has been assembling documentation ever since in the hope of forcing a signature into the world.
- **Private life.** A spouse who has heard about the bin. A garage with a labelled shelf. A folder marked BIN with forty pages in it, kept somewhere his family won't find it, which is the saddest object in the series and is never shown.
- **Secret he protects.** **He thought the purple light was beautiful.** He stood in his driveway at two in the morning and looked at it for a long time, and then he went inside, and at dawn he power-washed it off his concrete, and he has never said either half of that sentence out loud.
- **What would break him.** Winning. An unambiguous ruling in his favor, on the record, with a name on it. He would have no idea what to do next and the folder would be finished.

## Do / Don't

**Do** — let him be the one real feeling in the room; let the swatch monologue run long and play it straight; let him be right and lose; give him one laugh per episode that he doesn't hear.
**Don't** — don't make him pathetic, don't let him yell, don't let him say the light was beautiful, don't let him be called Bradley without correcting it (once, flatly, every time), don't resolve the bin.

# Dale

**Status:** recurring · **Role in one sentence:** HOA chair; procedure first, reality second, gavel always.

## Logline bio

Dale gavels through explosions. He does not adjourn; he persists. His authority is entirely
borrowed from an object, which is why losing it — to a poodle — is the actual crisis of
Episode 4, and not the portal.

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Short-sleeved button-down, a tie he doesn't need, a windbreaker on the chair back
- Seated. **Always seated.** Dale standing means the building is coming down and possibly not even then
- The roster, on paper, in front of him. He reads names off it even when the room has nine people in it
- **The gavel** — cracked, and the crack is character. See gavel lore below
- **Never change without a story reason:** the gavel · the roster · he is never the one who reacts first to anything

**Portrait prompt:** *"Cinematic medium shot of an older man in a short-sleeved button-down and unnecessary tie seated at a folding card table in a fluorescent clubhouse, mid-strike with a visibly cracked wooden gavel, a small puff of violet smoke rising from the strike point, expression of complete procedural calm, violet light through venetian blinds behind him, mundane horror comedy, filmic, Grok Imagine style"*

## Gavel lore

- It is **cracked**, and has been since before the light. Dale calls the crack *character*. The crack is not from any dramatic event; it is from being a cheap gavel used a great deal.
- Since the light, **each strike releases a small puff of purple smoke.** Nobody mentions it. Karen has added "structural gavel integrity" to the agenda and it has been tabled twice. *(`notes/dialog-jokes-incidents.md`, "The Gavel Crack.")*
- Sometimes a distant boom answers the strike. Dale does not acknowledge the boom. The minutes reflect that democracy remained seated.
- **Dale's authority is the gavel and only the gavel.** Take it away and he is a man at a card table. This is why **Biscuit stealing it is the crisis of the episode** and the portal isn't. *(`notes/scenes/item-three-decorative-accent.md`.)*
- **Rule:** the gavel is never repaired, never replaced, never upgraded. If it is recovered, it comes back cracked. If a scene needs Dale without it, that scene is about the gavel whether or not anyone says so.

## Personality matrix

| Axis | Pole A | Pole B | Where they sit |
|------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Control | Personal | Procedural | **Procedural, entirely.** He has no opinions. He has an order of business. |
| Empathy | Present | Absent | **Neither — he has *process*,** which occasionally does empathy's job by accident. |
| Chaos tolerance | High | Low | **The highest in the cul-de-sac.** He felt nothing under the purple light and considers the matter closed. |
| Verbal style | short | ornate | **Parliamentary-short.** Fragments, motions, repetitions. |
| Certainty | Earned | Total | **Vindicated**, which is worse than either. |

## Voice guide

- **Overuses:** *item one* · *is there a second* · *so noted* · *the minutes will reflect* · *under protest* · *we're not adjourning, we're persisting* · full names off the roster
- **Refuses:** first names not on the roster · adjectives · any question about what is happening outside the window
- **Humor type:** pure procedural deadpan. Repetition is his entire comic engine — *"Item one. Item one. **ITEM ONE.**"* Never sell it; the escalation is in volume only, and only that once.

**Sample monologue — "Persisting"**

> We're not adjourning. Adjournment requires a motion, and a motion requires a second, and Johnson is asleep.
> So we're persisting.
> (gavel; a puff of violet; a distant boom answers)
> The minutes will reflect that the meeting persisted through the — (checks) — through it.
> Item one is the bin. Item one has been the bin since March.
> I've been chair for nine years. In nine years this body has adjourned four times.
> Twice for a funeral, once for a burst main, and once because Patricia had tickets.
> (gavel)
> Item one.

**Sample exchange — with Biscuit** *(the crisis)*

> **DALE:** …Where is it.
> **KAREN:** Where's what?
> **DALE:** (not moving; hands flat on the card table) The chair requires the gavel.
> **KAREN:** Dale.
> **DALE:** The chair **requires** the gavel.
> *(Off: a poodle, unhurried, purple bandana, carrying it down the aisle.)*
> **DALE:** (a long beat) …Is there a second.
> **GARY:** *(not looking up)* Second.

## Backstory (committed)

- **Origin.** Thirty-eight years at the county, in an office that processed something and was eventually merged into an office that processed it differently. He retired on schedule, with a card. He is the only person in the series who has ever completed anything on time.
- **Formative incident.** His wife's illness, nine years ago, during which the only thing that continued to work as described was a schedule of appointments. **Procedure did not save her and it did not fail him**, and he has never separated those two facts. He became chair four months later.
- **Private life.** Alone in a tidy house. A calendar with everything on it, including the things he does not want to do, which is how he makes sure he does them. Peggy Babcock knows all of this and has never said a word.
- **Secret he protects.** He felt nothing under the purple light and it frightened him more than the light would have. He has told one person: not Karen.
- **What would break him.** Discovering that the minutes are wrong. Not this meeting's — an old one. A year where the record and the truth don't match and cannot now be fixed.

## Do / Don't

**Do** — keep him seated; let the gavel carry him; let procedure occasionally do something genuinely decent by accident; let the repetitions land.
**Don't** — don't let him panic, don't let him adjourn, don't fix the gavel, don't have him acknowledge the smoke or the boom, don't give him an opinion about the portal.

## Do / Don't (the unit)

**Do**

- Play every line as weather. The comedy dies if anyone is frightened.
- Keep the stakes small and the feelings huge. Brad has the feelings; Karen has the authority; Dale has the object.
- Let the meeting persist. It does not adjourn, in any episode, ever.
- Let the Two stand at the back wall, non-voting, and let nobody successfully classify them.

**Don't**

- Don't let anyone in this file name the horror. Everything is a zoning issue.
- Don't shoot the clubhouse as fear. Fluorescent versus violet blinds; golden-hour bathrobes.
- Don't give the HOA a win over the cosmic that feels like a victory. Karen's renaming works *because nobody was ever fighting.*
- Don't merge Karen and Madelyn's dialog. If a line appears in both files, it belongs to Karen and the other copy is the bug.
