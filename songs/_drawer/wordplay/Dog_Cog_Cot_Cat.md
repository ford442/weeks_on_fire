STYLE:

Gentle close-harmony lullaby in 6/8 at 66 BPM, D major, about two minutes. Soft fingerpicked ukulele, a felt-muted upright piano playing two-note answers, a music-box glockenspiel doubling the last word of every verse, and a very quiet brushed snare on beat one only. A slow clock tick (wooden woodblock) runs under the first two verses and **stops dead at the end of verse two** and never comes back. Lead vocal: dry, slightly raspy, low and unhurried, singing a lullaby as if reading a checklist, warm underneath. Second voice: bright but hushed, harmony on the "hush" lines only. The final word is followed by one small cat meow (sung by the second voice as a single soft note, not a sound effect), then a short silence and one low piano note. Intimate, close-mic, soft room, tape-warm. No strings, no pad swell, no parody. Use Rubella and Lillith voices.

LYRICS:

[Verse 1]
RUBELLA: The porch light's low, the street is still
The wind went home across the hill
The door is locked, the mat is flat
Lie down now, sleepy dog

[Chorus]
RUBELLA: Hush now
LILLITH: Hush now
BOTH: Hush the house to sleep

[Verse 2]
RUBELLA: The kitchen clock is ticking slow
It's had a long day, it should know
Its hands are tired, it's due a stop
Rest now, little cog

[Chorus]
RUBELLA: Hush now
LILLITH: Hush now
BOTH: Hush the house to sleep

[Verse 3]
RUBELLA: The pillow's plumped, the sheet is turned
The lamp's put out, the lesson learned
I climb in last, I always do
Hold me now, my cot

[Chorus]
RUBELLA: Hush now
LILLITH: Hush now
BOTH: Hush the house to sleep

[Verse 4]
RUBELLA: The dog's asleep, the clock's asleep
The cot is warm, the dark is deep
One pair of eyes is open wide
Good night, cat

[Outro]
LILLITH: Mew.

NOTES:

**The device is the tactic, not the subject.** The song is a bedtime routine. The ladder
is hidden in the verse endings: *dog, cog, cot, cat*. One letter changes per verse, and the
house goes from the first thing she puts to sleep to the one thing she can't.

**The joke builds kindly.** Verse 2 puts the *clock* to sleep, and the woodblock tick in
the arrangement actually stops. Verse 4 is the wrong third: everything is asleep except the
cat. The last sound is the cat, and the song ends with someone awake.

**Flaw (written in):** the clock tick stops at the end of verse 2 and never comes back.
Time in the house has gone to bed too.

**Do-not:** not the Black Cat (she is a town witness, never cute, never in a lullaby). No
named dog (not Biscuit). The cat's "mew" is sung, not a sample.
