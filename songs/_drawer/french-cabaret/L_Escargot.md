# L'Escargot

**Genre collision:** 1930s Paris chanson × slow 12/8 blues (bottleneck slide guitar, a shuffle on brushes, a lazy upright bass, a barroom piano). The slide guitar is the snail's trail.

**Visual concept:** It's July on a café terrace. Rubella sits down and orders: "One coffee, please." The waiter is a snail the size of a cat, in a tiny black waistcoat and bow tie, with a silver tray balanced on his shell and one steaming coffee cup on it. He sets off from the café door toward her table, ten metres away. The song takes the seasons with it: autumn leaves bury the table, snow falls on Rubella twice, a sapling next to her chair grows into a tree with blossom, and the café is repainted. Lillith plays slide guitar at the next table the whole time. On the last chorus the snail arrives, and the coffee is still steaming. Rubella drinks it and asks for the bill, and the snail slowly turns round.

**Laugh (kid words):** *The waiter is a snail, and by the time her coffee arrives two winters have gone by, and then she asks for the bill.*

**BPM:** 66 in a slow 12/8 shuffle, E minor (chorus in G)

**Target length:** ~2:30 · 13 segments
