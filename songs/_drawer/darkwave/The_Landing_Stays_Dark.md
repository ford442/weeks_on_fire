STYLE:

Darkwave, 112 BPM, D minor, about two minutes ten. Cold, not neon. Drum machine with a dry kick and a thin snare, no gated stadium reverb, no 155 acid, no 909 warehouse. Chorus bass guitar, picked, one line, a little chorus pedal, locked to the kick. One string machine, narrow, far back, holding D minor and never opening into a pad wash. A small arpeggio on a cold digital synth, short decay, no portamento show. No saxophone, no gated snare, no magenta lead, no outrun horns, no guitar solo. Lillith sings close and bright, almost on the mic, no belting, no vibrato show. Rubella answers one line per chorus, drier, a step under, and does not take a verse. Structural flaw: the chorus bass enters one bar before the chorus, every time, and stays early. The voices do not chase it. They come in on the bar they were given. Last chorus the bass is still early, then the machine stops and the bass plays one extra bar alone into a dark landing. No Kenji. No product name. No EXIT sign. Mood: a stair that has already decided.

NOTES:

Why it is not the neighbors:
- **Not Neon Exit Sign.** That song owns synthwave, mascara, the EXIT tube, magenta, walking away from the fire. This landing has a practical bulb and the bulb does not come on. No skyline. No heels-as-doctrine.
- **Not Night Side.** Not a 122–136 headphone grid. Not G Train Fluorescent. A song, not a bed for generating.
- **Not Lightning Strike.** No acid, no bolt, no moon receipt.
- **Not Car Twelve.** Claude's joke-metal card owns the elevator chant. Twelve is a painted-over number here, said once.
- **Not Quiet on the Landing.** That card is accordion-pop, an intercom poem. This is the darkwave. Do not mash them.
- **Not Christina's hill.** No snow, no gilt, no aria.

Flaw: chorus bass is one bar early on every chorus and is never corrected. Runtime about 2:10.
