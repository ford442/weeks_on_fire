STYLE:

Argentine tango duet, 116 BPM, between Lillith and a freight elevator.
Bandoneón, one nylon guitar, upright bass, no drums — heel-stomp on the car
floor for percussion. Lillith sings words, urgent and conspiratorial, the way
you flatter a machine you need something from. The elevator sings **only floor
numbers**, through a heavily vocoded low tenor with the building's PA
bandwidth (250 Hz–3.5 kHz, nothing outside it). It is a real duet — the
elevator has melody, phrasing, and better intonation than she does.
**Structural flaw:** every verse drops a beat as the car descends — 5/4, then
4/4, then 3/4, then 2/4 — and neither singer adjusts their phrasing, so by the
last section Lillith is arriving late to a bar that has already left. Do not
correct it. The door chime at the end is a semitone above the final chord and
is the last sound in the mix. Runtime ~2:00–2:20. No belting. No wink.


LYRICS:

[Intro — bandoneón alone, doors closing, 5/4]
(doors close)
ELEVATOR: Twelve.
LILLITH: Twelve. Good. We agree on twelve.

[Verse 1 — 5/4]
LILLITH:
I need the lobby and you need to be needed
That's a deal, that's the whole deal, that's the shape of it
I have said kind things about you to the boiler
I have never once called you a lift
ELEVATOR: Eleven. Eleven. Nine.
LILLITH: Ten. There's a ten. Ten exists.
ELEVATOR: Nine.

[Chorus 1 — 5/4]
LILLITH:
Take me down, take me down, take me anywhere but back
I'll say the building loves you — I will lie into the shaft
Whatever floor you're dreaming of, I'll pretend I chose it too
Take me down
ELEVATOR: Nine.
LILLITH: We're negotiating.
ELEVATOR: Nine.

[Verse 2 — 4/4, nobody mentions the missing beat]
LILLITH:
I fixed the neon and I fixed the ping-pong table
The table tells the weather now — I could do that for you
One evening, one screwdriver, one questionable capacitor
And you could tell them anything you want
ELEVATOR: Seven.
LILLITH: Seven's fine. Seven's progress.
ELEVATOR: Seven. Seven. Seven.

[Bridge — 3/4, bandoneón only, both voices quiet]
LILLITH:
What if I stopped asking
What if I just rode
Some nights I think you're not refusing
Some nights I think you're just afraid of the lobby too
ELEVATOR: Four.
LILLITH: ...Four.
ELEVATOR: Four.
LILLITH: Okay. Okay. Look at that.

[Final Chorus — 2/4, Lillith consistently a beat behind]
LILLITH:
Take me down, take me down, I'll say it was your idea
I'll tell Madelyn you were compliant, I'll tell the board you're new
I'll sign your inspection sticker with a name that isn't mine
Take me down
ELEVATOR: Two.
LILLITH: Two —
ELEVATOR: Two.
LILLITH: — I'll take two.
ELEVATOR: One.
LILLITH: You beautiful machine.

[Outro]
(doors open)
ELEVATOR: Thirteen.
LILLITH: There's no thirteen.
(door chime, a semitone sharp)
(doors close)


NOTES:

## What the song is
Lillith needs to get to the lobby. The elevator has terms. Two minutes of
genuine tango courtship in which one party can only say numbers and is
nonetheless the more persuasive negotiator. She wins — she reaches the lobby —
and then it offers her a floor that does not exist, which is either a joke, a
counteroffer, or an invitation. The song does not adjudicate. Neither does the
building.

Callback: Rubella's line, "The elevator just tried to negotiate. I told it I
already pay rent." That's the other tenant's approach. This is Lillith's.

## Craft (keep this off-mic)
The elevator's part must be **written as melody first** and only then assigned
numbers — if it reads as sound design instead of a vocal, the duet collapses
into a gag. Keep it inside PA bandwidth so it sits in a different frequency
room than Lillith, and let it be the more in-tune of the two.

The shrinking meter is the whole architecture: the descent is literally taking
beats out of the song. Lillith never adapts, so the last section is a woman
still phrasing a five-beat argument into a two-beat bar. Do not fix it in the
edit, and do not have her land the final line neatly.

## Production notes
- **Episode placement:** Building cast / any corridor-to-lobby transit. Sibling
  to *After Is a Floor* (Lillith whispering destinations as manners in a small
  elevator) — that one is the **passenger's** interior; this is the
  **negotiation**. Do not stage them in the same car in the same episode.
- **Voice:** Lillith bright and conspiratorial, with energy spikes; elevator
  vocoded low tenor, PA-bandlimited, numbers only. No other lyrics for the
  machine, ever — not "yes," not "no," not "thirteen" spoken plainly.
- **Structural flaw:** meter loses a beat per section (5/4 → 4/4 → 3/4 → 2/4);
  Lillith's phrasing does not compensate; final chime a semitone sharp.
- **Runtime target:** 2:00–2:20.
