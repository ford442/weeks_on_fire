## Video concept

Stairwell landing between floors 5 and 6, one flickering fluorescent, terrazzo with a
dinner-plate puddle in the low spot by the handrail. The air in the stairwell is clear.
Inside the plate only, a private Thursday rain is falling, a few drops, and stopping at
the rim. A white goose in a red lifeguard visor stands on a wooden stool that is too tall
for the puddle, plastic whistle in its beak. Rubella is already in the plate in evening
shoes, laundry basket held level at chest height, face of pure resignation, water not
climbing past the sole. Lillith stands on the dry side with safety glasses pushed up,
looking at the leak the way she looks at something she is not going to price. The goose
blows the whistle once, short. Nothing official happens. It blows it again. On the third
blast the landing **becomes** a pool: lane rope tied to the handrail, water one inch deep
across the whole floor, and forty geese **appear** in forty folding deck chairs crammed
on the steps. The chairs creak together. They do not clap. Rubella is mid-pool and does
not step out. Lillith does not offer a repair. A third whistle, longer, and the music is
gone. Then the water **reverses**: one sheet climbs the stairwell and leaves, geese with
it, except the first goose, who holds out a towel the size of a postage stamp at chest
height. Rubella dabs one shoe. The plate is a plate again. The Thursday rain is still
only in it.

`laugh:` The puddle has a lifeguard, and then the whole stairwell is a pool one inch deep.
`drop:` **Multiplies.** On the downbeat at 0:08 one goose becomes forty, and the plate
spreads to a one-inch pool across the landing. Second picture change at 0:31: the water
**reverses** up the stairs and the geese leave with it.
`end reaction:` Rubella, whispered, "Dry." (spent once; Lillith gets none).
`do-not:` put them on the roof or in 12-A; let Lillith price, patch, or mention the leak;
let anyone clap; give the goose a speaking part or a look to camera; put readable text on
the visor or the lane rope; mix in HOA, Glamora, or the cartoon water park.
`promote?:` no.

## Video use map

- **Target length:** 34s. Clip A (15s, 0:00–0:15) plate, two short whistles, drop at 0:08.
  Clip B (12s, 0:15–0:27) forty geese, third whistle kills the bed. Clip C (7s, 0:27–0:34)
  reverse and the stamp towel. Chain: last frame of A is the start still of B; last frame
  of B (chairs mid-creak, water flat) is the start still of C.
- **Pattern:** two seconds of picture-only, then a **continuous municipal jingle** that
  ducks under short whistles and **hard-cuts on the third whistle**. Chorus sits on the
  hold (the geese do not march; they sit). Verse sits on the small action (lane rope
  tugged once by a wing, basket stays level). After the cut, picture sound owns the
  landing until a low-passed scratch-back for the reverse.
- **Breaks:** (1) music absent 0:00–0:02 so wind, private rain, and the one-inch slap read;
  (2) duck −18 dB for 0.4s at 0:06.2 under whistle one; (3) duck −18 dB for 0.5s at 0:16.0
  under whistle two; (4) **hard stop at 0:22.0**, zero tail, for whistle three, the chair
  creak, and the flat water — speech is not in this gap; (5) scratch-back at 0:30, then
  duck out at 0:33 so the towel flick and "Dry." sit **instead of** the last beat.
- **Mouths:** Rubella and Lillith **off** the whole packet. The geese have no speech. The
  whistle is a beak prop, not a sung note. "Dry." is a close whisper over the back of
  Rubella's head, not lip-synced.

## Diegetic sound list

1. **Stairwell wind + ballast hum** 0:00–0:08, and again under the silence at 0:22–0:30.
   Does not cut the music; it is the room.
2. **Private rain on a dinner plate** 0:00–0:08 and again after 0:32, only as wide as the
   plate. Soft, close, a few drops. Stays under the jingle until the first duck.
3. **One-inch water slap** on terrazzo, small and regular, from 0:08 until the reverse.
   Sits under the chorus. Does not cut.
4. **Plastic lifeguard whistle, three takes.** Short at 0:06.2 (ducks the bed). Short at
   0:16.0 (ducks the bed). Long at 0:22.0, about 1.2s, dry, no gym reverb. **The third
   whistle cuts the music dead.**
5. **Forty deck chairs creaking once, together,** at 0:22.6, in the silence. No applause
   after. Cuts nothing; it lives in the gap the whistle opened.
6. **Speech:** one off-screen whisper, Rubella, 0:33.4, "Dry." Sits **instead of** the
   last beat. No other words. The goose does not speak.

## Minimax STYLE (paste-ready)

```
Municipal pool jingle, played completely straight, 120 BPM in F major, thirty-two seconds, sixteen bars. Genre: short whistle-pop interrupt, a recreation-department PA stuck on a cheerful organ loop, not cabaret, not rock, not a full song.
Farfisa organ plays a four-chord loop (F, C, D minor, B-flat) that never adds a fifth chord. Woodblock on the "and" of four every single bar, never corrected onto the downbeat. Soft four-on-the-floor kick, no hi-hat, no snare, no clap. Bass is one electric-piano note per bar, close and dry.
Bright female lead, quick and a little pleased with the rule, sings the lines on the beat, short notes, no vibrato, no melisma, no belt. A lower dry raspy flat female voice answers only the last word of each line, a half-beat late, like a sigh that still lands. Both close-mic, narrow, as if the speaker is a hallway PA with the grille half blocked.
Intro: organ and late woodblock only, two bars, no vocal. Chorus lands on bar four all at once and holds. Verse: organ drops an octave, kick stays, vocals go speech-sung and a little faster. Structural flaw held the whole track: the woodblock is late and nobody moves it.
Hard unison organ stab at the end of bar ten, clean, no tail, so an edit can cut the word off. Bars fifteen and sixteen: organ walks down one step and stops on a single bare "inch," no button, no reverb bloom.
Dry close mix, painted-cinderblock slap, dark top end, tape hiss, no synth pad, no guitar, no brass, no audience, no whistle sample inside the track. The whistle belongs to the picture.
```

## Board note

Whistle Twice is a 34-second building-stairwell gag: one goose lifeguards a dinner-plate
puddle, the third whistle fills the landing with forty geese and one inch of water, and
the water leaves upward. It stays in one world (the landing between 5 and 6). The roof
and unit 12-A stay untouched, so the "no" about the open ceiling is not reopened. The
chairs creak and do not clap; the Thursday rain stays inside the plate; the basket and
the tiny towel stay at chest height. Not a replacement for *The Roof Knows How to Wait*,
*Negotiating with the Elevator*, or *Two Left*. `promote?: no`; harden first.
