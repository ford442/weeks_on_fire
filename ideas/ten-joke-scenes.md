Ten joke animations and scenes, 6–30s each, all in the silly register: a kid can say
every laugh back. **Seven of the ten feature Lillith and/or Rubella.** Two have no people at
all, and one is an animal short with the Pizza Guy stuck in traffic. Every card has one
world, one visible change on the drop, and the calmest faces in the loudest frame.

## Clip math

Grok Imagine clips come in **6, 10 and 15s**. Every shot names its clip length. Multi-clip
cards chain: the **last frame of clip N is the start still of clip N+1**, so only the first
start still needs writing from scratch. ~30s cards are three 10s clips with the drop on beat
three.

**Default still style** (append to every photoreal still): *photoreal cinematic still,
2.39:1, no real actor names, 35mm, fine grain, Grok Imagine style.*
**Cartoon style** (#8): *2D hand-drawn cel animation frame in early-1990s television cartoon
style, NOT photoreal, NOT 3D, 4:3, thick ink outlines, flat saturated cel colours, painted
gouache background, huge readable expressions, Grok Imagine style.*
**Rubella** = a glamorous, exhausted woman with long dark hair and blood-red lipstick in a
modern black lace evening dress. **Lillith** = a glamorous woman in black lace with flawless,
never-smudged mascara and a bright, conspiratorial half-smile.

### 1. `balloon-rubella` — The Balloon Animals Come Alive

- **Laugh:** *Her balloon animals come alive, and the last one she makes is a balloon Rubella.*
- **Medium / world / length:** photoreal · ladies' building unit, living room · ~30s (10 + 10 + 10)
- **Engine:** rule of three with a wrong third.
- **Beats:**
  1. Lillith twists a long pink balloon into a dog. She sets it down. It shakes itself and
     trots off across the rug, squeaking.
  2. She twists a yellow giraffe. It stands, ducks under the ceiling lamp, and nibbles the
     houseplant. Rubella on the sofa doesn't look up from her book.
  3. She twists something black and long. On the **drop (~24s)** it **appears**: a life-size
     balloon Rubella, black balloon dress, red balloon lips, sitting down on the sofa beside
     the real one, with the same exact slump.
- **End reaction:** both Rubellas sigh at the same moment. The balloon one slowly deflates
  into the cushions. The real one turns a page.
- **Clips:**
  1. **10s.** *Start still:* cosy apartment living room at night, warm lamp light, Lillith
     kneeling on a rug with a bag of long party balloons, mid-twist on a pink balloon dog,
     Rubella on a sofa behind her reading, mismatched kitchen chairs at the edge of frame,
     50mm, f/2.8. *Motion:* she finishes the dog and sets it down; it shakes, comes alive and
     trots off squeaking.
  2. **10s.** *Motion:* a yellow balloon giraffe comes alive, ducks under the ceiling lamp and
     nibbles a houseplant leaf. The balloon dog sniffs it.
  3. **10s.** *Motion:* she twists a long black balloon; on the drop it becomes a life-size
     balloon woman with long black balloon hair and red balloon lips, who sits down beside
     Rubella in the same slumped pose. Both sigh. The balloon one deflates.
- **Music feel:** a bouncy tuba-and-clarinet oompah that gets one instrument bigger per animal.
  On the drop, the band stops and a slide whistle goes down with the deflate.
- **Do-not:** no popping animals, no scary balloon face, no text. Real Rubella never looks at
  her balloon twin until the shared sigh.

### 2. `one-yawn` — One Yawn Puts the Whole World to Bed

- **Laugh:** *Rubella yawns once, and it's so catching the sun yawns and goes to bed too.*
- **Medium / world / length:** photoreal · the building's front stoop and street, late
  afternoon · ~26s (10 + 10 + 6)
- **Engine:** things that keep happening.
- **Neighbour:** `intercom-yawn` (`possible-content-drawer.md`) is one dry yawn on an
  intercom. This one is contagious and goes worldwide.
- **Beats:**
  1. Rubella on the stoop with her World's Okayest Tenant mug yawns, long and dry. Lillith
     beside her catches it and yawns. A dog walking past yawns. Its owner (back to camera)
     yawns.
  2. It spreads down the street: pigeons on a wire yawn in a row, a cat in a window, a
     goldfish in a bowl, the bus driver, then the whole bus of passengers through the windows,
     faceless and in silhouette.
  3. **Drop (~20s), transforms:** the low sun over the rooftops gives one enormous slow yawn
     (glow stretching like a mouth-shaped flare), sinks below the horizon in two seconds, and
     it's night. Streetlights blink on.
- **End reaction:** in the sudden dark, everyone on the street has nodded off: Lillith on
  Rubella's shoulder, the dog curled up, the pigeons with heads tucked. Rubella, wide awake,
  sips from the mug.
- **Clips:**
  1. **10s.** *Start still:* brick apartment building stoop at golden hour, Rubella seated
     with a white mug, Lillith beside her, a quiet city street, a man walking a shaggy dog
     past (seen from behind), 35mm, warm low sun. *Motion:* Rubella yawns; Lillith yawns; the
     dog yawns; the man yawns.
  2. **10s.** *Motion:* camera pans along the street as the yawn travels: pigeons on a wire
     yawn in sequence, a cat in a window, a city bus whose silhouetted passengers all yawn at
     once.
  3. **6s.** *Motion:* the setting sun stretches and dims like a yawn, then drops below the
     rooftops in two seconds; night; streetlights click on; everyone on the street is asleep
     except Rubella, who sips.
- **Music feel:** a sleepy lounge vibraphone loop where each yawn is a trombone glissando
  down. On the drop, a gong, then crickets.
- **Do-not:** the sun gets no cartoon face. It's light behaving like a yawn. No text. Nobody
  falls over, they just nod off.

### 3. `ant-movers` — The Ants Move the Couch With Her On It

- **Laugh:** *A line of ants carries the couch away down the street, with Rubella still
  sitting on it reading.*
- **Medium / world / length:** photoreal · the building's curb on bulk-pickup day · ~30s
  (10 + 10 + 10)
- **Engine:** wrong scale (tiny thing doing a huge job).
- **Beats:**
  1. The building's curb: the old couch tagged for bulk pickup, with a row of forty folding
     chairs tagged beside it. Rubella sits on the couch reading, waiting for the truck. A
     single ant climbs a leg.
  2. A long black line of ants marches out of a crack in the sidewalk, gets under the couch,
     and lifts it an inch. The couch starts gliding down the sidewalk at walking pace. Rubella
     doesn't look up.
  3. **Drop (~22s), multiplies:** behind her, the forty folding chairs lift off the curb on
     forty lines of ants and follow her in a neat parade down the street, like a wedding
     procession.
- **End reaction:** at the corner, the bulk-pickup truck finally pulls up to an empty curb. On
  the couch going past it, Rubella turns a page.
- **Clips:**
  1. **10s.** *Start still:* city sidewalk outside a brick apartment building, a worn
     velvet couch at the curb with a paper tag on it, a row of forty grey folding chairs
     tagged beside it, Rubella seated on the couch reading a paperback, flat morning light,
     24mm low angle. *Motion:* one ant climbs a couch leg; a line of ants pours out of a
     sidewalk crack toward the couch.
  2. **10s.** *Motion:* the couch lifts one inch on a carpet of ants and glides down the
     sidewalk at walking pace, Rubella still reading. Camera tracks with her.
  3. **10s.** *Motion:* wide: the forty folding chairs rise on rivers of ants and follow the
     couch in a neat double line down the street; a garbage truck arrives at the empty curb.
- **Music feel:** a march for tiny brass (piccolo trumpet and snare), tempo of an ant
  procession. On the drop, it becomes a full wedding-march-style brass band, an original tune.
- **Do-not:** no ant close-ups that read as gross; no ants on Rubella. The tag text is never
  the joke. Nobody chases the couch.

### 4. `boomerang-picnic` — The Boomerang Brings Back a Picnic

- **Laugh:** *Every time she throws the boomerang it comes back with something new, and the
  last time it brings back a whole picnic table with a family still eating.*
- **Medium / world / length:** photoreal · a big city park lawn, Saturday · ~30s (10 + 10 + 10)
- **Engine:** rule of three with a wrong third.
- **Beats:**
  1. Lillith throws a wooden boomerang across the park. It curves back carrying a frisbee.
     Rubella, beside her, catches the frisbee without looking.
  2. Second throw: it comes back trailing a kite on its string, then a confused pigeon riding
     the top. Rubella takes the kite.
  3. **Drop (~22s), appears:** third throw. It comes back slowly towing a whole picnic table
     through the air, with a family of four still sitting at it, still eating sandwiches,
     calm. It lands gently in front of Lillith.
- **End reaction:** Rubella sits down at the picnic table, takes a sandwich, and the family
  passes her the lemonade.
- **Clips:**
  1. **10s.** *Start still:* sunny wide park lawn, Lillith mid-throw with a wooden boomerang,
     Rubella standing beside her holding nothing, trees and distant picnickers behind, bright
     daylight, 35mm. *Motion:* the boomerang flies off and curves back carrying a red frisbee;
     Rubella catches the frisbee one-handed without looking.
  2. **10s.** *Motion:* second throw; the boomerang returns trailing a diamond kite on its
     string with a grey pigeon riding on top; Rubella takes the kite string.
  3. **10s.** *Motion:* third throw; the boomerang returns slowly towing a wooden picnic table
     through the air with a family of four seated at it, calmly eating; it lands softly on the
     grass; Rubella sits down and takes a sandwich.
- **Music feel:** a whistled surf-guitar tune; each return is a rising three-note guitar
  lick. On the drop, full surf band and a big reverb splash.
- **Do-not:** nobody is hit by anything; the family never looks alarmed. The boomerang is
  plain wood, no logo.

### 5. `duckling-crossing` — The Ducklings Never Stop Coming

- **Laugh:** *A mother duck walks her ducklings across the street, and they keep coming all
  day and all night, and the last one is as big as a bus.*
- **Medium / world / length:** photoreal · one city crosswalk outside a park · ~30s
  (10 + 10 + 10)
- **Engine:** things keep happening → wrong third.
- **Beats:**
  1. Morning. A mother duck steps into the crosswalk. Eight ducklings follow. Traffic stops.
     At the front of the line of cars is a small delivery car: the Pizza Guy, red uniform,
     looking at his phone (23%).
  2. Time-lapse from the same angle: sun up, sun down, sun up. The line of ducklings never
     breaks. Hundreds of them. A bus driver (faceless, from behind) has started knitting. The
     Pizza Guy is in exactly the same position, phone at 23%.
  3. **Drop (~22s), appears:** the last duckling waddles out. It's a duckling the size of a
     bus, fluffy, peeping, and it fills the crosswalk. It crosses. The mother duck, on the far
     curb, counts with a nod.
- **End reaction:** the light goes green. The Pizza Guy puts the phone in his pocket and
  drives on at exactly the speed limit. He never looked at the ducks.
- **Clips:**
  1. **10s.** *Start still:* city crosswalk outside a green park on a bright morning, a
     mallard mother duck stepping off the curb with a line of fluffy yellow ducklings, stopped
     cars at the stop line, a small hatchback at the front with a delivery man in a red
     uniform at the wheel looking at his phone, 50mm from the sidewalk. *Motion:* the ducklings
     follow her into the crosswalk; traffic waits.
  2. **10s.** *Motion:* locked-off time-lapse: the sky cycles day, night, day; the line of
     ducklings never ends; the same cars and the same driver never move; the man's phone glow
     at night.
  3. **10s.** *Motion:* the final duckling waddles out, as big as a city bus, fluffy and
     yellow, peeping; it crosses; the mother duck on the far curb nods; the light turns green
     and the delivery car drives on.
- **Music feel:** a sweet marching xylophone tune that loops forever, and the loop is the
  joke. On the drop, the tuba comes in for the giant duckling.
- **Do-not:** the Pizza Guy is unnamed, name tag unreadable, never looks at the ducks, never
  honks, says nothing. Phone reads 23% every time. No duck is ever in danger.

### 6. `car-alarm-choir` — The Car Alarms Sing Together

- **Laugh:** *One car alarm goes off at night, and then all the car alarms on the street sing
  a song together.*
- **Medium / world / length:** photoreal · the building's street at 2 a.m. · ~26s (10 + 10 + 6)
- **Engine:** escalation into music. Needs the sound to land (the only card that does).
- **Beats:**
  1. 2 a.m. Rubella on the stoop with her mug, in the dark. One parked car's alarm starts its
     whoop-whoop.
  2. A second car answers a third higher. A third joins on a bassline honk. The alarms fall
     into rhythm, and hazard lights blink on the beat. Up and down the street, every parked
     car joins in harmony.
  3. **Drop (~18s), multiplies:** every car on the block flashes headlights in a synchronized
     chase down the street like a light show, wipers waving like a choir conductor's hands.
- **End reaction:** Rubella points her key fob and presses once. Every alarm stops dead. Her
  own little car, last in the row, chirps one tiny note. Silence. She sips.
- **Clips:**
  1. **10s.** *Start still:* city street at 2 a.m., wet asphalt, a row of parked cars of all
     kinds under orange streetlights, Rubella seated on a brick stoop with a white mug, a car
     key fob in her other hand, 35mm, low light. *Motion:* the nearest car's hazard lights
     flash as its alarm starts.
  2. **10s.** *Motion:* the cars down the street join one by one, hazard lights blinking in
     rhythm, the camera slowly pulling back along the row.
  3. **6s.** *Motion:* all the headlights flash in a chase wave down the block, wipers waving
     in time; Rubella presses the fob; every light goes dark at once; one small car's lights
     blink once.
- **Music feel:** an original a cappella-style arrangement built entirely from car-alarm
  sounds (whoops, honks, chirps, siren sweeps) at 120 BPM, layering into a real chord
  progression. The drop is the full block in harmony. The fob click is the cut.
- **Do-not:** no real car brands or badges. Nobody comes out of a window to yell. No text.

### 7. `toast-launch` — The Toaster Lillith Fixed

- **Laugh:** *Lillith fixes the toaster and it shoots toast to the ceiling, out the window,
  and then the toaster blasts off too, and toast rains on the town.*
- **Medium / world / length:** photoreal · ladies' building unit, kitchen → the street below
  (last shot only) · ~30s (10 + 10 + 10)
- **Engine:** rule of three with a wrong third.
- **Callbacks:** the part is a vacuum tube from the weather-predicting ping-pong table, and
  it works for one glorious morning. Rubella's full extinguisher is on the counter, unused.
- **Beats:**
  1. Lillith closes the back of an old chrome toaster with a glowing vacuum tube taped inside.
     Rubella holds a plate. Slice one pops, hits the ceiling and sticks.
  2. Slice two pops sideways out the open window. A passing gull catches it.
  3. **Drop (~22s), falls:** the toaster itself launches straight up and out the window like a
     rocket, trailing a crumb-smoke ring. Exterior: a moment later, toast rains gently over
     the whole street, and people below (faceless, with umbrellas) catch slices.
- **End reaction:** back in the kitchen, one slice drops from the sky through the open window
  and lands perfectly on Rubella's plate. She butters it.
- **Clips:**
  1. **10s.** *Start still:* homely apartment kitchen in morning light, an old chrome toaster
     on the counter with a glowing vacuum tube visible through a slot, Lillith closing a panel
     with a screwdriver, safety glasses pushed up on her head, Rubella holding an empty plate,
     a full red fire extinguisher on the counter, open window behind, 35mm. *Motion:* a slice
     pops up hard, hits the ceiling and sticks.
  2. **10s.** *Motion:* the next slice shoots sideways out the open window; a seagull swoops by
     and catches it.
  3. **10s.** *Motion:* the toaster blasts straight up out of the window trailing a puff ring;
     cut to the street below, where slices of toast fall gently like snow and people with
     umbrellas catch them; one slice drops into the kitchen onto Rubella's plate.
- **Music feel:** a perky kitchen-radio swing tune that becomes a rocket-launch countdown
  brass fanfare on the drop. Gentle celesta for the toast snow.
- **Do-not:** no fire, sparks or burns (the extinguisher stays sealed). No smart-toaster or HOA
  joke (`notes/dialog-jokes-incidents.md` owns it). Nobody on the street is hit; the toast
  falls slowly, like snow.

### 8. `cuckoo-lion` — The Cuckoo Sends a Substitute

- **Laugh:** *The cuckoo is too tired to come out at twelve o'clock, so a lion comes out of the
  tiny door instead and says "cuckoo" very quietly.*
- **Medium / world / length:** 2D cel cartoon · one cabin hallway with a cuckoo clock · ~16s
  (10 + 6)
- **Engine:** rule of three with a wrong third.
- **Beats:**
  1. The clock strikes twelve. The little door pops open: cuckoo, cuckoo, cuckoo... By nine
     the bird is panting. At ten it holds up one wing and goes back in. A sparrow comes out and
     says *cheep*. At eleven an owl comes out and says *hoo*.
  2. **Drop (12s), appears:** twelve. The tiny door opens and a lion's huge face fills it,
     whiskers squeezing out the sides. It says, very softly, *"cuckoo."*
- **End reaction:** the lion backs in and the door clicks shut. The cuckoo peeks out of a
  little side window, sees the lion's tail hanging out of the bottom of the clock, and closes
  its window.
- **Clips:**
  1. **10s.** *Start still (cartoon style):* a cosy wooden cabin hallway, a carved cuckoo clock
     on the wall, its little door open with a tired cartoon cuckoo bird leaning out, sweat drop,
     clock hands at twelve. *Motion:* the cuckoo pops out repeatedly and gets more tired; then a
     sparrow, then an owl take its turns.
  2. **6s.** *Motion:* the tiny door opens and a giant lion face fills it, cheeks squished,
     whiskers poking out; it whispers; the door shuts; the cuckoo peeks from a side window,
     sees a lion tail dangling under the clock, and closes the window.
- **Music feel:** a music-box waltz with a "cuckoo" woodwind call on each strike. On the drop, a
  low French-horn chord under the whisper, then the music box resumes.
- **Do-not:** the lion is not scary: big, gentle, polite. No text on the clock face.

### 9. `raindrop-derby` — The Slow Raindrop Wins

- **Laugh:** *Two raindrops race down a car window, a hundred join in, and the tiny slow one
  wins because the car turns and all the fast ones fly off.*
- **Medium / world / length:** photoreal macro · one rainy car side window, moving · ~20s
  (10 + 10)
- **Engine:** a race everyone has watched as a kid, plus a reversal.
- **Beats:**
  1. Macro on a rainy car window, city lights blurred outside. Two drops at the top start
     sliding down, side by side, wiggling. A third, tiny one barely moves.
  2. More drops join: a hundred racing, merging, overtaking, a big fat one barrelling through.
  3. **Drop (~14s), reverses:** the car turns a corner. Every fast drop on the glass gets blown
     sideways and off the window in one sweep. Only the tiny slow one is left, still creeping.
     It reaches the bottom of the window.
- **End reaction:** none. One last drip at the frame edge, like a bow.
- **Clips:**
  1. **10s.** *Start still:* extreme macro of a car side window at night, rain drops on the
     glass, blurred orange and pink city lights beyond, two drops at the top of frame and one
     tiny drop between them, 100mm macro, shallow focus. *Motion:* the two drops race downward,
     wiggling; the tiny one barely moves.
  2. **10s.** *Motion:* a hundred drops race and merge; the car turns, and a sideways gust of
     airflow sweeps every drop off the glass except the tiny one, which slowly reaches the
     bottom edge.
- **Music feel:** a horse-race galop on honky-tonk piano (original), getting faster. On the
  drop, a whoosh and silence, then a single triumphant trumpet note when the small drop
  finishes.
- **Do-not:** no faces on the drops, no reflection showing the driver, no text. Real-looking
  water.

### 10. `pet-rock` — Her Pet Rock Hatches

- **Laugh:** *Rubella takes her pet rock to the dog park, and it was an egg the whole time.*
- **Medium / world / length:** photoreal · a city dog park · ~26s (10 + 10 + 6)
- **Engine:** the very-end reaction, plus a transform.
- **Beats:**
  1. A dog park full of dogs doing tricks: catching frisbees, jumping, rolling. Rubella sits
     on the bench with a smooth grey rock on a red leash at her feet. It does nothing.
  2. She throws a stick for it. A labrador gets it. A poodle brings back a frisbee for no
     one. The rock sits.
  3. **Drop (~20s), transforms:** a crack runs across the rock. It splits, and a tiny baby
     dinosaur, grey and round, stumbles out with the leash still clipped to its shell
     piece. It totters to the stick and brings it back to Rubella.
- **End reaction:** the very last second. Rubella looks at the dinosaur, takes the stick, and
  pats its head once. That's the whole reaction.
- **Clips:**
  1. **10s.** *Start still:* sunny city dog park with grass and a chain-link fence, dogs
     mid-leap catching frisbees, Rubella seated on a park bench, a smooth grey stone the size
     of a melon on the grass at her feet with a red leash clipped to a little harness around it,
     35mm, bright daylight. *Motion:* dogs play around the rock; the rock doesn't move.
  2. **10s.** *Motion:* Rubella tosses a stick; a labrador fetches it; a poodle trots past with
     a frisbee; the rock stays still; a hairline crack appears on top.
  3. **6s.** *Motion:* the rock splits in two and a tiny round grey baby dinosaur stumbles out,
     totters to the stick, and brings it back to Rubella; she pats its head once.
- **Music feel:** a lazy whistled shuffle with a tiny woodblock tick for the rock (it ticks,
  like an egg timer). On the drop, a fanfare of kazoos.
- **Do-not:** not Biscuit, not the dog from *The Dog Walks Me*, no named animals. The dinosaur is
  a baby, cute, never scary. No eggshell gore.
