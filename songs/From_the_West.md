STYLE:

Plainchant, one voice, no instrument, no second singer, no drone, no pulse, no chord. Dorian mode on D. Riley, close and calm, straight tone, no vibrato, no ornament, no belting, reading a field entry as chant. One note per syllable except the single word "still," which rises one step and returns. Nothing else is allowed a neume. The metal hut is the room. Wind may be faintly outside. No kit, no bell, no piano, no choir. She does not explain the sky, the antenna, or herself. Do not sing her name. Structural flaw: when the sentence is over she stops on the pitch the last syllable already has. She does not move to the final D. There is no Amen and no second verse. Sung once, very slowly, about 80 to 100 seconds. Dark, small, a log with the acoustics of an empty hut.


LYRICS:

[One voice]

August.
This week, not next week.
Wind from the west.
Kestrel Ridge.
The rack is in the hut.
Last grade is on the card.
It is still there.
The finding is the same.
Leaving it up costs nothing.

[End]
(stop on the last syllable)
(do not move to the final)
(no amen)


NOTES:

Riley alone, above five thousand feet, one field entry. Plainchant: the syllables are the time. The only melodic rise is on "still." The chant does not cadence. She does not say whether the rack is a weather repeater or a transmitter.

Runtime about 80–100 seconds. No pulse.
