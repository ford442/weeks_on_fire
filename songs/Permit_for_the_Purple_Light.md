STYLE:

Bureaucratic bossa nova / clipboard lounge, 96 BPM, D minor drifting to F major on every chorus like a form being approved. Nylon-string guitar comping the classic bossa pattern, brushed snare, soft upright bass, a single vibraphone that only plays on the word "permit". One office-printer rhythm buried in the percussion — feed roller, page drop — treated as a shaker. Female lead voice, warm, bored, reading minutes aloud with perfect diction; she never raises her volume, only her eyebrows. Two-voice deadpan backing on the chorus (Lillith and Rubella, non-voting, tight unison at the back of the room). Bridge is the roll-call: spoken, dry mic, a pen clicking on 2 and 4. Mood: the morning after the portal, coffee still warm, cosmic horror reclassified as seasonal décor. No horns, no samba drums, no jokes in the arrangement — the arrangement is the straight man. End on an unresolved Dm add9 with the printer finishing a page.

NOTES:

## Production notes
- **Episode placement:** Episode 4 bureaucracy-afterglow cutaway — plays under the HOA meeting where the portal aftermath gets reclassified as seasonal décor. Brad's bin and the purple light's parking permit are the season-arc tags, sung verbatim.
- **Emotional arc:** Call to order → deadpan itemization → the chorus treats laminate as containment → roll call is the only place fear shows (the light does not respond) → adjourned, nobody leaves.
- **Characters:** Karen-adjacent lead voice (the minutes, personified). Lillith and Rubella as non-voting backing unison, back wall, never featured. Brad is heard, not sung.
- **Structural flaw:** the vibraphone hits only on "permit"; if Minimax spreads it, cut it entirely.
- **Runtime target:** ~2:30. 12s chorus bumper for an EyeWash clubhouse CRT.
- **Reference energy:** Astrud Gilberto reading a zoning variance; Stereolab office hours; the straightest possible Getz/Gilberto comp under the least possible situation.

## Music video treatment
Static clubhouse wide shot, fluorescent, folding chairs. The purple light is visible only through the window blinds, gaining a laminated tag between verses. The Two stand at the back wall in black lace and never move; in the last shot their reflections in the whiteboard have swapped places.
