STYLE:

Upbeat accordion pop, 120 BPM, F major, about two minutes five. Poemesque, deadpan and sincere, a small argument with itself, no wink at the listener. Lead instrument is a bright button accordion playing a bouncing oom-pah left hand and a nimble right-hand hook, bellows accents on the offbeat. Upright bass walking, brushed snare and a shaker, one glockenspiel doubling the hook once per chorus. Vocals: Lillith bright and quick on one side of the argument, Rubella dry and flat on the other, trading short lines, never overlapping, no belting, no stacked harmonies, no gang vocals. Verses are short lines of three to six words, specific nouns, patter that lands exactly on the beat. Chorus: both voices together on a plain, singable hook. Middle: eight bars of accordion alone, then back in. Room is a small tiled hallway, close mics, accordion slightly forward. No guitar, no horns, no electric instruments, no spoken asides, no laughter, no mugging, no chime, no door sound. Structural flaw: every line that names a button sits on the same F major chord in the accordion's left hand, so the harmony never decides which button is which. The lines that do not name a button move normally. The argument is never settled and the last chord does not settle it.

NOTES:

The flaw: both buttons are the same chord. Every line that says "this one", "the other one", "left" or "right" is over F, so the accordion refuses to take a side. Do not let the bridge or a key change decide it.

Method: short lines, specific nouns, a small argument with itself, deadpan sincerity. The model is the technique of a certain kind of two-minute accordion-and-wordplay pop song, not a cover, not a parody, and no wink at the listener.

Why it is not the neighbors:
- **Not After Is a Floor.** That owns the elevator directory with a floor called AFTER, the unmarked button, and the button that is warm. Here both buttons are labelled the same and nothing hovers. No AFTER, no "warm", no unmarked button.
- **Not Negotiating with the Elevator.** No counteroffer, no floor numbers, no singing car. The elevator never speaks in this song. The two singers argue; the machine is silent.
- **Not Car Twelve.** Bank-mate metal song: floor numbers and a title chant. This one has no numbers at all.
- **Not Paper Lung.** The other accordion lead is slow, A minor, an instrumental bellows study. This one is fast, major and sung.
- **Not Ground Rent / Article Seven / Unspent.** No "the floor is still the floor", no fourteen minutes, no clock, no Nilsson.
- **Not HOA ska.** No bin, no gavel, no horns.

Callbacks pulled (`notes/callback-bank.md`): *The elevator just tried to negotiate* (malfunction vs. negotiation is never adjudicated; neither is which button is the other one) · *Car Twelve / Twelve* (the car panel has twelve buttons, nine lit from behind and three from somewhere else; this plate is on the landing so that count is untouched, and nobody presses the alarm button) · *Shared structural flaw* (one cue, one wrongness).
