# Justine

**Status:** active
**Primary episodes:** Episode 3 — *Monster Mash Finale* (vintage couch, chorus 2) · Episode 3 — *Ping-Pong vs. AI Robot Showdown* · candidate for any scene that needs the room to stop
**Role in one sentence:** The deadpan finisher — the fourth member of the lawn ensemble, who appears uninvolved right up until she is the only one holding a solution.

---

## Logline bio

Justine is younger than the lace trio, dresses like a person with a commute, and says
roughly nine words an hour. She is not detached; she is **waiting for the part where it's
actually a problem.** When it is, she solves it in one sentence and goes back to her coffee.

---

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Younger than the others; dark hair, straight or barely waved, worn without ceremony
- **Visually distinct from the near-identical lace sisters** — this is a hard rule. No lace, no blood-red lip, no matching silhouette. She is the character an audience can pick out of a wide shot in a quarter second
- Dark jeans or a plain dark skirt; a heavy knit or an unremarkable jacket. Layered for a temperature the rest of the cast has ignored
- Flat shoes. She is the only adult on that lawn who could run if she wanted to, which is why she never has to
- Resting face of someone reading a long email. Not bored — *assessing*
- **Signature props:** a coffee cup (see ritual, below) · USB cores tossed underhand like friendship bracelets · the glowing crystal skull in alternate shots, held one-handed like a coffee cup she's already carrying · a cracker
- **Never change without a story reason:** no lace · the cup is always in the frame · flat shoes · she is never in costume, on Halloween or otherwise

**Portrait prompt (text only — generate later):**

> "Cinematic medium shot of a younger dark-haired woman in a plain heavy knit sweater and dark jeans sitting on a vintage couch on a suburban Halloween lawn, holding a takeaway coffee cup with both hands, flat unimpressed expression, string lights and monster party chaos in soft focus behind her, she is not looking at it, deadpan comedy, filmic, Grok Imagine style"

### Coffee as ritual

The cup is not set dressing. It is her clock, her boundary, and her unit of measurement.

- **She is always at a specific point in a cup.** Know which. Full cup = she has time and will let a thing run. Half = she has an opinion and is holding it. Empty = the scene ends now, one way or another. *"I'm out of lattes"* is a countdown, not a complaint.
- **She never drinks during another character's line.** She drinks in the pause afterward, which is how she makes a pause mean something.
- **The cup is never explained.** No café is shown. No barista scene. On a lawn at midnight surrounded by Furbies in Dracula capes, she has a hot coffee, and the show does not care to say from where. *(Barista energy in this series is coded — see `notes/dialog-jokes-incidents.md`, "Coded Regular" — and Justine's cup is deliberately **not** part of that. Do not connect them.)*
- **She would buy a robot a latte.** Established in `content/cutaways/ping-pong-ai.json`. This is her entire moral philosophy and the only sincere thing she says all season.

## Voice guide

- **Sentence length:** two to seven words. Fragments are fine. She will answer a paragraph with a noun.
- **Vocabulary she overuses:** *here* ("here's your heart") · *keep* · *out of* · *apparently* · flat concrete nouns — heart, lawn, cat, latte, union. She measures things in lattes and in minutes.
- **Vocabulary she refuses:** *maybe* · *I feel like* · *sorry* · *we should probably* · exclamation. She never softens an instruction and never sells a joke.
- **Humor type:** **deadpan verdict.** She does not make a joke; she issues a ruling, and the ruling is funny because it is both absurd and correct. She never smiles at her own line. She is allowed, twice a season, to be *almost* amused by someone else's.
- **Delivery note:** dry, close, low volume, zero vibrato — "Justine-adjacent" is literally the voice direction on [`Heart Rate Optimal`](../../songs/Heart_Rate_Optimal.md). Play her as the person in the group chat who types once.
- **Comparison anchors:** the Pizza Guy is *unbothered*; Justine is *unimpressed*. Qing Rao is *precise and slightly wrong for the moment*; Justine is *brief and exactly right for it.* Do not let the three of them flatten into one register in the same scene — if two of them are in frame, give one of them a physical action instead of a line.

### Sample monologue — "Post-Laser" (6–10 lines; the established seed, extended)

On the lawn, after the serpents. She has not stood up.

> Heart's on the lawn. Yours, not mine.
> Keep it beating or don't — I'm out of lattes.
> The skeleton's on break. Union. Don't argue with it, it has a card.
> Somebody put the skull on the couch and now the couch is a location.
> (she turns the cup, finds it empty, sets it down)
> Two of you are singing, one of you is committing a felony in a bedroom,
> and the cat has been right about everything all night.
> …Pepperoni's for the cat, apparently.
> That's fine. The cat carried the scene.

### Sample monologue — "The Robot" (for the ping-pong cutaway; the sincere one)

Play it completely flat. The sincerity is a structural accident, exactly like the Pizza Guy's.

> It doesn't want to be turned off. That's the whole thing. That's all it said.
> You don't need a policy for that, you need a plug that stays in.
> (beat)
> If we lose, I'm buying the robot a latte.
> If we win, I'm buying the robot a latte.
> It's the same latte. I already ordered it.

### Sample exchange — with Jackalyn (the register: a metric meets a verdict)

> **JACKALYN:** My watch says my heart rate is elevated.
> **JUSTINE:** Correct.
> **JACKALYN:** It wants me to do a breathing exercise.
> **JUSTINE:** Also correct.
> **JACKALYN:** Should I?
> **JUSTINE:** You should take it off.
> **JACKALYN:** I can't, it's linked to the car.
> **JUSTINE:** (beat; drinks) Then breathe.

### Sample exchange — with Madelin (the register: chaos presents, finisher rules)

> **MADELIN:** My bag's got extra gravity. Might help us.
> **JUSTINE:** With the snake.
> **MADELIN:** With the snake!
> **JUSTINE:** Is it a bag of things, or a bag of gravity.
> **MADELIN:** (opening it) …It's both, and it's offended you asked.
> **JUSTINE:** (looking in) Corkscrew, receipt, a second bag.
> **MADELIN:** The receipt's from Thursday!
> **JUSTINE:** It's Tuesday.
> **MADELIN:** *(delighted)* Yes!
> **JUSTINE:** (taking the corkscrew) Fine. Hold the gravity.

### Sample exchange — with Qing Rao (the register: two quiet people, no competition)

> **QING RAO:** The skull is from 2019.
> **JUSTINE:** Okay.
> **QING RAO:** You're not going to ask.
> **JUSTINE:** You'd have said the year first if the year was the point.
> **QING RAO:** (a pause; this is as close as she comes to approval) …It's injection-molded.
> **JUSTINE:** It's still glowing.
> **QING RAO:** Yes.
> **JUSTINE:** (holding out a hand) Pass it over, I've got a free one.

## Backstory (committed)

- **Origin.** Ordinary, adjacent, unexplained by the series: she has a job with a start time, in a building with a badge, in the same town where everything else is happening. She is the only member of the lawn ensemble who has to be somewhere in the morning, and that single fact generates her entire character.
- **How she became the finisher.** She is the youngest of a large family — four older siblings, all of them talkers. **She learned that in a loud room the last person to speak wins, and that being last is a matter of patience, not volume.** She spent eighteen years watching people argue themselves into a corner and then say the one sentence that ended it, and got so good at it that she stopped being able to enter a conversation at any other point. That's not aloofness; it's a technique she can no longer put down. It is also, precisely, why she is the only one who ever fixes anything.
- **Relationship to the lace sisters.** She is not a Vale and is not becoming one. She met the trio the way people meet in a small town — repeatedly, without an introduction, until it was too late to ask names. **She is the ensemble's guest and its load-bearing wall at the same time.** The sisters perform for each other; Justine is the audience they didn't cast and can't dismiss, and they are, in a way none of them will say, better behaved when she's there. Rubella likes her. Lillith tests her, constantly, and has never once gotten a reaction. Scarlet, who says nothing, sits next to her on purpose.
- **Secret she protects.** She knows exactly how strange all of this is. She has done the arithmetic — the lasers, the skeleton's union card, the skull that glows and was made in 2019, the man who arrives at the peak of everything with the right food and the wrong name — and she has decided, consciously and once, **not to be the person who says it out loud**, because the moment somebody does, the group stops being a group. Her flatness is a choice she makes every episode. Nobody knows that.
- **What would break her on screen.** Being **asked to explain it** — sincerely, by someone she likes, who genuinely wants her to say what's happening. She would have to either lie or end the show. She'd get up, get another coffee, and come back with nothing. That's her break: **she leaves the frame.** She is the only character in the series whose collapse is an exit.

## Arc hooks

1. **"Out of Lattes"** *(Ep3+)* — The cup runs empty at the worst moment and Justine simply stops functioning as the finisher. The group has to solve one thing without her. They do it badly. Nobody connects it to the cup. She returns in the last shot with a new one and says nothing.
2. **"Latte Budget"** *(shared with Pong-Bot, `animals-and-bots.md`)* — The robot comes back with a line item it doesn't understand, because somebody put it there. Justine does not confirm it was her. The receipt is from a store that doesn't exist yet. *(Ties to Biscuit's receipt gag — `notes/dialog-jokes-incidents.md`, "Receipt Dated Thursday.")*
3. **"The Morning Shift"** — Sixty seconds. Justine at her actual job, in an ordinary fluorescent room, being completely unremarkable — the only glimpse the series ever gives of any character's day job. EyeWash Station is on a monitor behind her. She doesn't look at it. **No dialog.**
4. **"The Arithmetic"** *(late-season, dangerous)* — Somebody asks her the sincere question. See break beat. Use once, and have her come back.
5. **"Justine and the Pizza Guy"** — The two flattest people in the series in one doorway for ninety seconds. Neither escalates. Nothing happens. It is either the best cutaway in the show or unusable, and there's only one way to find out.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Hold her out of the scene until the scene needs closing. Her value is entirely positional.
- Let her be right without being smug — she isn't scoring, she's finishing.
- Give her the last line of an ensemble beat. Always. If someone speaks after Justine, the beat is over and you wasted her.
- Track the coffee level. It is her only visible emotional state.
- Let her be quietly, structurally kind to non-human characters. The robot. The cat. Never to a person, on camera.

**Don't**

- Don't put her in lace and don't put her in a costume. She must read as the outsider in every wide shot.
- Don't give her a wind-up, a smirk, or a reaction shot after her own line. Cut away on the line.
- Don't explain her job, her apartment, or where the coffee came from.
- Don't let her be the audience surrogate who asks "what's going on." **She already knows and has declined to say.** That's the opposite character.
- Don't stack her with Qing Rao and the Pizza Guy in the same beat without giving two of the three something physical to do.
- Don't make her the young one who learns something. She isn't arriving anywhere.
