**Cast:** DALE (chair), KAREN (president, clipboard), BRAD (wrong blue), PATRICIA (raises hand), GARY JOHNSON (9:03), VIVIENNE & LILIANE (standing, lace, non-voting), PIZZA GUY (door), BISCUIT (poodle, larcenous)

Brad is the only person in the room having a genuine emotional experience, and it is about a bin.

### A — Roll Call

```
INT. HOA CLUBHOUSE — NIGHT

Folding chairs in crooked rows. A card table with a skirt that does
not fit it. A cracked gavel on a wooden circle worn pale in the middle.

Through the horizontal blinds: purple light. It pulses, slow, like a
guilty conscience with a pacemaker.

DALE (60s, short-sleeve button-down) stands at the card table. KAREN
sits front row center with a clipboard. The clipboard wears a sticker:
I SURVIVED THE PURPLE LIGHT '25.

BRAD slumps two chairs down, arms crossed, a manila folder in his lap
that is thicker than the agenda. PATRICIA has already raised her hand.
GARY JOHNSON has not looked up from his phone since 2019.

At the back wall — not sitting — VIVIENNE and LILIANE VALE, in black
lace, holding wine that did not come from this building.

BISCUIT, a standard poodle in a bandana that is currently and
inexplicably purple, sits in the aisle. Watching the gavel.
```

```
DALE
Emergency session of the Homeowners Association, called to order.
Roll call.
(reads)
Ainsworth.

KAREN
Here.

DALE
Ainsworth, Karen.

KAREN
Still here, Dale.

DALE
Bradley.

BRAD
Brad.

DALE
That's what I said.

BRAD
You said Bradley. It's Brad. It's been Brad for forty-one years.

DALE
The roster says Bradley.

BRAD
The roster is wrong. The roster's been wrong since the roster.

DALE
(marking it)
Bradley, present, under protest.
(beat)
Johnson, Gary.

GARY
(not looking up)
It was nine-oh-three.

DALE
Noted for a future meeting.
(beat)
Patricia.

PATRICIA
(hand still up)
Here. Also I have a — 

DALE
Hand's noted. Hands are item six.
(reading, slower)
Vale. Vivienne.

VIVIENNE
(pleasantly)
That's not my name tonight.

DALE
...Vale, Liliane.

LILIANE
Also not.

DALE
(writing)
Two guests. Unnamed. Standing.

KAREN
They were offered chairs, Dale. It's in the minutes that they
were offered chairs.

VIVIENNE
We'll stand.

LILIANE
We don't do polyester.

DALE
So noted. Quorum is achieved.
(taps the gavel — CRACK)
```

```
Somewhere outside, a long way down, something enormous ANSWERS the
gavel. A boom that arrives through the floor before it arrives
through the air. The blinds shiver. Dust comes off a ceiling tile
and lands on Gary's phone.

Gary wipes it off. Nobody stands up.
```

### B — Item One Was the Crater

```
INT. HOA CLUBHOUSE — CONTINUOUS

Dale consults the agenda. The agenda is one page. He turns it over.
It is one page.
```

```
DALE
Item one. Item one. ITEM ONE.

KAREN
We're past item one, Dale. Item one was the crater.
We tabled the crater.

BRAD
You tabled my driveway.

KAREN
We tabled the *crater*. Your driveway is a separate line item
because your driveway is a separate surface.

BRAD
It was one surface. Tuesday it was one surface.

KAREN
Tuesday was a different jurisdiction.

DALE
(gavel; a distant boom answers)
Minutes will reflect that democracy remained seated.

PATRICIA
(hand up)
Dale.

DALE
Hands are item six, Patricia.

PATRICIA
It's about item six.

DALE
Then you'll want to raise it at item six.

PATRICIA
(lowering hand, then raising it again)
I'd like to reserve my hand.

DALE
Reserved.
```

```
The purple light through the blinds shifts hue — deeper now, a bruise
color. Biscuit's bandana shifts with it. Nobody sees this except
Liliane, who notes it the way you'd note a woman wearing your dress.
```

```
LILIANE
(to Vivienne, barely)
The dog is coordinated.

VIVIENNE
The dog is the only one.
```

### C — Brad's Bin (The Monologue)

```
INT. HOA CLUBHOUSE — CONTINUOUS

Brad stands. He does not ask to stand. He opens the folder. It is
worse than anyone feared: it is organized.
```

```
DALE
Item two. Container coloration.

BRAD
I have swatches.

KAREN
He always has swatches.

BRAD
(laying them on the card table, one at a time)
This is the bin the city gave me. This is Municipal Blue.
This is the swatch the HOA sent me in a letter with no return
address, which you are calling Democratic Blue. This is
Democratic Blue under the sodium lamp at the end of Fenwick.
This is Democratic Blue under the LED they replaced it with in
March, which is a different blue, Karen, because *the lamp
changed*, and the bin did not. This is Democratic Blue at
four in the afternoon in October, which is the only condition
under which the two blues match, and October is eleven months
away from October.

(beat)

Three lighting conditions. One bin. I photographed it in all
three. I used a gray card. Do you know what a gray card is?

KAREN
I know what a gray card is.

BRAD
Then you know what I'm holding.
(he is not holding a gray card; he is holding a photograph
of a gray card)
I'm not asking for an exception. I've never asked for an
exception. I'm asking this body to acknowledge that the color
of my bin is a function of the light you put on it, and you
put the light on it, and then you fined me for the light.

(beat)

That's it. That's the whole complaint. That's four years.
```

```
Silence. Actual silence — the muzak even chooses this moment to be
between tracks.

Outside, something the size of a church exhales.

VIVIENNE, at the back, sets her wine down on the folding table
without a sound, which from her is a standing ovation.
```

```
KAREN
(writing)
The chair thanks Bradley for his—

BRAD
Brad.

KAREN
—for his *presentation*, and notes that lighting conditions are
a county matter.

BRAD
(sitting)
Of course they are.

DALE
Motion to move the bins to a working group.

GARY
(not looking up)
Second.

DALE
All in favor.
(a show of hands; he does not count them)
Carried. The bins are working.
```

### D — Item Three

```
INT. HOA CLUBHOUSE — CONTINUOUS

Karen stands. She does not need to stand. She stands the way people
stand when they've written the sentence in advance.

Behind the blinds, the purple light has stopped pulsing. It is now
simply on, and it is coming from lower than it was — street level.
Driveway level. Something in it is turning.
```

```
KAREN
Item three.

PATRICIA
(hand)
Is item three the—

KAREN
Item three is the light.

PATRICIA
Thank you. Because it's — it's in the Hendersons' driveway, and
it's *open*, Karen, it's open at the bottom, and there's a
sound coming out of it that I have been hearing in my molars
since Thursday—

KAREN
Item three is the light.
(consulting the clipboard)
The chair has reviewed the covenants. Section four-B addresses
unapproved hardscape. Section nine addresses illuminated
seasonal display. Section eleven addresses water features.

BRAD
Water features.

KAREN
It has a current, Bradley.

BRAD
Brad.

KAREN
The chair finds that the object in the Henderson driveway is
not a structure, because it has no footprint. It is not a
hardscape, because it is not hard. It is not a vehicle, a
shed, a trampoline, or a flag.
(beat)
It is a decorative accent.

PATRICIA
It's a *hole in the world.*

KAREN
It is a decorative accent, it requires no permit, and it is
therefore compliant.

PATRICIA
Compliant.

KAREN
Enjoy it, Patricia.
```

```
DALE
Motion to classify the light as decorative accent, unpermitted,
compliant, non-structural.

GARY
(not looking up)
Second.

DALE
Discussion?
```

```
The window BLOWS IN.

Not glass — the blinds. They come off the bracket in one piece and
sail across the room like a flatfish and land in the back row, where
Liliane steps over them without breaking her sentence, which she has
not started.

Behind the empty window: the driveway. The light. The turning thing
inside the light. A minivan alarm somewhere, briefly, and then not.

Purple fills the clubhouse. Everyone is lit like a bruise.

Dale raises the gavel.
```

```
DALE (CONT'D)
Seeing none —
(GAVEL — the boom arrives at the same instant, so
the two sounds become one sound)
— carried.

KAREN
(writing, entirely calm)
Carried unanimously.

PATRICIA
I abstained.

KAREN
There's no box for that.
```

### E — The Gavel

```
INT. HOA CLUBHOUSE — CONTINUOUS

Dale sets the gavel down to write. He writes for four seconds.

BISCUIT crosses the room at a trot, takes the gavel off the table
without slowing, and exits under the folding chairs.

Dale reaches for the gavel. It is not there. He looks at the place
where it is not.

This is the first time tonight that anyone in this room has been
afraid.
```

```
DALE
(quietly)
Karen.

KAREN
I saw it.

DALE
Where is the gavel.

KAREN
The dog has the gavel.

DALE
Whose dog.

KAREN
(a small pause — the first crack in her)
...The dog isn't on the roster.

BRAD
(without looking up)
The roster's wrong.

DALE
(to the room, holding the wooden circle where the
gavel used to land; he taps it with two knuckles;
outside, obligingly, a boom)
The chair is in possession of a functional substitute.

PATRICIA
That's your hand, Dale.

DALE
The chair is in possession of a *functional substitute.*
```

### F — The Door

```
INT. HOA CLUBHOUSE — DOOR — CONTINUOUS

Three knocks. Ordinary knocks. The wrongest sound in the building.

Nobody moves, so LILIANE opens the door, because she is closest and
because she has never once been afraid of a door.

THE PIZZA GUY. Red uniform. Perfect posture. Two boxes and a bag.
Behind him, the driveway, the light, the turning thing. His hair does
not move.
```

```
PIZZA GUY
Order for... Vivien?

VIVIENNE
(from the back, not moving)
Yes.

PIZZA GUY
(handing over the boxes)
Extra napkins are in the bag.

DALE
This is a closed session.

PIZZA GUY
(already turning)
Sure.

KAREN
Is that — are you parked in the — 
(she cannot finish the sentence in a way the minutes
can hold)
— is your vehicle in the driveway.

PIZZA GUY
(over his shoulder, walking into the purple)
Wouldn't fit.
```

```
He goes. He does not go around the light. The door closes on its own.

LILIANE returns to the back wall with the boxes and does not open
them.
```

```
VIVIENNE
(to Liliane, low, warm)
He said Vivien.

LILIANE
He did.

VIVIENNE
(after a beat, pleased about it)
He'll say it again next week.

LILIANE
I'm counting on it.
```

### G — We Are Not Adjourning

```
INT. HOA CLUBHOUSE — CONTINUOUS

The back half of the room is now open to the sky. The chairs are
still in rows. Nobody has moved a chair.

Dale looks at the agenda. One page. He turns it over. One page.
```

```
PATRICIA
(hand up, exhausted)
Item six.

DALE
Hands.

PATRICIA
I'd like to use my hand.

DALE
The chair recognizes Patricia.

PATRICIA
(standing, calm, finally)
I would like the minutes to say that it was beautiful.

KAREN
(pen up)
Say again?

PATRICIA
The light. Before it opened. When it was just the light on the
grass and it made the grass look like — 
(beat)
I'd like the minutes to say it was beautiful. That's my item.

KAREN
(a long beat, then writing)
Patricia notes the aesthetic.

PATRICIA
(sitting)
Thank you.

DALE
Motion to adjourn.

KAREN
Denied.

DALE
There are seventeen open items.

KAREN
There are eighteen. The dog.

DALE
(nodding slowly, tapping the wooden circle with his
knuckles; outside, obligingly, a boom)
We're not adjourning.
(beat)
We're persisting.
```

```
He knuckles the circle again. Boom. Again. Boom.

He has found the rhythm of it now. He is conducting.

The purple light pours in over the empty rows at the back where
nobody sits, and over the full rows at the front where everybody does,
and Karen turns to a fresh page.

Hold. Let it go long. Let the muzak win.
```
