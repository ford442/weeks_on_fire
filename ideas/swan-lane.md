## Tone

**Silly and simple, for kids.** Objects behaving wrongly, a freeze game (everyone knows
statues), and one cone that won't stop spinning. Played completely sincere: real
ballet structure, real stage lighting from the sodium lamps, and no winks.

## Cast (objects)

| Object | Ballet role | How it moves |
|---|---|---|
| 24 orange cones with reflective collars | corps de ballet | tilt, hop, slide on their bases, spin in place |
| one fat orange-and-white drum barrel | prima ballerina | slow spins, bows by tipping forward |
| one striped A-frame sawhorse | danseur | walks on its four legs, "lifts" the barrel by tipping under it |
| flashing arrow board on a trailer | conductor | blinks the tempo; arrow points the way of the closure |
| a single small cone, slightly scuffed | the soloist | does the 32 spins |

## Beat sheet

1. **Overture (0:00–0:10).** A perfect regulation taper of 24 cones closes the right lane
   under sodium lights. The arrow board blinks left, left, left in tempo. Nothing moves
   except the blink.
2. **Corps de ballet (0:10–0:20).** A ripple runs down the taper: each cone tilts and returns
   one beat after the one in front, like a wave through a line of dancers. Then they hop
   into two rows facing each other.
3. **Pas de deux (0:20–0:35).** The barrel glides out to centre. The sawhorse walks to her,
   tips, and lifts her a foot off the asphalt; she turns slowly in the air, reflective bands
   flaring. The corps frames them in a semicircle.
4. **Headlights (0:35–0:41).** Distant headlights sweep the overpass. **Snap:** every object
   jumps back into the exact regulation taper, perfectly still. A delivery scooter passes in
   the open lane at an unhurried pace: red uniform, box in the rear carrier, rider looking
   straight ahead. He doesn't slow down or look. Taillights fade.
5. **Fouettés (0:41–0:51).** The ballet resumes. The small scuffed cone spins in place on the
   beat, again and again, wobbling and never falling, while the others count it in with tiny
   hops. Things keep happening: it spins through the whole clip.
6. **Grand jeté, the DROP (0:51–1:01).** On the drop, the entire taper leaps as one: 24
   cones, the barrel and the sawhorse arc across the centre line and land as a perfect lane
   closure in the **left** lane. The arrow board flips to blink **right**. Dawn starts to
   grey the sky. A work truck's door opens out of frame and one orange work boot steps down
   onto the asphalt. The small cone, still spinning, stops dead the moment the boot lands.
   Everything is regulation.

## Music cue

**Original late-Romantic ballet pastiche.** Strings, harp, oboe solo, celesta, timpani. 3/4,
then 2/4 for the fouettés, 108 BPM. Sincere, big and lush. No quotation of any existing ballet
melody: write a new waltz theme.

## Do-not

Cones never grow faces, eyes, arms or tutus. No real ballet music quotes. No crash and no
car hitting anything; the headlights never reach the cones. No HOA cul-de-sac (wrong world).
No text on the arrow board, just the arrow.

## Why it's funny

Statues, but with traffic cones: a whole classical ballet that freezes into boring road
safety every time anyone could see it. And one cone that can't stop.

**Difficulty: 3 / 5.** Objects without faces are forgiving, but 24 identical cones moving in
sync and then reappearing in the right lane positions is a continuity job.

**Riskiest shot: #6, the grand jeté.** Two dozen objects leaping together and landing as an
ordered taper in the other lane. Generation will scatter them. Fallback: generate the leap
as the cones lifting into a blur (6s), then hard-cut on the cymbal to a fresh still of the
finished left-lane taper. The cut hides the landing.
