# Relationship Map

Who works with whom, why, and what it costs. This expands the pairing cheat sheet at the
bottom of [`../suggested-characters.md`](../suggested-characters.md) — that table is the
index; this is the argument.

**How to read a pairing:** every good pairing in this series is **two people applying
incompatible methods to the same event, both correctly, neither noticing.** If you can't
name the two methods, the scene is two characters talking.

## Core relationships (the ones the series is actually about)

### Vivienne ↔ Liliane — the pact

The only relationship in the series with a **score**, and the only one that isn't a joke.

- **Methods:** Vivienne proposes and controls nothing. Liliane accepts and controls everything. Neither has said so.
- **Mechanism:** the swap, every six months, six years running. Names, keys, apartments, debts, enemies, wreckage.
- **Asymmetry:** Liliane has been ahead since day nine of the first swap and Vivienne believes she is the original winner. **The not-checking is the relationship.**
- **The escalation ladder** *(track it across any scene they share; it is the actual plot)*: Liliane finishes Vivienne's sentences **incorrectly, on purpose** — habit → sport → weapon → **mercy**, where she finishes one correctly and it costs her. One mercy per script, maximum.
- **Continuity:** the score was **9–7** at the end of *We Play to Win*. If they appear again, it has moved, and **neither will say by how much.**
- **Never:** a normal conversation · either of them flustered · either of them naming a feeling · a winner.

### Jackalyn ↔ Madelin — seventeen years

- **Methods:** Jackalyn keeps the count; Madelin invents the lives. Both started in the same windowless room on the same temp contract and neither has stopped.
- **Mechanism:** Madelin produces; Jackalyn measures what was produced; neither has ever required the other to change.
- **Asymmetry:** Jackalyn is secretly keeping score of the friendship in a note called "gratitude log." Madelin would be thrilled, which is worse.
- **Best beat:** a unit of measurement dispute where **both are right** and Jackalyn goes with Madelin's.
- **Never:** a falling-out · Jackalyn winning an argument by being correct · either of them alone for a whole scene if it can be avoided.

### Karen ↔ Brad — petty vs. tired righteous

- **Methods:** Karen renames the problem; Brad documents it. Renaming beats documenting every time, and Brad keeps documenting.
- **Asymmetry:** Brad is the only person in the room having a genuine emotional experience, and Karen has never noticed, because it is about a bin.
- **The unspoken thing:** both of them were changed by the purple light and neither has said how. *(Karen: certainty. Brad: that he found it beautiful.)*
- **Never:** Brad winning · Karen losing her temper · either of them naming the light.

### Oz ↔ Marguerite — haunted vs. unscheduled

- **Methods:** Oz logs; Marguerite files. Same verb, different jurisdiction.
- **Mechanism:** she stands in the doorway and asks the question she came to not ask; he answers it completely; **the scene ends without adjudicating.**
- **Asymmetry:** he has the log and no authority; she has the license and has never seen it.
- **Never:** a resolution · either of them frightened · either of them lying.

| From → To | Permitted? | How |

## Pairings to avoid (and why)

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| **Justine + Qing Rao + Pizza Guy** in one beat, all speaking | Three flat registers flatten into one. Give at least two of the three a physical action instead of a line. |
| **Vivienne + Karen** as an antagonism | Both are authorities who never raise their voice; there is nothing between them to rub. Vivienne declines the category and the scene has nowhere to go. Two lines maximum, as texture. |
| **Continuity Voice + Radio Voice** | See above. The pairing's entire value is that it never happens. |
| **Marguerite + Karen**, casually | It is a season-level asset. Spending it early gets you one good scene and costs a finale. |
| **The Two + Rubella and Lillith**, with anyone remarking on the surname | Four Vales, no stated relation, **nobody ever remarks on it.** If they share a frame, the framing treats it as unremarkable. |
| **Pizza Guy + any rescue** | He is not a rescue. No paramedic, no firefighter, no police, no clergy beyond the laminated card. |
| **Black Cat + Pizza Guy exiting together** | Two identical exits cancel each other out. One stays. |
| **Anyone + a sincere question to the Pizza Guy about what's happening** | The moment somebody asks, he has to answer or refuse, and either one ends the gag. |

## The three open files

Three unresolved questions run across the whole map. They are **locked as unresolved.** Every
one of them is a relationship, and the relationship is the not-knowing.

1. **Haunted or unscheduled** — Oz says one, Marguerite says the other, the scene ends.
2. **Whether the Continuity Voice is the Radio Voice** — never confirmed, in dialog, casting, credits, or a slate.
3. **Whether the Pizza Guy knows** — never asked on camera. *(The writers' room has an answer; see [his sheet](the-pizza-guy.md#backstory-committed--writers-room-only-none-of-this-is-ever-spoken-on-screen). It is never spoken.)*

Adjacent, same treatment: the Kestrel Ridge antenna · Biscuit, smart or cosmically assigned ·
why the skull glows · the red lamp pulse at the end of *The Long Way Up*.
