# Madelin

**Status:** active
**Primary episodes:** Episode 3 — *Monster Mash Finale* (lawn, starts the sing-us-out chant) · Episode 3 — *Ping-Pong vs. AI Robot Showdown* · candidate for any scene that needs an impossible object produced cheerfully
**Role in one sentence:** Manic bag-lady energy — a woman with an overstuffed tote, a talent for improvising backstories for offended objects, and a cheerful refusal to be alarmed by any of it.

## Logline bio

Madelin arrives at every disaster with a giant beige tote and the unshakeable conviction
that something in it will help. She grins during catastrophes, improvises dignified life
stories for inanimate objects that she feels have been insulted, and is right about the bag
more often than probability allows.

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Layered, cheerful, slightly mismatched — a cardigan over a print, a scarf chosen at a different temperature. Warm colors. She is the only bright thing in the frame after dark
- Hair doing whatever it is doing. This is not a note to fix
- Wide, genuine, undimmable grin. She grins *during* the disaster, not after
- **The tote:** an enormous beige canvas bag with a huge moulded 3D skull on the side. It is heavier than it can possibly be and she never adjusts her posture for it
- Both hands busy. Madelin is always mid-retrieval
- **Signature props:** the skull tote · a corkscrew paddle · receipts from stores that do not exist · whatever the bag has produced this scene, which she keeps for the rest of the episode
- **Never change without a story reason:** the tote (size, color, the moulded skull) · the grin · she never puts the bag down, on any surface, for any reason

## The bag — rules of holding

The tote is a running gag with a physics. Keep the physics and the gag stays funny for four
seasons; break it and it becomes a magic wand in one scene.

1. **Everything in the bag is plausible.** Not impossible — *implausible.* A clock, a corkscrew, a bag of ice, a folding stool, forty feet of twine, a second bag. Never a sword, never a gun, never a solution the plot could not have had another way. The joke is *"why do you have that,"* never *"how do you have that."*
2. **It is never a search.** She knows where everything is. She goes in up to the elbow and comes out with it. If she has to rummage, the object is not in there and the scene has to solve itself.
3. **One retrieval per beat, two per scene, maximum.** The third one is always the funniest and always kills the gag for the next episode. Spend it in a finale.
4. **The bag never resolves the stakes.** It buys thirty seconds, changes the geometry, or gives somebody something to hold. Justine finishes; the bag furnishes.
5. **She never puts it down.** Not on a couch, not on a table, not while playing ping-pong. If a shot requires both hands, the bag goes on the shoulder, never on the ground. **If the bag is ever on the ground, something is badly wrong and the scene should mean it.**
6. **Contents persist.** Whatever comes out stays out for the rest of the episode, in frame, usually being held by someone who did not ask for it. Nobody gives anything back.
7. **The receipts are from stores that don't exist**, and at least one is dated in the future. *(Shared property with Biscuit's heist gag — `notes/dialog-jokes-incidents.md`, "Biscuit's Heist" / [`Receipt Dated Thursday`](../../songs/Receipt_Dated_Thursday.md). Madelin and the poodle are quietly shopping at the same place and nobody in the series will ever find out.)*
8. **"Extra gravity" is a claim, not a power.** She says the bag has extra gravity. It behaves like a heavy bag. She is not lying — she is describing it the way she describes everything, which is generously.
9. **The bag is never explained, never opened to camera, and never inventoried.** We see what comes out. We never see in.

## Improvising backstories for offended objects

Madelin's actual skill, and the reason she works. When an object has been insulted — called
fake, called broken, called ugly, called *a replica* — she gives it a better life, out loud,
immediately, in detail, with total conviction.

- It started as a game to survive a windowless room *(see [Backstory](#backstory-committed))* and it never stopped.
- The stories are specific and warm: the stapler's marriage, the ficus's legal trouble, the skull's shipwreck.
- **She is never doing a bit.** She believes the object deserves the dignity, and she is the only character in the series who extends that courtesy to anything that cannot leave a tip.
- **The skull** is her masterwork in progress. It was identified on camera as a 2019 injection-moulded replica *(`episodes/episode-02/synopsis.md`)* and Madelin has been rebuilding its reputation ever since. Qing Rao does not participate and does not stop her. *(See [Qing Rao's sheet](qing-rao.md#the-skull--committed-origin).)*
- **Rule:** the object never confirms the story. Not a glow, not a rattle, not a lucky bounce. The one exception in the series is the "Skull Replica Truth" incident, where a resentful — not evil, **offended** — skull demands a better backstory and Madelin provides one on the spot. Use once.

## Personality matrix

| Axis | Pole A | Pole B | Where they sit |
|------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Control | Controls events | Furnishes events | **Furnishes.** She has never tried to steer anything in her life. She hands you what you need and stays delighted. |
| Empathy | For people | For objects | **Both, and the objects are winning.** Her empathy for people is real but distractible; her empathy for a maligned stapler is absolute. |

| Self-doubt | Present | Absent | **Absent, structurally.** Not arrogance — she has simply never had a reason and nobody has given her one. |

## Voice guide

- **Sentence length:** short bursts, stacked, with exclamation carried by rhythm rather than volume. She interrupts herself to start a retrieval and finishes the sentence from inside the bag.
- **Vocabulary she overuses:** *might help!* · *hang on* · *ooh* · *poor thing* · *it's been through so much* · *I've got a—* (cut off by the retrieval) · *that's a unit* · superlatives applied to objects
- **Vocabulary she refuses:** *fake* · *junk* · *broken* · *useless* · *I don't have* — she will never say she doesn't have something; she says "not in this one," which implies another bag and the show has never confirmed one
- **Humor type:** **cheerful chaos.** The comedy is the mismatch of tone to stakes — she is genuinely having a nice time at a cosmic event. She is not oblivious; she has simply assessed the evening as *interesting* and moved on.
- **Delivery note:** bright, warm, quick, never shrill, never manic in the frantic sense. "Manic" here means *abundant*, not anxious. If she reads as nervous, it's wrong.

### Sample monologue — "The Skull Has Had a Life" (6–10 lines; the masterwork)

Delivered to the skull, at a party, with total sincerity, while holding it up.

> Okay, so. You were made in a factory, and people say that like it's the end of a sentence.
> It isn't! Do you know how many hands you went through? Somebody polished you.
> Somebody *chose* to polish you, which is more than anyone's done for me this year.
> Then the boat — and I know, I know, it's a box on a boat, that's still a boat.
> Eleven weeks on the water and you came out **glowing**, which the paperwork does not mention.
> (to the room) The paperwork does not mention the glowing.
> So we're not saying "replica." We're saying **early work.**
> Everyone's early work is embarrassing. Mine was a windowless room on the fourth floor.
> (back to the skull) You've been through so much. You're doing great.

### Sample monologue — "Not in This One" (the closest she comes to a sad scene; keep it to four seconds of air)

> Not in this one.
> (beat; she checks anyway, knowing)
> …No. Not in this one.
> (bright again, instantly) But I've got twine! Nobody ever wants the twine and then everybody wants the twine.

### Sample exchange — with Justine (the register: abundance meets verdict)

> **MADELIN:** I've got something for this.
> **JUSTINE:** For the snake.
> **MADELIN:** For the *situation.*
> **JUSTINE:** It's a snake.
> **MADELIN:** (elbow-deep) It's a situation **containing** a snake, and I have a stool.
> **JUSTINE:** A stool.
> **MADELIN:** *(producing it, delighted)* For height!
> **JUSTINE:** (a long beat; takes the stool) …Fine.

### Sample exchange — with Qing Rao (the register: generosity meets identification; the best pairing she has)

> **QING RAO:** Two-part mould. The seam's along the temporal.
> **MADELIN:** That's where it was *repaired.*
> **QING RAO:** That's where it was made.
> **MADELIN:** Both things can be where something happened!
> **QING RAO:** …That's not supported.
> **MADELIN:** (undimmed) Not *yet.*
> **QING RAO:** (a pause; she lets it stand) Hm.
> **MADELIN:** *(to the skull, thrilled)* She said "hm"!

### Sample exchange — with Jackalyn (the register: seventeen years, two methods, one joke)

> **JACKALYN:** I need us to timebox this.
> **MADELIN:** Ooh — how long's a box?
> **JACKALYN:** Fifteen minutes.
> **MADELIN:** (already digging) That's a rinse! That's a whole rinse.
> **JACKALYN:** That's — okay, yes, that's actually a really useful frame.
> **MADELIN:** *(producing a kitchen timer that is already running)* Started it before you asked.
> **JACKALYN:** (checking her wrist, then the timer, then her wrist) They don't agree.
> **MADELIN:** (warmly) They never do! Pick one!

## Backstory (committed)

- **Origin — shared with Jackalyn, and this is the load-bearing fact about both of them.** Seventeen years ago they were on the same temp assignment: eleven weeks of data entry in a windowless room on the fourth floor of a building neither can now find. They split the job. **Madelin invented lives for the objects on the desks; Jackalyn kept the count.** They have been doing exactly those two things ever since.
- **Formative incident (series-relevant).** Week six, a supervisor threw out the ficus. Not maliciously — it was dead, and it went in a skip, and nobody said anything about it. Madelin had given that plant a divorce, a lawsuit, and a sister in Perth, and she sat in a windowless room and understood, permanently, that **nothing gets to keep its story unless somebody carries it.** That is the whole engine of the character and it is never, ever stated on camera. It is why the bag is so heavy.
- **How the bag started.** After the contract ended she began carrying things people might need, because the room had taught her that the difference between a catastrophe and an inconvenience is usually whether anybody thought ahead. Seventeen years of thinking ahead, in one tote. It is not magic. It is **dread, sorted.**
- **Secret she protects.** She knows the stories aren't true. All of them. She has never once believed the skull was in a shipwreck. She does it because the alternative is a world where objects get thrown in a skip mid-divorce, and she has decided, deliberately and daily, to live in the other one. **Nobody suspects she is choosing. They think she's delightful.** She would be humiliated to be caught being deliberate.
- **What would break her on screen.** Somebody she likes correcting a story **unkindly** — not Qing Rao's "that's not supported," which is a fact and lands as weather, but a real one, meant to make her stop. She would agree, immediately and pleasantly, and put the story away, and the grin would stay on exactly as it is, and **that is the horror.** Bring it back the following episode without comment.

## Arc hooks

1. **"Early Work"** — The Skull Replica Truth incident, played properly *(`notes/dialog-jokes-incidents.md`)*. An offended skull demands a better backstory. Madelin improvises one, on the lawn, at length. The skull is satisfied. **This is the only time an object ever confirms one of her stories and it must never happen again.**
2. **"The Ficus"** *(late season, 45 seconds, no music)* — Madelin, alone, in an ordinary corridor, talking to a plant on a windowsill that nobody has watered. She gives it a life. She waters it. She leaves. The audience does not need the backstory above to feel it, and must never be told it.
3. **"Not in This One"** — One episode, the bag has nothing. The whole ensemble has to manage without a prop. Madelin stays cheerful the entire time, and it is the most anxious the show ever gets, and nobody names why.
4. **"The Third Retrieval"** *(finale)* — Bag rule 3, spent. Three retrievals in one scene, escalating, the last one impossible enough that somebody almost asks. Justine doesn't ask. Nobody asks. Retire the gag for a season.
5. **"Receipts"** — Biscuit returns a stolen object with a receipt from a store that doesn't exist, dated next Thursday. Madelin reads it, recognizes the store, and says nothing. One shot. **Never explain.**

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Let her be right about the bag more often than probability allows, within the rules above.
- Let her be genuinely, unironically kind to objects. It is the series' only reliable warmth.
- Give her Qing Rao to bounce off. Generosity versus identification is a better engine than agreement.
- Let her start things — a chant, a retrieval, a story. She has no plot power and enormous scene power.
- Keep her hands busy and her bag off the floor.

**Don't**

- Don't let the bag solve the episode. Thirty seconds, a geometry change, something to hold.
- Don't make the objects answer. One exception, once, ever.
- Don't play her as ditzy, nervous, or a scatterbrain. She is fast, competent, and choosing her tone.
- Don't let her say she doesn't have something. "Not in this one."
- Don't confuse her with **Madelyn** — different character, different building, one letter, see [README](README.md#3-madelin-and-madelyn-are-two-different-people). In every slate, script, and caption she is **MADELIN**.
- Don't explain the bag, open it to camera, or inventory it. Ever.
