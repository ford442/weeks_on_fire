# Regulation Powder — Dialog Versions

*Companion to [`../regulation-powder.md`](../regulation-powder.md). The main draft is the shootable baseline.
This page is the **scratch pad and audition room**: alternate opens, denser
pages, colder takes, softer takes. Nothing here is locked until it is pasted
into the main draft.*

**Parent scene:** [`../regulation-powder.md`](../regulation-powder.md)
**Register target:** sporting-procedure deadpan · two-hander · action-led
**Juxtaposition:** a sanctioned, scored, rule-governed match — terms, service, a conceded point, a scoring dispute — committed to absolutely, in a pitch-dark wood, at conversational volume, by two people throwing very hard.

---

## How to use this page

1. Keep the **parent** file as the current preferred cut.
2. Draft competing versions **here**, labeled, dated if useful.
3. When a version wins a beat, promote it into the parent and leave a one-line
   note under **Promotion log** below.
4. Do not delete losing versions immediately — mark them `shelved` so the room
   can still steal a line.

**Version status tags:** `active` · `preferred` · `alt` · `shelved` · `fragments`

**Special note for this scene:** the parent is **mostly blocking**. A version that
adds dialog is almost always the wrong version. The useful auditions here are
*subtractive* — fewer lines, same choreography.

## Juxtaposition (why the procedure exists)

*One short paragraph. Same family as:*

- **Monster Mash (1958 energy on a laser-snake lawn)** — full commitment to a
  party that does not belong in the frame.
- **Car Twelve, This Is Twelve** — full commitment to crisis-negotiation
  technique, correctly applied, to an elevator.
- **This scene:** full commitment to **match officiating** — service order,
  legal target area, a clause proposed and not accepted, a disputed point
  traded away in a settlement — by two unsupervised people in a forest, where
  the only scoreboard is an abandoned piano and the only official is whoever
  says the number first.

The joke is never "ha ha they talk a lot." The joke is **the show changed
channels and nobody in the cast noticed**.

Keep **keeper lines** that every version must preserve or consciously break:

- "I started building at the treeline. The no is late."
- "Then stop hiding behind furniture." (and its escalation to the tree)
- "The piano is the scoreboard." / "The piano is furniture."

- "That's not a product. That's a policy."
- "Call it nine-all and I don't file about the cabinet."

And the non-negotiables from the parent: no apology after the ice, no laughing
anywhere, nobody wins, the last note plays with nobody in frame.

## V1 — Rubella declares first

**Status:** active
**Beats covered:** A, G
**Register:** flatter, more adversarial at the top
**What it adds / changes:** Rubella opens the match instead of refusing it, which makes Lillith's hidden stockpile read as *anticipation* rather than ambush — she knew Rubella would call it. Slightly warmer, slightly less cruel.
**Risks:** loses "the no is late," which is the best line in Beat A.

### A — The Terms (V1 open)

```
RUBELLA
Match.

LILLITH
(not looking up from the ball she is already packing)
To eleven.

RUBELLA
I hadn't said a number.

LILLITH
You were going to say eleven.

RUBELLA
How long have you been out here.

LILLITH
Long enough to know you were going to say eleven.
```

### G — The Count (V1 button)

```
RUBELLA
Nine-all.

LILLITH
Ten-nine.

RUBELLA
You've been out here since eight o'clock building a warehouse for a
match I hadn't agreed to.

LILLITH
And you agreed to it.

RUBELLA
Nine-all.

LILLITH
Nine-all.
```

### Notes

- If V1 is used, Beat C's stockpile reveal needs one fewer line — the
  premeditation is already established in A and the pyramid should land as
  confirmation, not revelation.

## V2 — The colder ice

**Status:** alt
**Beats covered:** C, E
**Register:** colder, closer to Vivienne than to Lillith — use with caution
**What it adds / changes:** Lillith narrates the slab while she builds it, so the illegality is announced rather than discovered. Makes Rubella's decision not to take cover the strongest choice in the scene.
**Risks:** tips Lillith from *competitive* into *cruel*. The parent's version — silent construction, one word of warning from Rubella — is more frightening precisely because nothing is argued.

### E — The Ice (V2)

```
(Lillith lifts the slab of old crust. Breaks it on her knee.
Holds up the piece.)

LILLITH
This is the crust from November.

RUBELLA
I can see what it is.

LILLITH
It's under everything. It's been under everything the whole time.

RUBELLA
Don't.

LILLITH
Body and below.

RUBELLA
Lillith.

LILLITH
Body. And below.
```

### Notes

- If V2 is used, **cut Rubella's "I know" exchange in G** — two acknowledgements
  of the same cruelty is one too many.
- `shelved` candidate: a version where Lillith *shows* Rubella the slab and
  Rubella nods permission. Too tender. It makes the red line consensual and the
  scene stops being about a rule being broken.

## V3 — No terms at all

**Status:** alt
**Beats covered:** A–B
**Register:** faster, colder open
**What it adds / changes:** No negotiation. Lillith throws on the first frame and the rules are retrofitted between exchanges, mid-rally, at throwing pace — legal target area agreed on *after* two hits have landed.
**Risks:** loses the formal service offer, which is the only courtesy in the scene and the thing the ice later violates.

### A/B — Retrofit (V3)

```
(A ball takes Rubella in the shoulder before she has finished
walking into the clearing.)

RUBELLA
One.

LILLITH
One.

RUBELLA
(packing)
To eleven?

LILLITH
To eleven.

RUBELLA
Body and below?

(Rubella throws. Miss. Wood.)

LILLITH
Now you want terms.

RUBELLA
Now I want terms.

LILLITH
Body and below.
```

### Notes

- Works better if the piano's first cluster arrives earlier — inside the first
  four exchanges — because without terms the scene needs the scoreboard sooner.

## V4 — Wordless end

**Status:** alt
**Beats covered:** E, G
**Register:** the silent show remembering itself
**What it adds / changes:** After the ice lands, nobody speaks again. The count is held up on fingers — nine against ten — across thirty feet, twice, and then Rubella lowers her hand first and both of them walk out. No settlement, no cabinet clause, no "that's a policy."
**Risks:** loses "That's not a product. That's a policy," and loses the only moment Lillith looks at her properly. Gains an ending that matches the series' default volume.

### E/G — Fingers (V4)

```
(The ice lands. The red line. Rubella carries the grey ball to
the piano and sets it on the strings. The low note holds.

She turns. She holds up six fingers.

Lillith holds up six.

...

[F plays as written — the bough, the burial, no dialog]

...

Lillith stands out of the snow, entirely white, and holds up ten
fingers.

Rubella holds up nine.

Lillith holds up ten.

Rubella holds up nine.

Eight seconds.

Lillith lowers her hand. Rubella lowers hers a moment later — the
wrong order, and neither of them corrects it.

They walk out together.)
```

### Notes

- If V4 is used, keep the two-finger key-brushing business in G. It becomes the
  only gesture of the last ninety seconds and it carries the whole settlement.
- V4 makes the final unattended piano note substantially stronger. Consider
  holding the empty clearing four seconds longer than the parent.

## Fragments (orphan lines, not yet a full version)

- "You're bleeding." / "I'm scoring." — *too clever; it breaks the no-apology rule from the wrong side.*
- "There's a piano in the woods." / "There's always a piano in the woods." — *good, but it explains the furniture. Parked.*
- "Do you want my coat." / "I want the point." — shelved; the coat is a comfort gesture and this scene has none.
- "Is this a sport or a grievance?" / "Yes." — too quippy for Rubella, too eager for Lillith. Possible Madelyn line elsewhere.
- "Nine balls in a bathtub in a forest." — *Rubella, to nobody, in G. Cut from the parent for pace; still the best summary of the scene.*
- A version where the bough drops on **Rubella** instead: ten-nine the other way, and it makes Lillith the one who has to concede. Worth one read-through before the parent locks.
