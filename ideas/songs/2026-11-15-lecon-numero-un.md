# Leçon Numéro Un

**Genre collision:** 1960s French yé-yé pop × a language-lab teaching record (plain spoken prompts, a soft electronic beep before every answer, a gap for the student to repeat).

**Visual concept:** A 1960s language lab, with rows of wooden booths, headphones and reel-to-reel tape decks. Rubella is the teacher at the front, with Lillith at the teacher's desk playing a jangly electric guitar. The class is twelve grey geese, one per booth, each wearing headphones. Rubella says "Bonjour" and the class answers "Honk." Then "Merci", and "Honk" again. Meanwhile the front row eats the textbook and the back row stands on the bookshelf. Then on the final chorus the geese get it all at once and sing the French back in perfect four-part harmony. On "Au revoir" they leave through the open window in a V formation.

**Laugh (kid words):** *She tries to teach French to a class of geese and they only say honk, until the end, when they sing it perfectly and fly out the window.*

**BPM:** 126, bouncy yé-yé, G major

**Target length:** ~2:15 · 13 segments

## Styles

**English gloss of the French lines (for the room, not for MiniMax):**
- *V1:* Hello, class, sit down. Put on your headphones, open the book. *Bonjour* = hello.
- *Pre:* *Presque* = almost.
- *Chorus:* Lesson number one. *Encore une fois* = once more.
- *V2:* The front row is eating the book. A feather is floating in my tea. *Merci* = thank you.
- *Outro:* *Au revoir* = goodbye.
