## Tone

Dry, academic, and very fast. Two lecturers presenting a field report as if to a
conference, while their sky is a seven-year-old's hand. The register never breaks. Kids
will get the shaking and the cat, and adults get the lecture. Kid-friendly to watch, but the
humour is the adult register. **Not** the silly-drawer kid card.

## Why the figurines

The figurines are **painted resin, mouths painted closed**. The whole lecture is
voice-over. They move like stop-motion figures: small stiff turns, a pointer arm that
lifts, and sliding on the base when the globe tilts. **Zero lip-sync in the whole piece.**
Faces are 4 mm tall and never need to act.

## Script (voice-over, ~240 words in ~73s, about 195 wpm)

Delivery: both voices close-mic and dry, conference-paper cadence, no pauses for laughs. Lines
overlap the shakes; nobody stops for the weather.

**A. Establishing shot (0:00–0:15)**

> **LILLITH:** Good evening. This is a field report on enclosed weather. Population, two.
> Elevation, one shelf. Sky, glass.
> **RUBELLA:** Climate is stable. Visibility is excellent. The snow is on the ground, which is
> where we keep it.
> **LILLITH:** Our ground is resin, our tree is load-bearing, and our water has not been
> changed since purchase.
> **RUBELLA:** Don't put the water in the report.

**B. First shake (0:15–0:25)**

> **LILLITH:** Here comes the front.
> **RUBELLA:** There is no front. There is a hand.
> **LILLITH:** Barometric pressure: grip. Wind speed: whatever a seven-year-old has left after
> dinner.
> **RUBELLA:** The snow isn't falling. The snow is touring.

**C. Upside down (0:25–0:35)**

> **LILLITH:** Note the inversion. Snow now falls toward the sky, which is the floor, which is
> the sky.
> **RUBELLA:** My hem is on the ceiling. I'd like that in the report.
> **LILLITH:** Noted. Hem, ceiling. Lectern, also ceiling. Tree, holding.

**D. Settling (0:35–0:41)**

> **RUBELLA:** It settles in ninety seconds. It always settles in ninety seconds.
> **LILLITH:** Eighty-eight. The glitter is faster than the flakes. The glitter has somewhere to
> be.

**E. Second shake (0:41–0:51)**

> **LILLITH:** Second front, stronger. The tree is turning. The tree has never turned.
> **RUBELLA:** Hold the lectern.
> **LILLITH:** I am the lectern now. Recommend further study.

**F. The cat (0:51–1:01)**

> **RUBELLA:** Cat.
> **LILLITH:** A cat is not weather.
> **RUBELLA:** A cat is about to be weather.

**G. Drop (1:01–1:07):** no lines. The globe goes off the shelf.

**H. Landing (1:07–1:13)**

> **LILLITH:** Conclusion: the climate is stable.
> **RUBELLA:** Indoor weather.

## Music cue

**Original music-box waltz that keeps getting interrupted by a full orchestra.** 3/4, 84 BPM,
E♭ major. A single music-box comb plays the melody whenever the globe is still. Every shake
hits a **full romantic-orchestra storm** (timpani roll, brass, a cymbal swell) that lasts
exactly as long as the hand moves, then cuts back to the music box mid-phrase.

| Hit | Time | Shot |
|---|---|---|
| Music box in | 0:00 | 1 |
| Orchestra storm 1 | 0:15 | 2 (hand lifts) |
| Storm sustains, inverted chord | 0:25 | 3 |
| Back to music box, mid-phrase | 0:35 | 4 |
| Orchestra storm 2, bigger | 0:41 | 5 |
| Strings hold one high note, no beat | 0:51 | 6 (cat) |
| **The big drop:** full orchestra crash, then silence | 1:01 | 7 (globe falls) |
| Music box picks up exactly where it left off | 1:07 | 8 |

Voice-over sits on top of all of it, unbothered.

## Callbacks

- **The Black Cat knocks one object per appearance** and then leaves at a walk, with no
  reaction shot. Her rules are why the fall plays at real speed with no sting on the knock
  itself: the orchestra crash is on the cut to the inside, not on her paw.
- **"Indoor weather."** Filing an impossible thing under weather. It's Rubella's closing line,
  spent as a reference and never adjudicated.
- **"It's not broken. It's early."** cousin: Lillith's "Eighty-eight. I timed it." It's the
  same certainty applied to glitter, a reference to the line and not a quote.

## Do-not

The child is a hand only, never a face. The globe never cracks, leaks or breaks. The cat is
never cute, never chases and never looks back. No Vivienne or Liliane (Rubella and Lillith
only). No readable text on the chalkboard. The figurines' mouths never move.

## Why it's funny

Two serious lecturers, completely calm, reporting on their whole sky being shaken like a
toy. The bigger the storm, the drier the paper gets.

**Difficulty: 3 / 5.** The figurines are cheap (no faces, no lip-sync), but they need to stay
consistent across 8 clips, especially upside down.

**Riskiest shot: #3, upside down.** Models tend to turn the globe right-side-up or let the
figurines fall off the base. Hardener: *figurines glued to the base, which is now on top;
snow drifts toward the glass dome at the bottom of frame.* If it fails, cut #3 and run B
straight into D. The script still works.
