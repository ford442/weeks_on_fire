STYLE:

Slow-disco dress-code liturgy at 98 BPM. Dry analog bass, muted four-on-the-floor that is felt more than heard, mirrorball as a dull click not a sparkle, ash as room tone, no bright hats. Deadpan baritone vocal, professionally neutral, zero vibrato, close-mic — a bouncer reciting policy, not a crooner, not the Pizza Guy. Dark top end; the city fire is orange in the picture, not in the mix. No belting. No wink. **Structural flaw:** the kick never lands on the word “Approved.” It lands one beat later, on the stiletto click, every chorus, every time. Do not quantize it. Do not “fix” the downbeat. Mood: heels or no entry while the skyline burns. Runtime ~1:50–2:10. Use a dry baritone lead; optional distant crowd hush, never a gang vocal.

NOTES:

**What the song is**
Door-policy liturgy for **The Velvet Rope at the End of the World**. The bouncer is sincere. The skyline is on fire. Neither of those facts revises the dress code. She produces emergency stilettos from a purse. He says Approved. The kick is late on purpose.

The joke is the policy, not the apocalypse. Nobody winks. Nobody says “end of the world” like a chorus.

**Craft (keep this off-mic)**
- *Neon Exit Sign* owns mascara / empire / walking away in pearls. This is **the door**, not the exit.
- *Pepperoni or Margherita* owns professional neutrality as a delivery superpower. This is professional neutrality as a **stanchion**.
- *House of Mirrors Rally* owns the ping-pong metronome. Do not put a ball in this mix.
- Do not name The Two. The gown may be theirs. The bouncer does not care.
