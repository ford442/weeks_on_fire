*Drawer, not a farm. Text only. 2026-10-19. `promote?: no` on every card.*

**Rules:** [`funny-rules.md`](funny-rules.md) · **Lane model:** [`../songs/Big_City.md`](../songs/Big_City.md)
(hair so tall it's a city, sung completely straight) · **Song format:** `STYLE:` / `LYRICS:`
blocks as in `songs/*.md` and `songs/_drawer/`
**Cast locks:** [`../characters/building-cast.md`](../characters/building-cast.md) ·
[`../characters/bible/`](../characters/bible/)
**Farm:** songs [#18](https://github.com/ford442/weeks_on_fire/issues/18) /
[#33](https://github.com/ford442/weeks_on_fire/issues/33). Not #42.

Big City works because it never winks. The hair is a city; the song treats that as a
fact and builds on it. Every card below does the same: **one absurd literal premise in
the title**, sung sincerely in a real genre, verses that escalate the premise, last verse
furthest, and a chorus a kid can sing back after one listen. Spoken tags are deadpan.

None of these are hair songs — Big City owns that image. No paperwork premises. No
`songs/*.md` file, no `audioFile`, no bound `songId`. Promotion is a copy into `songs/`
(with `added:` frontmatter) by whoever promotes, after a Nova pass, then codegen.

## 1. There's a Cloud in the Dryer

| field | value |
|---|---|
| premise | A real rain cloud lives in the basement dryer. |
| genre / bpm / key / length | 1960s classroom filmstrip jingle / 116 / C major / ~2:05 |
| who sings | Lillith (bright narrator) verse 1, Rubella (dry) verse 2, both verse 3; Qing Rao spoken intro; Rubella spoken outro |
| world | building laundry room → building exterior (last verse only) |
| neighbor it must not replace | *Rinse Cycle* · *The Laundromat Saints* · *Three Dryers One Opinion* (drawer) |
| start JOB → last JOB | the cloud is in the dryer → the building is in the cloud |
| promote? | no |

STYLE:

Sincere 1960s classroom filmstrip jingle, 116 BPM, C major, about two minutes. Bright
xylophone and glockenspiel melody doubling the vocal, plucked upright bass, brushed snare,
a small muted brass section with cheerful stabs on the chorus, and a children's choir
answering the chorus hook. A single clean filmstrip "page-turn" chime between every
section, same pitch every time. Two female leads: one bright and teacherly on verse 1,
one dry and slightly raspy on verse 2, both together on verse 3. Spoken intro and outro
are dry, close-mic, no music under them. Earnest, educational, tape-warm, mono-leaning
mix. No sound-effect jokes, no laugh track. Use Lillith and Rubella voices, with one
spoken line from Qing Rao.

LYRICS:

There's a cloud in the dryer!

And it's raining on me!

[Verse 2 — Rubella]
I put my socks in, warm and dry
They came out full of rain
I put a towel in, set to high
It thundered once again
The lightning dried my sweater
The rainbow lit the drain

It made a hurricane

[Chorus]
There's a cloud in the dryer!
(A cloud in the dryer!)
Round and round and round it goes
There's a cloud in the dryer!
It's fluffy and it's wet
As wet as it can be
There's a cloud in the dryer
And it's raining on me!

[Bridge — Lillith, teacherly, over glockenspiel only]
Let's review!
Water goes up — (up!)
Cloud comes down — (down!)
Cloud goes round — (round and round!)

[Verse 3 — Lillith and Rubella]
The cloud grew bigger every spin
It filled the laundry hall

1. **Appears** — the dryer door swings open and a fat white cloud billows out into the
   laundry room. Rubella stands holding an empty basket, calm.
2. **Multiplies** — the cloud fills the room to the ceiling and rains; tiny lightning

3. **Transforms** — exterior wide: the whole apartment building lifts off its lot on a
   giant cloud and floats over the town, raining gently on the street below.

**Do-not:** no readable labels on the dryer. Qing Rao says her one line and does not sing.
No Glam bottle in the laundry room.

Sincere late-80s hair metal anthem, 138 BPM, E minor verses lifting to a G major chorus,

## 3. The Bathtub Is the Ocean

| field | value |
|---|---|
| premise | Rubella's bathtub is the actual ocean. It has tides, ships, and a whale. |
| genre / bpm / key / length | Sea shanty / 100 / D minor / ~2:40 |
| who sings | Rubella lead (dry, captain); building gang chorus (Lillith loudest); Lillith spoken intro; Pizza Guy spoken outro |
| world | ladies' building unit, bathroom → the open sea (last verse only) |
| neighbor it must not replace | *Heavy Water* · *Well Fall* · *Lace Waterfall* |
| start JOB → last JOB | a rubber duck sets sail → the building sails out to sea |
| promote? | no |

STYLE:

Sincere sea shanty, 100 BPM, D minor, about two minutes forty. Starts a cappella: one
dry, slightly raspy female lead calling each line and a gang chorus of mixed voices
answering. Foot stomps and hand claps on the beat from the first chorus. Concertina and
fiddle enter on verse two, a low tuba-like bass on verse three, a bodhrán on the last
chorus. The lead never sings louder than she has to; the gang gets louder every chorus.
Close room sound on the first verse widening to a big open hall by the end. Earnest
working-song energy, no pirate voices, no "arr." Use Rubella and Lillith voices, gang
chorus of the building cast, and one male spoken line at the end.

[Chorus]

The bathtub is the ocean, oh!

   delivery uniform holding a pizza box level at chest height, unhurried.

**Do-not:** the Pizza Guy is never named, never hurries, never looks at the whale, and says
only the napkin line. The whale is friendly; nobody is in danger.

## 4. The Moon Is a Disco Ball

| field | value |
|---|---|
| premise | Lillith covered the moon in mirrors. Now it's a disco ball and the sky dances. |
| genre / bpm / key / length | Late-70s disco / 120 / A minor verse, C major chorus / ~2:45 |
| who sings | Lillith verse 1 and chorus lead; Rubella verse 2; both verse 3; Rubella spoken intro; Lillith spoken outro |
| world | building exterior at night, the sky above it (not the night lot) |
| neighbor it must not replace | *The Moon Is Collect* · *Second Sun* · *Deep Space Drift* |
| start JOB → last JOB | the moon starts spinning → the sun shows up at 3 a.m. to dance |
| promote? | no |

STYLE:

Sincere late-70s disco, 120 BPM, A minor verses opening into a C major chorus, about two
minutes forty-five. Four-on-the-floor kick, open hi-hat on the off-beats, octave-jumping

bright brass hits, a hand-clap on every backbeat. Lead vocal one: bright, conspiratorial,
gleeful. Lead vocal two: dry, slightly raspy, cabaret-cool, singing like she's already
bored of the miracle. Backing vocals answer "Dance!" and "Spin!" in the chorus. A four-bar
breakdown of bass and claps before the last chorus, then the strings come back up. Glossy,
wide, earnest — no parody, no laugh samples. Use Lillith and Rubella voices.

The moon is a disco ball
Spinning up so high
The moon is a disco ball

[Breakdown — bass and claps]

[Verse 3 — Lillith and Rubella]

## 6. My Coffee Is So Strong It Walked Away

| field | value |
|---|---|
| premise | Rubella's coffee is so strong it grew legs and left. |
| genre / bpm / key / length | Mid-90s pop-punk / 180 / D major / ~2:00 |
| who sings | Rubella lead (dry, raspy, fast); Lillith gang shouts; Lillith spoken intro; Rubella spoken outro |

**Do-not:** not Justine's cup — her cup is a countdown and never leaves her hand. The mug
