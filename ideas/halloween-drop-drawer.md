Four drop-triggered joke interrupts, 6–15s each, all on the same Halloween street (Elmwood,
the porch at 117 and the houses either side). Silly register: a kid can say every laugh
back. Each card is one world, one visible change on the drop, and calm faces in a loud
night.

**Already spent elsewhere — do not farm here:** laser snakes and the shed (Ep3 scenes) ·
the skeleton union break and PROBABLY FINE (Ep3 lawn, S&P) · Monster Mash lawn staging,

**Counts:** funny with the sound off: **4 of 4**. Animals: **3 of 4** (bear, Biscuit, the

**Callbacks, spent as references** (`notes/callback-bank.md`): the Pizza Guy arriving

## Clip math

Grok Imagine clips are **6s, 10s, 15s**. Single-clip cards: generate the start still,
image-to-video to the last still. Two-clip cards chain: the last frame of clip 1 is the
start still of clip 2. Drops land in the back half.

**Default still style** (append to every prompt): *photoreal cinematic still, 2.39:1,
Grok Imagine building-cast, no real actor names, locked camera, 35mm, fine grain, Halloween
night, warm porch light, orange jack-o'-lantern glow, Grok Imagine style.* **Rubella** = a
glamorous, exhausted woman with long dark hair and blood-red lipstick in a modern black
lace evening dress. **Lillith** = a glamorous woman in black lace with flawless,
never-smudged mascara and a bright, conspiratorial half-smile. Faces calm unless the card
names an end reaction.

### 1. `take-one` — A Bear Comes Trick-or-Treating

- **id:** `take-one` · **world:** 117 Elmwood porch · **length:** 10s
- **laugh:** *A real bear dressed as a ghost comes trick-or-treating, and she gives it one
  candy.*
- **drop:** **appears** — at 6s, after two little kids (a ghost, a witch), the third
  trick-or-treater steps up into the porch light: a full-size brown bear wearing a white
  bedsheet with two eye holes, holding open a pillowcase in one paw. Rule of three, wrong
  third.
- **end reaction:** Rubella drops exactly one candy bar in the pillowcase and says, flat:
  *"Nice costume."* The bear looks in the pillowcase, turns, and pads off down the steps,
  the sheet dragging.
- **Clip plan:** one **10s** clip, used whole. 0–3s kid ghost takes one, leaves. 3–5s kid
  witch takes one, leaves. **6s drop:** the bear's head rises into the light. 7–9s one
  candy. 9–10s the line, the bear going.
- **Start still (copy-ready):** *Rubella seated in a folding lawn chair beside the front
  door of a suburban porch at night, a big plastic bowl of wrapped candy on her lap, a
  World's Okayest Tenant mug on the porch rail beside her, two small children in homemade
  costumes — a bedsheet ghost and a pointy-hat witch — climbing the steps holding plastic
  pumpkin buckets, carved jack-o'-lanterns glowing on every step, string lights, house
  number 117 beside the door, Rubella's face calm and unimpressed* + default style.
- **Last still:** the same porch, Rubella holding one candy bar over a pillowcase, a large
  brown bear in a white bedsheet ghost costume with two eye holes standing on the top step,
  pillowcase open, the two kids small on the sidewalk behind, unbothered, Rubella's face
  exactly as calm.
- **Music feel:** a creepy-cute harpsichord tiptoe that drops into one big tuba-and-timpani
  stomp as the bear rises.
- **do-not:** the bear never roars, never threatens, never takes more than one. No kid is
  scared. Rubella does not look twice. No sign is read for the joke (a PLEASE TAKE ONE card
  may sit in the bowl as background).
- **promote?:** no

### 2. `snowball-pumpkin` — The Cat Pushes One Little Pumpkin

- **id:** `snowball-pumpkin` · **world:** Elmwood sidewalk, porch steps on a slope ·
  **length:** 10s
- **laugh:** *The cat pushes one tiny pumpkin and it rolls down the street getting bigger
  and bigger like a snowball, until it's as big as a house.*
- **drop:** **transforms** — at 5s, the little pumpkin bouncing down the sidewalk starts
  growing as it rolls, picking up dry leaves, a garden gnome, a rake, a plastic tombstone,
  until it is a house-sized orange ball trundling down the hill past calm trick-or-treaters
  who step aside without hurrying.
- **end reaction:** none. The cat has already turned to look at something else (a moth on
  the porch light). No reaction shot after the knock.
- **Clip plan:** one **10s** clip, used whole. 0–2s the cat sits on the top step beside a
  row of three pumpkins, smallest last. 2–3s one clean push of the paw, real weight, no
  music sting on the knock. 3–5s the small pumpkin bumps down the steps onto the sloped
  sidewalk. **5s drop:** it starts to grow. 5–10s it rolls away down the street, huge,
  carrying the yard junk; the cat, foreground, faces the other way.
- **Start still (copy-ready):** *A sleek black cat, glowing yellow eyes, short coat, real
  animal proportions, tail carried high as a weather vane, sitting on the top step of a
  suburban porch at night beside a row of three carved jack-o'-lanterns, the smallest
  one closest to her paw, the sidewalk below sloping steeply downhill into a street of
  glowing porches and string lights, a few small trick-or-treaters in costumes far down the
  hill, photoreal, not cute, filmic* + default style.
- **Last still:** the same top step, the cat now facing the porch light, tail high, two
  pumpkins left on the step; far down the hill a giant rolling orange pumpkin the size of a
  house, studded with leaves, a gnome and a rake handle, passing a group of small
  trick-or-treaters who have calmly stepped onto a lawn to let it by.
- **Music feel:** a one-note plucked bass walking down the scale; on the drop it turns into
  a big rolling marimba-and-brass glissando that keeps going downhill.
- **do-not:** one knock only — she does not touch anything else. Not sped up, not slow
  motion, no sting on the push. No squashed anything; the giant pumpkin only rolls away.
  No Pizza Guy in this shot (he and the cat don't share an exit). Keep her in town.
- **promote?:** no

### 3. `biscuit-candy-parade` — The Dog Steals the Candy Bowl

- **id:** `biscuit-candy-parade` · **world:** a neighbour's porch and the Elmwood sidewalk
  · **length:** 15s (two clips)
- **laugh:** *The dog steals the whole candy bowl, and every kid on the street follows the
  dog instead.*
- **drop:** **multiplies** — at 7s, as Biscuit trots down the middle of the sidewalk with
  the bowl, one trick-or-treater falls in behind her, then three, then a whole long parade
  of costumed kids, two by two, all the way up the street, following a poodle with a bowl.
- **end reaction:** Rubella, on the porch, watching the parade go: *"Whose dog."* Lillith
  says nothing; she watches it like it is going to plan. Nobody answers.
- **Clip plan:**
  1. **10s** clip, trimmed to 9s. 0–3s an empty porch table with a big candy bowl on it,
     Rubella and Lillith on the porch swing beside it. 3–5s Biscuit trots up the steps,
     takes the bowl in her mouth off the table (never from a hand), and turns. 5–9s she
     trots down the steps and down the center of the sidewalk; **7s drop:** kids start to
     fall in behind her. Last frame = the parade stretching up the street.
  2. **6s** clip, used whole. Start still = clip 1's last frame. 0–3s the parade keeps
     going past the porch. 3–4s Rubella: *"Whose dog."* 4–6s tag, wide: the empty table,
     the parade gone, and the bowl back on the table, full, with a small paper receipt
     tucked under it (background only; dated next Thursday, barcode one digit short — never
     read on screen).
- **Start still (copy-ready):** *Rubella and Lillith seated calmly on a white porch swing
  on a suburban porch at night, a small table beside them holding a large bowl heaped with
  wrapped candy, carved jack-o'-lanterns on the rail, string lights, a fluffy white
  standard poodle groomed in a way that suggests somebody cares and is not present,
  wearing a small plain undyed bandana, trotting unhurried up the porch steps, a few
  trick-or-treaters in costumes on the sidewalk below* + default style.
- **Last still (clip 1):** the porch swing in the foreground, Rubella and Lillith calm,
  the table empty; down the sidewalk the white poodle trotting away down the center with
  the candy bowl in her mouth, a long double line of small trick-or-treaters in costumes
  following her up the street into the string lights.
- **Music feel:** a sneaky muted-trumpet walk that drops into a full kazoo-and-snare
  marching band as the parade forms.
- **do-not:** Biscuit never runs, never gets chased, never gets called and comes, no leash,
  no reaction shot from her. Bandana stays **plain** (nothing glowing tonight on this porch).
  Nobody answers "Whose dog." The receipt is never the joke.
- **promote?:** no

### 4. `the-moon-gets-a-face` — She Plugs In One Pumpkin

- **id:** `the-moon-gets-a-face` · **world:** 117 Elmwood porch, the street beyond ·
  **length:** 15s (two clips)
- **laugh:** *She plugs in one pumpkin and it lights up every pumpkin on the street, then
  the moon gets a pumpkin face too.*
- **drop:** **appears** — at 6s, her jack-o'-lantern blazes on; then the pumpkins on the
  next porch, and the next, a chain of light running down the whole street; on the last hit
  a glowing jack-o'-lantern face appears on the full moon. Wrong scale, one plug.
- **end reaction:** none from the cast. The porch light clicks on and the Pizza Guy is
  standing there, exactly at the peak.
- **Clip plan:**
  1. **10s** clip, trimmed to 9s. 0–4s Lillith on the porch steps unwraps a glowing vacuum
     tube from a dish towel like an egg, sets it inside her carved pumpkin, and plugs a
     length of cloth-covered speaker wire into the porch outlet. Rubella beside her, a full
     fire extinguisher at her feet, unmentioned. **6s drop:** the pumpkin lights, the chain
     runs down the street. 8–9s the moon gets its face. Last frame = the moon face over the
     glowing street, the two women calm.
  2. **6s** clip, used whole. Start still = clip 1's last frame. 0–1s the porch light
     *click*. The Pizza Guy is on the top step in the red uniform, box at chest height; he
     has already checked his phone (23%, one beat). 1–4s *"Order for… Rubelle."* Rubella
     takes the box. 4–6s *"Extra napkins are in the bag."* He turns and walks down the steps
     at his pace, not looking at the moon.
- **Start still (copy-ready):** *Lillith sitting on the steps of a suburban porch at night,
  safety glasses pushed up on her head, carefully unwrapping a glowing vacuum tube from a
  dish towel beside a big carved jack-o'-lantern, a coil of cloth-covered speaker wire
  running to the porch outlet, Rubella standing beside her holding a candy bowl, a full red
  fire extinguisher at her feet, house number 117 beside the door, a quiet street of
  unlit pumpkins on the porches behind them, a plain full moon in the sky* + default style.
- **Last still (clip 1 / start of clip 2):** the same porch, Lillith's pumpkin blazing
  orange, every pumpkin on every porch down the street lit in a long glowing chain, a
  huge glowing jack-o'-lantern face on the full moon above the rooftops, Lillith watching
  it with a bright, conspiratorial half-smile, Rubella calm.
- **Pizza Guy prompt (paste in clip 2):** *Red delivery uniform, clean, correctly worn ·
  dead eyes — not menacing, not sad, the eyes of a man on his fourth stop of a normal shift
  · perfect posture, the straightest vertical line in the frame · professionally neutral
  face · pizza box held at chest height · standing on the top step in the porch light,
  name tag illegible,* the glowing moon face behind him that he does not acknowledge.
- **Music feel:** a twinkly music-box loop that drops into a full spooky organ chord, one
  stab per house down the street, the biggest on the moon; out on a single doorbell-dry
  porch-light click.
- **do-not:** nobody explains how it works and the tube is not named. Lillith does not
  cheer; her energy is in the pumpkin. Nobody corrects "Rubelle." The Pizza Guy never looks
  at the moon, never flinches, never asks. No crane up to the moon (that move belongs to
  the Ep3 finale); locked camera, the moon is just in the sky.
- **promote?:** no
