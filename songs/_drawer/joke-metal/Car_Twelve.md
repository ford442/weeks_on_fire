STYLE:

1970s–80s heavy metal played completely straight, 120 BPM, A minor, about two minutes ten. Elevator metal, committed, no wink. One heavy riff on one overdriven guitar through a warm vintage tube amp, bass doubling it, dry 1970s kit, no double-kick. The verse vocal is the elevator itself: a vocoded low tenor at PA-speaker bandwidth, roughly 250 Hz to 3.5 kHz, perfectly in tune, one flat floor number every two bars, no words except the number, no emotion, no ornament. Chorus: a gang of stacked Lillith and Rubella double-tracks chants the title in unison, hard but not shouted, riff locked underneath. Nothing else is sung, only floor numbers and the title. At the break the whole band slows to a stop over four bars, like a car stopping between floors, and leaves one sustained in-tune hum for about six seconds, a steady dryer-like hum with no melody, then the riff restarts. After the break, one melodic guitar solo of about eight bars, warm, no shred. Bass and kit dry and close, small concrete room, mixed like a 1978 record. No djent, no dropped tuning, no scream, no growl, no blast beat, no occult lyric, no Kenji, no product name, no chime, no ding, no door sound, no spoken words other than numbers. Structural flaw: the elevator announces twelve, then eleven, then twelve again. It never reaches any higher floor, never corrects itself, and nobody reacts.

NOTES:

The flaw: twelve, then eleven, then twelve again. The title chant keeps saying twelve over the eleven verse, so the car and the gang disagree and neither moves. The sequence stops at twelve and does not resolve to a floor anyone could confirm.

Why it is not the neighbors:
- **Not Negotiating with the Elevator.** That is the tango: it loses a beat per floor, owns the floor-number countdown ("Eleven. Eleven. Nine." down to "One.") and the counteroffer. This is a riff song. No counteroffer, no countdown, no tango, no bandoneón.
- **Not After Is a Floor.** No directory, no AFTER, no hovering. The car never leaves the numbers it is given.
- **Not the scene.** *Car Twelve, This Is Twelve* is a title echo only. Nobody is in the car, nobody presses anything, and the alarm button stays unpressed and unmentioned.
- **Not thirteen.** The service diagram's thirteenth floor is a two-second, never-mentioned open file. It is not sung, not implied by a skipped number, and not named in this file's lyric.
- **Not Ultra Screech / Well Fall / Halloween Snake Battle.** No ad, no fall, no fight.
- **Not the Lace pack.** Those stay sincere.
- **Not The Other Button.** Bank-mate pop song: that one argues about buttons on a landing; this one only announces.

Callbacks pulled (`notes/callback-bank.md`): *Car Twelve / Twelve / 12½* numbers (the Elevator says "Twelve.") · the elevator hum is the laundromat dryer's note, so the held break hum is that note, not a chord · *Nothing happening, extremely well* (a written silence; here the break is a held hum, about six seconds, not the nine-second hold on twelve, which stays unscored).

Bed for: a freight-car beat or a stuck-between-floors tile. Promote only after a Nova pass. She sets the fade; she does not set a mashup.
