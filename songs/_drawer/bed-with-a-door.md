STYLE:

Instrumental production bed at 100 BPM in G minor, one minute ten, no fade.
Count-in is four taps of a pen on a clipboard, dry, then the band on the one.
Drums are a tight small kit, brushes on the snare, kick on one and three, no fills.
Upright bass walking a two-bar figure, slightly ahead of the beat, like someone keeping a schedule.
Muted trumpet plays a four-bar phrase, once every sixteen bars, never developed.
Rhodes comps on the off-beats only, thin, mono.
The arrangement is a loop that knows it is a loop: sixteen bars, four times, no variation on purpose.
At 1:08 a heavy office door latches — one real latch sound — and every instrument stops on it.
Two seconds of room tone after the latch, then nothing.
No reverb tails, no ring-out, no outro. The latch is the last note.
Dry, close, mid-century office ambience.

NOTES:

Why it is not the neighbors:
- **Not Cue Sheets.** Nova's second record is sung, in mix language, with a Session Voice.
  This has no voice at all and is not sequenced by Nova; it is Julian's shelf.
- **Not Marble Tick Tack.** No cabinet, no magnets, no night-lot, no Roley. The door here is
  an office door, not the cabinet coming home.
- **Not Daisy / Hanshaw.** No heart, no period, no tandem.
- **Not Ultra Screech / Big City.** No guitar, no Glamora, no hair.
- **Not Lightning / Well Fall.** No warehouse, no drop.

The joke is procedural: Julian refuses to slate a bed with no door, so his own record is
seventy seconds and a latch. Any further *Slate* tracks would need the same rule — a
literal out, never a fade.
