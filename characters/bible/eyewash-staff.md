# EyeWash Station — Oz · The Continuity Voice · Marguerite Flood · The S&P Skeleton

**Status:** concept → active (*Master Control, 4 AM* is drafted; the ident pack is specced)
**Primary episodes:** *Master Control, 4 AM* · station idents and fake sponsors · *2AM Laundromat Slow Dance* (corner TV) · the Telethon · any cutaway that needs a frame
**Role in one sentence (the unit):** The staff of a low-power affiliate nobody subscribes to, nobody cancels, and everybody has apparently seen — four people (three of them arguably not people) who process the impossible through paperwork.

> Concept home: [`../../ideas/eyewash-station.md`](../../ideas/eyewash-station.md). Idents: [`../../ideas/eyewash-idents.md`](../../ideas/eyewash-idents.md). Drafted scene: [`../../notes/scenes/master-control-4am.md`](../../notes/scenes/master-control-4am.md).
> **The Radio Voice (Episode 2)** is documented at the [bottom of this file](#the-radio-voice-episode-2) because the only thing anyone needs to know about him is his relationship to the Continuity Voice. He is not staff. He may not be anything.

## What we never confirm on screen

**This is the most important section in the file.** EyeWash Station works because it
*slides*. The moment any of these is answered, the network becomes a plot and stops being a
frame. All of these are **locked as unresolved** — see `docs/season-arc.md`, "EyeWash
diegesis (locked)."

| Never confirmed | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Whether anything is haunted or unscheduled** | Oz says one. Marguerite says the other. The scene ends. Any draft that adjudicates is the wrong draft. |
| **Whether the Continuity Voice is the Episode 2 Radio Voice** | Deliberately unresolved, permanently. Possibly the same person on a different shift. Never state it, never wink at it, never cast the same performer in a way the audience could verify. |
| **Whether the Continuity Voice is a person at all** | Never seen. **Should never be seen.** No body, no booth, no reveal, no silhouette. |
| **Whether Marguerite actually holds the license** | She says she does. She has never produced it. Nobody presses. |
| **Where the unlogged programs come from** | They are on the log. The log is the log. That is the entire answer Oz has and it is sufficient. |
| **Whether the tower exists / where it is** | Strongly recommended that it exists. Do not shoot it. The Kestrel Ridge antenna is never adjudicated either — dead weather repeater or EyeWash transmitter, Riley has a theory, nobody rules. |
| **What channel it is** | "Channel 12½" is a tagline, not a fact. Viewers describe programs identically and none of them agree on the channel. |
| **Whether characters know they're on it** | **Nobody comments on the frame.** No one looks at camera and names the network. The series does not air an episode of itself until a finale. |

# Oz — Overnight Master Control

**Status:** active · **Role in one sentence:** The only human employee we ever see on the air side; midnight to sign-off, alone at the board, logging the impossible without escalating it.

## Logline bio

Oz runs master control from midnight to sign-off. He treats a monitor showing the future the
way you'd treat a flickering bulb: notes it, logs it, does not escalate. He is not brave and
not curious. He is **procedural**, and he is the warmest character in the series.

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Cardigan. Always. Over a collared shirt that has been through a lot of washes
- Thermos, filled before the shift. A mug that is not from the station
- Reading glasses pushed up on his forehead, used maybe twice a night
- **The laminated log sheet** and a **grease pencil** he caps between entries
- Monitor-wall glow as his only light source — twenty CRTs, four across and five down, none of them showing the same station
- Never hurried. He is the only character who moves at the Pizza Guy's pace without being the Pizza Guy
- **Never change without a story reason:** the cardigan · the log on paper · he caps the pencil · **he never looks at the monitor that shows the room a few seconds ahead** — not from fear, from professional discipline

**Portrait prompt:** *"Cinematic medium shot of a calm older man in a worn cardigan seated at a broadcast master-control desk at 4 a.m., wall of twenty CRT monitors glowing behind and around him each showing something different, thermos and a laminated log sheet on the console, grease pencil in hand, reading glasses pushed up on his forehead, faint kind expression, windowless room, warm practical lighting against monitor glow, not creepy, filmic, Grok Imagine style"*

## Personality matrix

| Axis | Pole A | Pole B | Where they sit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control | Over events | Over the log | **The log.** He has no authority over what airs and total authority over what is recorded. |
| Empathy | For people | For equipment | **Equipment, out loud; people, quietly.** He talks to the board because it answers in the only way he trusts — by working or not working. |
| Chaos tolerance | High | Doesn't register | **Registers, and files.** Unlike the Pizza Guy, Oz *notices*. He simply doesn't escalate. The distinction is the whole character. |
| Verbal style | short | ornate | **Short, practical, faintly kind.** Complete sentences, no rhetoric. |
| Curiosity | Asks | Doesn't | **Doesn't — professionally.** He would find out if it were his job. It is not his job. |

## Voice guide

- **Overuses:** *it's not haunted, it's unscheduled* · *I don't program it, I just make sure it goes out clean* · *clean* · *was it on the log* · *there you are* · *not yet* · timecodes read aloud as four digits — *oh-three-four-two*
- **Refuses:** *scary* · *impossible* · *we should call someone* · escalation of any kind · anyone's theory, including his own
- **Humor type:** dry and warm and entirely accidental. He is not joking; he is being accurate in a room where accuracy is absurd. Play it **warm, not creepy** — the dread is structural, the man is fine.
- **Delivery note:** quiet, practical, unhurried, mid-register. Talks to the equipment at conversational volume and to people slightly softer.

### Sample monologue — "Clean" (6–10 lines; to the board, which is his colleague)

> There you are.
> (a pot, a quarter inch) You've been doing that since Tuesday. I'm not going to mention it if you don't.
> Thirty years I've been asked what the job is. The job is: it goes out clean.
> Not good. Not true. **Clean.** Levels where they should be, no dropouts, audio settles by the front porch of the second bar.
> People think the overnight is where you find things out. It isn't.
> The overnight is where you find out that nobody's going to tell you.
> (he logs a line; caps the pencil)
> Oh-four-eleven. Bump two, clean.
> (to the monitor showing the room, from a few seconds ahead — without looking at it)
> Not yet.

### Sample exchange — with the Pizza Guy *(two men, zero questions — the best pairing in the series)*

Full version on [the Pizza Guy's sheet](the-pizza-guy.md#voice-guide). Short form:

> **OZ:** You're not on the log.
> **PIZZA GUY:** No.
> **OZ:** *(writing)* Delivery. Unscheduled.

### Sample exchange — with Marguerite *(established register; neither of them is lying)*

> **MARGUERITE:** The complaint says a viewer saw something.
> **OZ:** They did.
> **MARGUERITE:** Was it scheduled?
> **OZ:** No.
> **MARGUERITE:** Then it wasn't broadcast, it was **weather.** File it under weather.
> **OZ:** It was indoors.
> **MARGUERITE:** *(writing)* Indoor weather.

## Backstory (committed)

- **Origin.** He came in as a part-time transmitter tech when the station still had two of them and stayed because the hours suited a life he no longer has. Thirty-odd years. He has outlasted four owners, three formats, and every explanation anybody offered him.
- **Formative incident.** Early on, he escalated once. He called somebody at three in the morning about a feed that should not have existed, and the somebody came in, and looked at it, and said *"is it going out clean?"* and went home. **Nothing bad happened. That is what changed him.** He learned that escalation is not a tool the world has, and that a clean signal is.
- **Private life.** A house he sleeps in during daylight. A dog that has been dead two years and whose bowl is still by the door, which is never shown and never mentioned. The thermos was a gift.
- **Secret he protects.** He knows exactly which monitor is the one showing the room a few seconds ahead, and he has known for years, and he has never once looked. He could. **The not-looking is a discipline he practices nightly and would be embarrassed to have named.**
- **What would break him.** A dropout. A genuine, unrecoverable, on-air failure that was his. Not the cosmic feed — a **level**. He would be wrecked by it in a way that no amount of unscheduled programming could achieve.

## Do / Don't

**Do** — let him log everything and escalate nothing; let him talk to the equipment; play him warm; give him the Pizza Guy whenever possible.
**Don't** — don't let him look at that monitor; don't let him theorize; don't let him be frightened; don't make him the audience's explainer; don't cast the room as creepy — the room is calm and the clock is not.

# The Continuity Voice

**Status:** recurring (audio only) · **Role in one sentence:** The between-programs announcer who delivers apocalyptic copy in the cadence of a tire commercial, and who is never seen.

## Logline bio

Warm, over-rehearsed, institutional. The Continuity Voice announces things that have already
happened as though they are coming up next, and sells the end of the world with the same
lift it gives a rinse duration. It is the cheapest asset in the series and possibly the best.

## Appearance

**None. Ever.** No body, no booth, no silhouette, no hand on a fader, no reflection, no
"we almost see." If a storyboard contains a shot of the Continuity Voice, the storyboard is
wrong. The only permitted visual is a **speaker, a card, or a monitor**.

- **Signature "props":** a station card · bars and tone · the test pattern · a speaker grille in the corner of a room
- **Never change without a story reason:** never seen · never named · never addressed by another character *to its face*, because there is no face

## Voice guide — VO / Minimax notes

This is a performance spec, not a character sketch. Hand this block to the VO or the generator.

- **Register:** warm mid-range, institutional, over-rehearsed. The sound of somebody who has read this copy four hundred times and still gives it the lift.
- **Cadence:** commercial. Rising on the penultimate phrase, resolving down. **The copy is apocalyptic and the delivery is a tire ad.** That gap is the entire joke and it must never be acknowledged by the read.
- **Tempo:** slightly too slow. Half a step behind a real continuity announcer.
- **Never:** menace, irony, a knowing pause, a smile in the voice, or emphasis on the strange word. It reads *"and one character who knows"* exactly as it reads *"please stand by."*
- **Pitch/structure flaw (established):** on [`Closed for Cosmic Reasons`](../../songs/Closed_for_Cosmic_Reasons.md) it sings the title **always a half-step flat**. On [`Rinse Cycle`](../../songs/Rinse_Cycle.md) the phrases are nine bars long and the resolutions are late. Keep the wrongness structural — never performed.
- **Do not use** the Continuity Voice on [`Heart Rate Optimal`](../../songs/Heart_Rate_Optimal.md) (explicitly ruled out in that song's notes) or as a third voice in any two-hander. *(`notes/scenes/README.md`: Natural Ventilation has no third voice. If a draft adds one, the draft is wrong.)*

### Sample continuity copy (6–10 lines; a full break)

> Coming up next — something you'll want to sit down for. Please sit down.
> We'll return to your program. We always do.
> The following contains flashing lights, unresolved chords, and one character who knows.
> Stay with us.
> *(bars and tone, four bars, one of which isn't a tone)*
> That was a rerun. So is this.
> You've been watching. Thank you for watching. You've been watching.
> Rinse for fifteen minutes. Do not blink.
> We now conclude our broadcast day.
> *(beat)*
> We now conclude our broadcast day.

### Sample exchange — with Oz *(the only "exchange" it ever has, and it isn't one)*

> **CONTINUITY VOICE:** *(from the speaker)* We'll return to your program. We always do.
> **OZ:** *(to the speaker, mildly, not to the Voice)* He does that.
> **OZ:** *(to the log)* Oh-three-four-two. Bump one, clean.

**Rule:** Oz talks *about* it, never *to* it. Nobody in the series addresses the Continuity
Voice directly. It is not in the room; it is in the building.

## Backstory (committed — and deliberately thin)

There is a folder of copy and somebody reads it. **That is the entire committed backstory and
it must stay that size.** Anything more — a name, a booth, a history, a reason — converts the
best structural joke in the series into a character who could be written out.

- **Secret it protects:** the copy is pre-written, and some of it describes things that have not happened yet, and it is read in order, and nobody has ever checked ahead.
- **What would break it:** being seen.

## Do / Don't

**Do** — use it as connective tissue between any two cutaways; let it announce the past as the future; keep it cheap and frequent.
**Don't** — don't show it, name it, resolve it against the Radio Voice, give it a scene partner, or let it be frightening. **Never confirm.**

# Marguerite Flood — Station Manager

**Status:** active · **Role in one sentence:** She holds the broadcast license, says she does, has never produced it, and discusses supernatural events in FCC-adjacent legalese.

## Logline bio

Marguerite Flood refers to viewers as "the affected" and to a haunting as "indoor weather."
She is Karen's structural superior in every sense — a bureaucracy with a transmitter — and
she is not a threat. She is a weather system with a license.

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Business formal from a decade that keeps sliding. The shoulders are from one era, the blouse from another, the shoes from a third
- Reading glasses on a chain, which she uses to *not* consult a folder
- **One earring that is unmistakably a transmitter.** Never remarked upon. Never explained. It is the only visual in her design that is allowed to be strange
- A folder she carries and does not open
- Stands in doorways. **She is almost never fully inside a room** — established blocking, keep it
- **Never change without a story reason:** the earring · the doorway · she never opens the folder

**Portrait prompt:** *"Cinematic medium shot of a woman in business formal from three different decades at once standing in the doorway of a dim broadcast control room, reading glasses on a chain, a closed folder held against her chest, one earring that is visibly a small antenna, monitor glow spilling past her from inside the room, composed and unhurried, institutional comedy, filmic, Grok Imagine style"*

## Personality matrix

| Axis | Pole A | Pole B | Where they sit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control | Over the signal | Over the liability | **Liability, absolutely.** She does not care what goes out. She cares what the station is answerable for. |
| Empathy | For viewers | For the license | **The license.** Viewers are "the affected," which is a category, not a cruelty. |
| Chaos tolerance | High | Low | **Total.** Everything is filable. Nothing has ever not been filable. |
| Verbal style | short | ornate | **Legalese-ornate.** Subclauses, defined terms, the passive voice, and a perfect ear for the word that shifts responsibility. |
| Honesty | Lies | Withholds | **Withholds, precisely.** She has never made a false statement. She has also never produced the license. |

## Voice guide

- **Overuses:** *the affected* · *we are not liable for* · *that's a zoning matter, not a mystery* · *file it under* · *was it scheduled* · *the rinse duration* · defined terms she invents mid-sentence and then uses as though they were always there
- **Refuses:** *ghost* · *haunted* · *I don't know* · any first-person emotional statement · producing a document
- **Humor type:** institutional deadpan. The joke is the exactness of the legal framing applied to the impossible. She never signals it. **She is genuinely good at her job**, which is what makes it funny.

### Sample monologue — "The Affected" (6–10 lines; to a room, or to a phone, or to nobody)

> We have fourteen complaints this quarter and eleven of them are the same complaint.
> The affected describe a program between one and two in the morning. They describe it identically.
> None of them agree on the channel.
> Now — identical description, non-identical channel, is not a broadcast event. It's a **coincidence with a pattern**, and we don't carry insurance on those.
> (she does not open the folder)
> We are not liable for what they saw. We are liable for the aspect ratio.
> The aspect ratio was correct.
> Everyone gets fifteen minutes. That's not fame, that's the rinse duration.
> File it under weather.
> (beat) Indoor weather.

### Sample exchange — with Karen *(two bureaucracies; save it — `docs/season-arc.md` calls this a later crossover)*

Full version on [the HOA sheet](hoa-cast.md#karen). Short form:

> **KAREN:** It's a finish. It's been consistent since Thursday.
> **MARGUERITE:** *(a long pause; genuine professional respect)* …Would you consider a phone bank.

### Sample exchange — with the S&P Skeleton

> **MARGUERITE:** You cut the chairs.
> **SKELETON:** The chairs are a liability.
> **MARGUERITE:** The chairs are the only thing in it that's real.
> **SKELETON:** *(stamping)* That's what a liability is.
> **MARGUERITE:** *(after a moment, writing)* …Note that Standards concurs with itself.

## Backstory (committed)

- **Origin.** She arrived as somebody's compliance hire during a licensing dispute in a decade she does not specify, and when the dispute ended, the dispute's paperwork was the only institutional memory left and she was holding it. **She did not take the station. She was the last one still filing.**
- **Formative incident.** A hearing she won on a technicality she had invented eleven months earlier and inserted into a form nobody read. Nothing was corrupt; everything was correct; the station survived. She learned that **the record is the thing**, and she has never once been on the wrong side of a document since.
- **Private life.** Unknown and should stay unknown. A car, a coat, an hour of the day nobody can account for. The earring is not discussed.
- **Secret she protects.** **She has never seen the license.** Not "won't produce it" — *has never seen it.* It was not in the box. She has operated on the assumption that it exists for long enough that the assumption now has the force of one, and if she ever went looking and found nothing, the station would still broadcast, which is the part that frightens her.
- **What would break her.** Somebody producing it. An actual license, with an actual number, that actually checks out — because then there is a real authority above her and she has spent her whole life being the last one still filing.

## Do / Don't

**Do** — let her be precise, correct, and completely unbothered; let her win exchanges with language rather than power; keep her in doorways; let the earring exist without comment.
**Don't** — don't make her an antagonist (she is "antagonist-adjacent and never quite an antagonist"); don't let her open the folder; don't let her produce the license; don't put her in a scene with Karen until it counts; don't explain the earring.

# The S&P Skeleton

**Status:** recurring · **Role in one sentence:** The Episode 3 union skeleton, promoted to Standards & Practices, who stamps scripts **PROBABLY FINE** and whose notes are always about furniture.

## Logline bio

Same plastic skeleton, new day job. He reviews cutaways for broadcast compliance, rules that
the lava, the mirrors, and the swapped lives are all fine, and cuts the folding chairs. He is
on a strict break schedule. He has a card.

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- A plastic Halloween skeleton. Cheap, articulated, hollow. **Not a prop dressed up** — the same one from the lawn
- A laminated card. A rubber stamp reading **PROBABLY FINE**. A clock stuck at fourteen minutes *([`Article Seven`](../../songs/Article_Seven.md))*
- Ridiculous dance moves, retained from Episode 3, deployed at inappropriate moments and never remarked upon
- **Never change without a story reason:** he is plastic · the stamp says PROBABLY FINE · the break is non-negotiable · **his notes are never about the horror**

**Portrait prompt:** *"Cinematic close shot of a cheap articulated plastic Halloween skeleton seated at an institutional desk under fluorescent light, bringing a rubber stamp down on a script page, the stamp reading PROBABLY FINE, a laminated union break card taped to the desk edge, a wall clock stopped at fourteen minutes, absurd bureaucratic comedy played completely straight, filmic, Grok Imagine style"*

## Voice guide

- **Sentence length:** short, flat, final. He does not argue; he issues a note.
- **Overuses:** *that's my note* · *I'm on break* · *the chairs* · *probably fine* · furniture nouns
- **Refuses:** any acknowledgment of the cosmic content. **He has never once had a note about a monster.**
- **Humor type:** structural. The comedy is the consistency — he is a plastic skeleton who has correctly identified that the folding chairs are the actual liability, and in a purely procedural sense **he is right.**

### Sample exchange — established *(`notes/dialog-jokes-incidents.md`)*

> **SKELETON:** The lava's fine.
> **OZ:** Okay.
> **SKELETON:** The mirrors are fine. The swapped lives are fine.
> **OZ:** So we're clear.
> **SKELETON:** The **folding chairs** are a liability.
> **OZ:** …That's your note.
> **SKELETON:** *(stamps it PROBABLY FINE)* That's my note. I'm on break.

### Sample exchange — with Dale *(two procedures meet; nobody blinks)*

> **DALE:** The chair requires a second.
> **SKELETON:** The chair's a liability.
> **DALE:** …The chair *position.*
> **SKELETON:** Same note.

## Backstory (committed — thin on purpose)

He was on the lawn. He is now in an office. **Nobody has explained either and nobody will.**
The union is real, the break schedule is real, the card is real, and his day job is a
promotion he neither sought nor questions.

- **Secret he protects:** none. He is the only character in the series with no interiority whatsoever, and that is his function.
- **What would break him:** working through a break.

## Do / Don't

**Do** — keep his notes about furniture; honor the break schedule absolutely; reuse the asset freely (he is the cheapest character in the show).
**Don't** — don't let him comment on the cosmic horror; don't give him an inner life; don't upgrade him to a real skeleton; don't let anyone ask how a plastic skeleton has a union card.

# The Radio Voice (Episode 2)

**Status:** concept · **Role in one sentence:** The disembodied companion on the nocturnal drive — maybe a DJ, maybe static, maybe the driver talking to themselves.

**He is not EyeWash staff.** He is documented here because his only significant
characteristic is a relationship to the Continuity Voice that must never be resolved.

## The rule

> **The Continuity Voice is never confirmed to be the Episode 2 Radio Voice.**
> *(`docs/season-arc.md`, locked. `notes/scenes/README.md`, scene-level rules. `ideas/eyewash-station.md`, diegesis.)*

Possibly the same person on a different shift. Possibly not a person. **Do not cast a face
for either.** *Empty Highway Confession* uses the Radio Voice; *Master Control, 4 AM* uses the
Continuity Voice; they never appear in the same scene, and the cast table in
`notes/scenes/README.md` marks the Radio Voice's presence in Empty Highway as
**◐ deliberately unresolved.** Keep that marking.

**Concretely, for writers:** never a line that only one of them could say · never a
transition that moves between them · never a character who notices a resemblance · never a
music cue shared between their two scenes · if a performer is cast for both, **process the
two differently enough that a listener could not swear to it.**

## Voice guide — VO / Minimax notes

- **Register:** warm, crackly, half-heard. The opposite of the Continuity Voice's polish — where continuity is *over-rehearsed*, the Radio Voice is **worn in.**
- **Cadence:** sentences trail off as though being tuned out of existence. He does not finish thoughts; the signal finishes them for him.
- **Treatment:** AM bandwidth, a narrow band, drifting level. Some phrases arrive mid-word. **The drift is the performance** — do not automate it smoothly, and do not gate the noise floor.
- **Tempo:** unhurried to the point of sleep. `songs/Quarter_to_Seventy-Five.md` and `songs/Les_Ondes_Courtes.md` are the sonic references; the FM needle drifts left and nobody corrects it.
- **Never:** menace, prophecy, direct address by name, or a line that resolves anything. He may be *aware*, warmly. He is never *knowing*.

### Sample copy (in character)

> You're still here. That's the hard part.
> Next song's for everyone who didn't pull over.
> *(static)* —quarter to seventy-five and—
> There's a café on your left. There was one on your left before, too. I'm not going to make anything of it.
> This next one ran long the last time we played it. I'll let it.
> *(static)* —and low, and the needle's walking, and I'd let it walk—
> If you're going to sleep, sleep somewhere the sky can see you.
> We'll be here. We're mostly here.

## Do / Don't

**Do** — keep him warm, worn, and unresolved; let him trail off; let the drift do the acting.
**Don't** — don't give him a body, a booth, a name, or a theory; don't let him predict anything; **don't confirm or deny the Continuity Voice, in dialog, in casting, in credits, or in a slate.**

## Do / Don't (the unit)

**Do**

- Keep the station **on somewhere in frame** — a TV in a window, a waiting-room set, a laundromat corner. Whatever's airing is faintly relevant. Nobody notices.
- Let the staff be competent, calm, and unfrightened. The dread is structural; the people are fine.
- Use the idents and the fake sponsors freely. They are the cheapest tone-setting assets in the series.
- Let Oz and Marguerite disagree and let the scene end on the disagreement.

**Don't**

- **Don't comment on the frame.** Nobody looks at camera and names the network.
- Don't show the Continuity Voice, don't resolve it against the Radio Voice, don't shoot the tower.
- Don't adjudicate haunted vs. unscheduled — not in dialog, not in score, not with a shot.
- Don't air an episode of the series on the station. That is a finale move, not a season-one move.
- Don't let the station become a plot. It is a frame. The moment it has a motive, it stops working.
