## The video first (`docs/song-method.md`)

**Keeping Their Sheep.** Early on a weekday, the sun low in the front window. Rubella and
Madelin sit at a corner table in a little square of café tables with one gap in it, under a
white paper star lantern. Seven tired customers stand in a single line at the counter, each
holding one wrong thing (a ladder, a surfboard, a potted fern). A border collie in a plain
green apron sits at the end of the counter, watching them. Madelin starts the carol, bright,
grinning. When the verse says "keeping their sheep", the dog gets up and does exactly that: it
walks the line, the line shuffles together, the seven bunch into a clump and take one step
left on every Noel, and when the verse says "they looked up" all seven tip their heads back
together at the paper star. On the line about the star's great light the dog barks once, the
band stops dead, and the seven trot as one animal into the pen. A folding chair slides across
the gap, the band comes back, and all seven sit down at the same instant. Rubella sings the
chorus alone, flat and dry, to seven silent heads. At the end Madelin passes the dog a biscuit
from the tote, the dog fetches one lidded cup from the counter and sets it, unasked, at
Rubella's elbow. Rubella blinks once and puts both hands round it.

NOTES:

### What the song is
A traditional carol sung straight in a coffee house, by a woman who is bright and a woman who is tired. The words are the carol's and nobody in the picture treats a word of it as anything but a carol. The picture only takes one line of it literally: the first stanza says "keeping their sheep", so a dog keeps the customers. The rest of the lyric lands where it lands: "they looked up" and all seven look up, "stop and stay" and the dog drops and stays. The song's one wrongness is that the chorus is built for a crowd and has one voice. The picture then gives it a crowd of exactly seven, sitting in a pen with their heads tipped towards the singer, and none of them makes a sound. The words never explain the picture, and the singers never notice it.

### Public domain
The text is the traditional English carol, first printed in 1823 (Gilbert) and 1833 (Sandys): public domain. The three stanzas above are the traditional wording, spelling modernised only (Noel, north-west); the traditional wise-men stanza is left out for length. No modern arrangement, hymnal edit or recording is followed, and the tune is Minimax's own. If Minimax reads "Noel" as "no-ELL", use the 1823 spelling "Nowell" in the lyric. The title keeps the owner's spelling, *The First Noell*.

### The structural flaw (held, never fixed)
**The crowd part is one voice.** It still makes sense on the new picture, and better: the chorus is built with gaps for an answer, the picture puts seven people in the room to give it, and the seven only breathe in, tip their heads and say nothing. Rubella sings every chorus alone, single-tracked, dry, centred, and nobody ever joins her, including in the last chorus, where the band is at its biggest. No double, harmony, stack, choir, ooh pad or crowd answer, ever; the band's gaps stay as they are. It is a different kind from the flaws on the other three carols (a stuck sustain pedal, a blower hum off the key, rising runs that stop one step short) and from the catalog's (a miscount, a dead tine, a wrong-sized cymbal, an empty drum slot, a late voice): it is a number of voices, held at one, against a part written for many.

### Why it is not the neighbors
- **Not *Mysterium*** (`songs/Mysterium.md`). That is the show's bright Christmas song with a choir and a key lift, in Italian and Latin. This is English, a porch-stomp band, and its crowd never sings.
- **Not *Silver Bells* / *Tap the Mug*.** Also a coffee house, but that is a Christmas Eve night in the snow, with a street outside that rings back, a hidden crowd on the drop and a stuck sustain pedal. This is early morning, no snow, no street, seven visible customers from the first frame, a dog, and one voice that is never joined.
- **Not *Sleigh Ride*.** Also an animal pulling a load, but a marmot team at Pulpit Overlook in a fast bluegrass hoedown with a fiddle that never reaches the top. Here the animal is a dog, indoors, steering people, and the band is a slow-to-mid 12/8 stomp with a concertina.
- **Not *Silent Night* or its pitches.** Its *Night Shift* pitch is also animals in a coffee house (owl baristas, late night). The owls make coffee; this dog does not, it keeps the line, and its cup is a single unasked favour at the end. Different time, animal and joke.
- **Not `ideas/crab-ground-crew.md`.** There crabs marshal gulls in like ground crew; here a dog herds people, on a lyric's cue.
- **Not `ideas/halloween-drop-drawer.md` or `ideas/snow-globe-field-report.md`.** Nothing is plugged in, nothing is snow, nobody is a figurine.
- **Not *Pans Down*.** Also picture as percussion, but the percussion here is only the seven pairs of shoes under the stomp and the beat is not the joke.
- **Not *Box, Box, Hat*, *Just a Sunday*, *Thank You for the Lava*, *Pans Down* or *Eleven Stops Since Thursday*.** Different flaws (see above), and none is a carol.
