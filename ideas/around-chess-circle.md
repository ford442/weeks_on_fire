# Around — chess circle

Rubella and Lillith play a short game of chess at a small table in the middle of a large empty
hall. The floor is the board, enlarged. They call the moves out flat, like an inventory. A small
robot poodle races a perfect circle around the table for the whole piece and never once looks at
the board. The circle and the calls are the cutaway.

**Kind:** musical cutaway (short packet). Board + game + nuances only.

## The game: Légal's mate, 7 moves, 13 half-moves

A known miniature that still plays dry. White gives up her queen on move 5 without blinking, Black
takes it, and three moves later a quiet knight move (not a capture) ends it. Said as inventory, the
queen sacrifice lands as one more line on the list. Checked as legal checkmate with chess.js.

```
1. e4 e5  2. Nf3 d6  3. Bc4 Bg4  4. Nc3 g6  5. Nxe5 Bxd1  6. Bxf7+ Ke7  7. Nd5#
```

**Lillith = White**, and she starts. **Rubella = Black**, and she closes. They strictly alternate.
The closer falls on Rubella's turn, in place of a move.

## Production nuances

### Dog circle vs. pulse vs. calls

**Recommended: lock the lap to the bed and ignore the calls.** One full lap per bar, or per two
bars if the bar is under 2.5s. Keep that speed constant for the whole piece: no speeding up into
the queen capture, no easing off after the closer. If the lap followed the calls, the dog would read
as a reaction, and a dog reacting to the game is a dog interacting with the board.

- **Allowed by chance, never staged:** the dog crosses behind Rubella's chair on "Knight d5." If a
  take happens to do it, keep it. Don't cut to force it.
- **The alternative, deliberately ignoring the pulse too:** a constant, slightly-too-fast lap that
  belongs to nothing. Use this only if the bed has no steady pulse.
- **Direction:** counter-clockwise seen from above, the whole piece, every shot. Never reversed
  between shots. In the profile two-shot the dog always crosses the far
  side of the table from right to left.
- **Radius:** about three floor squares out from the table edge. It's the same circle in every
  shot, so the wide shot can show the chrome wearing a faint ring into the gloss (optional, very
  subtle).

### Floor registration

**Recommended: perfectly registered. No drift.** The floor *is* the board enlarged, so:

- The floor's files and ranks run parallel to the table board's.
- The table sits centred on the floor's middle four squares, the enlarged d4/e4/d5/e5. The mating
  square d5 is right under the tabletop.
- Light square on each player's right, on the table and on the floor alike.
- Floor squares are about 1.2 m. The table board is 8×8 with about 5 cm squares.

If the floor drifted it would start telling a story, and this piece doesn't have one. If an Imagine
take drifts or skews the grid, fix it in the **wide** (P3), where registration actually shows. In the
two-shot the floor is background, so a slight misread there is fine.

### Board dressing (mostly static)

The table board is dressed **once**, at the position after 6… Ke7, and stays that way in every shot
except the optional last take:

```
rn1q1bnr/ppp1kB1p/3p2p1/4N3/4P3/2N5/PPPP1PPP/R1BbK2R w KQ - 1 7
```

- A **white queen** stands upright, off the board, at Rubella's right elbow. She captured it.
- **Two black pawns** stand off the board at Lillith's right elbow.
- Nobody reads the board in a two-shot, so early coverage doesn't need its own position. Don't
  generate a sequence of board states.

### Optional accent on the finish (pick one, or none)

- **A (preferred):** on "That's the game," Rubella lays her king on its side with one finger. She
  doesn't look at it. It's the same downward economy as her two-finger brush, and nothing more.
- **B:** as the dog passes after "Knight d5," its amber LEDs blink once, then return to steady for
  the rest of the piece. Use only if A is cut. Never both.
- Don't change the light. No piece glow, no lens flare.

### Mouth timing (Imagine)

- **One call per clip.** Each clip should be 3–5s, with the mouth closed in both its start and last
  frames. That way the clips cut together and the gaps stay silent.
- Only the player calling moves her mouth: one short phrase, two to four syllables, quick, low
  energy, no smile. The other player's mouth stays closed, eyes on the board.
- Any piece moved in a call clip moves **in the gap after the call**, by hand, and the board
  dressing above still holds. In practice, mostly don't show the hand at all.
- After 0:55, mouths are closed in every frame. The hold clips are generated with the prompt
  saying "mouths closed, not speaking."
- Lipsync the calls in post if Imagine's mouth timing doesn't land. The picture never has to
  carry the words.

### Continuity tokens (repeat word for word in every prompt)

- **Hall:** *large empty hall, pale plaster walls, no windows in frame, one soft overhead light pool
  over the table falling off to dim walls*
- **Floor:** *continuous black-and-white checkerboard floor, high-gloss lacquer, squares about
  1.2 metres, perfectly registered to the table board*
- **Table:** *small square oiled walnut table, single pedestal, black-and-white inlaid board flush
  in the top*
- **Chairs:** *two plain black bentwood chairs*
- **Pieces:** *weighted Staunton chess pieces, ivory-white and black lacquer, felt bases*
- **Cast:** *Rubella and Lillith, two women with different faces, modern black lace dresses,
  long dark hair, deadpan, mouths closed when not calling*
- **Dog:** *small robot poodle, compact chrome chassis, white synthetic poodle-cut panels on head,
  ears and tail, two amber LED eyes and a thin amber LED collar ring*
- **Dog path:** *a perfect circle about three floor squares out from the table, counter-clockwise
  seen from above*
- **Gloss:** *the dog's amber LEDs leave short streaks of reflection in the lacquer floor*
