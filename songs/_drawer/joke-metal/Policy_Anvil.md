STYLE:

1970s–80s heavy metal played completely straight, 100 BPM, C minor, about two minutes twenty. Slow stomp, committed and doctrinal, no wink. One heavy riff on one overdriven guitar through a warm vintage tube amp, bass doubling it, dry 1970s kit, no double-kick. A single low iron-anvil strike, sampled and dry, is the accent on the one. Vocals: Rubella dry and level in the verses, reading a form number and a procedure like a clerk, close, unhurried, no belting. Chorus: a gang of stacked Lillith and Rubella double-tracks chants the title in unison, hard but not shouted, exactly on the downbeat. Verses are four short lines. Middle: one melodic guitar solo of about eight bars, warm, no shred. Bass and kit dry and close, small room, mixed like a 1978 record. No djent, no dropped tuning, no scream, no growl, no blast beat, no occult lyric, no male voice, no Kenji, no product name, no slide whistle, no cartoon sound effects, no laugh track. Structural flaw: in every chorus the anvil strike lands one beat early, on beat four of the bar before the downbeat, and the chant arrives exactly on time on the downbeat. The band does not move the strike. Nobody fights it and nothing is fixed, including in the last chorus.
