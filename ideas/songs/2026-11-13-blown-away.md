# Blown Away

**Genre collision:** 1990s dream trance (piano arpeggio, string pads, rolling bass, hands-in-the-air lead) × 1980s power ballad (gated tom fills, big clean-to-crunch guitar, the solo on a hill). The weather is the guitar.

**Visual concept:** It's autumn in a park, with a bandstand, a pond, a picnic and forty folding chairs. Lillith's wall of amp cabinets faces the park, and Rubella stands right in front of it at the mic. Every time Lillith plays, the park gets blown away a bit more: the leaves, a picnic, the forty chairs, the ducks and the pond, then the bandstand roof, and in the end the trees bend flat. Rubella's long dark hair never moves. At the end Lillith turns the amp right down, and in the silence a tiny breeze blows one strand across Rubella's face.

**Laugh (kid words):** *Her guitar is so loud it blows the whole park away, but her friend's hair doesn't move, until she turns it off.*

**BPM:** 136, E minor, lifting to G major in the chorus

**Target length:** ~2:40 including a 5-second written silence at the end · 14 segments

## Styles

Styles: 1990s dream trance colliding with a 1980s power ballad, 136 BPM, E minor verses lifting into a G major chorus, about two minutes thirty-five plus a held silence at the end, a dreamy rolling grand-piano arpeggio hook, lush wide string pads, warm offbeat rolling bass, four-on-the-floor kick with big gated-reverb snare, huge gated tom fills into every chorus, long rising wind-like white-noise sweeps before each drop, an electric guitar that starts clean and chorused in the verses and turns into a massive crunchy power-chord wall on every drop, a soaring melodic power-ballad guitar solo over the full trance beat, a short breakdown where only piano and pad remain, wide cinematic stereo, windswept, euphoric, romantic, totally sincere, no parody, no comedy sound effects; lead vocal is a dry, slightly raspy, flat female voice, calm and unbothered, close-mic, no belting, no vibrato, no ad-libs, every line sung at the same level even when the band explodes; the breakdown has one short spoken line in the same flat voice, dry, no reverb; the track ends with the full band cut dead and silence; no male voice, no choir. Use the Rubella voice.
