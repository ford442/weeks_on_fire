## Video concept

Sixth-floor corridor, 3 AM fluorescent, a ceiling tile rattling. Lillith, safety glasses
pushed up, a coil of cloth-covered speaker wire over one shoulder, walks toward the far end
where a mismatched speaker sits taped under the flickering **E X I** sign; the wire runs
along the baseboard to it. Rubella walks beside her, a full fire extinguisher under one arm.
Lillith says, "Finish it, Rubella." Rubella says, "Fine." The funk starts, and the pair keep
walking at one pace for the entire song. Lillith raps a line and leaves the last word hanging.
Rubella says it. The hallway delivers it, always on the beat: a giant striped **sock** lands in
front of them, a boulder-sized **rock** thumps down beside it, an enormous bag of **snack**
chips splits open, and a shaggy **yak** ambles in and eats the chips. A supply-closet door
opens (**bear**), a tin **knight** clanks past (**night**), and when Lillith asks if Rubella
rested her voice and Rubella rasps "Hoarse," a **horse** walks in and the knight climbs on and
trots off. Then Lillith says "orange." One orange drops and rolls. Rubella offers "door hinge"
(a hinge squeaks) and "four inch" (a nail clinks), and neither fits. Lillith says "orange"
again. Rubella says "orange." Now the oranges are coming from the ceiling tiles and from
under doors, a ball pit rising past their ankles, and Lillith raps her own name at them while
they wade on. "Finish it, Rubella." The band holds one unresolved chord. Rubella does not
answer. A smoke detector off-screen gives one chirp. Rubella peels a single orange and eats a
segment. Cut on black. Both stay the calmest things in frame; the zoo and the oranges are the
loud world.

`laugh:` The hallway gives her whatever she says, and when nothing rhymes with orange, it
just keeps giving her oranges.
`drop:` **Appears / multiplies.** One object arrives on the last beat of every line (sock,
rock, snack, yak, bear, knight, horse). From 0:34 the oranges **multiply** until the floor is
a ball pit.
`end reaction:` Rubella peels an orange and eats a segment (spent once; Lillith gets none).
`do-not:` let either woman look at an animal; name the oranges or the horror; show a Pizza Guy
or any third person; explain why the hallway obeys; resolve the chord; use text on screen;
show a rhyming dictionary, a book, a pen or a microphone.
`promote?:` no.

  (a knight on a horse leaving frame, one orange in the foreground).
- **Pattern:** speech-first, then **verse-on-action** straight through with no chorus: every
  Lillith line is action, every Rubella word is the drop. A one-bar **break** at 0:22.2,
  then a **hold** from 0:34.2 where the band keeps one chord under the oranges and never
  lands on the tonic.
- **Breaks:** (1) music absent 0:00-0:03.0 for the pre-roll speech; (2) bar 9 (0:22.2) drums
  and bass drop out, one clav stab, the horse whinnies; (3) bar 11 (0:27.0) only bass under
  Rubella's "door hinge"; (4) bar 16 (0:39.0) the band holds a dominant chord, no resolution,
  fading under the chirp.
- **Mouths:** **On** for exactly two picture-speech lines in the pre-roll (Lillith "Finish it,
  Rubella." / Rubella "Fine."). **Off** for the entire song: the rap and the single words
  are score, not lip-sync. Rubella's mouth stays closed through the chirp and the orange.
  Animal and knight mouths do whatever animals do.

## Minimax STYLE (paste-ready)

```
1970s clavinet funk with a spoken-sung rap, joke-adjacent but played completely straight, 100 BPM in E minor (Dorian), about thirty-eight seconds, sixteen bars.
Genre: tight funk rhythm section behind a fast, bright female rap-chant, light and playful.
One bar intro: clavinet riff on sixteenths, slap-and-pop bass, dry tight drums with a ghosted snare and a closed hi-hat, a muted wah guitar chopping on the offbeats.
Bright female lead, quick and conspiratorial, rapping dense internal rhymes in speech-song, every line leaving its final word open on beat four.
A lower, dry, raspy, flat female voice speaks-sings only that one final word, on the beat, in the exact rhythm the line left open, like a sigh that still lands on the beat. Never more than two words.
Verse: groove locked and relentless, a short two-note horn stab after each answered word.
Bar nine: drums and bass drop out, one clav stab, then the groove returns.
Final four bars: the band thins to bass, clav and one held organ chord, and stays on the dominant chord. It never resolves to the tonic and it does not fade out on a cadence.
Dry close mix, small tiled corridor slap, no big reverb, no synth pad, no strings, no electronic drums, no autotune, no choir, no spoken intro.
```

**Rhyme note (not for the paste):**
- **Mutating chain.** Verse 1 runs *-ock* (tick-tock, lock, stock, frock, smock, dock, flock)
  and slides to *-ack* in the middle of line 3 (*back, track, crack, stack*), landing on
  *snack*, then holds *-ack* through *yak* with *shaggy and black, hump on his back, attack*.
  The listener hears the vowel step, mid-line, with the pictures stepping up in size.
- **Homophone as the rhyme.** *Bare / bear* (the cupboard really was bare: a bear walks out),
  *the dead of the / night / knight* (Lillith ends on "of the"; the word that completes it is
  the one that arrives), *of course / of hoarse* (the usual phrase is "of course"; Rubella's
  raspy voice makes it a horse). The third one is the wrong third: it's a sound-alike across a
  word boundary and it's about her actual voice.
- **Multisyllabic and mosaic.** *Sore throat, sour note*, *hair care, spare chair, nothing to
  wear*, and the name chain *Lillith / built it / filled it / spilled it / still with it* (one
  name, four rhymes). The line "Lillith built it" is the closest the song gets to a boast.
- **The rhyme that can't resolve.** *Orange* has no perfect rhyme. The tries get worse:
  *door hinge* (the last syllable lines up), *four inch* (closer in the mouth, farther in the
  ear), then the same word again. The picture proves it: each failed try leaves an orange,
  and the chord never goes home. The final "Finish it, Rubella." gets the chirp instead of a
  word.
- Rubella's tags are one word until verse 3, where the two-word tries (*door hinge*, *four
  inch*) are the failures. She never gets longer than two words.
- In bars 11 and 13 Rubella lands on beat three (+1.2s), not beat four, so the wrong rhyme
  arrives early and sits in the gap.

## Board note

Finish It, Rubella is a 43-second corridor gag where a funk rap leaves each line one word
short and Rubella, rationed to one word per line, fills it in. The hallway makes every word
real, and the last word (orange) has no rhyme, so the hallway simply keeps delivering
oranges. It stays in one world (the sixth-floor corridor under the E X I sign). The pair are
the calmest things in frame the whole way. The track must never land on its tonic; the one
in-tune sound in the building, the smoke detector, gets the last word. Not a replacement for
*Sad Ballad, Bad Salad* (spoonerism call and response) or *Ring Once* (the doorbell band).
`promote?: no`; harden first.
