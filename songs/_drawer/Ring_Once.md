## Video concept

Sixth-floor corridor, 3 AM fluorescent. Lillith has rewired the 6-C doorbell with a vacuum
tube wrapped in a dish towel like an egg (the 6BQ5, out of the ping-pong table). She presses
the button. It goes a plain, small *ding*. Nothing. Then the ceiling tiles rattle, a snare
roll climbs, and on the downbeat a full marching band **appears** in the hallway: forty
players, a sousaphone bent against the light fixtures, a drum major twirling a baton at
the smoke detector, a piccolo wedged next to the laundry basket Rubella is holding. Lillith
watches it like it is going to plan. Rubella endures it like a dryer cycle. At the peak
there are three ordinary knocks. The music stops dead, mid-word. The band freezes mid-note.
The Pizza Guy, red uniform, phone checked at 23%, stands at the door and reads the order
off the bag: "Order for... Rubelle?" Rubella takes the box. "Extra napkins are in the bag."
He walks out through the band at his pace, never looking at the tuba. The band, polite,
parts for him. Lillith presses the button once more: one plain ding, the hall is empty,
and from far down the elevator shaft one tuba note arrives. "It's early."

`laugh:` The doorbell is a whole marching band, and the pizza guy just knocks.
`drop:` **Appears / multiplies.** On the downbeat at 0:08 the empty hall fills with forty
band members. Second picture change at 0:24: the whole band **freezes** (stillness is
allowed here because the *silence* is the drop and the knock is the event). Third: at 0:34
the band is **gone**, reversed.
`end reaction:` Lillith, whispered, "It's early." (spent once; Rubella gets none).
`do-not:` correct the name; let the Pizza Guy look at any instrument; name the horror; show a
band that Rubella or Lillith reacts to with fear or surprise; give Madelyn a face.
`promote?:` no.

## Video use map

- **Target length:** 36s. Clip A (15s, 0:00-0:15) setup through drop. Clip B (15s, 0:15-0:30)
  band, then the knocks at 0:24.5. Clip C (10s, trimmed to 6s, 0:30-0:36) exit and button.
  Chain: last frame of A is the start still of B; last frame of B (Pizza Guy mid-stride in
  the band) is the start still of C.
- **Pattern:** silence-first, then **verse-on-action / chorus-on-hold** compressed into a
  hard-stop interrupt. Music enters late (0:04 snare roll), lands on the band at 0:08,
  runs to a **hard cut on the knocks at 0:24**, then is gone until a scratch-back tag at
  0:32.
- **Breaks:** (1) music absent 0:00-0:04 so the plain ding and room tone read; (2) hard stop at
  0:24.0 for the three knocks, the piccolo squeak, and the Pizza Guy's two lines (silence
  0:24-0:31, speech sits **instead of** music); (3) music scratches back in at 0:32 as he
  walks away, low-passed like it is behind a door; (4) ducks out for the last ding at 0:34.
- **Mouths:** Rubella and Lillith **off** the whole packet (closed, deadpan). The Pizza Guy
  **on** for exactly two lines. Band mouths are on instruments only. Lillith's "It's early"
  is a whisper over the back of her head, not lip-synced.

## Diegetic sound list

1. **Plain doorbell ding** at 0:03 and again at 0:34. Dry, small, one note. Music is off for
   both. (Not the sampled ding in *Pepperoni or Margherita*; this is a building door
   buzzer, short and a little flat.)
2. **Hall hum + ceiling tile rattle** 0:00-0:08, rising under the snare roll. Cut by the drop.
3. **Three ordinary knocks** at 0:24.5, 0:25.2, 0:25.9. Knuckles on a wooden door, even,
   no echo. **Cuts the music dead.**
4. **Piccolo squeak** at 0:26.6, one cracked note in the silence. Cuts nothing; it lives in
   the gap.
5. **Speech, Pizza Guy (picture sound, instead of music):** 0:27.5 "Order for... Rubelle?"
   and 0:30.2 "Extra napkins are in the bag." Flat, courteous, zero vibrato, no punchline
   read. Nobody answers except Rubella taking the box.
6. **Distant tuba** at 0:35, one low note up the elevator shaft, over the last ding. Plus
   the **off-screen whisper** "It's early." (Lillith, close-miked) under the last bar.

## Minimax STYLE (paste-ready)

```
Marching-band brass pop, joke-adjacent but played completely straight, 120 BPM in B-flat major, about thirty-two seconds, sixteen bars.
Genre: high-school marching band meets New Orleans second-line, with a pop vocal hook.
Snare roll crescendo for two bars with no brass, then on bar three the whole band lands at once: sousaphone on the roots, three trumpets, two trombones, a piccolo doubling the hook an octave up, bass drum on one and three, snare on two and four with ghosted rolls.
Bright female lead, quick and conspiratorial, sings "Ding" on the downbeat. A lower, dry, raspy, flat female voice answers "Dong" a half-beat later, like a sigh that still lands on the beat. Both short, chant-like, no vibrato, no melisma.
Verse: drums, bass and tuba with the trumpets answering each line, vocals drop to speech-song.
The song resolves nowhere: bar ten ends on a hard, clean unison stab for the edit to cut at. Bar fifteen to sixteen: tuba walks down two steps and ends on a single bare "ding" with no tail.
Dry close mix, hallway slap reflection, no big reverb, no synth pad, no guitar, no electronic drums, no spoken words in the track.
```

## Board note

Ring Once is a 36-second building-corridor gag: Lillith's rewired doorbell summons a marching
band, the Pizza Guy knocks three times, and the music stops mid-word. It stays inside one
world (the sixth-floor corridor outside 6-C); the Pizza Guy crosses as a free-radical cameo,
and the "It's early" tag is a reference to the weather-predicting ping-pong table, not a
second set. Speech is limited to his two lines plus one whispered tag. Not a replacement
for *Pepperoni or Margherita* or *Call Forty-Seven*, which own the sampled doorbell ding
and the Karen-door button. `promote?: no`; harden first.
