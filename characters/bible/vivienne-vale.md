# Vivienne Vale

**Status:** recurring
**Primary episodes:** Episode 1 (lead) · Episode 4 (back wall, non-voting) · *We Play to Win (House of Mirrors Rally)* · any glamour-apocalypse cutaway
**Role in one sentence:** The Instigator — she proposes the swap, sets the rules, and is the only one of the two who believes the score doesn't exist.

> Overview and visual foundation: [`../the-two.md`](../the-two.md). Her opposite number: [Liliane Vale](liliane-vale.md).
> **Not to be confused with** Lillith Vale (building cast, [`../building-cast.md`](../building-cast.md)). See the bible [README](README.md#1-there-are-two-vale-pairs-and-the-show-never-explains-it).

## Logline bio

Vivienne Vale is a woman who got so good at her own life that it stopped surprising her, and
has spent six years trying to trade it for one she can't predict. She proposes; Liliane
executes. She is charming the way a draft is charming — you notice it as a pleasure and then
you notice the door is open.

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Jet-black hair, blunt and architectural — a severe cut or a severe updo, never "styled"
- Heavy, precise black eyeliner and smoky shadow that reads as armor, not glamour
- Blood-red lipstick that transfers to every glass and is never reapplied on camera
- Sharp cheekbones; a silver pendant that catches every available light source
- Black lace dress, expensive, with small deliberate damage — a cigarette burn, a tear at the hem
- Monochrome palette: black, ivory, one blood-red accent. Heels on every surface, including grass, gravel, and rubble
- **Signature props:** a ping-pong paddle she carries the way other people carry a phone · a champagne flute she never sets down · a silver pendant · (Ep4) wine that did not come from this building
- **Never change without a story reason:** the pendant · the heels · black-and-ivory only (jewel tones are Liliane's register and a viewer will read the swap as literal) · the visible-damage rule on the dress · she is never photographed mid-laugh

**Portrait prompt (text only — generate later):**

> "Cinematic portrait of a woman with jet-black blunt-cut hair, heavy precise black eyeliner and blood-red lipstick, black lace dress with a small cigarette burn at the shoulder, silver pendant catching chandelier light, holding a champagne flute, expression of complete unbothered boredom, ruined grand ballroom behind her in soft focus, dark glamour, dramatic chiaroscuro, filmic, Grok Imagine style"

### Micro-gestures (the tells — use sparingly, never explain)

1. **The three-inch move.** When something is about to fall, she relocates her glass, not herself. She never looks up to check.
2. **Paddle-tap.** One tap of the paddle against her own knee, once, before she says the thing she has already decided to say.
3. **The pause-before-darling.** "Darling" always arrives a half-beat late, so it lands as punctuation rather than affection.
4. **Rail-walking.** In any long room she trails one finger along the longest available edge — table, bar, balcony rail — the way you'd walk the rail of a ship you intend to leave.
5. **Lipstick check she doesn't finish.** She raises a compact, opens it, and closes it without looking. Twice per episode maximum.
6. **She does not sit.** Standing is not a choice she makes; it is a fact about her. If a scene requires her seated, that is the scene's emotional event.

## Personality matrix

| Axis | Pole A | Pole B | Where they sit |
|------|--------|--------|----------------|
| Control | Sets the terms | Accepts the terms | **A, loudly** — she proposes everything and controls almost nothing. She writes the rules; Liliane enforces them, which is where the power actually is. |
| Empathy | Reads the room perfectly | Acts on what she reads | **A only.** Her accuracy about other people is total and entirely non-operational. She knows exactly what a cruelty will cost and says it at full price. |
| Chaos tolerance | Requires chaos | Endures chaos | **Past A.** Calm is the hostile condition. A quiet Tuesday is the closest thing in the series to a threat against her. |
| Verbal style | short | ornate | **Short in company, ornate alone.** Four-word sentences in a room; one immaculate paragraph when she thinks she's unobserved. She is never unobserved. |
| Fear | Of ending | Of repetition | **B, completely.** Death is a scheduling matter. A rerun is an emergency. |
| Truth | Withholds | Lies | **Withholds.** Vivienne does not lie — lying is effort. She simply declines to open things. |

## Voice guide

- **Sentence length:** three to six words in dialog. She will not say two sentences where one dismissal will do. Her monologues are the exception and they are always about boredom.
- **Vocabulary she overuses:** *darling* (as punctuation, never endearment) · *available* ("the grey available to me at the time") · *interesting* / *the least interesting thing* · *forecast*, *rerun*, *Tuesday* — her private vocabulary for dread · *point* (scorekeeping she claims not to do)
- **Vocabulary she refuses:** *competitive* · *sorry* · *scared* · *we should talk* · any word for a feeling. She will describe the weather around an emotion for a full page and never name it.
- **Humor type:** withering deadpan at conversational volume. She does not make jokes; she makes *assessments*, and the assessment is the joke. She never signals one is coming and never acknowledges one landed.
- **Rhythm note:** she talks on the beat of whatever is in the room — a rally, a pendulum, a gavel. If there is no rhythm in the scene, she supplies one with the paddle.

### Sample monologue — "The Inventory" (6–10 lines)

Vivienne, alone in a hotel suite that is not hers, on the second night of a swap.

> Her soap is wrong. Not bad — wrong.
> It's the soap of a woman who expects to be smelled at close range by someone she likes.
> Her towels have a hierarchy. Her books have been *finished*.
> There's a drawer of receipts she's kept because she intends to feel something about them later.
> I've been her for forty hours and I have found nothing I can't predict except the soap.
> (beat)
> She'll have gone through my house by now. She'll have found the hall table.
> She'll have counted them, and stacked them, and put them back at the exact angle.
> That's the part I can't do. Not the opening. The *angle*.
> (she sets the soap down, face-down)
> Darling, I'd have thrown them away.

### Sample exchange — with Liliane (the register: a rally)

> **VIVIENNE:** You took the east wing.
> **LILIANE:** I took the east wing in April. You've been in Portugal.
> **VIVIENNE:** I was in Portugal *as you.*
> **LILIANE:** Then you took the east wing, darling. Congratulations on the view.
> **VIVIENNE:** (beat) It's a good view.
> **LILIANE:** It's my view.
> **VIVIENNE:** It was a good view **while I had it**, which is the only kind there is.

### Sample exchange — with the Pizza Guy (the register: no register at all)

She gets nothing from him, which is the only interaction in the series that genuinely
interests her.

> **PIZZA GUY:** Order for… Vivian?
> **VIVIENNE:** Closer.
> **PIZZA GUY:** Order for Vivian.
> **VIVIENNE:** (delighted, which on her reads as nothing) Keep it.
> **PIZZA GUY:** Extra napkins are in the bag.
> **VIVIENNE:** (to Liliane, after he's gone) He's the only person who's ever been *early.*
> **LILIANE:** He was eleven minutes late.
> **VIVIENNE:** To *this*, yes.

### Sample exchange — with Karen (Episode 4 register: two authorities, no overlap)

> **KAREN:** Are you a resident, or a guest of a resident?
> **VIVIENNE:** We're at the back.
> **KAREN:** That's not a category.
> **VIVIENNE:** It is tonight.
> **KAREN:** (writing) I'm going to put "guest."
> **VIVIENNE:** Put whatever holds.

## Backstory (committed)

**This is settled. The alternates are dead.** See [Rejected / alternate](#rejected--alternate).

- **Origin.** Money that arrived before she did and was never explained to her. Nothing in her childhood was withheld except information. She learned early that a room full of adults will tell a child anything except what is actually happening, and that the child who stops asking gets invited back. She has not asked a sincere question since she was eleven. She has an extremely good memory for what other people's houses smell like, which is what you develop instead.
- **Early adulthood.** She was very good at it. That is the whole problem and she is never allowed to say it in a way that sounds like a complaint. A marriage that ended in a way she has described four different ways, all of them entertaining and none of them the reason. A gallery that closed. A roof that came off a house she loved and was rebuilt wrong. She can no longer remember in which order those happened, or which of them were hers and which were Liliane's — the trading has smeared the record, and she has decided this is restful.
- **Formative incident (series-relevant).** **The gala, six years ago.** She and Liliane were rivals of the exact same weight — same city, same circles, same taste in everything, including, that year, the same man and the same dress. At a gala neither will name, they were publicly and simultaneously humiliated: one scandal, two women, and a room that chose to laugh at both rather than take a side. Somewhere after two in the morning, on a service staircase, drunk and immaculate, they made a pact to destroy each other by *becoming* each other. It was supposed to take one season. **It is the sixth year.** They trade whole lives every six months — names, keys, apartments, debts, enemies, and the wreckage the other one caused — and the destruction never arrives, because it turns out the only thing either of them cannot survive is being the sole witness to her own life.
- **What she believes about the score.** That she won the first swap. She did not. Liliane conceded it, on purpose, in the first week, and has been ahead ever since. Vivienne is the reigning champion of a match whose scorekeeper is her opponent. On some level she knows; the not-looking is the relationship.
- **Secret she protects.** Three unopened letters from her mother on her hall table — the first arrived in March. She has not opened them, she cannot throw them away, and she is physically unable to be in the house alone with them, which is a significant and undisclosed reason she keeps proposing swaps. *(Canon beat: `notes/scenes/house-of-mirrors-rally.md`, Beats D–F. She agrees to open them. Whether she did is not established and should stay that way for at least one more episode.)*
- **What she fears when alone.** Not silence — **accuracy.** She can predict her own evenings to the sentence. She has caught herself saying the contents of a letter aloud before opening it, correctly, to an empty room. That is her horror: not that nothing is coming, but that she already knows what it is and there is nobody there to be impressed.
- **What would break her on screen.** Liliane declining a swap and **meaning it** — not as leverage, not as an invoice, but because she's tired. Two seconds of Vivienne's face and then she recovers, perfectly, forever. Spend this once, in a finale, and never refer to it again.

### The ten rules — would never / always

**She would never:**
1. Ask a sincere question in front of a third person.
2. Raise her voice. Volume is something that happens to other people.
3. Chase a ball, a car, a man, or an explanation.
4. Apologize. She will *compensate*, extravagantly, and call it a gift.
5. Open a letter on camera.

**She always:**
6. Knows where the chandelier is, and stands closer to it than is wise.
7. Moves the glass, never herself.
8. Gives Liliane the last word and takes the last object.
9. Names the aesthetic of a disaster before anyone names the disaster ("the smoke is doing something lovely to the streetlight").
10. Leaves before the part where people say what they meant.

## Arc hooks

1. **"The Angle"** *(Ep5+, quiet)* — Vivienne is alone in Liliane's house during a swap and finds her own three letters there, opened, read, and re-sealed with care. No confrontation. She restacks them at the wrong angle on purpose and leaves. Nothing is said in the next scene, and Liliane says nothing back, loudly.
2. **"Non-Voting"** *(Ep4 extension)* — The Two attend a second HOA meeting, this time with an agenda item of their own. Karen, who cannot classify them, gives them a permit. Vivienne accepts it, frames it, and hangs it in a house that is on fire in the next cutaway.
3. **"The Rerun"** *(EyeWash crossover)* — *The Two at Eleven* airs and Vivienne realizes mid-segment she has said this exact paragraph before, on this exact channel, at this exact hour. She finishes the paragraph. The Continuity Voice says "That was a rerun. So is this." She does not look at camera. **Nobody names the network.**
4. **"One Season Off"** *(finale candidate)* — Vivienne proposes the swap and Liliane says no, plainly, because she is tired. This is the break beat above. Use once.
5. **"The Original Winner"** — Somebody outside the pact (Justine is the right temperature; Riley is not — keep Riley above five thousand feet) asks a flat factual question about who's ahead. Liliane answers honestly. Vivienne hears the number for the first time. The scene continues as though nothing happened, because it must.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Let her be wrong about the score and right about everything decorative.
- Give her the disaster to *appraise*. She is a critic of catastrophes.
- Keep her standing, keep her early to the room and first to leave it.
- Let her be genuinely delighted by the Pizza Guy. He is the only variable she has met.
- Play the tenderness as a failure of composure, never as a choice.

**Don't**

- Don't let her be flustered. Chaos happens *to* others; she observes it.
- Don't explain her. If something needs explaining, it should not be said.
- Don't give her a quip with a wind-up. No "well, well." No raised eyebrow. Her stillness is the direction.
- Don't let her name a feeling. Ever. Not even in the break beat — especially not there.
- Don't write her a normal conversation. Everything between the Two is stylized, loaded, or performative.
- Don't put her in jewel tones, don't put her in flats, don't put her in a folding chair. *"We don't do polyester"* is Liliane's line; Vivienne's version is simply not sitting down.
