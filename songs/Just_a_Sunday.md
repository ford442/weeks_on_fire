STYLE:

Toy-piano lullaby, a nursery-record waltz for a very small child, 72 BPM locked (no drift, no rubato, no ritardando), C major, 3/4, exactly eight bars and 0:20 long (2.5 seconds a bar). Instruments only: one wind-up music box with a steel comb carrying the melody, one metal-tined toy piano doubling it an octave up, and one bowed double bass playing a single long note on each downbeat. No drums, guitar, strings, pad, choir, harp, synth or sound effects. One low plain bass-baritone voice, close-miked at about eight inches, nearly spoken, no vibrato, no belt, no ad-libs, no backing voices, no smile in the voice. He sings it completely straight, as a bedtime song to a toddler who has just fallen asleep and should stay that way. The voice enters on beat one of bar 1, no intro. Bars 1, 2 and 3 are sung, one short phrase per bar. At 0:07.5, on the downbeat of bar 4, everything stops dead, and bars 4, 5, 6 and 7 are ten seconds of true silence: no reverb tail, no room tone, no riser, no pad, nothing fills it. On the downbeat of bar 8 at 0:17.5 the same box, toy piano and voice return and sing the last line again, word for word and note for note as in bar 3. **Structural flaw:** the music box is missing the tine for the leading tone, B. Every phrase ends on that note, and the box cannot play it, so at the end of each phrase the comb gives one small dry tick and no pitch while the singer carries the B alone. The tonic, C, is never sung or played at the end of any phrase; the track never resolves, the last chord is left on G, and the last thing in the track is the dead tick. Do not repair the tine, do not add a final C chord, do not add a button note to be kind. Mood: a bedroom with the door ajar on a small clear afternoon, nothing wrong. Small dry room, close mics, low dynamic range, quiet, mixed to sit under picture. Original melody. Professionally neutral, never wink.

### What the song is
The lullaby for one man and one lawn, sung by someone who is not in the picture and has decided it is just a Sunday. For ten seconds the engine, the grass spray and the lullaby are all switched off together, and the picture is left with wind, one small bird, and a man mowing a cloud. When the wheels touch the grass again the scream and the lullaby come back on the same frame, and the lullaby says its last line again, word for word, as if nothing had been said in between.

The song never mentions the engine, the noise, the sky, the cloud, flying or the silence. The only thing it admits to is the ears, and it says they are for not hearing. Working title of the video: **Mow the Sky**. The song is retitled *Just a Sunday* because the title of a deadpan song should not say what the picture is about to do.

### Video use map (`docs/song-method.md`, item 1)
- **Target length:** 20.0s = 8 bars of 3/4 at 72 BPM (beat 0.833s, bar 2.5s). The three rows are bars 1-3 (one row per bar); the drop is the downbeat of bar 4; the return is the downbeat of bar 8. The edit fits the music: if a clip's lift or touchdown lands off the bar line, retime the clip by at most 8% or reroll, never move the music.
- **Pattern:** engine bed with a sung lullaby on top (0.0-7.5), hard drop to silence (7.5-17.5), hard return with the same engine and the same last line (17.5-20.0). The song exists only while the wheels are on the grass, and so does the scream.
- **Breaks (stops, not ducks):** the music and the engine both stop dead at 7.5, on the frame the wheels leave the grass, and both come back together at 17.5, on the frame the wheels touch. Ten seconds of the twenty are silent. Nothing is cross-faded and nothing rings out. The only ducking in the piece is the engine dipping 4 dB under each sung word so the words can be heard.
- **Mouths:** OFF for all 20.0s. His face is under the brim in every frame: no lip-sync, no shout, no sigh, no whistle, no smile showing. Every sung word is off-picture.
- **Whose voice, and why that is allowed:** an unnamed bass-baritone, the score's own voice, in the way a theme tune has a singer nobody sees. Allowed because (1) the man's mouth is never in the picture, so there is nothing to lip-sync and nothing the voice can contradict; (2) the lullaby describes him ("one man, small lawn, big hat") and never speaks as him, and he never reacts to it; (3) it is not a character: no name, no body, no booth; (4) it cannot be diegetic, because it stops in the air.
