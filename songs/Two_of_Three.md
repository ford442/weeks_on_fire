STYLE:

Tintinnabuli duet, 56 BPM, A minor, about 100 seconds, dead equipment hut above five thousand feet. No kit, no guitar, no choir, no pad, no reverb longer than the metal room. One small bell that can sound only A and E. There is no C in the bell. Riley sings the melodic line: calm, close, unbothered, no vibrato, no folk ornament, stepwise motion only, no leaps, site facts in a speaking volume. She does not explain the sky. She does not say what the antenna is for. Rubella sings the tintinnabuli line and nothing else: the single word "Still," on A, C, or E only, whichever triad tone is nearest Riley's note, straight tone, no other words, no harmony she invents. Do not sing their names. Structural flaw: whenever the rule asks Rubella for C, she skips, because the bell has no C, and she does not borrow a pitch. Riley does not fill the skip. The second pass uses the same words. The piece ends on the skip: bell on A, no C, no cadence added. Dark top end. Mood: a report, two tones present, one tone absent.


LYRICS:

[Bell — A, then E]

[Pass 1 — Riley, stepwise]
The hut is shut.
The gear is racked.
Wind from the west.
Last grade is on the card.
The bell sounds A.
The bell sounds E.
It is still there.

[Rubella — triad word only, skip every C]
Still.
Still.
(skip)
Still.

[Pass 2 — same words, same rule]
The hut is shut.
The gear is racked.
Wind from the west.
Last grade is on the card.
The bell sounds A.
The bell sounds E.
It is still there.

[Rubella]
Still.
Still.
(skip)
Still.

[End — the skip]
(bell on A)
(no C)


NOTES:

Ridge hut. Riley sings the facts, by step. Rubella has only the word "Still" and only the tones A, C, and E. The alarm bell is short the C. That skip is never written back in. Riley does not adjudicate the antenna, name a brother, or explain a pendant.

Runtime about 100 seconds.
