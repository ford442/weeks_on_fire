STYLE:

1970s–80s heavy metal played completely straight, 128 BPM written but felt in half-time, D minor, about two minutes thirty. Heavy, slow, committed, no wink. One riff on one overdriven guitar through a warm vintage tube amp, bass doubling it, dry 1970s kit, kick on one, snare on three, no double-kick. Vocals: Lillith sings the verses clean and bright, plain and sincere, like reading a flyer aloud, no growl, no scream, no rasp, no belting; Rubella sits dry and low under her on the second half of each line. Chorus: a gang of stacked Lillith and Rubella double-tracks chants the title in unison, hard but not shouted. The build-up reaches for a blast beat: snare roll accelerating, ride and toms climbing, the whole band leaning in. It never arrives; the kit falls back to half-time every time, and nobody remarks on it. Middle: one melodic guitar solo of about eight bars, warm, no shred. Bass and kit dry and close, small room, mixed like a 1978 record. No djent, no dropped tuning, no blast beat actually played, no occult lyric, no male voice, no Kenji, no product name, no laugh track. Structural flaw: every time the word quiet is sung it is the loudest syllable of the line, hit hardest in the vocal and the guitar, and the mix never ducks it, in any verse, chorus or the last line.

NOTES:

The flaw: "quiet" is the loudest syllable every time, and nobody turns it down. The kit is the second wrongness that is not a flaw: the build-up keeps reaching for a blast beat and the half-time groove takes it back. Do not "fix" the build-up into a blast beat; the flyer never gets one.

Why it is not the neighbors:
- **Not Article Seven / Call Forty-Seven.** Both already own "quiet hours" as Karen's: the fence flyer and the ten o'clock hold line. This flyer is unsigned, taped in the building, and the hours are one to six. No Karen, no fence, no skeleton, no 911.
- **Not Quiet Formula.** The banned Glamora ingredient and its taped-over vending slot stay in the Glam lane. No product, no bottle, no shampoo, no Kenji.
- **Not Well Fall.** No fall, no drop, no instrumental.
- **Not Ultra Screech.** The laundry room is rented to the shampoo company; this is the corkboard in it. No scream, no sting.
- **Not Halloween Snake Battle.** Not a fight.
- **Not the Lace pack.** Those stay sincere (and bright). This is heavy and slow and deadpan about a flyer.
- **Not Stamp the Riff / Policy Anvil / Car Twelve.** Bank-mates with their own objects: stamp, anvil, car. This one is a piece of paper.
