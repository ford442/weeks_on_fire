# Big City — Giant Hair-dos

Think tank for the **downtown / Fleet Street video** (Daisy Bell crowded-city
act: `db-x01` and siblings). The city is already staring. Push the *stare*
into architecture: hairdos so large they become traffic, weather, and
furniture. Strange. Awkward. Committed.

**Parent cutaway:** [`daisy-bell-cutaway.md`](daisy-bell-cutaway.md)  
**Site:** Daisy Bell page, street-sights grid  
**Not:** Ultra Screech / Glam-Sham-Poo (that lane owns teased-hair commercials).  
**Not:** marble magnets, mountain lion, well shaft, HOA gavel.

---

## Lock

- **The hair is hair.** Pins, rats, pomade, horsehair pads, a real 1890s
  Gibson Girl taken past the building line. Not foam, not a monster, not a
  wig gag that looks at camera.
- **Scale is civic.** A hairdo can be a kiosk, a traffic island, a second
  story, a bus shelter. People navigate it the way they navigate a parked
  van: annoyed, practiced, polite.
- **Awkward, not slapstick.** Missed eye contact. Delayed apologies. Two
  people saying “after you” to a curl. No pie-in-the-face. No wink.
- **Rubella & Lillith keep small hair.** Black lace, bangs, no hats, no
  gigot. They *fit*. Everyone else is the skyline. If they are in frame they
  thread through; they do not wear the joke.
- **Period London is the default city** for this video (Fleet Street energy,
  St. Paul’s deep). A still may go tighter (cafe, revolving door, elevator)
  so long as the street remains 1890–96 when you can see it.
- **Worlds do not mix.** No Glam bottle. No college. No laser snakes. No
  living flower *as hair*. The bike may pass; the hair is the subject.

The juxtaposition is the same family as Monster Mash on a laser lawn: wrong
channel, full stack. A nearly silent city has developed a millinery zoning
problem and nobody will name it.

## Sight dump

### Traffic and civic works

- A Gibson Girl pompadour occupies the whole turning radius of a hansom.
  The driver waits. She is looking in a pocket mirror the size of a coin.
- Hairdo used as a **roundabout**. Carriages orbit. A policeman on a box
  in the middle, directing with both arms, never touching the hair.
- A traffic lantern cycles red-amber-green *inside* a curl. Nobody argues
  with it. Pedestrians still wait.
- Crosswalk: the woman arrives on the beat; her hair arrives three seconds
  later, like a barge. A boy with a hoop has to stop twice.
- Parking: two spaces taken by one updo. A meter still ticking. She is in
  a shop buying pins.
- Bridge clearance. She ducks. The hair does not need to. Sparrows lift
  off the underside like barnacles.

### Doors, rooms, service

- Revolving door: one compartment is 100% hair. The other three keep
  turning. She is already inside, mortified, saying nothing.
- Cafe: two giant hairdos meet over a small marble table. Menus held at
  arm’s length. No eye contact is physically possible. They order by
  pointing at the waiter’s shoes.
- The waiter stands on a chair to be seen. He is used to this. The chair
  is part of the place setting.
- Office lobby: the hair will not fit through the door. Interview happens
  on the stoop. Both parties pretend this is the appointment.
- Elevator: doors close on the hair. Next floor, the hair arrives first.
  Then she does. Both nod, as if this is a known schedule.
- Diner ceiling fan turning slowly *in* an updo. Pie uneaten. Fan still
  doing its job. She reads a newspaper through it.

### Weather, roosts, utilities

- Rain. Four strangers wait out the shower **under** a stranger’s hairdo,
  not looking up, the way you wait under an awning you do not own.
- Pigeons roost in a Gibson Girl as if it were a hotel cornice. She knows.
  She does not shoo. One pigeon has a ribbon.
- Overhead telephone wires rest across a hairdo like a clothesline.
  Somebody’s laundry, two stockings, a telegram. She walks. The line goes
  slack and taut.
- A window-washer on a belt has mistaken a hairdo for the building. He is
  halfway down a ringlet, professional, not making eye contact with her.
- Lamp-post hatpin: a single pin the height of a flagpole. The hair does
  not sag. A small flag would be too much. Do not add a flag.

### Social physics (the awkward ones)

- Two pompadours collide in a crosswalk. Both people apologize to the
  *hair*, not to each other. A little bow. They cannot get past. A third
  person says “I’ll go around the block.”
- A couple trying to kiss. The hairdos hold them a sofa-width apart. They
  shake hands instead, very formally, and look at the cobbles.
- “After you.” “No, after you.” The curl does not move. Thirty seconds.
  A horse sighs.
- She turns to look at the flower bicycle. The hair turns a full second
  later, like a turret. Rubella and Lillith are already past.
- A milliner on the pavement arguing with a pigeon that has nested in a
  client’s finished head. The client is still wearing it. The argument is
  quiet and legal.
- Job reference: a man trying to raise his hat. The hat is somewhere in
  the hair. He greets with an empty hand. Everyone pretends they saw a hat.

### Period-London specific (Daisy Bell downtown)

- Landau with a **sunroof cut** for the hair. Four society ladies. Only
  faces visible above the leather. Hats like weather vanes. They lean in
  unison anyway.
- Stylish walking-suit woman on an ordinary bicycle. Her hair is a second
  passenger. She looks back at the living tandem; the hair looks forward.
- Broken hansom from the lyric, but the missing wheel is not the problem —
  a hairdo has filled the cab. The resigned horse is facing the other way.
- News boy shouting under a hairdo as if it were a kiosk roof. Headline
  unchanged. He is dry. It is raining past the hair.
- Policeman double-take, then pretends the hair is a hedge. He directs
  traffic around it with the same gesture he uses for a stalled cart.
- St. Paul’s in the deep background; a hairdo in the midground is the same
  silhouette, slightly wrong, closer. Fog does not care which is which.

### Endings / hold frames (do not overuse)

- Skyline: real chimneys, then a second skyline of hair. Hold. No title.
- The street empty except one updo, parked, owner gone for pins.
- Rubella and Lillith, small, in a gap the hair forgot to occupy. They do
  not comment. Pedal.

## Camera grammar (paste into stills)

Photoreal cinematic, 16:9 or 2.39:1. Late-Victorian London street or a tight
interior that still belongs to that city. Giant period hairdo as the largest
object in frame after the buildings. People at real human scale. Awkward
negative space where faces cannot meet. Soft daylight or nitrate grain if
the Daisy Bell flip is on. No text. No Glam bottle. No modern salon chrome
unless it is a single pair of scissors.

If Rubella & Lillith appear: black lace, bangs, small hair, living flower
tandem optional. They are the ones who fit.

## Why it earns a slot

Daisy Bell’s city already has hats that are “almost as ridiculous as the
bike.” Take that sentence literally and stop winking. The flower tandem
is alive. The hair is zoning. Rubella and Lillith remain the only people
who can still pass each other a look.
