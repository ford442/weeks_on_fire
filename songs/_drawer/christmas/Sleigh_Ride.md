## The video first (`docs/song-method.md`)

**The Team.** Photoreal live action, 16:9, golden hour on the granite slab at Pulpit Overlook.
The dusty sedan sits with its engine off and all four windows down. Two thin cloth-covered
wires (Lillith's salvage) run from the front bumper to a harness on one small marmot with a
brass bell on its collar. Lillith sits at the wheel with her hands at ten and two, Rubella has
her feet on the dash, Riley sits in the back with her notebook. The marmot leans into the
harness and the car does not move. Cut to the trio (nobody looks at the marmot). The car rolls
one inch; the marmot falls on its belly and gets up. The marmot sits up, looks at the sun, and
gives one whistle. **On the downbeat there are eight marmots in four pairs, the wires snap
taut, and the sedan trots round the picnic table with eight bells going and the cliff behind
it.** The three women have not moved. Rubella brushes a speck of dust off her sleeve once.
The bumper taps the end of the table, the team slumps in a heap, the bells rattle out, and
Lillith, who built it, gives one small nod.

### Video use map (`docs/song-method.md`, item 1)
- **Target length:** picture 30.0s = 18 bars of 4/4 at 144 BPM (bar 1.667s, beat 0.4167s, eighth 0.2083s). The music is 17 bars, 28.33s: it stops dead on the bumper tap and the last bar is picture and bells only. 144 was chosen so 3 bars = 5.0s, 6 bars = 10.0s and the drop lands on a clean 15.0s.
- **Pattern:** hard cuts on bar lines. Bars 1-2 intro. Bars 3-6 verse-on-action, one sung line per bar, each line cut to what it says (bar 3 the marmot, bar 4 the seats, bars 5-6 the roll and the cliff). STYLE:

Fast bluegrass hoedown played completely straight, 144 BPM locked (no drift, no swing, no tempo change), G major, 4/4, straight eighths, exactly 17 bars and 0:28.3 long (1.667 seconds a bar). Instruments only: one fiddle bowed near the fingerboard, dry and slightly dark, carrying the tune; one upright bass playing the root on beat 1 and the fifth on beat 3; one resonator guitar with a slide playing sparse chords on the off-beats; one mandolin chopping dry on beats 2 and 4; one snare with brushes keeping a steady train beat. No banjo, no electric guitar, no cymbals, no shaker, no tambourine, no bells or sleigh bells of any kind, no choir, no strings section, no pad, no sound effects. The top end is dark and rolled off, like a tape of a small wooden hall played on a small speaker, with a little tape hiss and a low-ceilinged room. Bars 1 and 2: the band alone, the fiddle plays a two-bar tune. Bars 3 to 6: the voice enters on beat 1 of bar 3 (0:03.3) over bass, mandolin and brushes, and the fiddle drops to long soft notes. Bars 7 and 8: bass and brushes drop out, leaving the voice, the slide guitar and a slow fiddle climb. At 0:13.3, on the downbeat of bar 9, everything stops dead for exactly one bar, with no tail and nothing filling it. At 0:15.0, on the downbeat of bar 10, the whole band comes back at once and full, with the voice on beat 1, and runs through bar 17. Everything stops dead at 0:28.3 on the downbeat of bar 18, with no tail and no ring-out. One dry, plain baritone voice, close-miked, half spoken and half sung, low and flat, no vibrato, no twang, no belt, no yodel, no ad-libs, no backing voices, no crowd, no hand claps, no call-outs, no "yee-haw", no laugh, the sound of a man who has said all this before. **Structural flaw:** every rising run in the track, whether the fiddle's scale runs, the bass climbs or the rising sung phrase in bars 7 and 8, stops one step short of the note it is climbing toward and falls back down. Every falling run finishes. Nothing ever reaches the top of a climb. Do not repair a run, do not add the missing top note, do not finish on a high note. Mood: late sun on bare rock, the last hour of a county fair. Original tune. Professionally neutral, never wink.

### What the song is
A hoedown sung flat by someone who is not in the picture, about a team that starts as one. The
verse says "one in the harness" while the picture shows a single marmot that cannot move a
sedan. The break is one bar of nothing and one whistle. On the downbeat the song says "eight in
the harness" and the picture has eight, the bells go, and the car goes round the table. The
fiddle keeps trying to climb and never reaches the top of anything. Nobody in the car looks at
the team. The bells are theirs and not the band's: the track has none of its own.

The song never says marmot, sleigh, Christmas, snow, car, cliff, lion or "you". The only
Christmas it admits to is the one line "no reindeer round here, so we made do", said as if
reindeer were something the county had run out of. The dust is the snow; it never becomes
snow. The picture holds the warmth: three people riding to the end of a ridge behind a team
they did not ask for, and the one who built it nods at the end. No nativity, no Santa, no
faith joke, and nothing in the lyric is aimed at one.

### Minimax generation (`docs/song-method.md`, item 3)
- **Genre:** fast bluegrass hoedown. The picture is a team trotting a car round a table at the end of a ridge in dusty golden light, so the bed is a boom-chick trot (bass on 1 and 3, mandolin chop on 2 and 4) played on dry acoustic instruments in a small wooden hall. A hoedown goes round and round, the sedan goes round and round, and the music does not say so. No banjo (too bright for the sonic bible's dark top end), no sleigh bells (the picture owns them).
- **Locked:** 144 BPM, G major, 4/4, 17 bars, 0:28.3. Instruments: fiddle, upright bass, resonator slide guitar, mandolin, brushed snare. One baritone, half spoken. Original tune and lyric, official section tags, no parentheses.
- **The structural flaw (held, never fixed):** see below.
- **Expect to fix in the edit.** Minimax will not reliably stop dead on a bar line, leave exactly one bar of nothing, or keep the climbs short; it likes to land the top note, and it may add a banjo or bells. Make several takes and keep the one whose climbs fall shortest and whose first three bars are cleanest. ### The structural flaw (held, never fixed)
**The music can go down the hill but not up it.** Every rising run stops one step short of its destination and falls back:

- the slow fiddle climb in bars 7-8 stops short, shorter the second time;

It is never fixed: no run reaches its top, no final chord is approached from below, no button, no high note at the end. The track stops dead on the root, falling.
