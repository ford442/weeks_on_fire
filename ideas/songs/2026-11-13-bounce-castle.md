# Bounce Castle

**Genre collision:** 1990s UK happy hardcore × Renaissance-faire fanfare (herald trumpets, krummhorn, shawm, tabor, lute). A rave played by a royal court.

**Visual concept:** Lace Waterfall play a gig on a giant inflatable castle on a village green, with a small royal court bouncing on it too. Every kick drum launches everything into the air: the amp, the king, a birthday cake, and a pantomime horse. Rubella goes up as high as the flags and comes down every time with her hair, lace and mic exactly where they were. She never stops singing and never looks down. Lillith plays upside down in mid-air with a flat face. The drops make it worse: the first doubles the height of every bounce, and the last bounces the whole castle off the ground and away over the trees.

**Laugh (kid words):** *Everything on the bouncy castle goes flying, and the singer goes up to the sky and back without messing up her hair.*

**BPM:** 175, F major, up a whole step to G for the final chorus

**Target length:** ~2:23 · 13 segments (8-bar blocks ≈ 11 s)

## Styles

Styles: 1990s UK happy hardcore colliding with Renaissance faire fanfare, 175 BPM, F major with a whole-step key change up into the final chorus, about two minutes twenty, relentless bouncy four-on-the-floor kick, offbeat open hi-hats, rolling breakbeat fills into every section, bouncy plucked sub bass, bright euphoric piano stabs, hoover synth swells, herald trumpets playing the chorus hook in unison with a supersaw lead, krummhorn and shawm doubling the verse melody, tabor drum rolls before each drop, lute arpeggios alone in the intro, clean bright electric guitar octave riffs through the verses and a shredding electric guitar solo that trades bars with a krummhorn, half-time breakdown where the kick stops and only a held pad and one tabor remain, huge glossy joyful wall of sound, sincere, no parody, no comedy sound effects; lead vocal is a dry, slightly raspy, completely flat female voice, unhurried and deadpan, nursery-plain phrasing, never pitched up, no chipmunk effect, no belting, no ad-libs, no vocal runs; the breakdown has two short spoken lines in the same flat voice, close and dry, no reverb; no male voice, no choir, no crowd noise. Use the Rubella voice.
