STYLE:

Sincere early-1900s Montmartre café-concert comic song in a brisk 2/4 at 132 BPM, D major, about two minutes fifteen, sung entirely in French with crisp diction. A bright honky-tonk cabaret piano plays an oom-pah left hand, a musette accordion and a clarinet trade little runs between lines, a tuba walks the bass, and a snare plays with brushes and one rim-click per bar. Lead one: bright, conspiratorial, quick patter, delighted by every bad development. Lead two: dry, slightly raspy, unhurried, delivering each refrain tag flat, as if annoyed by a bakery. A small mixed chorus shouts the first three lines of each refrain. The refrain lifts each time; the last one modulates up a step. Spoken intro and outro, dry, no music under them. Warm, wooden small-room sound, earnest, music-hall energy, no parody, no comedy sound effects. Use Lillith and Rubella voices.

LYRICS:

[Intro]
RUBELLA: Une baguette, s'il vous plaît.

[Verse 1]
LILLITH: Elle sort de la boulangerie, la baguette sous le bras
Mais la baguette continue, la baguette ne s'arrête pas
Elle passe la porte, le trottoir, et la rue d'en face
Le boulanger la tient encore, au fond, derrière la glace

[Chorus]
TOUS: La baguette, la baguette,
Elle est longue, elle est chaude, elle est dorée!
La baguette, la baguette,
RUBELLA: Elle est un peu trop longue.

[Verse 2]
LILLITH: Elle prend le bus, la baguette sort par les fenêtres
Le chauffeur klaxonne, les passagers en prennent un mètre
Un pigeon s'assoit dessus, puis un autre, puis cent
Ils voyagent sur la croûte comme des passagers contents

[Chorus]
TOUS: La baguette, la baguette,
Elle est longue, elle est chaude, elle est dorée!
La baguette, la baguette,
RUBELLA: Elle est toujours trop longue.

[Verse 3]
RUBELLA: Je rentre à la maison, je monte l'escalier
La baguette monte avec moi, sur six étages entiers
Elle passe chez la voisine, chez le concierge, chez le chat
Chacun casse un morceau, chacun dit merci, et voilà

[Bridge]
LILLITH: Attends. Regarde par la fenêtre.

[Verse 4]
LILLITH: Du haut de notre fenêtre, on voit toute la ville
RUBELLA: La baguette fait le tour, comme un ruban tranquille
LILLITH: Elle passe le pont, la gare, l'église et le marché
BOTH: Et revient, toute chaude, à la boulangerie d'à côté

[Final Chorus]
TOUS: La baguette, la baguette,
Elle fait le tour de la ville!
La baguette, la baguette,
RUBELLA: Demain, je prends un croissant.

NOTES:

**English gloss:**
- *Intro:* "One baguette, please."
- *V1:* She comes out of the bakery with the baguette under her arm, but the baguette keeps
  going and doesn't stop. It passes the door, the pavement, the street opposite. The baker is
  still holding the other end, at the back, behind the glass.
- *Refrain:* It's long, it's warm, it's golden! / "It's a bit too long." (Then: "It's still too
  long.")
- *V2:* It takes the bus and sticks out of the windows. The driver honks; the passengers each
  take a metre. A pigeon sits on it, then another, then a hundred, riding on the crust like
  happy passengers.
- *V3:* I go home and climb the stairs. The baguette climbs with me, six whole floors. It goes
  through the neighbour's, the concierge's, the cat's. Everyone breaks off a piece and says
  thank you, and there you go.
- *Bridge:* "Wait. Look out of the window."
- *V4:* From our window you can see the whole town. The baguette goes all the way round like a
  calm ribbon, over the bridge, past the station, the church and the market, and comes back,
  still warm, to the bakery next door.
- *Final:* It goes around the town! / "Tomorrow I'll get a croissant."

**Engine:** things that keep happening, then wrong scale in verse 4. Every verse is a new
place the bread gets to; the last verse shows it was a circle all along.

**Do-not:** no real bakery or brand. "The cat" in verse 3 is a neighbour's cat, not the Black
Cat (she doesn't do apartments or cute). No knife, nobody hurt.
