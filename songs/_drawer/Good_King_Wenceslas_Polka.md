# Good King Wenceslas (Brass Polka) — drawer sketch

**Drawer.** `promote?: no`. Not in `songs/` proper, not codegen'd, no `songId` bound.
Video idea: [`../../ideas/good-king-wenceslas.md`](../../ideas/good-king-wenceslas.md).

**Rights:** the words are John Mason Neale's (1853) and the tune is the spring carol *Tempus
adest floridum* from *Piae Cantiones* (1582). Both are in the public domain. The arrangement
below is new. Don't base it on, or name, any modern recorded arrangement.

| field | value |
|---|---|
| bpm / key / length | 132 (2/4 polka) / F major, up a whole step to G for verse 5 / ~2:50 |
| who sings | Lillith (the King's lines), Rubella (the Page's lines), a small mixed village choir (the narration) |
| why a polka | Wenceslas was Duke of Bohemia. A Bohemian brass band is the most local thing that could play his carol, and it's the wrong channel for a solemn hymn. |
| neighbor it must not replace | *Mysterium* (bright Christmas Italo-pop, also lifts a whole step at the end) · *Regulation Powder* (snow, the woods, the two of them; no music bed) |

STYLE:

Sincere Bohemian village brass-band polka arrangement of a traditional English Christmas carol, 2/4 at 132 BPM, F major, rising a whole step to G major for the last verse, about two minutes fifty. Full village brass band: two flugelhorns carrying the melody in thirds, tenor horns and baritone playing the oom-pah afterbeats, a big tuba on the downbeats, clarinet runs between phrases, a snare, a bass drum and a crash cymbal, and one handbell rung on the first beat of every verse. A small mixed village choir sings the narrated verses in plain unison, warm and slightly rustic. Two female solo voices for the dialogue: the King is bright, conspiratorial and grand; the Page is dry, slightly raspy and unhurried, and sounds cold. Brass fanfare intro, a short stormy brass interlude after verse three with a tuba trill and wind-like clarinet trills, a key change into verse five, and a big final cadence with cymbal. Outdoor winter festival sound, earnest and joyful, no parody, no sleigh bells, no comedy sound effects. Use Lillith and Rubella voices.

LYRICS:

[Intro]

[Verse 1]
CHOIR: Good King Wenceslas looked out on the feast of Stephen
When the snow lay round about, deep and crisp and even
Brightly shone the moon that night, though the frost was cruel
When a poor man came in sight, gath'ring winter fuel

[Verse 2]
LILLITH: Hither, page, and stand by me, if thou know'st it, telling
Yonder peasant, who is he? Where and what his dwelling?
RUBELLA: Sire, he lives a good league hence, underneath the mountain
Right against the forest fence, by Saint Agnes' fountain

[Verse 3]
LILLITH: Bring me flesh, and bring me wine, bring me pine logs hither
Thou and I will see him dine, when we bear them thither
CHOIR: Page and monarch, forth they went, forth they went together
Through the rude wind's wild lament and the bitter weather

[Interlude]

[Verse 4]
RUBELLA: Sire, the night is darker now, and the wind blows stronger
Fails my heart, I know not how, I can go no longer
LILLITH: Mark my footsteps, good my page, tread thou in them boldly
Thou shalt find the winter's rage freeze thy blood less coldly

[Verse 5]
CHOIR: In his master's steps he trod, where the snow lay dinted
Heat was in the very sod which the saint had printed
Therefore, Christian men, be sure, wealth or rank possessing
Ye who now will bless the poor shall yourselves find blessing

[Outro]

NOTES:

**Words unchanged.** All five verses are sung exactly as the 1853 text, including "King,"
"monarch," "his master" and "Christian men." Lillith plays the King and the song doesn't
adjust the pronouns. The juxtaposition is that nobody thinks it needs adjusting.

**The drop** is verse 5, line 2: *Heat was in the very sod which the saint had printed.*
That's the line the video turns literal. The key change one line earlier sets it up.

**Production rule:** the choir sings the narration and the soloists only sing dialogue. If
MiniMax puts the soloists on the choir lines, regenerate; the King and Page should only open
their mouths when they're talking to each other.
