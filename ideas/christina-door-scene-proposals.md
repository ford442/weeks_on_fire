## 1. Examine

### The scene as it stands

The door scene is beat 2, **"Thirty Seconds,"** of the Sweden heart cutaway. It is the reason the
cutaway exists. Rubella and Lillith come up Christina hill in the snow at night. They reach the
wrong door in a packed-snow mound that is really a roof. The door is carved dark wood with a gilt
throat. The aria is already playing when they get there, over the Wooden House continuo, and they
have arrived late. They stand inside the gilt throat. Christina stands in the white marble atrium,
"one cut behind them, as if the house folded." She welcomes them in Swedish. Lillith looks at the
gold and asks about the harpsichord. Rubella says they're not staying. Christina has already
agreed. She thanks them for coming and they leave. She doesn't turn, and the aria keeps going.

All the material for it is spread across five pages, and none of it is a screenplay:

### Contradictions and soft spots

Ranked by how much they affect this scene.

**C1. The night-home is a party by the house's own count.** The company page says "Christina plus
one. That is a house. Christina plus two is company. Five faces is a party." But the night-home's
fixed seats are the accompanist and the listener, which makes Christina plus two. Add Rubella and
Lillith and the scene holds **exactly five faces**, the number that gets thrown out. The bible's Do
list squares this by calling the accompanist and listener "one extra body per plate," which only
works if they sit in different location plates. The gilt plate then holds the accompanist *and*
both ladies, which is three.
→ **Working fix used below:** (a) the accompanist is **hands only, never a face** (that's the
company page's "we may never see a face" reading), so the scene has four faces at most. (b) "Per
plate" means **per frame**: one principal plus at most one other body. **Christina never shares a
frame with both ladies.** In practice the ladies stay in the gilt, she stays in the white, and the
cut connects them. That is beat 2's "as if the house folded," taken literally. The owner should
confirm this.

**C2. "Already on" vs. "never across it."** The aria is locked as already playing when we arrive.
The bible's delivery note says she speaks "before the aria or after the last *Ah*, never across
it." The "Threshold" monologue even has "(the aria is about to begin; she stops speaking)," which
puts the aria *after* her welcome. Read strictly, she can't say a word during the 30 seconds.
→ **Working fix used below:** the aria is on, and she speaks **only in its rests**, the breaths
between sung phrases. She never speaks over a sung note. The guests *can* talk over the aria,
because that rule is hers, not theirs. That asymmetry turns into a joke: the guests talk across the
song and the hostess waits for the gaps. **This needs an owner ruling.** If "never across it" means
the whole aria, rests included, then only the bible's "after the last *Ah*" option is left. That
would end the aria inside the 30 seconds and break "the aria goes on without them."

**C3. "Heard first" vs. ducked.** Beat 1 has the harpsichord heard first from the snow, "thin,
dry, D minor," with "the aria on somewhere under it." The music lock says the continuo **ducks 8–12
dB for the aria**. If the aria is on, the harpsichord is the quieter layer, so it can't be what
reaches the door first unless it's heard *through* the aria.
→ Proposals keep the continuo ducked about 10 dB for the whole 30 seconds. "Heard first" lives only
in the optional pre-roll (§2, bookends), where the snow muffles the voice more than the plucked
attack. That's a Nova call.

**C4. *Hovsångare* is the masculine form.** A female court singer in Swedish is a
**hovsångerska**. All four files say *hovsångare*. It's either a spelling bug or the clerk got the
title almost right. If it's on purpose, it's a cousin of the Pizza Guy's almost-right name (one
syllable off, never corrected). Either way it belongs to clerk night, not the door. It's flagged
here so whoever writes the folder decides deliberately.

**C5. Drift vs. roof.** The bible exterior says "from outside it could be a drift," and beat 1 says
"a drift that isn't." Elio's note on issue-22 says it "has to read as a **roof, not a drift**." The
plate-credit row calls it a "snow-dome door," which suggests a snow globe. The exterior prompts
below lean toward roof: a clean eave edge and a crisp load line.

**C6. Is the accompanist ever seen?** The company page says "we may never see a face — hands, a
candle, the lid." The bible says "heard before **she** is seen," which implies she is seen
eventually, and it genders a seat the company page leaves unnamed. Proposals follow the company
page: hands at most.

**C7. Saying "Thirty seconds" out loud.** In the bible's sample exchange Rubella says "Thirty
seconds." Christina's secret is "how long guests are allowed to stay … She will not say it aloud,"
and building-cast says Rubella "knows the number before anyone says it." Christina technically
never says it, but the number still gets spoken in her house. **None of the three versions speaks
the number.** Rubella measures it in other units instead.

**C8. The seam is Qing's.** Building-cast has Lillith wanting to "put her hand on the seam." Qing's
night spends the seam: "she nearly asks… It's a good seam." If Lillith touches it at the door,
Qing's beat has already been used. Proposals let Lillith **look** toward where the gilt stops and
never reach it.

**C9. Who opens the door?** She is never at the snow door, and no file says who opens it.
Proposals: **it's already open**, like the aria. We're late for that too.

**C10. Subtitles vs. "not a translation over the Swedish."** The voice guide forbids an English
bed laid over the Swedish. Burned-in subtitles are a text layer, not an audio bed, so this page
treats them as allowed. **Only her spoken lines get subtitled, never the aria**, which has no
lyric yet and shouldn't get one by accident.

**C11. Other nights (not this scene, noted while reading).** On Nova night, Nova sits in the gilt
hall listening to the continuo. If the accompanist is playing live, that's Christina plus two.
Wrap-student night has the same problem if the accompanist is still at the bench. The faceless
accompanist from C1 fixes both.

**C12. The callback bank has no Christina entries.** It was last refreshed 2026-10-05, a week after
the Christina pages landed. The bank's own rule is "add the path here in the same commit." Not done
here, per instructions. It's a follow-up for whoever promotes.

## 2. Three versions of the door

### Shared rules for all three

- **Plates.** The ladies are always in the gilt or outside. Christina is always in the white. They
  never share a frame. Each frame holds one principal plus at most one other body.
- **Clock.** At 68 BPM a 4/4 bar lasts 3.53s, so **the visit is eight and a half bars.** The half
  bar is the late arrival.
- **Mix.** The aria is in progress at 0:00. The continuo stays ducked about −10 dB throughout. The
  last *Ah* comes **after** they've gone, so the ladies never hear the continuo come back up.
  That's why Lillith hums it on the way down.
- **Rests.** "Rest" in the tables below means a breath between sung phrases. Times are proposals.
  When the aria exists, Nova moves the **line** to the nearest real rest and never the rest to the
  line.
- **Her mouth.** Spoken Swedish lipsync is the riskiest thing Grok Imagine will be asked to do in
  this cutaway. Each version says how it handles that. Version C avoids it entirely.
- **Generation.** Generate silent and lay audio in post (the aria and continuo files don't exist
  yet). Add this negative block to every prompt below:
  > *no other people, no crowd, no jewelry, no crown, no tiara, no fjord, no village, no chimney smoke, no lit windows, no headlights, no television, no hallway, no likeness to any real singer or celebrity*

### Version A — "Less Than a Chirp"

**Comic angle: two professionals of the threshold.** Rubella keeps a private list of every door in
the building and how long it's safe to stand at each one. The house keeps a secret number. They
meet and **agree on everything without either side naming the number**. All the comedy is
agreement: she welcomes them, they decline, she approves, they go. Rubella buttons her coat on the
way in, which is the opposite of taking it off.

**Grammar:** five 6s segments of shot/reverse across the century cut, then an exterior exit, then
a final shot in the white. **30s.**
**Her mouth:** she's on camera in A2 and A5. Every line is two words or fewer and lands in a rest,
so a closed-mouth hold reads fine if the lipsync fails.

leaves her nowhere to go.

### Version C — "The Whole Evening"

**Comic angle: the wrong channel, full commitment.** Christina performs **an entire Swedish dinner
evening** in correct set phrases: welcome, come in, an offer of coffee, thanks for the evening,
come again. Rubella answers each with the proper English courtesy. Nobody acknowledges that the
evening lasted thirty seconds and no one ever came in. *Tack för i kväll* ("thank you for this
evening") is what a host says at midnight after four courses. Here it comes at 0:18.

**Grammar:** two 15s segments, **two long takes and one cut.** "Two centuries in one cut" stops
being a description and becomes the edit. The ladies hold the gilt take with Christina off-screen.
Christina holds the white take with the ladies off-screen. **Every line crosses the cut as O.S.
dialogue.** **30s.**
**Her mouth:** **no lipsync anywhere.** She never speaks on camera, and the ladies' lines in C2 are
off-screen. It's the cheapest version to generate: two segments, no mouth risk.
**Bodies:** C2 is the version's one use of the **atrium listener**, kept soft and small with their
back to the glass, which makes Christina plus one. The scene's only guest who's staying is the one
nobody explains.

**Why it plays:** The first word of the visit comes back as the last one, changed. *Välkommen*
becomes *Välkommen åter*, the phrase on a Swedish shop door. It's polite, and it means goodbye. The
coffee is offered and politely refused, the evening is thanked, and the door is pointed out as
help, not as a hint. Every courtesy is real and every one has an end, which is the bible's
"warm in form, cool in duration" line acted out.

**Lock check:** She never asks about the house or answers about it (*Kaffe?* is an offer, which
her voice allows). Rubella never says the number. Lillith's "This was lovely" is a guest's
goodbye spoken 13 seconds in, not a request to stay. *Ni hittar dörren* is help, and Lillith wasn't
asking to stay, so it's not a no.

## 3. Swappable beats

Each of these can drop into any version. Every line is Swedish with a subtitle, or English from the
ladies. Risks are flagged.

**1. "Välkommen åter."** *(for A or B; it's already C's closer)*
Replace the final *Hej då* with **Välkommen åter.** *(Come again.)* The shop-door goodbye. She has
said "welcome" twice and meant two opposite things.
*Risk:* none. Don't use it in C as well as its own closer.

**2. "Load-bearing."** *(any gilt shot)*
**LILLITH** *(looking up at a ceiling that is snow)*: Is the roof—
**RUBELLA:** Load-bearing.
Callback: *Natural Ventilation* Beat D ("load-bearing, not a punchline"). It also answers Elio's
note in dialogue: the snow is a roof.
*Risk:* "Nobody rules the building safe or unsafe" is a rule for the building. This is the house,
and Rubella's line describes it without ruling on it. Deliver it flat.

**3. Rubella closes the door.** *(replace "the draft stops" in A5 or C2)*
On the way out, Rubella pulls the carved door shut behind them, gently, with one hand. The latch
clicks and the gown pile goes still at the same instant. Callback: *the never-closed gate* (Forest
Road 4N22, where nobody closes the gate in any shot). Rubella, who keeps a list of every door, is
the one who shuts this one.
*Risk:* lock C9 above has the door already open on arrival. Closing it on the way out is fine. Just
don't show anyone opening it.

**4. The hostess gift.** *(replace Lillith's hand-to-the-gold in A3 or her offer in B2)*
A Swedish guest brings a gift. Lillith has a vacuum tube wrapped in a dish towel like an egg
(callback: *Lillith's 6BQ5*, "a vacuum tube wrapped in a dish towel like an egg"). She holds it out.
**CHRISTINA** *(rest)*: Tack. *(Thank you.)* Christina carries no props, and the house has nowhere
to put a 1962 Dutch tube, so Lillith puts it back in her pocket on the way out.
*Risks:* (a) the tube **must leave with her**, because a tube left in the gilt throat would be a
second leak; (b) hold it **low or at the waist, never at chest height**, since a held object at
chest height is the Pizza Guy's grammar and he's barred from this hill; (c) if it plays in B, it
replaces B's offer, so don't use both.

**5. Shoes.** *(any gilt shot; the highest-risk swap here)*
Swedish custom is shoes off at the door. On arrival Lillith reaches down to unbuckle a heel.
**CHRISTINA** *(O.S., rest)*: Ni behöver inte. *(You don't need to.)* Lillith straightens. The
courtesy and the eviction are the same sentence.
*Risk:* **this is the nearest the series can come to "a guest who takes off their coat," which the
bible says never to write.** It only works if the buckle is **never undone**. The hand reaches and
stops. It also isn't a *no* to Lillith. It's a release from a custom, and she wasn't asking for
anything. Owner's call.

**6. "Extended dwell is an amenity."** *(A3 or C1)*
**LILLITH** *(quoting the building packet, hopefully)*: Extended dwell is an amenity.
**RUBELLA:** Not here.
Callback: *Madelyn's laminated memo* ("Extended dwell is an amenity. It's in the packet. Page
four."). Madelyn can't come up the hill, but her memo crosses lanes as a line, which the lane rule
allows.
*Risk:* "Not here" is Rubella to Lillith, not Christina. It's a correction, not the one *no*. Keep
it flat and kind.

**7. The listener, unnamed.** *(C1 or any gilt shot)*
**LILLITH** *(squinting past the gold toward something in the white)*: Who's—
**RUBELLA:** Not ours.
It's the only acknowledgement the listener ever gets, and it introduces no one and names no one.
*Risk:* the ladies can only see into the white because the house "folded." Keep it a glance and
never an eyeline match that puts both plates in one frame.

**8. "Gå försiktigt i backen."** *(replace "Tack för att ni kom" in any version)*
**CHRISTINA** *(rest)*: Gå försiktigt i backen. *(Careful on the hill.)*
**RUBELLA** *(already going)*: Always.
A farewell phrased as weather advice. At four words it fits her two-to-seven, and it's the closest
she ever comes to looking outside.
*Risk:* it nudges toward "refuses questions about the house." It's a statement, not an answer, so
it's fine. Don't follow it with anything about the cars.

## Recommendation

**Version C** is the strongest picture and the cheapest to make: two generations, no lipsync, and
the one cut does the "two centuries" work by itself. **Version A** has the most laughs per second
("Less than a chirp" and the three-second goodbye to an empty door). **Version B** is the one that
develops a character, but it spends the cars and the hum, which beats 1–5 want spread across other
cuts. If you want to mix: C's two-take structure, with swap #3 (Rubella closes the door) in place
of the footsteps in C2, so the pile goes still on the latch.

Rulings this page needs from the owner before promoting: **C1** (does "per plate" mean per frame,
and is the accompanist faceless) and **C2** (may she speak in the aria's rests).
