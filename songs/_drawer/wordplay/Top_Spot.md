STYLE:

Sincere fairground waltz-pop, 3/4 at 132 BPM, F major, about one minute fifty. A band organ (fairground pipe organ) plays the oom-pah-pah with bright glockenspiel and a ukulele strumming the waltz, upright bass, light brushed snare and a triangle on beat one of every fourth bar. The melody climbs step by step through the first half, peaks on one held phrase, then descends using the same melody backwards, so the tune mirrors the lyric. Lead one: bright, conspiratorial, delighted. Lead two: dry, slightly raspy, singing the same lines back down with no more excitement than a weather report. At the peak both sing together over a held organ chord, and the band drops out for one bar. The opening and closing spoken words are identical, dry, close-mic, with no music under them. Sunny, warm, tape-saturated, earnest, no parody, no comedy sound effects. Use Lillith and Rubella voices.

NOTES:

**The device is the tactic, not the subject.** The song never mentions palindromes,
mirrors or words. It's a Ferris wheel ride. The structure makes the ride: up eight lines,
one line at the top, down the same eight lines in reverse. The lyric sheet reads the same
from either end, and so does the wheel.

**Lines that work both ways.** Every line was written to be true going up *and* coming
down: corn dogs "changing size" (smaller on the way up, bigger on the way down), the goose
"at eye level" (passing both times), and the ticket in hand (Rubella ends holding a ticket,
because she's going again).

**The peak line is a letter palindrome:** *Noon, top spot, noon.* Strip the spaces and
commas and it reads the same backwards. It's sung, not explained.

**The joke:** Lillith opens with "Again." and Rubella closes with the same word. She
enjoyed it. She will not say so in any other way.

**Callback:** *Thursday's weather rolling in on a Tuesday* is Lillith's weather-predicting
ping-pong table ("It does Thursday's weather. On Tuesday."), seen from the top of the wheel.

**Do-not:** no famous palindrome sentences (Tattarrattat's rule). No list of palindromic
words. No tape-reversed vocals: the mirror is in the writing.
