# The Pizza Guy

**Status:** recurring
**Primary episodes:** Episode 3 (porch reveal) · Episode 4 (doorway, burritos) · *House of Mirrors Rally* (button) · *Ambient Lighting with Ambition* (order name) · EyeWash Station — **Out For Delivery** block
**Role in one sentence:** The universe's only constant — the one person who arrives exactly when chaos peaks, and the only character in the series who has never once asked what is happening.

---

## Logline bio

The Pizza Guy is not brave and he is not enlightened. He is **post-surprise**: at some point
before Episode 1 he stopped being surprised, and never restarted. He arrives when the
disaster is at its loudest, says the name on the order — which is almost right — and leaves
extra napkins. Nobody has ever explained him and nobody ever will.

---

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Red delivery uniform, clean, correctly worn. Whatever the uniform is that week, it fits and it is **never improvised**
- Dead eyes — not menacing, not sad. The eyes of a man on his fourth stop of a normal shift
- Perfect posture. He is the straightest vertical line in any frame he is in
- Professionally neutral face. He has one expression and it is the one you'd wear reading a street number
- Phone held at chest height, screen visible for exactly long enough: **23%**
- Same shoes in every uniform. Same pace in every uniform. Match-cut on the stride
- **Signature props:** the box (or bag, or clipboard, or ladder, depending on the week) · the phone at 23% · extra napkins · a receipt pad nobody signs
- **Never change without a story reason:** 23% · the shoes · the pace · the posture · the neutrality. **He never runs. He never flinches. He never looks at the thing behind him.**

### The name — a deliberate refusal

**He does not have one on screen. He will not get one.** This is load-bearing, not coy.

- He has a name tag. In every uniform. It is **always** illegible in frame — turned, obscured by a strap, blown out by the porch light, cropped, or reading something that is clearly the *company's* name rather than his.
- Nobody asks. Not Karen, who asks everybody. Not Liliane, who knows the exits. Not Oz, who logs everything.
- In slates, scripts, subtitles, and `content/` he is **PIZZA GUY**, in caps, always — even in the burrito episode, even in the parking-enforcement vest. The joke is that his job changes and his title doesn't.
- If a scene absolutely requires a name spoken aloud, it is spoken by a stranger who is wrong about it, and he does not correct them. Which is also the other bit:

### The almost-right name

> "Order for… Viviane?" *(`notes/scenes/house-of-mirrors-rally.md`, Beat G)*

**Origin (writers' room only, never spoken):** the names are not wrong because of bad
handwriting, a mis-keyed app, or an accent. They are wrong because **the universe mislabels
people, and he refuses to correct the universe.** He reads what the order says. The order is
what he was given. Adjusting it would be a claim about who someone actually is, and that is
above his pay grade and outside his route.

**Rules:**
- The name is always *almost* right. One vowel, one letter, one syllable off. Never a different name; never a joke name.
- **It is never corrected**, by him or by anyone. If a character corrects him, the scene is wrong. *(`notes/scenes/README.md` scene-level rules.)*
- It is never explained, never lampshaded, never made into a mystery beat. No character says "how did he know."
- **On "never wrong about the name on the order"** (`content/characters.json`): both statements are true and they are the same statement. The order says what it says; he reads it correctly; the *order* is wrong. He is never wrong. He is just never right about you.
- Running list of established misses — extend, don't repeat: **Viviane** (for Vivienne) · **Vivian** (alt) · one for Rubella in *Ambient Lighting with Ambition*. Keep a new one per appearance.

### Twenty-three percent

Not a joke about batteries. **It is a clock he trusts more than wall clocks.**

- It is 23% when he arrives and 23% when he leaves. It is 23% in the morning episode and 23% at 4 AM. Nobody notices; nobody comments; nobody ever sees him charge it.
- He checks it the way other people check a watch — before the knock, not after. It is how he knows he's on time, and he is always on time, and "on time" in this show means *exactly when it gets worst*.
- If a script needs him to be early or late, the number does not change. **The number never changes.** It is the one supernatural fact about him and it is never presented as one.
- Do not confuse with [`Nine Percent Battery`](../../songs/Nine_Percent_Battery.md) — that is Rubella's smoke detector, a different building, and a completely unrelated dying object.

## Voice guide

- **Sentence length:** four to nine words. Flat, courteous, brief. He uses complete sentences and no filler — no "uh," no "so," no "anyway."
- **Vocabulary he overuses:** *order for* · *extra napkins are in the bag* · *tip is appreciated* · the crust, the address, the bag, the schedule — concrete nouns only
- **Vocabulary he refuses:** *sorry* · *careful* · *are you okay* · *what the hell* · any question about the scene. He asks exactly one kind of question: a menu question. ("Pepperoni or margherita?" is, in context, the funniest sentence in the series because it is sincere.)
- **Humor type:** none. **He is not making jokes.** Every line is the plainest available true statement, and the comedy is entirely the gap between the sentence and the frame. If a take sounds like a punchline, it's wrong.
- **Delivery note:** deadpan male or unfazed androgynous, zero vibrato, professionally neutral, never "acting." Same instruction as the song. *(`songs/Pepperoni_or_Margherita.md`.)*

### Sample monologue — "Route" (6–10 lines; for the EyeWash *Out For Delivery* block, over 90 seconds of him walking)

Not a confession. A man answering a question nobody asked, at the volume of a weather report.

> Fourteen stops tonight. This is nine.
> Nine is always the long one — there's no sidewalk on the north side, so you cross twice.
> People think the hard part's the dark. It isn't. The dark's fine. The dark holds still.
> The hard part's the gates. Every gate's a different gate.
> (he checks the phone: 23%)
> I've got time.
> Somebody asked me last week how I stay calm. I said the crust is fine.
> They wanted a different answer. There isn't one — the crust was fine.
> (beat)
> Ten's on the corner with the lights. Ten tips.

### Sample exchange — with Justine (the register: two flat people, mutual respect, no acknowledgment of it)

> **JUSTINE:** You're early.
> **PIZZA GUY:** I'm on time.
> **JUSTINE:** The thing hasn't finished yet.
> **PIZZA GUY:** Order for… Justin?
> **JUSTINE:** (taking it) Close enough.
> **PIZZA GUY:** Extra napkins are in the bag.
> **JUSTINE:** (to the others, not looking up) He's the only one of us with a plan.

### Sample exchange — with Karen (the register: authority meets a man with no interest in it)

> **KAREN:** Do you have a permit to be on this street after nine?
> **PIZZA GUY:** I have an address.
> **KAREN:** That's not the same thing.
> **PIZZA GUY:** No.
> **KAREN:** …Are you going to argue with me?
> **PIZZA GUY:** Order for Karyn?
> **KAREN:** (beat) *Karen.*
> **PIZZA GUY:** (handing it over) Extra napkins are in the bag.
> *(She takes it. She does not amend the agenda. This is the only recorded instance of Karen dropping an item.)*

### Sample exchange — with Oz (the register: two men, zero questions — the best pairing in the series)

> **OZ:** You're not on the log.
> **PIZZA GUY:** No.
> **OZ:** (writing) Delivery. Unscheduled.
> **PIZZA GUY:** Order for… Ozzie?
> **OZ:** Oz.
> **PIZZA GUY:** Oz.
> **OZ:** (taking the box, still writing) Thanks. It's not haunted, it's unscheduled.
> **PIZZA GUY:** Tip is appreciated.
> **OZ:** It's in the drawer. It's always in the drawer.
> *(He takes it from the drawer without being shown which drawer.)*

## Backstory (committed — writers' room only; **none of this is ever spoken on screen**)

- **Origin.** He has always had this job. Not mystically — the way a man who started at nineteen has always had this job. There is a first day in there somewhere and it is not interesting and we will never see it.
- **Formative incident (series-relevant).** **The night he stopped being surprised**, which happened before Episode 1 and is never depicted, never flashed back to, never described. The only thing established about it: it was not a cosmic event. It was an ordinary bad night — a wrong address, a long wait, a door that opened on something a person shouldn't have to look at — and he finished the shift. The meter never reset. *(If this is ever tempting to dramatize: don't. The moment we see it, he becomes a man with a backstory instead of a constant.)*
- **Secret he protects.** Nothing, deliberately. He has no secret and this is the strangest thing about him in a show where everyone else is a locked drawer. What he *has* is a policy: he doesn't mention what he sees, at any door, ever. Not out of discretion — it's simply not part of the delivery.
- **Does he know?** **Yes — the way you know a bus route.** He knows that when he's dispatched somewhere, something is usually happening there; he has noticed; he finds it unremarkable, the way you'd find it unremarkable that the last stop of the night is always the one with the dog. He does not know *why*, has not theorized, and would find the question rude.
  **On screen: never confirmed, never denied, never asked.** *(Locked as unresolved — see [README](README.md#open-questions-do-not-resolve-casually).)*
- **What would break him on screen.** Being *thanked properly* — someone stopping, using the right name, and meaning it. He would have no procedure for it. Two seconds of nothing, then: "Extra napkins are in the bag," and he leaves slightly faster than his pace. **This is the only permitted crack and it costs the whole series something. Spend it once, late.**

### Ten rules — would never / always

**He would never:**
1. Run, flinch, duck, or turn to look at what's behind him.
2. Ask what is happening.
3. Correct a name — his or anyone's.
4. Accept credit. *("I didn't. I brought extra napkins.")*
5. Appear in a scene that needs him to save someone. He is not a rescue.

**He always:**
6. Arrives exactly when the chaos peaks — never early, never late, and never acknowledged as remarkable.
7. Checks the phone before the knock. 23%.
8. Says the name on the order, almost right.
9. Leaves the extra napkins and mentions them.
10. Walks away at the same pace he arrived, without looking back — **on schedule, not cool.**

## Arc hooks

1. **"The Silent Sixty"** *(pure-visual cutaway — already half-specced in `ideas/cat-pov.md`)* — Sixty seconds, no dialog until the last line. He delivers through three separate disasters — a laser battle, a flooded lobby, a hallway that is the wrong length — and says one thing at the final door: "Extra napkins are in the bag." Sibling piece to Cat POV; proves the series can hold without a joke being said.
2. **"Out For Delivery"** *(EyeWash programming block)* — Ninety seconds of him walking somewhere. No plot. Weirdly calming. Airs at erratic hours. This is the single cheapest recurring asset in the show and it should exist by the end of the season.
3. **"Perfect Attendance"** *(Ep4 tail)* — The HOA gives him an award for perfect attendance at events he was never invited to. He accepts it, thanks them, and hands Dale a burrito. Dale gavels. *(Seeded in `notes/scratchpad.md`.)*
4. **"Nine Is the Long One"** — The *Out For Delivery* block, once, with the "Route" monologue above laid over it. He is never shown speaking to anyone. The audience decides whether he was talking to himself, to them, or to the radio. **Do not resolve which.**
5. **"The Crossing"** — Uniform #8. Thirty seconds. He stops a laser battle with a handheld stop sign so three kids can cross. The serpents wait. Nobody ever refers to this again.
6. **"Twenty-Three Percent"** *(finale-adjacent, use with extreme care)* — One frame, one time, the phone reads something else. **Nobody in the scene sees it.** No line, no music sting, no reaction. If this is ever used, it is the last thing the series does with him.

## Do / Don't

**Do**

- Put him in the doorway at the exact peak and let the scene continue around him.
- Let him be sincere. Every line is literally true and delivered as such.
- Let other characters project onto him. They may decide he's an angel, a fixed point, or a guy. The show never rules.
- Match-cut his stride between uniforms. Same shoes, same pace — that's the whole gag.
- Give him one new almost-right name each appearance.

**Don't**

- Don't explain him. Not with a line, not with a look, not with a slow zoom.
- Don't let him react. Not to lasers, not to a collapsed ceiling, not to a cherub on the floor. He steps over it.
- Don't make him wise. He is not enlightened, he is **post-surprise**, and those read completely differently on camera.
- Don't make him the hero. No rescue, no key item, no plot function beyond arriving.
- Don't let the camera find the name tag.
- Don't let anyone correct the name, and don't let anyone ask how he knew.
- Don't change the battery percentage.
