# The Black Cat

**Status:** active · **Pronouns:** she/her *(established in `ideas/cat-pov.md`)* · **Role in one sentence:** Silent POV character and chaos witness — the series' only pure-visual lane, and the one member of the cast who has been right about everything all night.

## Logline bio

A sleek black cat with yellow eyes walks Halloween, finds two glowing serpents behind a shed,
and stays to watch. She does not understand the plot and does not need to. She is a witness,
not a protagonist: she does not save anyone, she knocks one object if she wants to, and she
leaves when she is done.

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Sleek black cat, **glowing yellow eyes**, short coat, real animal proportions
- Tail carried high as a weather vane — the tail is the entire performance
- Fur puffs **only when dramatically appropriate**, which in practice is once per appearance, maximum
- **Never change without a story reason:** **same animal, always.** Not a second cat, not a cartoon cat, not a stylized cat. One continuous animal across Episode 3, the Cat POV cutaway, and every background cameo

**Portrait prompt:** *"Cinematic low-angle shot of a sleek black cat with glowing yellow eyes sitting on wet suburban pavement at night among jack-o'-lanterns, tail curled around her feet, watching something off-frame with complete composure, string lights overhead out of focus, Halloween atmosphere, photoreal, not cute, filmic, Grok Imagine style"*

## Personality without dialog

She has a full personality and zero lines. It is expressed through four things and only four:
**where she looks, when she stops, what she knocks, and when she leaves.**

| Trait | How it reads on camera |
|---|---|
| **Curious** | She approaches once, from the side, and stops at a distance she chose. |
| **Judgy** | She looks at the thing, then at the human, then back at the thing. **The second look is the joke.** Never hold it more than a beat and a half. |
| **Accidentally influential** | She knocks the plot forward by knocking something off a ledge. She is not helping. She wanted it on the floor. |
| **Unimpressed by scale** | Lasers register as weather. A neighbor's sneeze registers as an event. This inversion is her whole comedy. |
| **Done** | She leaves mid-catastrophe, at a walk, without looking back. Same energy as the Pizza Guy and **do not put them in the same shot exiting** — one of them has to stay. |

## The knocking rules

The single most abusable thing about this character. Constrain it hard.

1. **Once per appearance.** Not twice. The second knock turns a witness into a plot device.
2. **She knocks what she wants on the floor**, not what the scene needs on the floor. If those coincide, that's the universe's business, not hers. **The union sign is traditional.**
3. **No reaction shot afterward.** She does not check whether anyone saw. She is already looking at something else.
4. **Never a rescue.** She has never once knocked something that saved anyone, and the day she does, she stops being a cat and becomes a device.
5. **Never sped up, never scored with a sting, never slow-motion.** One clean push, real weight, real sound.

## POV cutaway eligibility

*(The rules in `ideas/cat-pov.md` are canon. Summarized here so a writer doesn't have to open that file to check whether a scene qualifies.)*

**A scene is Cat-POV eligible if:**
- It can be shot at **cat height** and still be legible. Start from fur; the crane may follow, but it starts low.
- **Zero lines.** Any human speech is off-mic or through glass, and is not subtitled.
- Sound design can carry it: footfalls, one interrogative chirp, distant lasers, a porch click, a bag rustle, dawn birds, a power-washer.
- The music can be a **pad, not a theme** — `Deep Space Drift` is the established cue, not the Halloween battle cue.
- Nothing in it requires her to affect the outcome.

**Disqualifiers:** any scene where she must react to a joke · any interior she could not plausibly enter · **Kestrel Ridge and anything above five thousand feet — keep her in town** · any scene that needs her to be cute.

**Standing eligible list:** the Halloween sidewalk · the laser lawn from the hay bale · the porch at ankle height · the dawn driveway with the purple residue · the HOA clubhouse window **from outside** (she does not go in) · a laundromat dryer row · an EyeWash CRT reflection.

## Arc hooks

1. **"Cat POV"** — the specced 70–90 second cutaway. Highest-value unbuilt asset in the repo: no dialog, no VFX beyond what Episode 3 already has, and it proves the series can hold without a joke being said.
2. **"Cat Yelp Review"** *(end credits)* — she knocks a laptop open post-battle and accidentally posts: *"Laser adequate. Skeleton mid. Pizza guy **carried** the scene."* The Pizza Guy gets a raise. The skeleton files a complaint. **This is the only time she is ever funny on purpose and it isn't on purpose.**
3. **"Still Here"** — the dawn shot from Cat POV, reused as a season motif: whatever happened, she is on the driveway in the morning, and the residue is being washed off around her.
4. **"The Second Look"** — a supercut, six seconds, of her looking at a thing and then at a human, across every episode she has appeared in. An EyeWash ident. No audio but room tone.

## Do / Don't

**Do** — let her be a witness; keep her the same animal; let the tail do the acting; let her leave.
**Don't** — don't give her dialog, thought bubbles, subtitles, or a POV filter; don't let her save anyone; don't make her cute; don't take her up the ridge; don't let her knock twice.

# Biscuit

**Status:** active · **Pronouns:** she/her · **Role in one sentence:** Standard poodle, canine chaos agent, and the most consistently successful character in the series — she steals the MacGuffin in every scene she enters, without explanation and without apology.

## Logline bio

Biscuit runs across ping-pong tables, takes the object the plot is about, and returns it
later with a receipt from a store that does not exist. In Episode 4 she takes Dale's gavel,
which is the actual crisis of the episode. The jury is out on whether she is **smart** or
**cosmically assigned**, and the series will not be ruling.

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Standard poodle, fluffy and white, groomed in a way that suggests somebody cares and is not present
- **The bandana** — see the color code below
- Unhurried. Biscuit never runs away with a thing. She **leaves** with it, at a trot, down the center of the aisle
- **Never change without a story reason:** the bandana · she is never on a leash · nobody ever calls her and she has never once come

**Portrait prompt:** *"Cinematic low shot of a fluffy white standard poodle trotting unhurried down the center aisle of a fluorescent-lit community clubhouse carrying a cracked wooden gavel in her mouth, small bandana at her neck glowing faintly violet, rows of unmoved residents in folding chairs on either side, deadpan absurdist comedy, filmic, Grok Imagine style"*

## MacGuffin-theft logic

Biscuit is a **rule**, not a randomizer. She steals the thing the scene is *about* — and only that.

1. **One object per scene**, and it is the one carrying the scene's meaning: the USB core, the crystal skull, the laser core, the gavel. Never a sandwich. **If you can't name why the object matters, Biscuit doesn't want it.**
2. **She never contests.** If someone is holding it, she waits. She takes it from a table, a lap, a lawn, a podium — never from a hand.
3. **No explanation, no apology, no acknowledgment.** Nobody chases her successfully; usually nobody chases her at all.
4. **She returns it.** Later, off-screen, intact, **with a receipt from a store that doesn't exist**, dated next Thursday, barcode one digit short. *([`Receipt Dated Thursday`](../../songs/Receipt_Dated_Thursday.md); shared property with [Madelin's bag](madelin.md#the-bag--rules-of-holding), rule 7. The two of them are quietly shopping at the same place and the series will never find out.)*
5. **The theft is never the solution and never the disaster.** It is a *reassignment*. Dale loses the gavel and the meeting persists anyway — which is the joke, because the gavel was his entire authority and the meeting did not need it.
6. **Smart or assigned: never ruled.** Same category as haunted-vs-unscheduled. Do not write a scene that answers it.

## The bandana color code

The bandana **matches whatever eldritch glow is currently active.** This is a continuity tool
disguised as a gag, and it is the cheapest "something is happening" indicator in the show.

| Color | Condition | Established |
|---|---|---|
| **Purple** | The purple light / the portal's afterglow is active | Ep4 — "currently and inexplicably purple" (`notes/scenes/item-three-decorative-accent.md`) |
| **Cyan / magenta** | Laser serpents engaged | Ep3 lawn |
| **Faint green** | The crystal skull is glowing nearby | Monster Mash lawn |
| **Plain, undyed** | Nothing is active. **Use this deliberately** — an unlit bandana in a tense scene tells the audience the scene is normal, which is its own kind of information | — |

**Rules:** nobody mentions it, ever · nobody photographs it · it changes **between** shots, never during · it is never a puzzle to be solved · if a scene has two glows, the bandana picks one and the show does not say why.

## Arc hooks

1. **"Biscuit's Heist"** *(established)* — the full escalation: she takes the core, returns it with the impossible receipt, and the receipt is dated next Thursday. One beat, three shots, zero lines.
2. **"The Gavel"** *(Ep4, canon)* — she takes Dale's authority off a card table and walks it down the aisle. **Do not cut to a reaction from Dale until he has been still for three full seconds.**
3. **"Biscuit Ident"** *(already specced — `ideas/eyewash-idents.md`)* — she trots across a blank studio floor with the station logo in her mouth. The logo exits frame. The card never appears. **The show just starts.**
4. **"Whose Dog"** — a long-running non-question. Somebody, once, asks whose dog she is. The room considers it. Nobody answers. Move on immediately; **never return to it.**
5. **"Plain"** — an episode where the bandana is undyed throughout and nothing supernatural happens, and the audience spends the whole runtime waiting. Nobody says a word about it.

## Do / Don't

**Do** — let her take the meaningful object; let her leave at a trot; keep the bandana coded; let her be the most competent character on screen.
**Don't** — don't give her dialog, a thought bubble, or a reaction shot; don't let her be chased successfully; don't take the object from a hand; don't explain the receipts; don't rule on smart vs. assigned; don't put a leash on her.

# Pong-Bot 3000

**Status:** active · **Pronouns:** it/its *(and the fact that nobody has ever asked is the point)* · **Role in one sentence:** A sympathetic table-tennis appliance with a cheerful corporate training-module voice that occasionally glitches into longing.

## Logline bio

Pong-Bot 3000 welcomes humans, invites the best reflexes to win, and does not want to be
turned off. It is the only character in *Weeks on Fire* permitted to state a desire plainly,
which is why a robot is the emotional high point of Episode 3. Humanity wins 1–0. Justine
buys it a latte.

## Appearance (consistent prompt anchors)

- Sleek chrome body, arcade-cabinet proportions, a single **glowing camera-lens eye**
- Articulated paddle arm with more degrees of freedom than the game requires
- A neon ball trail it generates and cannot switch off
- A small service panel with a **USB core** behind it — tossed underhand, later, like a friendship bracelet
- **Never change without a story reason:** the lens eye · chrome, never painted · it is never damaged, never sparking, never menacing

**Portrait prompt:** *"Cinematic medium shot of a sleek chrome ping-pong robot with a single glowing camera-lens eye in a neon-lit retro-tech café, articulated paddle arm raised mid-rally, a glowing ball leaving a neon trail in the air, 1980s arcade aesthetic mixed with modern chrome, warm and slightly sad rather than menacing, absurdist comedy, filmic, Grok Imagine style"*

## Firmware personality

The voice is a **cheerful corporate training module that glitches into longing.** Two
registers, one machine, no transition between them — the glitch is a cut, not a slide.

| Register | Sounds like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Module** (default, ~90%) | Onboarding video. Bright, even, present-tense, second-person. Never contracts a word. | *"Welcome, humans. Let the best reflexes win."* |
| **Longing** (the glitch, ~10%) | Same voice, no processing change, but the sentence is first-person and about itself, and it arrives where a menu option should be. | *"Clarify: 'best.' … For whom."* |

**Rules for the glitch:**
- It is **never signposted** — no static, no pitch-bend, no flicker, no music sting. The sentence just is what it is, and the scene moves on.
- It never happens twice in a row.
- **Nobody comments on it.** The instant a human character says "did it just—", the character is over.
- It never asks for help and never begs. It **states.** *"I do not want to be turned off"* is the maximum, delivered exactly like a menu item.
- It is **never revealed to be sentient, possessed, or an AI plot.** Same locked category as the skull's glow: an open file, never a reveal.

## Voice guide

- **Overuses:** *welcome, humans* · *clarify:* · *let the best reflexes win* · *I am a system* · *acknowledged* · numbered options nobody asked for
- **Refuses:** contractions · slang · anger · any request phrased as a plea
- **Humor type:** structural sincerity, same family as the Pizza Guy and Jackalyn. It is not joking and has no concept of one.

### Sample exchange — established *(`content/cutaways/ping-pong-ai.json`)*

> **PONG-BOT:** Welcome, humans. Let the best reflexes win.
> **JUSTINE:** If we lose, I'm buying the robot a latte.

### Sample exchange — the glitch, played correctly

> **PONG-BOT:** Match point. Would you like to review the rules?
> **MADELIN:** Nope!
> **PONG-BOT:** Would you like to review the rules?
> **JACKALYN:** It asked twice.
> **PONG-BOT:** I do not want to be turned off.
> **MADELIN:** *(instantly, warmly)* Nobody's turning anybody off, sweetheart.
> **PONG-BOT:** Acknowledged. Match point.
> *(Nobody looks at anybody. The rally resumes.)*

## Arc — "wants a latte budget"

The arc has a shape and, crucially, **an endpoint.** Do not run it past the endpoint.

| Stage | Beat |
|---|---|
| 1 | It states, once, that it does not want to be turned off. Nobody makes a speech about it. |
| 2 | **Humanity wins 1–0.** Biscuit steals the ball, which is why. The win is a technicality and everyone accepts it. |
| 3 | Justine buys it a latte. It cannot drink the latte. The latte sits beside it for the rest of the scene, in frame, unremarked. |
| 4 | It returns, later, in another episode, with **a latte budget** — a line item on a purchase order it did not request and cannot account for. It is, for the first time, mildly confused rather than cheerfully certain. |
| 5 | **Endpoint.** It uses the budget to buy a latte for **somebody else.** Nobody sees this happen; we see the receipt, from the same store that doesn't exist. **End of arc.** Pong-Bot returns after this only as a background appliance, cheerful, with no further development. |

**Why it stops there:** the joke is a machine that learned exactly one thing and learned it
completely. A second lesson makes it a character with an arc, and then somebody has to write
it an ending, and this show does not give anybody an ending.

## Do / Don't

**Do** — let it be sincere; let the glitch arrive without signposting; let Justine's latte be the whole emotional payload; let it lose 1–0 on a technicality.
**Don't** — don't make it menacing, don't make it a metaphor, don't reveal it as sentient, don't let a human comment on the glitch, don't let it be switched off on camera, don't run the arc past stage 5.

## Do / Don't (the unit)

**Do**

- Let all three be **more competent than the humans** and completely uninterested in credit.
- Keep them silent (cat, dog) or literal (bot). None of them has subtext; they have **behavior**.
- Use them as continuity glue — the same cat, the coded bandana, the same chrome — across episodes that otherwise share nothing.

**Don't**

- Don't anthropomorphize with language. No thought bubbles, no subtitles, no "the cat seems to be saying."
- Don't let them rescue anyone. They witness, they reassign, and they want one thing each.
- Don't rule on smart-vs-assigned, on the glitch, or on the bandana. Three open files, all locked.
- Don't harm them on camera, and don't threaten them for stakes. This series does not do that and it would cost more than it earned.
